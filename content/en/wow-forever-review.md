Some games change how people think about an entire genre. World of Warcraft in 2004 was one, and millions of players, plenty of them Israelis who spent nights on slow ADSL lines, still remember riding into Westfall alone for the first time. Since then the game has changed many times over: expansions, flying, automatic group finders, everything faster and more convenient. And, according to more than a few veterans, less interesting.

**World of Warcraft: Forever** is Blizzard's answer to them. It came out on November 4 (1 AM on November 5, Israel time), and it isn't another Classic release that recreates 2004 one to one. Blizzard describes it as a separate branch of Azeroth that starts from the original level 1 to 60 world and grows in a different direction, without the expansions that followed. I went through everything Blizzard has published and what players and critics are saying three weeks in. Here's my verdict.

![World of Warcraft: Forever at a glance: monthly sub, Heroic and Epic packs, release date, new content and languages](img/posts/wow-forever-glance-en.webp "The short version. Graphic: TechSite")

## What it actually is

The starting point is "Year 1" of the original World of Warcraft: before the Dark Portal reopened and before Northrend. Same map, same cities, same long runs between zones. But Blizzard has added a lot on top:

- **More than 1,000 new quests** across the journey from level 1 to 60
- **New and reimagined zones:** Mount Hyjal, Shen'dralas, the Riverglades and Zephras Isle
- **Nine new five-player dungeons**, from level 13 to 60. In the Eastern Kingdoms: Hall of Thanes, Ruins of Lordaeron, Excavation Site, City of Dalaran, The Drowned City and Krol'dok Stronghold. In Kalimdor: Alcaz Prison, Blackmaw Hold and Shaper's Terrace
- **Raids** open on December 9, per the official roadmap

## Combinations that never existed

If you waited twenty years for a dwarf shaman, relax. Forever opens combinations the original game didn't allow: for the Alliance, Human Hunter, Dwarf Shaman and Gnome Priest. For the Horde, Orc Mage, Troll Warlock and Undead Paladin. The Undead Paladin is probably the one people talk about most, and Blizzard even built a new Warcraft III campaign around the first Forsaken Paladin.

## Skyborne: the race that costs extra

Here's the part that sparked the most argument. The only new race, the **Skyborne**, can join either the Alliance or the Horde, with different racial abilities for each, and starts in its own zone, Zephras Isle, at levels 1 to 12. It can be a Druid, Hunter, Rogue or Warrior, plus Mage on the Alliance side or Shaman on the Horde side.

But to play one, you need to buy a pack:

- **Skyborne Heroic Pack:** $29.99 (about ₪101). The race, the starting zone, a mount and some cosmetics
- **Skyborne Epic Pack:** $59.99 (about ₪203). Everything in Heroic, plus 30 days of game time and more cosmetics
- **Warcraft Forever Collection:** $79.99 (about ₪270), only until January 11, 2027. Also includes Warcraft III: Reforged and the new Forsaken Kingdom campaign

My view is simple: paying the price of a full game for one race, on top of a monthly sub, is a lot. The good news is that everything else, the quests, zones, dungeons and raids, comes with the regular subscription. You can skip the packs entirely and still get all of Forever.

## The world looks different

One thing players are most excited about is the lighting. It isn't a filter slapped over the old game. It was hand-built zone by zone: blue shadows in Durotar, a sickly green in Felwood, thick mist in Duskwood, lightning that lights up the stones of the Blasted Lands. Draw distance has grown, and from Durotar you can see the World Tree on top of Hyjal. Dun Morogh and Winterspring get snowstorms that cut visibility to nothing.

On first login you choose between two presets. **Classic** gives a more original look: standard-definition character models, no quest markers on the map and no transmog. **Enhanced** turns on HD models, quest markers, a single bag for everything and other conveniences. You can switch later, and each character can use HD or SD models separately. It's a smart way to settle the endless argument between "leave it as it was" and "I'm not going back to 2004 without a quest map".

## The friction is the point

This is where Forever does something modern games have nearly forgotten. It doesn't try to be convenient. Lots of running between places. Quest chains that send you to the far side of the continent. Elite monsters you can't kill alone. And Blizzard moved most dungeon XP into dungeon quests rather than the monsters inside, so it pays to go in with the quests instead of running the same place five times.

The result, judging by most of the accounts I've read, is that people are talking to each other again. Asking for help in chat, teaming up with strangers to kill an elite, sticking around to help after their own quest is done. Blizzard added a campfire mechanic that nudges this along. PC Gamer's reviewer described tossing 15 gold to a stranger who was short of money for a mount, simply because so many people had been kind to him along the way. That's the kind of moment Forever is built to create.

If you want to hit 60 fast and start raiding, this is less your game. It's for people who want the journey to be the fun part.

## What else is new

- **Legacy system:** account-wide progression that doesn't add power, just small perks like a little heal when you kill an enemy or more resources when gathering. It encourages alts, but everything can be unlocked on a single character
- **Reworked professions:** crafting gives good gear all the way up, not just a couple of items at the end, and there are tasks that unlock new recipes
- **Items with uses:** an axe that lets you mine, boots that let you walk underwater, a sword that slows your fall
- **PvP:** Honor and Rank points, and a new 15 vs 15 battleground, Darkspear Islands, for levels 30 to 60
- **Two-part names:** every character gets a first name and a second name, and the combination must be unique. You can finally get the name you always wanted
- **Rulesets instead of realms:** Normal, PvP, Roleplay and Roleplay-PvP. A Hardcore mode, where death is permanent, is coming later. Coordinate with friends before you create a character, or you won't be able to play together

## Good to know before you start

- **No Hebrew.** Like all of World of Warcraft, the interface and text come in English and other languages, not Hebrew
- **PC only**, through Battle.net
- **You need a subscription.** $14.99 (about ₪51) a month. There's no one-off purchase, and the sub covers regular WoW too
- **Time.** This game eats whole evenings. PC Gamer's reviewer logged 230 hours in under three weeks. You don't have to go that far

## The bottom line

8.5/10

World of Warcraft: Forever is more than nostalgia. Blizzard kept 2004's difficulty, the long runs and the need to talk to people, and added over a thousand quests, nine dungeons, new zones, gorgeous lighting and combinations players have wanted for twenty years. It's slow on purpose, and that's exactly its strength. The paid race leaves a bad taste, and the early game feels too close to the original until you reach the genuinely new content. But for the price of a sub, it's one of the best adventures you can buy at the end of 2026.
:::

**What I liked**

- Over 1,000 quests and nine new dungeons, included in the sub
- Hand-built lighting for every zone
- A choice between a classic and a modern look, plus HD or SD models per character
- New race-class combinations, including the Undead Paladin
- Design that gets players talking to each other again
- Two-part names and rulesets instead of a realm list

**What I liked less**

- The only new race costs at least $29.99 on top of the sub
- The big new zones arrive only midway and late: the Riverglades at level 38, Hyjal near 60
- Lots of running around, which isn't for everyone
- No Hebrew, and no console version
