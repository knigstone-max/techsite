Most Israeli water bars give you cold and hot water, and you still fish the ice out of the freezer. Electra Consumer Products wants one box on the counter to handle all three. It's called the Electra MHC 4YI-AQUA, it costs ₪1,290 (about $414), and the company says roughly 15,000 have sold with almost no advertising.

The gadget is the small part of the story. Speaking to Walla this week, Electra framed it as one example of how it now does business: buy straight from the factory, skip the middlemen, and sell under its own brand in its own stores.

## What it does

Going by the Shekem Electric product page, it's a tank-fed bar with a removable 3-litre reservoir, so there's nothing to plumb in. Water goes through five filtration stages, and a filter lasts 30 days or 100 litres, whichever comes first.

- **Cold water:** down to 5°C using a compressor. The first chill takes 22 minutes.
- **Hot water:** anywhere from 50 to 95°C, with a child lock.
- **Ice:** 8 cubes every 6 minutes, with the first batch after 40 minutes, for up to 8.6 kg a day. The bin holds 700 ml, or 56 to 80 cubes.
- **Also:** a UV lamp, 9.8 kg of weight and about 37 cm of height. It comes in grey or black.

It's rated at 2,200 W, most of that for heating. Electra gives a one-year warranty, and delivery is ₪149 (about $48).

## Running costs

Shekem Electric lists it at ₪1,290, down from ₪1,490. Electra puts filters at about ₪132 a year (around $42), which is far below a plumbed-in bar subscription. The catch is that you fill the tank by hand, and a busy household will be doing that several times a day.

Electra also says a version that brews capsule coffee is coming next year. It's aiming for about 33,000 units a year by 2029.

## The bigger picture: buying direct

Electra Consumer Products also owns the shops. It runs Machsanei Hashmal and Shekem Electric, 100 branches in all including Shekem Duty Free. The company says its share of a roughly ₪8 billion-a-year market grew from 22% in 2022 to 28% in 2025.

What changed is how it sources stock. A few years ago, 57% of the white goods in its chains came in through direct import. Today it's 70%, and sales of those products are up 42.9% in two years. Yoram Badash, co-CEO of Electra Retail, put it simply: when the product goes from the factory to the customer's home with fewer layers in between, the saving lands with the customer.

To make the point, Electra shared a handful of price comparisons. Keep in mind it picked them itself:

![Infographic: Electra's comparisons for a washing machine, two fridges and a coffee machine against Haier and Nespresso](img/posts/electra-direct-import-prices-en.webp "The comparisons Electra chose to show. Graphic: TechSite")

Electra also says average prices fell in the first nine months of 2026: about 16% for fridges, 14% for washing machines, 13% for dryers and 7% for freezers. There's no proof all of that is down to Electra. Rival importers are bringing in more from China too, and AliExpress and Amazon put pressure on everyone. Still, the house brand is clearly selling. By Electra's count, from January to August it had about a third of freezer sales, a quarter of washing machine sales and 22% of fridge sales.

## What it means if you're shopping

If you're buying a washing machine or fridge, put Electra's house brand on the list next to the familiar names. The gap can run into thousands of shekels. Just check the warranty, who handles service, and what owners say about that exact model. Price is only part of the deal.

As for the water bar: if you want ice on tap and don't want anything installed under the sink, ₪1,290 is fair. Measure your counter first, though. It's about 42 cm deep.
