Earlier this month we [reviewed the Beam Ultra](/techsite/en/posts/sonos-beam-ultra-review-israel/), the soundbar Sonos launched in September. The headphones announced alongside it have now reached Israel too. The **Sonos Ace Ultra** is on sale in Israel at a list price of **₪1,699** (about $511), after a pre-sale at ₪1,599 (about $481), according to Bizportal. In the US it costs $449 (about ₪1,493), the same launch price as the original Sonos Ace from 2024.

## The main feature: the sound moves with you

Every pair of Bluetooth headphones connects to a phone. The Ace Ultra also connects to your home over Wi-Fi. Sonos calls it **Headphone Linking**, and it works like this:

- Music is playing on a Sonos speaker in the living room. You press and hold the content key on the headphones, and within up to five seconds the music stops on the speaker and continues in your ears. The headphones pick the nearest Sonos product in the room you're in.
- Press and hold again near a speaker, and the sound goes back out of the headphones.
- No phone in range is needed, and nothing has to be paired over Bluetooth.

It works with music started from the Sonos app, with line-in sources and with your local music library. It needs Wi-Fi, the Sonos app on iOS or Android, and a compatible speaker. The list is long and includes the Arc, Arc Ultra, every Beam, the Era 100 and 300, the Roam and Move models, the Ray, the Five and the Play. Speakers used as rear surrounds in a home theatre setup don't count.

The catch: **Headphone Linking is still in Early Access**. Sonos itself says it's an opt-in feature still being refined. Anyone who remembers Sonos's app troubles in 2024 knows that "early" at Sonos can last a while.

## TV sound, for your ears only

Separately, you can send the TV's audio from a compatible soundbar to the headphones, Dolby Atmos included, with head tracking that keeps the sound in place when you turn your head. **TrueCinema** tunes the experience to the room and the soundbar. That's the use case that sells these headphones in many Israeli homes: a film in the living room at night while everyone else is asleep.

## What's inside

- **Driver**: a 40 mm driver designed by Sonos in each ear cup
- **Noise cancelling**: Adaptive ANC with ten microphones, which also handle calls, Aware mode and TrueCinema
- **Battery**: 1,060 mAh, up to 35 hours with ANC or Aware mode on. Three minutes of charging gives up to three hours of listening, and a full charge takes up to three hours
- **Connections**: Bluetooth 6.0, Wi-Fi for the system link, and both USB-C and 3.5 mm wired audio
- **Lossless**: over USB-C, or over Bluetooth with Qualcomm aptX on a supported device. iPhones don't support aptX, so iPhone owners get lossless only by cable
- **Adaptive EQ**: adjusts the sound for fit, glasses and hair
- **Weight**: 322 g
- **Colours**: black, soft white, agave and sand
- **In the box**: a travel case, a USB-C cable and a USB-C to 3.5 mm cable

## What the full setup costs

The headphones work fine on their own with a phone, like any premium headphones. But the reason to buy Sonos over Sony or Apple is the system around it. The Beam Ultra sells in Israel for ₪2,899 (about $872; $699 in the US), so the soundbar plus the headphones together come to ₪4,598 (about $1,382), according to Bizportal's figures. If you already have a recent Sonos soundbar or speaker, you only need the headphones.

## Who it's for

If you already have Sonos at home, the Ace Ultra is the first pair of headphones that's genuinely part of the system and not just another Bluetooth device. If you don't, you're paying ₪1,699 for very good noise-cancelling headphones with long battery life, and you should compare them with the alternatives from Sony and Apple on comfort, calls and noise cancelling before you decide. Either way, try them on first: at 322 g, fit matters.
