In Israel, WhatsApp isn't just another app. It's the kindergarten parents' group, the building committee, the extended family, work, and the army friends who've been planning a reunion for three years. So when WhatsApp adds a feature, it changes something for almost everyone.

November was a busy month. The most requested feature, scheduled messages, started reaching everyone, along with a handful of other changes, some already on your phone and some still in beta. Here's what's new, how to turn it on, and why it's useful here in particular.

![WhatsApp's five November 2026 updates: scheduled messages, Events, 1,500-member groups, stickers with sound and cloud backup, and which are still in beta](img/posts/whatsapp-nov-2026-features-en.webp "What's new and where it stands. Graphic: TechSite")

## 1. Scheduled messages

WhatsApp calls this one of its most requested features, and with good reason. Until now, if you wanted a message to land at a specific time, you set a reminder and hoped you'd remember.

**How to schedule:**

1. Write the message as usual, in a one-to-one chat or a group
2. Press and hold the send button
3. Pick a date and time, anywhere from 10 minutes to 14 days ahead
4. That's it. It arrives at the time you chose and looks like any other message to the recipient

You can schedule text, photos and videos. There's a screen listing everything you've scheduled, where you can delete a message before it goes out if you change your mind. And according to WhatsApp, the message is delivered even if your phone is off or the battery is dead, and it stays end-to-end encrypted.

**Why it's useful here:**

- **A birthday message at midnight** without staying up. Or to a friend abroad, landing at midnight their time, not yours
- **"Good luck on the exam"** that you remembered at 11 PM, timed to arrive when they wake up
- **Holiday greetings** to the whole family, written in advance and sent on time
- **A reminder to the parents' group** about a payment or a school trip, arriving in the morning instead of the middle of the night

WhatsApp announced it on November 25, and it's rolling out globally over the coming weeks. If you don't see it yet, update the app and give it a little time.

## 2. Events, even for people without WhatsApp

Events have been in WhatsApp for a while, but the early-November update makes them genuinely useful. **The big change: you can invite people who don't have WhatsApp.** Create an event, add a cover photo, share a link, and the guest can see the details and RSVP without downloading anything.

What else is new:

- **An Events home:** under Settings → Events there's a list of every event, ones you created and ones you were invited to, past and upcoming
- **Custom reminders** and the option to add a plus-one
- **Privacy:** your phone number isn't shown to guests you don't know, and people without WhatsApp only see the address after they RSVP

![WhatsApp's new Events screens: editing an invite with a cover image, the RSVP list and an invite link for people who aren't on WhatsApp](img/posts/whatsapp-events-official.webp "Events in WhatsApp, including link invites. Image: WhatsApp")

In Israel it's a natural fit for a kids' birthday party, a Hanukkah evening with friends, or a residents' meeting where there's always one neighbour who isn't in the group. The update is rolling out to everyone over these weeks.

## 3. Groups of up to 1,500 members

WhatsApp is raising the group cap from 1,024 to 1,500 members, according to WABetaInfo and Engadget. For most people that sounds pointless, but anyone running a neighbourhood group, a whole school or a community knows the moment you have to open "Group 2". It's rolling out gradually on Android and iPhone.

Alongside it, large groups recently got a few improvements: pinned messages that last longer, filtering chats by mentions and replies, and the option for existing members to share message history with people the admin adds.

## 4. Stickers with sound (beta)

This is still in beta, but it's already reaching some users. In the sticker picker, tap Create sticker, choose a video from your gallery, and WhatsApp turns it into an animated sticker. An audio button at the top of the editor lets you keep the original sound, pick a preset effect, import a file or record a voice note.

Sound stickers are muted by default, so they won't disrupt the group. Anyone who wants to hear one taps the speaker icon, and the audio loops three times and stops. According to GSMArena it's in Android beta 2.26.47.1, and on some iPhones too.

## 5. WhatsApp's own cloud backup (Android beta)

Until now, WhatsApp backups on Android went to Google Drive and ate into your storage there. Beta version 2.26.46.4 adds a new option: **WhatsApp cloud storage**, with **2GB free**, still end-to-end encrypted.

If you need more:

- **WhatsApp Plus:** $2.99 (about ₪10) a month for 100GB
- **Meta One:** from $7.99 (about ₪27) a month, up to 4TB

According to 9to5Google, if you cancel, WhatsApp keeps your backup for at least four months, even if it's over 2GB. And you can keep backing up to Google Drive as before. The important caveat: there's no date for a wide release, and the published prices are in dollars. When it reaches Israel, check the local price before you sign up.

**Tip:** 2GB isn't much if you keep every video from the family group. Under Settings → Chats → Chat backup you can leave videos out of the backup, which shrinks it a lot.

## The bottom line

Scheduled messages and Events are the changes you'll notice this month, and both are easy to use. Bigger groups mostly help community admins. Sound stickers and cloud backup are on the way. To get everything first, make sure automatic updates are on in your app store.
