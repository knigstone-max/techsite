Another Israeli cyber story that ran through New York and San Francisco and came back with a large check. Upwind Security, the cloud and AI security company led by CEO and co-founder Amiram Shachar, said on October 28 that it closed a $400 million Series C (about ₪1.28 billion) at a $3.8 billion valuation (about ₪12.1 billion).

![Upwind Series C: $400M, $3.8B valuation, more than $800M raised to date](img/posts/upwind-series-c-numbers-en.webp "The numbers behind walking away from Datadog. Graphic: TechSite")

## Who led, and who was already in

Bessemer Venture Partners led the round, with Canapi Ventures and existing backers including Cyberstarts, Greylock Partners, Craft Ventures, Leaders Fund, Salesforce Ventures, TCV and others. It comes nine months after a $250 million Series B at a $1.5 billion valuation. Total capital raised now tops $800 million.

## Why the round nearly fell apart

Per TechCrunch, the raise nearly collapsed after Datadog, the public observability company, tried to buy Upwind. The offer did not include a premium above the $3.8 billion valuation set by the venture investors, and Upwind chose to stay independent. In plain terms: the company thinks it is worth more than Datadog was ready to pay today.

## What Upwind actually does

Upwind focuses on runtime security: spotting and fixing threats and exposures while cloud services are live, not only in periodic scans. The platform ties together context from workloads, APIs and AI agents, and runs on AWS, Azure, Google Cloud and Oracle. TechCrunch reported that the company has been adding $20 million to $30 million in annualized recurring revenue each quarter and is on track to end 2026 around $90 million ARR.

## The Israel angle

Amiram Shachar is an Israeli founder who already sold one company in a large exit: Spot.io went to NetApp for $450 million. Upwind was founded in 2022 and counts large enterprise customers such as Siemens, Roku and Carvana. In a stretch where Israeli cyber startups keep raising heavy rounds, this one also comes with an explicit decision not to sell.
