Smart rings have stopped being a curiosity. If you hate sleeping with a watch on, a ring covers a lot of the same ground: heart rate, sleep and recovery, on something you barely notice. Since yesterday, November 16, Israeli buyers have one more official option. Bug, the local importer for Ultrahuman, has started selling the Ring PRO, the Indian company's third-generation ring.

## Price

Bug's launch price is **₪1,499** (about $465), valid until December 2. After that it goes up to **₪1,799** (about $558). There's no monthly subscription, which is one of the clearest ways Ultrahuman stands apart from rivals that charge a monthly fee for some of the data.

For comparison, Ultrahuman's US site lists the ring with its charging case at **$479** (about ₪1,544), and right now an early Black Friday deal there brings it down to $378.41 (about ₪1,220). Keep in mind that US prices exclude sales tax, while the Israeli price includes VAT and an importer warranty. So Bug's launch price is roughly in line with the US list price. The regular ₪1,799 price is a few hundred shekels more.

## What's in the ring

According to Bug and Ultrahuman:

- **Battery:** up to 15 days per charge, by the company's numbers. CEO Mohit Kumar claims that's three to four times what competitors manage. As with any battery claim, real life depends on what you switch on.
- **Body:** a single piece of titanium, in four finishes: Bionic Gold, Space Silver, Aster Black and Raw Titanium.
- **Processor:** a dual-core chip that runs machine learning on the ring itself, plus a redesigned heart-rate sensor array meant to clean up the signal during sleep.
- **Storage:** up to 250 days of data kept on the ring, even without your phone nearby.
- **ProRelease:** a mechanism that lets the ring be cut off easily if your finger swells or gets hurt. It sounds minor until you've had a ring stuck on a swollen finger.

## The case matters as much as the ring

The PRO Charging Case comes in the box. The ring snaps onto it magnetically (Ultrahuman calls this UltraSnap), and the case extends total battery life to up to 45 days while backing up as much as a year of data. The case charges wirelessly over Qi, has a small speaker so the app can help you find it (Find My Case), vibrates for alerts, shows battery status with an LED and has a direct connection for faster firmware updates.

## Jade, an AI layer for your health data

Alongside the ring, the company is pushing Jade, an AI system that reads your ring data in real time. Ultrahuman says it does more than answer questions: it can kick off a breathing exercise in the app or trigger atrial fibrillation (AFib) detection. It also pulls in data from the company's other products, like Blood Vision blood tests, the M1 glucose monitor and Ultrahuman Home, and it has a standard mode and a Deep Research mode.

The promises are big. Ultrahuman compares it to Tesla's self-driving system and talks about one day ordering food or adjusting your room temperature. We'll see. For now, some of the services it connects to aren't officially sold in Israel at all.

## Before you buy

- **Sizing.** A smart ring has to fit just right. On its US site Ultrahuman recommends a sizing kit before ordering. Ask Bug how sizing and exchanges work locally.
- **Health features.** AFib detection is a sensitive feature, and the announcement doesn't say which of these features are active in Israel. Check with the importer if that's why you're buying.
- **Timing.** The launch price ends on December 2. If you're interested, saving ₪300 (about $93) is a good reason to decide before then.

Bottom line: ₪1,499 with no subscription, a charging case and an importer warranty is a fair deal if you want sleep and heart-rate tracking without a watch. At ₪1,799 it's less compelling, especially while the US price is on sale.
