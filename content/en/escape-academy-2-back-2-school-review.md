Some games you play alone. Others are best with someone next to you on the sofa, both of you yelling "wait, look at the clock!". The first Escape Academy, from 2022, was that kind of game: a run of digital escape rooms with clever puzzles and a sense of humour, played by more than 4 million people. Today, November 5, the sequel arrives. Escape Academy 2: Back 2 School is trying to do something bigger.

To be upfront: this review is based on what the developers have shown and published, the trailers and the official information, not on dozens of hours of play. The game only comes out today.

## What it is

You're a student at a secret academy that teaches exactly one subject: escaping. Every class is an escape room full of locks, codes, odd machines and clues tucked into corners. The difference is there are no safety regulations here, so rooms include spiked ceilings coming down on you, drops of 30 metres and final exams, which are the scariest of all.

The rooms were built by a team of veteran puzzle designers at Coin Crew Games, the studio behind the game. If the new rooms live up to the first game, you'll get exactly that "aha!" moment you get in a real escape room in Tel Aviv, minus booking a month ahead.

![An aerial view of the Escape Academy 2 campus: colourful buildings, a lighthouse, bridges and gardens, with a tall clock tower in the middle](img/posts/escape-academy-2-campus.webp "The open campus, which is one big escape room in itself. Image: Coin Crew Games / iam8bit")

## The campus: the big new idea

In the first game you went from room to room through a menu. This time the developers call it "the first open world escape room game". Between classes you wander a large campus with secret passages, hidden tunnels and traps, plus side quests and puzzles that have nothing to do with your next class. According to the developers, the whole campus is one big puzzle room.

On paper, it's the most interesting thing about the game. It's also the biggest risk. The first game was strong because its rooms were tight and precise, and an open campus can easily turn into long walks between puzzles. In the trailers it looks packed with detail, but you'll only know whether it adds something, or just pads things out, after a few hours of play.

## Two players, even on the sofa

This is where Escape Academy 2 shines. You can play as a pair online or on one screen in split screen, which is far less common these days than it used to be. In split screen each of you sees a different part of the room and holds different items, so you have to talk to get anywhere: "I've got a dagger coin, what have you got?".

![Split-screen co-op in Escape Academy 2: one player picks an item to hand to their partner while the other stands in a dark room by a red cabinet, with a timer at the top](img/posts/escape-academy-2-split-screen.webp "Split-screen co-op, with the timer up top. Image: Coin Crew Games / iam8bit")

There's also a small but important addition: a No-Timer Mode. In the first game the clock ticking at the top was part of the fun, and also a source of stress. Now you can switch it off and solve things at your own pace. That makes the game a much better fit for couples who just want a relaxed evening, or parents playing with their kids.

## Story, characters and music

There's a new story about a dark mystery tied to how the school was founded, with new teachers and familiar faces from the first game. The developers say it's meant for newcomers and returning players alike.

The look has been rebuilt, with new environments, much more detail and new outfits for the cast. DJ Doseone is back too, with a fresh soundtrack built for exactly this: playing in the background while you work out the code to the safe.

![A room in the headmaster's tower in Escape Academy 2: a library with a ladder, a large round window, a chandelier and a patterned red rug](img/posts/escape-academy-2-headmaster-tower.webp "One of the rooms in the headmaster's tower. Image: Coin Crew Games / iam8bit")

## Hebrew? No

The game supports seven languages, and Hebrew isn't one of them. The puzzles lean quite a bit on reading notes, clues and signs. If you're comfortable with basic English you'll be fine, but younger kids who don't read English yet will need an adult beside them. Then again, that's a great excuse to play together.

## Price and where to play in Israel

It's out today on Xbox Series X|S, PC and PS5. On the PlayStation Store it unlocks this evening at 20:00 Israel time. Game Pass Ultimate and PC Game Pass members get it at no extra cost from day one, which is probably the cheapest way to try it.

On the Microsoft Store the standard edition is $19.99 (about ₪63), and the Deluxe Edition with the Season Pass is $29.99 (about ₪95). There's a free demo on Steam, so you can check whether the puzzles click for you before buying. Prices in the Israeli stores may differ a little because of VAT and exchange rates.

## The bottom line

7/10

Escape Academy 2 keeps what worked in the first game, clever and funny escape rooms, and adds an open campus, same-screen co-op and a no-timer mode. For couples, friends and parents with older kids, it's hard to find a better shared game at this price right now, especially on Game Pass. The score stays cautious because of the open campus: it's the game's big gamble, and it's too early to say whether it keeps the pace of the rooms or slows it down. And there's no Hebrew.
:::

**What I liked**

- Escape rooms built by veteran puzzle designers
- Co-op online and in split screen on the sofa
- A no-timer mode for a relaxed pace
- On Game Pass from day one, and cheap without it
- A free demo before you buy

**What I liked less**

- Still unclear whether the open campus adds anything or just pads it out
- No Hebrew, and the puzzles lean on reading English
- Like any puzzle game, once you've solved everything there's little reason to go back
