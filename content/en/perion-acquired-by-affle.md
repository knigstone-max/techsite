Another long-standing Israeli tech name is heading abroad. Perion, listed on both Nasdaq and the Tel Aviv Stock Exchange, said on November 1 that it has signed a merger agreement with Affle, an Indian ad-tech company. The deal puts Perion's equity value at about $420 million, or roughly ₪1.31 billion.

## What shareholders get

Each Perion share converts into $11.70 in cash, about ₪36.48. That's 34.5% above the October 30 close, the last trading day before the news. Perion's board backed it unanimously.

Citibank is providing the financing, and the agreement has no financing condition, so the deal shouldn't collapse over money. Worth noting, though: Perion had $228.3 million in cash, deposits and marketable securities as of September 30, more than half the price tag. A good chunk of what Affle pays effectively comes back out of Perion's own bank account.

## A 40-day window for a better offer

The agreement includes a 40-day go-shop. Until 11:59 PM Israel time on December 11, 2026, Perion can actively look for a higher bid. It's a standard clause that mostly protects the board from claims it didn't test the market. Competing offers rarely show up.

Closing is expected in early 2027, pending a shareholder vote and US antitrust clearance under the HSR Act. After that, Perion becomes a private company owned by Affle through its subsidiary Affle 3iL, and the shares come off both Nasdaq and the TASE.

## What Affle is after

Perion has been around for a long time in Israeli tech terms. It started in consumer software and has spent recent years on digital advertising. Its main products today are Perion One, a platform that pulls advertising across several channels into one place, and Outmax, an AI agent for running campaigns.

Affle's strength is mobile advertising. Its CEO, Anuj Khanna Sohum, says Perion brings an established presence in channels where Affle is lighter: retail media, connected TV and digital out-of-home screens. He also talks about growing in developed markets. Once the deal closes, Perion becomes a wholly owned Affle subsidiary and keeps operating under its own brand.

## Some context

Anyone who has followed Perion knows the last couple of years were rough. In 2024 it lost its big search business tied to Microsoft Bing, and the stock fell hard. Since then the company has rebuilt around Perion One. $11.70 looks good next to the pre-announcement price, but it's a long way below the stock's peak.

It caps a busy month for Israeli tech. According to Globes, Israeli startups raised more than $900 million in October and about $11.9 billion over the first ten months of the year.
