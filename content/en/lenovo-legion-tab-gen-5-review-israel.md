There is a small class of devices that never quite finds a home on the shelves of Israeli electronics chains: compact tablets built for gaming first. The Lenovo Legion Tab, sold in China as the Legion Y700, is probably the best known of them, and the fifth generation arrives with the most powerful chip Qualcomm currently makes for phones.

In Israel it comes in exactly one configuration: 12GB of memory and 256GB of storage, Wi-Fi only, in black. Bezeq Store and White Tiger both list it at ₪2,889 (about $905), and on Zap it starts at ₪2,700 (about $845).

I have not lived with it for a month. I went through Lenovo's official specs, what is actually on sale here and what published testing has shown so far. The short version up front: if you game on mobile a lot and want a bigger screen than a phone that you can still hold in two hands for hours, very little competes at this level. If you want a tablet for the living room, Netflix and the kids, there are cheaper and more sensible options.

![Lenovo Legion Tab Gen 5 annotated: 8.8-inch 3K 165Hz screen, 50MP camera with RGB ring, 9,000mAh battery and two USB-C ports](img/posts/legion-tab-gen-5-annotated-en.webp "The tablet at a glance. Graphic: TechSite")

## Design and size

8.8 inches is an odd size in a good way. Bigger than any phone, far smaller than a regular iPad. It weighs 360 g and is 7.59 mm thin, small enough for a coat pocket or a little bag. The body is metal, and the bezels leave room for your thumbs when you play in landscape.

Around the back there is a 50MP main camera next to an RGB ring shaped like the Legion logo. It changes color for notifications, charging or games, and also works as the flash. A bit too gamer for my taste, but you can turn it off. Up front there is an 8MP camera for video calls. Nobody buys this tablet for photography, and rightly so.

The box includes a glass screen protector and a folio case. A small thing, but it saves a few dozen shekels and a trip to the accessories shop.

## The screen

This is the heart of the device. A PureSight IPS panel at 3040×1904 (3K) in 16:10, refreshing at 165Hz, with 600 nits typical brightness and a brighter high-brightness mode outdoors. On 8.8 inches that is a very high pixel density, so text, game HUDs and photos look sharp.

165Hz pays off only in games that support it, mostly shooters and competitive titles. The rest of the time you notice it in scrolling and in the interface, which feel smoother. The relative weakness is that it is not OLED: blacks will not be truly black, and films in a dark room look less striking than on an OLED tablet or phone. The screen supports a stylus, but one is not included.

## Performance

Not much to argue about here. The Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 is the chip in late-2026 flagship phones, and it runs any Android game on high settings. Emulation of older consoles, something many buyers of this tablet care about, should be comfortable too.

12GB of memory is plenty for games. 256GB of storage is less impressive when big games weigh tens of gigabytes each, but here the new generation helps: a microSD slot that takes cards up to 2TB. A big memory card solves the problem without paying for a pricier configuration.

The thing to know about is heat. A chip like this in a 7.59 mm body warms up under load, and published testing found the tablet gets less pleasant to hold after long sessions. The included case helps a little. Lenovo added bypass charging, which lets you play plugged in without charging the battery. That cuts heat and is kinder to the battery over time.

## Battery and ports

The battery grew to 9,000mAh, with up to 68W fast charging. In normal use that should last a few days between charges, but heavy games at 165Hz will drain it in a few hours. That is what the two USB-C ports are for: one on the bottom, at 10Gbps with DisplayPort output for a monitor or TV, and one on the side. So you can play in landscape with the cable coming out of the side instead of jabbing your palm.

What is missing: there is no 3.5 mm headphone jack and no cellular. If you want to play on the train or on holiday, you will be tethering from your phone. Two Dolby Atmos speakers sit on the frame and should be fine for a session at home.

## Software

It ships with Android 16 and Lenovo's ZUI skin. Legion Space gathers all your games in one place, with a quick panel for performance settings mid-game, and there is a desktop mode with floating windows. It is not iPadOS, but for browsing, video and games it does the job.

## Israeli price and the alternatives

₪2,889 is a lot for an Android tablet. On the other hand, it has almost no direct rival in Israel: most tablets this size are much cheaper and much weaker. Against a gaming phone, it gives you a far bigger screen and battery. Against a handheld console, it runs the whole Google Play store, cloud gaming and emulators, and doubles as a reader and video screen.

![Legion Tab Gen 5 Israeli price and specs: ₪2,889 at Bezeq Store, from ₪2,700 on Zap, 12GB/256GB and Wi-Fi only](img/posts/legion-tab-gen-5-specs-en.webp "The configuration sold in Israel, and what is in it. Graphic: TechSite")

Before you buy, check that the unit comes with a local importer warranty and that the charger is in the box. If you see it close to ₪2,700, that is the right price.

## Bottom line

8/10

The Legion Tab Gen 5 is a niche device, and it nails its niche. A sharp, fast 3K screen, flagship phone silicon, a big battery, two USB-C ports, bypass charging and a microSD slot. It loses points for heat under load, the missing headphone jack and cellular, an IPS panel at nearly ₪3,000, and no higher-storage configuration in Israel. For mobile gamers, it is my pick at this size.
:::

**What I liked**

- Sharp 3K 165Hz screen in a comfortable size
- Flagship phone performance
- 9,000mAh, bypass charging and two USB-C ports
- microSD slot up to 2TB
- Case and screen protector in the box

**What I liked less**

- Runs warm in long sessions
- No headphone jack and no cellular
- IPS, not OLED, at this price
- Only 12GB/256GB on sale in Israel
