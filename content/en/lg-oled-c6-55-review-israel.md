Every November we get the same question: which TV should I buy for the living room? For a few years now, if you want OLED and don't want to pay for a flagship, the answer starts with LG's C series. The C6 is the 2026 model. It launched worldwide in May, and now, near the end of the year, its Israeli price has dropped far enough to make it a real contender.

I looked at the version you'll find most often here, the **55-inch OLED55C66LA**. I went through the spec sheet, the software and everything that matters in an Israeli living room. Here's how it comes out.

![The 55-inch LG OLED evo C6 at a glance: Israeli prices, US launch price, refresh rate, inputs, HDR, sound, processor and warranty](img/posts/lg-oled-c6-glance-en.webp "The numbers that matter. Graphic: TechSite")

## What it costs in Israel

The official importer is **H.Y Electronics**, and the warranty is **three years** through the importer. On the day of this review:

- **Zabilo**: ₪4,489 (about $1,377), down from ₪4,799
- **BetterSHOP**: ₪4,490 (about $1,377)
- **Pisga HaHashmal**: ₪4,890 (about $1,500)

For comparison, this model launched in the US in May at $1,999 (about ₪6,519 before tax). So buyers in Israel right now pay less than the US launch price, which doesn't happen often with TVs. Prices move fast around Black Friday, so check again before you order.

## What's new over the C5

The big change is the **processor**. The C6 gets the α11 AI Gen 3, the same chip as the flagship G6 and W6. In practice that means an improved Brightness Booster (more light from the same panel), 13-bit image processing and better upscaling of non-4K content.

The second change is **gaming**: up to 165Hz at 4K, up from 144Hz last year.

One thing to know about the range. The 42- to 65-inch sizes use a Tandem WOLED panel. The big 77- and 83-inch sizes are called C6H this year and get the flagship's upgraded panel. Everything here applies to the regular C6.

## Picture

This is where the C6 does what OLED does best. Every pixel lights itself, so black is actually black: no halos around subtitles, no grey haze in night scenes. If you're coming from a regular LED TV, you'll notice in the first minute of a dark film.

Brightness is what's improved most in the series over the past few years, and the C6 continues that. It matters here, because plenty of Israeli living rooms are bright: big windows, open shutters, afternoon sun. OLED still doesn't match a strong mini LED for brightness, but on the C6 the gap is small enough not to bother most homes.

**HDR support:** Dolby Vision, HDR10 and HLG. There's no **HDR10+**, and LG has no plans to add Dolby Vision 2 to the C6. Most of the time you won't notice, since the big streaming services carry plenty of Dolby Vision content. Anything only available in HDR10+ plays as standard HDR10, without the dynamic metadata.

**The big caveat is reflections.** The screen is glossy. In dark scenes in a bright room you'll see the window, the lamp and yourself. The flagship G6 has an anti-glare coating that handles this much better, but it costs a lot more. If the TV will face a big window, this is the most important point in this review.

## Gaming

If you have a PS5, an Xbox or a gaming PC, the C6 is about the best you can buy at this price:

- **Four HDMI 2.1 ports**, all side-facing, so you can plug in a console even when the TV is mounted flush to the wall
- **4K at 165Hz** for PCs that can drive it
- Full **VRR**, including Nvidia G-Sync and AMD FreeSync Premium
- **HGiG**, **Dolby Vision Gaming** and **ALLM**, which switches the TV into game mode on its own
- **Game Optimizer**, one menu with every gaming setting

On burn-in: modern OLEDs are far less prone to it than they used to be, and LG builds in protection features. If you play one game with a fixed HUD for hours, or leave a news channel with a logo on all day, leave those protections on and carry on.

## Sound

Two speakers and two small woofers (2.2 channels), with Dolby Atmos. LG cut the sound modes from eight to four this year, mostly AI-based ones. There's no **DTS**, which matters if you connect a Blu-ray player with DTS soundtracks.

The speakers are fine for news, series and everyday viewing. For action films they're thin, like nearly every slim TV. If the living room doubles as a home cinema, budget for a soundbar.

## Software and apps

The C6 runs **webOS 26**, LG's own platform. It's quick and easy to use, and the Magic Remote works like an air mouse: move it and the pointer follows. Menus were trimmed this year, and settings are easier to reach.

The less pleasant part: **ads** in the top row of the home screen and on the screensaver. Rivals do it too, but it's still annoying on a TV that costs thousands of shekels.

As for Israeli apps, local stores list support for yes+, HOT, Partner TV, Cellcom TV, 12+ and 13, alongside Netflix, YouTube and Disney+. If there's a specific service you can't live without, check that it's on webOS before you buy.

## Power

The model is rated **G** on the European energy label. That sounds bad, but it's common for large 4K TVs, since the EU scale is strict. A few hours a night won't change your electricity bill dramatically.

## Who it's for

**Yes:**
- People who mostly watch in the evening, in a dark or shaded room
- Gamers with a console or a strong PC
- Anyone who wants real OLED without paying for a flagship

**Think twice:**
- If the TV faces a big, bright window. Look at a bright mini LED, or the G6 with its anti-glare coating
- If you need HDR10+ or DTS
- If you want good sound without a soundbar

## The bottom line

8.5/10

The LG OLED evo C6 is the OLED most living rooms need: perfect blacks, accurate colour, the flagship processor and a gaming feature set with almost no rivals. At around ₪4,500 for 55 inches with a three-year warranty, it's a very good buy. The glossy screen, average speakers and ads in the interface hold it back a little, and if your TV faces a window, take that seriously.
:::

**What I liked**

- True OLED blacks and contrast
- α11 AI Gen 3 processor, same as the flagships
- Four HDMI 2.1 ports and 165Hz
- Dolby Vision and Dolby Atmos
- Fast webOS and a handy remote
- Israeli price below the US launch price, with a three-year warranty

**What I didn't**

- Glossy screen that reflects in bright rooms
- No HDR10+ or DTS
- Average speakers
- Ads on the home screen and screensaver
