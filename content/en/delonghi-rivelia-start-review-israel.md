Any home with a bean-to-cup machine knows this argument. One person wants dark, punchy beans for the morning espresso. The other wants something light and fruity for an afternoon filter-style cup. The machine has one hopper. De'Longhi built the Rivelia Start around exactly that problem: it ships with two bean hoppers you can swap in a second.

It arrived in Israel in early October at about ₪3,200 (roughly $1,026). I went through the full spec sheet and what comes in the box, and looked at where it sits in the De'Longhi lineup. Here's where I landed.

## The big idea: Bean Switch

There's one grinder but two 250 g hoppers. One sits on the machine, the other waits on the counter with its own lid so the beans stay fresh. To switch, you lift one off and drop the other on.

The clever part is the transition. In Switch Mode the machine lets you make one last cup from the old beans, or grind out whatever's left in the grinder, before the new beans go in. That way your first cup after the swap doesn't carry leftovers from the last batch. If you've ever changed beans on a regular machine, you know how annoying that gets.

If someone at home drinks decaf, there's also a chute for pre-ground coffee, so you can make a single cup without touching the beans at all.

## What it makes

Ten one-touch drinks: espresso, coffee, filter-style coffee, cappuccino, flat white, hot milk, latte macchiato, cold brew, iced coffee and iced espresso. There's hot water for tea as well.

Cold brew is the feature I find most interesting for Israel. Proper cold brew steeps for hours, and De'Longhi promises a result in under five minutes. It won't match an overnight steep, but in an Israeli summer, a cold cup that doesn't get watered down by melting ice might be reason enough to pick this machine.

## Milk

The box includes the LatteCrema Hot carafe, which froths and heats milk for cappuccinos and lattes. It goes in the fridge between uses, so you don't pour milk away after every cup.

There's also a LatteCrema Cool carafe that froths cold milk and adds six more cold drinks. It's sold separately and I couldn't find a clear Israeli price for it. If cold foam drinks are what draw you in, check that before you buy.

## What's missing

This is where it differs from the regular Rivelia (EXAM440.55), which came out before the Start. That one has a colour screen, 16 drinks, four user profiles and Bean Adapt, which tunes grind and brew to the beans you're using. The Start has a simple touch panel, no colour screen and no profiles.

If you just want to press a button and get coffee, that's no loss. If you like to tweak things and save settings for everyone at home, you'll miss it.

## Day-to-day upkeep

- A 1.4-litre water tank, good for several cups between refills
- A grounds container that holds ten servings
- Adjustable water hardness, which matters with Israel's hard tap water. Set it on day one so the machine asks for descaling at the right time.

## Cost per cup

With decent supermarket or roaster beans, an espresso shot works out to roughly ₪0.58–1, depending on the beans. If you're on capsules or buy coffee out every morning, the machine pays for itself faster than you'd think.

## The alternatives

- **Rivelia EXAM440.55:** sometimes found in Israeli stores for around ₪3,000 (about $962), which is less than the Start. At that price it gives you more: a screen, profiles and more drinks. What it lacks is the Start's cold brew and iced drinks.
- **Magnifica Start:** about ₪2,000 (around $641). A basic machine with one hopper. If you only drink espresso and cappuccino, it does the job.
- **Eletta Explore:** about ₪5,200–5,700. For people who want lots of cold drinks and will pay for them.
- **Ninja Luxe:** about ₪1,500–2,600 depending on the model. Semi-automatic, so more hands-on work and more control.

## The bottom line

8/10

The Rivelia Start is the simplest way I've seen to keep two coffees at home without wasting beans. Fast cold brew and iced drinks suit Israel well. It loses points on price: if the regular Rivelia, with its screen and profiles, sells for less right next to it, that's almost always the better deal.
:::

**What I loved**

- Two bean hoppers and a switch mode that doesn't leave leftovers
- Cold brew in under five minutes, and iced coffee straight from the machine
- A pre-ground chute for decaf drinkers
- A milk carafe that lives in the fridge
- Adjustable water hardness

**What I liked less**

- No colour screen and no profiles
- The cold milk carafe is extra, with no clear Israeli price
- The price is too close to the regular Rivelia's, and sometimes higher
