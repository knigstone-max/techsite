Most Israelis know Dreame from robot vacuums, and lately from hair dryers and washing machines too. Now the company is moving onto the kitchen counter. Walla reported yesterday on the arrival of the Ecceluxe line, Dreame's first bean-to-cup coffee machines, and both models are already listed on the official Israeli site.

There are two of them, and they're aimed at quite different kitchens.

## Ecceluxe Master FCM60: the full-size one

This is the bigger, more complete machine. It makes 6 drinks at the touch of a button, and one of them is **cold brew**, which you rarely see on a home machine at this price. It has 19 bar of pressure, a built-in grinder that Dreame says is quiet, with 9 grind levels, and automatic milk frothing.

The capacities suit a home where several people drink coffee: a 500 ml milk container and a 2-litre water tank. It weighs 9.8 kg, so once it's on the counter, it's staying there. It also cleans itself.

On Dreame's Israeli site the Master costs **2,110 ₪ (about $668)**, down from a list price of 2,490 ₪ (about $789).

## Ecceluxe Slim FCM30: for tight counters

The Slim is the more interesting one for Israeli kitchens, where counter space is usually short. It's just **18 cm wide**, about 35% narrower than a typical bean-to-cup machine, according to Dreame. It still has 19 bar, 9 grind levels and a colour touchscreen, and makes 5 drinks.

The narrow body comes with trade-offs. The water tank holds 1.5 litres, and the milk container only 240 ml, which is enough for about two milky drinks before you refill it. It weighs 8 kg. Cleaning is well thought out: the machine automatically rinses the brew unit, the milk lines and the water path, and has a descaling program.

The Slim comes in white or black, and on Dreame's site it costs **1,686 ₪ (about $534)** instead of 1,990 ₪ (about $630). It's worth checking other retailers too, since we've seen it listed lower elsewhere.

## Which one makes sense for you?

**The Master** fits a household where coffee gets made several times a day, often with milk. The 500 ml milk container and 2-litre tank mean fewer refills, and cold brew is a nice bonus for the Israeli summer, which sometimes feels like it runs until November.

**The Slim** fits a small kitchen, a rental flat, or one or two people who mostly drink espresso or a single cappuccino in the morning. If you drink a lot of milky coffee, the 240 ml container will get annoying fast.

## Things to keep in mind

This is Dreame's first go at coffee. It has plenty of experience with motors, sensors and apps, but not with brewing. We'd want to see how the machines hold up over time and how easy it is to get spare parts and service here before calling either one a safe buy. Before you order, ask the seller who handles warranty and repairs in Israel.

It's also worth comparing with the brands that have been here for years. Bean-to-cup machines from De'Longhi, Philips and others sell in Israel in the same price range, with a long track record and a wide service network. Dreame's advantage is mainly the narrow design of the Slim and cold brew on the Master.
