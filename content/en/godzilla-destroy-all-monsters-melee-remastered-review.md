If you owned a GameCube or the original Xbox in the early 2000s, you might remember Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee. It was a loud, simple fighting game: two giant monsters in a city, buildings coming apart, and a couple of friends on the couch yelling at each other. Today, November 3, which happens to be Godzilla's 72nd birthday, Atari is bringing it back.

Pipeworks, the studio behind the original, rebuilt it from scratch in Unreal Engine 5. It's out on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Switch 2 and PC through Steam. The digital version costs $29.99 (about ₪94), so does the PS5 disc, and the Switch 2 cartridge is $39.99 (about ₪125).

## What kind of game this is

Don't expect Street Fighter or Tekken. There are no long combo lists to memorise. Every monster can punch, kick, grab, fire an energy beam and, most importantly, use the city as a weapon. You can pick up a building and throw it, slam your opponent into a tower, and swat away the army's tanks and helicopters, which annoy both sides equally.

There's also Rage Mode. It fills up as you take hits and gives you a few seconds of stronger attacks. It's a simple idea, but it makes the end of a round tense, because whoever looks beaten can turn the fight around.

## The roster

There are 12 monsters, and the line-up respects TOHO's history: Heisei-era Godzilla, Godzilla 2000, Anguirus, Gigan, Megalon, Rodan, King Ghidorah, Mecha-King Ghidorah, Destoroyah, Kiryu, Orga and Showa-era Mechagodzilla.

Each monster has its own moves and beam attack, and the differences show. It isn't a huge cast, and fans will notice a few familiar faces missing, but there's enough variety that a whole evening of fights doesn't feel repetitive.

![King Ghidorah, a golden three-headed dragon, hovering over a jungle at night and firing lightning](img/posts/godzilla-dam-melee-remastered-ghidorah-night.webp "King Ghidorah in the jungle arena, night version. Image: Atari / Pipeworks / TOHO")

## How it looks

This is where the remaster earns its keep. The monsters are richly detailed, all rough hide and shiny metal, and buildings break apart in a way that never stops being satisfying. There are 8 arenas, each with a day and a night version, plus changing weather. The night stages, with city lights and beams lighting up the sky, are the best-looking part of the game.

PC requirements are modest: 64-bit Windows 10, an i7-9700 or Ryzen 5 3600, 16GB of RAM, a GTX 1650 with 4GB, and 8GB of free space. If you want to try before you buy, a free demo has been on Steam since October 15.

## Alone or with friends

This game is built for two players. Local versus on one screen is its heart, and that's where it's most fun. The big addition is online play, which the original didn't have, so you can fight friends who aren't sitting next to you.

Solo, it's a weaker deal. Each monster has a short campaign, but it's mostly a string of fights against the computer, without much story or variety. It's useful for unlocking monsters and arenas, and that's where the other problem shows up: unlocking everything takes a lot of play and sometimes feels like a chore. On the plus side, you can unlock things in any order you like instead of following a fixed path.

## For players in Israel

There's no Hebrew, but the game barely asks you to read anything. Menus are simple and there's no long dialogue. At the time of writing Steam wasn't showing an Israeli price yet, so check the store before you buy. The Switch 2 cartridge costs $10 more than the digital version, which is worth keeping in mind if you're ordering from abroad.

## The bottom line

7/10

This remaster does exactly what it promises: it brings back a simple, fun brawler with modern visuals and a lot of destruction. With a friend on the couch or online it's a treat, and at $29.99 it doesn't ask much. If you mostly play alone you'll run out of things to do quickly, and the time it takes to unlock the full roster will wear on you.
:::

**What I liked**

- Great visuals and satisfying building destruction
- 12 familiar monsters, each with its own style
- Online play alongside local same-screen versus
- 8 arenas with day and night versions
- A fairly low price

**What I didn't**

- Thin single-player
- Unlocking content takes too long
- Some monsters fans will look for are missing
- No Hebrew
