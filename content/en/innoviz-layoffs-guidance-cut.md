Innoviz, one of Israel's best-known lidar companies, had a rough quarterly report on Wednesday. The Rosh HaAyin company is laying off about 20% of its staff, around 80 people according to Calcalist, most of them in Israel. On the same day it cut its full-year revenue forecast from $67-73 million to $44-46 million (about ₪140 to ₪147 million).

It's the third round of cuts in three years. Innoviz trimmed about 13% of its workforce in early 2024 and about 9% in early 2025. At the end of 2025 it employed 372 people, 344 of them in Israel.

## What happened

An unnamed automotive customer told Innoviz near the end of the third quarter that it was pushing series production of a model with Innoviz sensors back to 2027. In this business, lidar suppliers get paid on development milestones long before a car reaches the road. When the schedule slips, so does the money.

CEO Omer Keilaf told analysts on the earnings call that the delay has nothing to do with the lidar itself and comes from the carmaker's own internal problems, Techtime reported. He said the Daimler Truck and Audi programs are on schedule, and that the company is still working with Volkswagen and Mobileye.

## The numbers

- **Q3 revenue:** $12.1 million (about ₪38.6 million), down from $15.3 million a year earlier
- **Net loss:** $19.6 million, up from $15.4 million
- **Expected savings:** about $17.5 million a year (about ₪56 million), roughly $12 million of it from layoffs
- **Liquid assets at the end of September:** $54.4 million, including about $30 million raised in July

The cuts focus on R&D and operations, and the company says the effect will show from early 2027. The CFO said the plan gets Innoviz into 2028 without raising more capital.

## The new direction: defense

While the car business stalls, Innoviz is trying to sell its sensors to militaries and security agencies under the Perciz brand. The main uses are drone detection, protecting vehicles and convoys, and securing infrastructure. Last quarter that business brought in about 10% of revenue, roughly $1.2 million. For next year, Keilaf expects bigger orders in the first half and a 20% to 30% share of revenue.

In Israel, according to Techtime, the Defense Ministry's R&D directorate has recognised the sensors as suitable for defense use, though that recognition carries no purchase commitment.

## Why it matters if you don't own the stock

Lidar scans the surroundings with lasers and builds a 3D map, like the image above. It's one of the pieces meant to make real self-driving possible. When a carmaker delays a program by a year, it's a sign that cars with advanced driving systems are coming more slowly than promised.

The market price tells the rest. Innoviz went public in 2021 at a valuation of about $1.4 billion. Before the report it was worth about $66 million, and the shares fell around 36% in early trading afterwards.
