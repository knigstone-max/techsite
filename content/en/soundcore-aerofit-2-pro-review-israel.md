I like open earbuds. On a run through Park HaYarkon, walking the dog at night or on a long Zoom call, nothing is more comfortable: you hear the music, you hear the bike coming up behind you, and your ears don't sweat. The trouble starts on the train into Tel Aviv or a packed bus. Open earbuds just can't cope there, and you end up turning the volume up until it hurts.

soundcore's AeroFit 2 Pro, from Anker, are built to fix exactly that. Each bud slides along its ear hook through five positions. At the low settings it sits outside your ear canal, and at the high ones it tucks in a little and switches on noise cancelling. On paper, that's one pair instead of two. I didn't carry these around for a month. I went through the official specs, the prices here and abroad, and the testing published since they launched early this year. The result: excellent open earbuds, and noise cancelling that can't yet replace a regular pair.

## The price in Israel

KSP, the official importer, was selling them in October for ₪730 (about $229) with a one-year warranty, in black, white, blue and purple. soundcore's US store lists them at $179.99 (about ₪574). So you pay roughly ₪150 more here, which is worth remembering when sales come around.

There are alternatives around that price. The regular AeroFit 2, with no ANC but longer battery life, went for ₪380 at KSP (about $119) at the same time. The Shokz OpenFit 2, also open, were about ₪600 at Bug. And if noise cancelling is what you're really after, regular in-ear buds do more for less money.

## How it works

The hook is soft silicone, and where it meets the bud there's a mechanism that moves in clicks. Published testing agrees it feels sturdy and won't wear out after a few weeks of sliding back and forth. Levels 1 and 2 are open mode. Levels 4 and 5 are what Anker calls semi-in-ear, with ANC on. Level 3 is in between, and you pick the mode in the app. One catch: ANC only works when both buds are at level 4 or 5.

Anker says they fit about 90% of ears, and its fit chart includes a measurement worth taking if your ears are on the small side. They sit better on medium and large ears, and the sealed mode is the most fit-sensitive of all.

## Open mode: where they shine

This is the part almost every tester liked. The 11.8 mm drivers deliver more bass than most open earbuds and clear detail up top. Electronic music and hip-hop, which usually sound hollow on open buds, get some weight here. soundcore's Signature EQ adds even more bass. On sealed earbuds that can be too much, but on open ones it actually balances things out.

On an Android phone with LDAC you get a bit more detail. On iPhone they run over AAC, which still sounds good. If you're used to very open designs that sit completely outside the ear, you'll notice these block a little more even at level 1. You feel it when talking to someone next to you.

Spatial audio with head tracking works, but it sounds artificial with music. With films and shows it gives a wider feel. I'd leave it off for music.

## ANC mode: where they fall short

This is the feature the whole product is built around, and it's the weakest part. Without silicone tips sealing the canal, these buds can't block noise like in-ear models. Low, steady sounds like an air conditioner, a fridge or engine hum drop nicely. Voices, ringing and midrange noise get through much more. On a full train it's still better than open mode, but you won't get the quiet of good sealed earbuds.

Two more complaints come up again and again in testing. First, at level 5 the buds shift a little with sharp head movements, like running, and the noise cancelling changes as they move. Second, switching modes is slow. Move to level 4 and the sound takes a few seconds to adjust, with heavy bass in the meantime. There's also a loud alert tone on every switch, which you can turn off in the app.

Anker itself says the experience depends heavily on fit and isn't meant to replace sealed in-ear ANC buds in very loud places. That's honest, but it also means the two-in-one pitch is a bit of a stretch.

## Comfort and build

Each bud weighs 10.4 g, more than most open earbuds. For an hour or two it's fine. After a full day you feel them. They're rated IP55, enough for sweat and rain, and in testing they survived wet winter runs without trouble. The case has no rating.

The case is huge: 107.5 by 57 mm and 88.2 g. It doesn't sit comfortably in a jeans pocket. On the plus side, it has wireless charging, an LED bar that shows the remaining battery, and room for a big cell.

Controls are physical buttons rather than touch, which I prefer. No accidental presses while adjusting the fit. The app lets you remap every press, set a separate EQ for each mode, turn on a low-latency gaming mode, and find a lost bud.

## Battery and calls

Anker quotes 7 hours in open mode and 34 with the case, or 5 hours with ANC and 24 with the case. Published tests came in very close. LDAC shortens those times. Ten minutes of charging gives about three and a half hours.

Calls go through four microphones with AI noise reduction. At home or in the office they work well. On a loud street, like almost all open earbuds, the other person hears some of what's around you.

## The bottom line

6/10

The AeroFit 2 Pro are a great idea with half the execution. In open mode they're among the best earbuds you can buy at this price, with surprising bass, good buttons and sweat resistance. The noise cancelling, the reason they exist, is too weak and too unsteady to let you give up a pair of in-ear buds. At ₪730 they're expensive. If you're buying for running and walking, the regular AeroFit 2 cost half as much. If you're buying for the train, get sealed earbuds.
:::

**What I liked**

- Full sound with good bass for open earbuds
- A sturdy level mechanism that makes sense after a few days
- Physical, remappable buttons
- IP55 and battery life that matches the claims
- LDAC for Android users, and wireless charging

**What I didn't**

- Weak ANC on voices and midrange
- They shift at level 5 when running
- Slow switching between modes
- A huge, heavy case
- About ₪150 more than the US price
