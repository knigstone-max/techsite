NICE, one of the biggest Israeli software companies, is laying off about 500 employees. That's roughly 5% of its workforce of around 10,000, according to a Calcalist report published on Ynetnews. The company has about 1,000 employees in Israel, and according to the report, they'll be largely spared. In July, CEO Scott Russell committed not to cut the Israeli workforce.

The timing isn't random. NICE reports its third-quarter results today, Wednesday, November 4.

## What NICE says

In a statement, the company explained that it is "making changes to its organizational structure and resource allocation to better serve customers deploying AI-powered customer experience solutions at enterprise scale." It added: "As part of this plan, we expect to reduce our global workforce by approximately 5%."

The company also called it "a necessary step to position NICE for the future," and said its immediate priority is to speak directly with the affected employees and give them full support during the transition.

In plain terms, NICE is moving people and money towards AI agents for customer service, and away from other areas.

## Why now

NICE sells software for customer service centres. Its products route calls, record and analyse conversations, and increasingly let bots answer customers on their own. It has about 25,000 clients, including 85% of the Fortune 500, among them Marriott, Lidl, Lufthansa and Disney.

The problem is that generative AI is changing this market fast, and investors aren't sure who'll come out ahead. NICE's shares have fallen 17% over the past year, though they've held steady since the start of 2026. The company is worth about $6.5 billion (about ₪20.5 billion), compared with a peak of around $20 billion (about ₪63 billion) during the pandemic.

To catch up, NICE bought the German startup Cognigy, which builds AI agents for customer service, for about $1 billion (about ₪3.2 billion). According to the report, it's also considering selling Actimize, its fraud-prevention and anti-money-laundering software unit, which would let it focus entirely on customer experience.

## What to watch in today's results

For the third quarter, NICE has guided for revenue of $780–790 million (about ₪2.46–2.49 billion) and adjusted earnings per share of $2.73–2.83. In the first half of the year, customers delayed signing contracts, and the company said it expects things to pick up in the second half. Today we'll see whether that happened.

For the full year, NICE expects revenue above $3 billion (about ₪9.5 billion) for the first time. Earnings per share, however, are expected to come in around $11, almost a dollar lower than before, despite the share buybacks.

## What it means for the Israeli tech scene

For the roughly 1,000 NICE employees in Israel, the news is relatively calm, at least for now. For the industry as a whole, it's another sign of a trend we've been seeing for months: large companies cutting staff in their older businesses and putting the money into AI. NICE isn't a struggling startup. It's a mature, profitable company with $3 billion in annual revenue, and even it feels it has to reorganise.
