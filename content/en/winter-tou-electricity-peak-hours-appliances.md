In November you barely notice time-of-use pricing. Peak and off-peak are a few agorot apart, and Friday and Saturday count as off-peak all day. In two weeks that flips. Winter pricing starts on December 1, and anyone who switched to Israel's time-of-use tariff, usually along with a smart meter, will pay more than double in the evening what they pay the rest of the day. That's exactly when everyone gets home and turns everything on.

## What changes in December

According to the Electricity Authority's tariff book (07/2026), winter means December, January and February, and the peak runs from 17:00 to 22:00. The big difference from the shoulder season: in winter the peak also applies on Friday, Saturday and holidays. There's no cheap weekend evening anymore.

Prices for a regular home (low voltage), VAT included:

- **Winter peak:** 114.70 agorot per kWh, about 36 cents.
- **Winter off-peak:** 46.63 agorot per kWh, about 15 cents.
- **For comparison, the flat rate** (if you never switched): 63.52 agorot per kWh, about 20 cents, at any hour.

So every evening kWh costs roughly 2.5 times a morning or night one, and about 1.8 times the flat rate.

## Why winter is the tricky one

The summer peak is even pricier, at 172.23 agorot, but it only covers Sunday to Thursday. The winter peak is cheaper, yet it runs seven days a week and lands right on evening showers, laundry, dinner and heating. If your household's habits cluster between 17:00 and 22:00, time-of-use can end up costing more than the flat rate. There's another detail people miss: the fixed monthly charge is higher on time-of-use. Per SunCal's summary of the tariff book, it's ₪47.63 a month before VAT (about $14.89), versus ₪25.57 (about $7.99) on the flat rate with a single-phase meter.

## A simple example

A 2 kW space heater running for two hours uses 4 kWh. At peak that's ₪4.59 (about $1.43). On the flat rate, ₪2.54 (about 79 cents). Off-peak, ₪1.87 (about 58 cents). Every evening. Over three winter months, it adds up.

## What to move, appliance by appliance

**Tumble dryer.** The obvious candidate. Most newer dryers, especially heat-pump models, have delayed start. Run it in the morning before work, or set it to begin after 22:00. Heat-pump dryers run cooler but their cycles are long, so check that a late-afternoon start won't spill past 17:00.

**Washing machine and dishwasher.** Same idea. Nearly every machine from the last decade has a delay-start button. Load the dishwasher after dinner and set it for 22:30. If your machines are app-connected, you can do it from the couch.

**Electric water heater.** In winter, when the sun is weak, a lot of Israeli homes lean on the heating element in the *dud*. Switching it on at five in the afternoon for evening showers means paying peak rates the whole time. Heat it at midday or early afternoon, before 17:00, and let the tank's insulation hold the heat. A smart timer switch on the water heater does this automatically.

**Air conditioner and space heaters.** No magic here, because evenings are cold. But you can warm the house a bit before 17:00, turn it down during the peak, and close doors to rooms nobody's using. A modern inverter AC heats far more efficiently than a resistive heater, so make it your main heat source.

**Electric car.** If you have a home charger, make sure it's scheduled for after 22:00, weekends included. If you're used to charging on Friday evenings because "it's off-peak," update the schedule on December 1.

**What not to bother with.** Fridges and freezers run around the clock, and there's no point trying to manage them. Dinner is dinner, too. A small oven or an air fryer that heats a smaller space may use less than a full-size oven, but that depends on the model.

## And if you're with a private supplier?

If you moved to one of the private electricity suppliers rather than the Israel Electric Corporation's time-of-use tariff, your discounts follow that supplier's terms. It's worth opening the contract to see whether your discount depends on the hour, and which hours. If you're still on the flat rate, none of this affects you: 63.52 agorot at every hour until the next tariff update.

## The bottom line

Time-of-use pays off if you shift consumption out of the evening. In winter that means timers on the dryer, dishwasher, water heater and car charger, plus a small change in heating habits. If you can't or won't do that, run the numbers. With a higher fixed charge and a five-hour peak every single day, some households will find the flat rate was cheaper all along.
