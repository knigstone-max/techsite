Plenty of Israelis already know Revolut from trips abroad: an app, a card, currency exchange in a tap. Last week the company took a big step toward something else entirely, an actual Israeli bank. On November 10 the Bank of Israel granted it a conditional licence to set up a bank called Revolut Bank Israel (in formation), along with a control permit for the group's controlling shareholder, Nikolay Storonsky.

## What was actually approved

The key word is "conditional." The licence doesn't let Revolut operate as a bank. It lets it finish the regulatory, technological and operational work needed to build one, while the Banking Supervision Department tracks its progress against set milestones. Only once it meets them will the licence be activated and the restrictions lifted. Until then there are no deposits and no customers, and the Bank of Israel's announcement didn't say when the bank might start serving the public.

Nothing has been said about products either: not which services Revolut will offer here, nor what they'll cost. Supervisor of Banks Daniel Hahiashvili said only that Revolut provides payments, foreign exchange, credit and deposits around the world, and that its entry should increase competition and widen the choice for households and businesses.

## The governor who flew to London

There's a personal backstory. Bank of Israel Governor Amir Yaron said talks with Revolut began at his initiative in late 2022, when he met its leadership in London and tried to persuade them to come to Israel. "The completion of the review process for granting the banking license today brings things full circle," he said, adding that "the primary path to increasing competition is by introducing additional players into the sector."

The road since had several stops. In August 2024 the Bank of Israel assigned Revolut identification code 78, which lets it issue payment account numbers. In July 2025 it got a payment services licence from the Israel Securities Authority. In May the central bank confirmed it could proceed with licensing, and earlier this month, as Calcalist revealed, it renamed its Israeli company from Revolut Payments Israel to Revolut Bank Israel (in formation).

## Who will run it

The Israeli bank will be led by Uri Nathan, former CEO of Pepper, Bank Leumi's digital bank, with Shmuel (Muli) Ben Zvi as chairman. Nathan said the goal is to "create genuine competition in the Israeli banking market through a digital financial services environment," and Ben Zvi said the company is adapting Revolut's international experience to the local market. According to Calcalist, Revolut is already hiring in Israel for credit, risk management, compliance, collections and banking operations.

## The third since 2019, and not all of them opened

According to the Bank of Israel, this is the third licence granted to a new bank since December 2019, after four decades in which no new bank was set up here. The previous two went to One Zero and Esh. That's worth remembering: Esh received a similar conditional licence in December 2022 to finish development, testing and operational prep, and as Finance Magnates notes, not every new bank has made it to a public launch.

Revolut does have something local newcomers didn't: more than 80 million customers worldwide, and banking licences in Lithuania, the UK, Mexico, Australia, France, Colombia, Argentina and New Zealand. It doesn't have to build an app from scratch, just fit it to Israeli regulation.

## What it means for you

In the short term, nothing. If you use Revolut's app when you travel, keep using it as before. There's no Israeli current account or deposit to open yet. In the long run, if the bank does launch, it would be the first time a global digital player of this size competes head-on with Israel's five big banking groups on accounts, fees and foreign exchange. Worth watching, but too early to close your bank account.
