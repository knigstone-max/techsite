Intel is shopping in Israel again. On October 26 it announced a deal to buy Classiq, an Israeli startup that makes software for quantum computers. Calcalist puts the price at about $2.3 billion (roughly ₪7.2 billion) in cash and stock. If that figure holds, it's the largest quantum deal ever, and by a wide margin. The previous record was IonQ's purchase of Oxford Ionics for about $1 billion.

Intel's law firm, Hogan Lovells Cadwalader, describes it as a deal worth up to $2.2 billion, with up to $350 million (about ₪1.1 billion) tied to milestones. Either way, that's a lot of money for a company founded six years ago. Intel expects to close in the first half of 2027, once regulators sign off.

![The Intel and Classiq deal figures in dollars and shekels, with a timeline from Classiq's founding in 2020 to the expected close in 2027](img/posts/intel-classiq-deal-numbers-en.webp "The deal in numbers, per Intel, Calcalist and Intel's lawyers")

## What Classiq actually does

Quantum computers don't just run normal code faster. They work on completely different principles, and programming them is still slow, hands-on work, closer to writing machine code than to using a modern language. Classiq's platform lets developers describe what an algorithm should do. The software then builds the quantum circuit and tunes it for whichever hardware will run it.

Nir Minerbi (CEO), Amir Naveh and Prof. Yehuda Naveh founded the company in 2020. Its customer list includes BMW, Rolls-Royce, HSBC, Deloitte, Toshiba and Citi. Those banks aren't running quantum workloads every day yet. They're getting ready for when they can, and they want tools that aren't locked to one type of machine.

## Why Intel wants it

Intel has spent years building its own silicon-based quantum chips. What it lacked was the software layer on top. Classiq fills that gap, so Intel can now offer the whole package, from the chip to the developer's tools. Intel's own pitch for the deal is a "full-stack" quantum offering.

One detail stands out: Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan invested in Classiq personally before he took the job. According to the reports, he stayed out of the deal talks.

## Who gets paid

Classiq raised about $200 million in total. Its last round, in 2025, brought in $110 million at a valuation of $600–700 million, so the price roughly tripled in a year.

- **Employees:** Classiq has about 150 people, who hold around 10% of the company, according to Calcalist. That's roughly $230 million (about ₪717 million). Intel is also setting aside more than $100 million for retention grants.
- **Investors:** Entrée Capital is the biggest shareholder and is reported to be getting more than $300 million. Team8 holds about 9% and AWZ about 4%.

## Intel's track record in Israel

Intel has been part of Israeli tech for decades, and Classiq isn't its first big local buy. It paid about $1 billion for Moovit, about $2 billion for Habana Labs and also picked up Granulate in 2022. This deal comes after a rough stretch for Intel, including layoffs in Israel, so it's a sign the company still sees the country as a place to buy technology.

There's nothing here for consumers to buy tomorrow. For Israel's tech scene it matters more: Israeli quantum companies have a market, and someone is willing to pay billions for one.
