Ramon.Space said on Tuesday that it has raised **$124 million**, about ₪402 million, in a Series B. Growth fund PSG led the round. Memory maker Micron joined as a strategic investor, along with Senvest and Phoenix Financial, and existing backers StageOne, Deep Insight and Grove Ventures also took part. According to Calcalist, all of it is new money going into the company.

The company is named after Colonel Ilan Ramon, Israel's first astronaut, who was killed when the space shuttle Columbia broke apart in February 2003.

## What it does

Ramon.Space builds the computers that sit inside satellites. Its systems process, store and move data, and that includes running AI right there in orbit. Today most satellites beam huge amounts of raw data down to Earth, where ground stations do the analysis. Ramon.Space wants a big share of that work to happen up there instead.

The catch is that ordinary computers don't survive in space. There's radiation, extreme temperatures and tight power budgets, and once a satellite launches, nobody can go up and fix it. "We don't deal with the satellite's flight system," CEO Avi Shabtai told Calcalist. "For us, the satellite becomes a computer flying in space."

The company says its systems are already working in orbit and match the performance of computers on the ground. It says it can produce millions of computing units, and that its hardware also suits satellite-based 5G networks and future data centers in space. Shabtai told Calcalist that SES, the big satellite operator, is a customer, and that there are others it hasn't named yet.

## An old company in a startup's clothes

Ramon.Space was founded in 2004 as Ramon Chips by Professor Ran Ginosar, Dov Alon and Tuvia Liran. The current business started in 2020, when Shabtai joined. It now has about 130 employees in Israel, the US, the UK and Germany, and the funding announcement itself came out of Denver, Colorado.

According to Walla, earlier rounds included about $1 million in seed money, roughly $17.5 million in a Series A and about $26 million in an internal round in June 2023. Total funding comes to about $170 million to $180 million depending on who you read. Walla says about $170 million, while Calcalist puts it at about $180 million.

## Why it matters

A few reasons:

- **Micron is in.** When a major memory maker invests strategically, it suggests it sees space as a real market for its chips, not a side experiment.
- **Mega-constellations.** The more satellites there are, like the ones behind satellite internet, the more data needs processing, and the more demand there is for computers that do it on board.
- **Israel's space sector.** The Israel Space Forum counts about 115 organizations and companies working in the field locally. A KPMG study for the Israel Space Agency estimates the sector adds about ₪6 billion a year to the economy, with about 7,200 direct employees.

The company says the money will go toward scaling production, delivering faster to customers, its next generation of products and expanding into new markets, mainly the US and Europe. Shabtai says contracts in this field can run to tens of millions of dollars or more.
