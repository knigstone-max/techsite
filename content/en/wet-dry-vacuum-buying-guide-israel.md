Shopping for a cordless wet-dry vacuum is confusing. Every maker prints 25,000 pascals, 180 degrees and self-cleaning, and none of it tells you how the machine will behave in your living room on a Tuesday night. We gathered what has been said about the models sold in Israel: Israeli tests, tests abroad, owner ratings, and prices from stores and importers.

**We did not test any of these machines ourselves.** Everything here comes from other people's tests and from what owners write, and we say who said what. The large owner-rating samples we found come from the US and Australia, not Israel. Anything we could not verify stays out.

## The short version

- **Balanced pick: Roborock F25 Ace Pro, ₪1,190 (about $366).** Scored 9.0 on WiseBuy, Editor's Choice at Tom's Guide, and fairly light at 5.1 kg. The catch: a one-year warranty.
- **Best value: Xiaomi W30 Pro, from ₪1,074 (about $331).** Scored 8.7 on WiseBuy and was the best value in their test. No app and no Hebrew voice prompts.
- **No compromises: Dreame H16 Pro Steam, from ₪2,068 (about $637).** Scored 9.0 on WiseBuy and has one of the best self-cleaning cycles tested. It is also the heaviest at 6.2 to 6.8 kg, and comes with a three-year warranty.
- **Tineco S9 Artist, about ₪1,989 (about $613):** 8 out of 10 at ynet and a two-year warranty. See below for what is unclear about it.
- **Dyson WashG1, ₪1,990 (about $613): we don't recommend it.** Owner feedback is weak, and the reasons are below.

## Self-cleaning and drying decide whether you keep using it

A roller that stays wet starts to smell. So the first thing to check is what happens after the wash, not how many pascals are on the box.

The Dyson WashG1 rinses its rollers in the dock in 140 seconds, but it does not dry them. Homes & Gardens point out that without self-drying there is a risk of mildew and smell, and one Australian owner returned a Dyson for exactly that reason: the roller sat wet in dirty water after every use. The other models here all dry with heat.

WiseBuy checked this after staining a granite porcelain floor with tomato sauce, Turkish coffee and raspberry juice:

- **Dreame H16 Pro Steam:** a 100-degree hot-water wash and 90-degree hot-air dry, about five minutes. Almost no dark marks, no smell, and only a few grains left in the plastic above the roller. Walla called it one of the best self-cleaning systems they had seen in the category.
- **Roborock F25 Ace Pro:** five minutes, no smell, and a warm, dry roller. Some dark stains and a few hairs were left at the side of the roller.
- **Xiaomi W30 Pro:** drying at 80 degrees that runs for an hour and a half. A long press shortens it to 15 minutes. Slight smells and some residue remained.

Two things to know. First, "hot water" does not always mean hot water. For Roborock, the 95 degrees is the air temperature at the dock outlet, according to a footnote on the maker's site, and Tom's Guide wrote that the model has no heated mopping mode. For Dreame it means 100-degree water. Second, even the best self-cleaning does not clean the dirty tank. You empty and rinse that by hand, and it is what everyone complains about. An Australian H15 Pro owner wrote that the most important habit is emptying it right after each use.

Running cost: according to Roborock, fast drying uses about 0.05 kWh and quiet drying about 0.1 kWh, and a self-clean cycle uses 170 to 230 ml of water.

## Edge cleaning

The closer the roller gets to the wall, the less often you go back with a cloth. Here the maker's figure and what testers saw don't always match.

Roborock claims 0 cm edge cleaning. Tom's Guide wrote that it gets very close to baseboards, but WiseBuy noted that the brush is narrow and sometimes misses the edge in a single pass. Dreame adds an arm that finishes the edge, and Walla wrote that kitchen corners, wall-panel joints and table legs come out clean. For Tineco, ynet and CyberShack both said edges are good but not always perfect. On the Narwal the head swivels up to 63 degrees and gets into corners well, per WiseBuy.

One thing to measure at home: Business Insider wrote about two models, the Tineco S7 Stretch Ultra and the Roborock F25 BX, whose heads are too thick to fit under kitchen cabinet toe-kicks. If your kitchen has a low plinth, measure it.

## Weight and lay-flat

Every model WiseBuy tested lies down to 180 degrees and reaches under sofas and beds. The difference is weight:

- Roborock F25 Ace Pro: 5.1 kg.
- Xiaomi W30 Pro: 5.15 kg, with the weight at the front. WiseBuy wrote that the hand tires after 10 to 15 minutes and that there is some vibration.
- Tineco S9 Artist: about 5.5 kg according to CyberShack. ynet wrote that it feels solid and heavy, and that people with a large home and stairs should look for something lighter.
- Dreame H16 Pro Steam: 6.2 kg at WiseBuy and 6.8 kg at Walla. With full tanks it reaches 7 to 8 kg, per WiseBuy.
- Narwal S30 Pro: 4.3 kg, the lightest in WiseBuy's comparison.

Self-propulsion cuts the effort of pushing, not of lifting it off the dock, over a threshold or up stairs. Walla said so directly about the Dreame: if lifting almost 7 kilos is hard for you, think twice.

If weight comes first, there is also the Bissell CrossWave Reach Plus Select at 3.99 kg, which we covered [separately](/techsite/en/posts/bissell-crosswave-reach-plus-select-israel/). We haven't seen an independent test of it yet, so it is not in the recommendations.

## Tanks

- Roborock F25 Ace Pro: 1 litre clean, 720 ml dirty, plus a 100 ml detergent slot.
- Dreame H16 Pro Steam: 800 ml clean and 600 ml dirty, the smallest in WiseBuy's comparison.
- Narwal S30 Pro: 790 and 720 ml, and the only one with a cloth bag that collects hard debris.
- Dyson WashG1: 1 litre and 800 ml, per Dyson Israel.

Size matters less than how easy the dirty tank is to empty. Roborock has a basket that separates crumbs and hair from the water, and Tom's Guide wrote that it lets you bin everything without touching it. On the Dreame, Walla called cleaning the dirty tank a "nightmare". On the Xiaomi, hair spreads along the tank rather than gathering in one spot, as WiseBuy put it.

## Noise

WiseBuy measured at 80 cm with a phone app, not in an acoustic room, so treat these as a comparison and not as exact figures:

- Mopping: Narwal 52 dB, Dreame 53, Roborock 54, Xiaomi 57.
- Self-cleaning: Dreame and Narwal 45, Xiaomi between 50 and 60, Roborock 54.

Roborock offers a fast dry of about five minutes at 75 dB and a quiet dry of about 30 minutes at 48 dB, per Tom's Guide. If kids nap in the afternoon, pick the quiet one. In the owner ratings for the F25 Ace on ProductReview, noise scored 3.6 out of 5, the lowest of the categories listed there.

## Real battery life

The makers' numbers are for the economy mode. Switch to automatic and you get less:

- **Roborock F25 Ace Pro:** 60 minutes on Eco, 40 on Auto, 30 on Max. Tom's Guide measured the same figures. Business Insider got 34 minutes on Auto from the related F25 BX. Charging takes four hours.
- **Dreame H16 Pro Steam:** up to 72 minutes in quiet mode, but about 18 to 20 minutes on steam, and with hot water, per Walla, runtime drops by about two thirds. Walla wrote that in an average apartment that barely covers it, and in a garden flat or two floors it won't.
- **Tineco S9 Artist:** 50 minutes per the importer, and ynet measured about 40.
- **Xiaomi W30 Pro:** up to 40 minutes per the spec at WiseBuy. A full charge takes about five hours, the longest in the comparison, and the battery indicator is basic.
- **Dyson WashG1:** 35 minutes, with a removable battery. On the four models WiseBuy tested, the battery is fixed.

So in a big apartment or on two floors, don't count on steam or max mode, and check the runtime in automatic mode, not in economy.

## Pet hair and long hair

This is the big maintenance issue, because hair wrapped around a roller means scissors.

- **Dreame:** the TangleCut mechanism cuts hair. Walla wrote that in a home with long hair or pets, the tangled-clump experience never happened and the roller stayed clean.
- **Roborock:** owner feedback is good. An Australian F25 Ace owner who looks after other people's dogs wrote that nothing clogs and that a round dropped from an hour to 15 minutes. Still, at WiseBuy a few hairs were left at the side of the roller after self-cleaning, and with the earlier F25 Ace the hair caught in the filter fell into the water when the tank was emptied.
- **Tineco:** ynet wrote that it is excellent at picking up pet hair.
- **Narwal:** has a mode that only cuts hair, with no wash or drying.

One owner tip: someone with a heavily shedding dog wrote about the H15 Pro that it pays to go over the floor with a regular vacuum first, because a lot of hair sticks to the wet roller. That holds for any model.

## Sticky messes and spills

WiseBuy smeared the three messes across half a 60 by 120 cm tile and counted passes. Most models lifted most of the mess in one pass, but nearly always a thin strip was left that needed another pass. On the Xiaomi the coffee took three to four passes, and WiseBuy wrote in their summary that its washing "still doesn't replace a mop".

Mess that has dried is another story, and some spills just get smeared:

- Tom's Guide on the Roborock Ace Pro: wet soy sauce smeared on the first pass, and dried mud smeared until they added foam. With the foam it came up.
- Walla on the Dreame: stains that have sat for days, something sweet spilled, old grease by the stove don't vanish, even with steam. On fresh mess it is excellent.
- Homes & Gardens on the Dyson: mustard smeared across the floor and took several extra passes.

What to do in practice: clean spills right away, and for a dry stain start with foam or solution and be ready to go over it twice. The Dreame's steam suits spots in the kitchen and bathroom, not the whole house.

## Floors: porcelain, parquet and rugs

**Porcelain and granite:** the easy case. WiseBuy tested on 60 by 120 granite porcelain, and the results we quote are on that floor. The problem is grout lines and rough tiles. An Australian H15 Pro owner wrote that on lightly textured tiles it only does a superficial job and does not clean the grout. That is one review, not a statistic, but you should know it exists.

**Parquet:** Dreame Israel's page says the H16 is not suitable for real wood parquet with a natural finish. The Barvaz Hahashmal store writes that on parquet you wash only sealed parquet, with little water, following the floor maker's instructions. Check with your parquet's maker first.

**Rugs:** every model here is for hard floors. On a rug they leave it damp, as Tom's Guide wrote about the Roborock. Homes & Gardens tested a Dreame model that is also meant for carpets and wrote that fine dust stayed between the fibres, so it is no replacement for a regular vacuum.

## Consumables and what they cost

- **Replacement roller:** Roborock, ₪80 (about $25) at ANYFIX. Dreame H16 Pro, ₪99 (about $30) at Dreame Israel. Tineco, ₪130 (about $40) at the importer's site, and a filter at ₪80 (about $25).
- **Cleaning solution:** Tineco sells a litre for ₪69 (about $21) and a 480 ml bottle for ₪30 (about $9). Its advice: one capful per full water tank. We found no price for Roborock's or Dreame's solution, but the box has 480 and 500 ml.
- **Don't skimp on the solution:** Tineco's site says a non-original solution may void the warranty, Dreame's says to use only liquid approved by the importer, and Roborock recommends its own.

How long does a roller last? We found no reliable figure from any maker, so we won't invent one. When we checked the Tineco importer's site on November 18, a large share of the rollers and filters for S9 and S7 models were marked out of stock. That is not a finding about the machine, but it is worth asking about before you buy.

## Warranty and service in Israel

- **Roborock F25 Ace Pro:** one year, Modan Electronics.
- **Xiaomi W30 Pro:** one year, Hamilton.
- **Dreame H16 Pro Steam:** three years (two plus one limited, per the terms), official importer.
- **Tineco S9 Artist:** two years, Unico.
- **Dyson WashG1:** two years, BNZK.

Two notes. First, on Zap you see offers for the same model from stores that don't state the import channel, and sometimes the warranty comes from the store, not the importer. Check the warranty card for who gives it before you buy. Second, we have no data on actual service in Israel, only on warranty terms. Australian owner ratings include complaints about Dreame support and Dyson repair times, and about a Roborock that failed after seven months, but those don't necessarily reflect what happens here.

## The models, one by one

### Roborock F25 Ace Pro, ₪1,190 (about $366)

That is the price at most shops: Kravitz, Lior, Partner and Zap. By phone order we also found ₪1,013 at Barvaz Hahashmal. It scored 9.0 on WiseBuy, and in an earlier WiseBuy test the regular F25 Ace won. Tom's Guide gave it Editor's Choice and wrote that it clears tough stains fast, glides over the floor with almost no effort and leaves it nearly dry.

The downsides: a one-year warranty, a battery that drains fast on the stronger modes, and no heat. On Amazon US it is rated 3.9 out of 5 from about 2,550 ratings, lower than the 4.2 of the Dreame H15 Pro Heat and the 4.4 of the Tineco S7 Stretch Ultra. The previous F25 Ace has 4.5 from 59 reviews on ProductReview Australia, 88 percent positive, though one reviewer wrote that hers broke after seven months. We found no large owner site for the Ace Pro itself beyond Amazon.

### Xiaomi W30 Pro, from ₪1,074 (about $331)

On Zap prices run from ₪1,074 to ₪1,279, with a one-year warranty from Hamilton. WiseBuy called it the best value and wrote that models costing far more beat it in the score by only a tenth of a point. What it lacks: an app, Hebrew voice prompts, a separate detergent slot and a detailed battery indicator. What to know: a five-hour charge, a front-heavy body and a wash that doesn't fully replace a mop. We found too few owner ratings to say anything about reliability.

### Dreame H16 Pro Steam, from ₪2,068 (about $637)

On Zap prices run from ₪2,068 to ₪2,990. On Dreame Israel it is ₪2,533 at a 15 percent sale (regular price ₪2,990, about $921), and it was marked out of stock when we looked. It carries a three-year importer warranty: two full years plus one limited. WiseBuy named it the best and scored it 9.0, and Walla called it "a good wet-dry vacuum with an impressive feature list".

What works: edges, TangleCut, self-cleaning, Hebrew voice, and an LED light that shows dirt. What doesn't: the weight, steam that drains the battery in twenty minutes, a small dirty tank that is hard to clean, and old sticky mess. We found no real owner ratings for it yet, so we don't know how it holds up after a year. The previous H15 Pro has 4.2 from 611 ratings on Amazon US, and 3.0 from 15 reviews in Australia, almost half positive and half negative. The causes: app problems, water that doesn't come out, and customer service.

### Tineco S9 Artist, about ₪1,989 (about $613)

That is how we found it at ElectricShop, which lists it as Unico import with a two-year warranty, subject to the supplier's stock. On Zap the range is wide, ₪1,028 to ₪3,499, and many offers don't state the import channel. ynet gave it 8 out of 10: excellent cleaning, good pet-hair pickup, but heavy and expensive. At launch, per the same article, it cost ₪3,690. CyberShack gave it 4 out of 5. On ProductReview Australia the S9 series scores 5.0 from 13 reviews and the S7 series 4.1 from 45. The S7 Stretch Ultra has 4.4 from 3,550 ratings on Amazon US, and Business Insider picked it as the best overall wet-dry vacuum after testing 30 models. It is a related model, not the same one, so we mention it only as background.

Note that on the importer's site on November 18 the S9 Artist was marked out of stock, and the new S9 Artist II was listed at ₪2,050. We found no independent test of the II, so every test here is of the S9 Artist. The importer's list also has cheaper models: the S7 Steam at ₪1,690 and the i6 Stretch at ₪1,590, but we haven't read tests of them.

### Dyson WashG1, ₪1,990 (about $613): we don't recommend it

It sells directly on Dyson Israel for ₪1,990 and at other shops for ₪2,499 to ₪3,190. Homes & Gardens summed it up as "struggles to compete": mustard smeared across the floor, a messy debris tray, no self-drying and no floor sensor. On ProductReview Australia it is rated 2.4 out of 5 from 56 reviews, nearly 60 percent of them negative, with complaints about the debris tray, a battery that won't charge after a repair, and water not reaching the roller. It does have real advantages: a removable battery, a two-year warranty and a brand store in Israel. That is not enough for us.

## How to choose

- **Porcelain floors and a regular home:** Roborock F25 Ace Pro or Xiaomi W30 Pro. Choose by how much you care about an app and Hebrew voice prompts.
- **A home with a dog or long hair, and you're fine carrying it:** Dreame H16 Pro Steam, but not for the steam.
- **Stairs or trouble lifting:** the lightest are the Bissell (3.99 kg) and the Narwal S30 Pro (4.3 kg, from ₪1,702, about $524), followed by the Roborock and Xiaomi at about 5.1 kg.
- **You want a two-year warranty and an established brand:** Tineco, and ask about spare-part stock.

Rules of thumb that repeat across every test: empty the dirty tank after each use, use the original solution, and wipe old stains with a cloth first.

- Large owner-rating samples for the Xiaomi W30 Pro and the Dreame H16 Pro Steam.
