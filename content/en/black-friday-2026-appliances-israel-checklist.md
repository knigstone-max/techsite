Two weeks to go. US Thanksgiving falls on Thursday, November 26 this year, so Black Friday is Friday, November 27, and Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30. Many Israeli chains start their sales a few days early and stretch them to Cyber Monday. Bug's Black Friday page, for example, still only promises that "the best deals" are coming.

On earbuds or a charger, a bad call costs you a few dozen shekels. On a four-door fridge or a washer-dryer, it's thousands, plus costs that never appear on the sign: delivery, installation, warranty and removal. This is the list we'd go through before hitting "pay."

![Checklist: six things to check before buying a big appliance on Black Friday, from screenshotting the price to your right to cancel](img/posts/black-friday-appliances-checklist-en.webp "Six checks before you pay. Graphic: TechSite")

## 1. Screenshot the price now

The struck-out price next to the sale price tells you nothing. What matters is what the product cost in the weeks before. Price-tracking site SmartPick follows several Israeli stores, and its data shows how much prices move even without an announced sale: at KSP, the Dreame X60 Ultra robot vacuum went from ₪4,219 to a "sale" price of ₪4,469 (about $1,397), and on the same day, November 10, Hashmal Neto sold it for ₪4,089 (about $1,278). A regular price at one store can beat a sale price at another.

The fix is simple. Take a screenshot of the product you want, today, at two or three stores. When the "30% off" sign appears, you'll know whether it's real.

## 2. Match the full model number

With big appliances, a letter or two at the end of the model name can mean a different color, capacity or version of the same product. Before comparing prices, make sure it's the exact same model number, not just the same series. That goes double for TVs, where one screen size sometimes comes in several versions with different panels.

## 3. Who gives the warranty, and on what

According to Israel's Consumer Protection Authority, new electrical products priced above ₪150 carry at least a one-year warranty. For anything that needs installation, that year only starts once it's installed. That's the legal floor. Above it, sellers and importers differ a lot:

- On a 317-liter Samsung fridge sold by Sali Electric, official importer Samline gives a full year plus nine more years on the inverter compressor.
- At Machsanei Hashmal, ten years of compressor warranty (1+9) on some Haier fridges costs an extra ₪199 (about $62).

So when comparing a fridge between two stores, compare the warranty line too. A parallel import can be cheaper, but check who repairs it, and where.

## 4. Delivery, stairs and installation

This is where the extras hide. At Sali Electric, for example, delivery in a building without an elevator costs ₪80 (about $25) per floor above the second, and taking fridge doors off is paid to the mover directly. A gas cooktop or oven needs a licensed gas technician, which costs ₪249 to ₪299 (about $78 to $93) there. Rates vary by store, but ask in advance: is there an elevator, does the fridge fit in it, and what does the price include?

Delivery time is worth checking before the big day too. SmartPick found that in mid-October, none of the eight TVs it tracked were in stock at store branches, and seven were marked for direct shipping from the importer. If the machine has to arrive before Hanukkah, which starts on the evening of December 4 this year, confirm the date before you pay.

## 5. They take the old one for free

Plenty of people don't know this. Under section 30 of Israel's law on environmental treatment of electrical and electronic equipment, a seller of household electrical equipment must accept a similar old appliance, of similar quantity or weight, at the time of sale or delivery, without charging for it. It applies to online sales too.

"Similar" is the key word. Buy a fridge, hand over a fridge. Buy a washer, hand over a washer. You can't buy a fridge and ask them to take the old dryer. If the delivery crew asks for money to haul away a comparable machine, that's against the law.

## 6. Your right to cancel, and what it costs

When buying online or by phone, you can cancel within 14 days, even if you've used the product. The cancellation fee is 5% of the price or ₪100 (about $31), whichever is lower. There's an important catch with big appliances, though: according to the Consumer Protection Authority's guide, the consumer has to return the product to the business at their own expense. With a four-door fridge, that's not a quick trip to the post office.

In a physical store the rules are stricter. In the authority's example, you can cancel an electrical product bought in a store within 14 days as long as it hasn't been plugged in, again with the same fee. If the product is faulty, doesn't match what was promised, or wasn't delivered on time, no cancellation fee may be charged at all.

## The bottom line

Black Friday can be a good time to buy a big appliance, especially when it falls a week before Hanukkah. Just remember that the big number on the sign isn't the final price. The real price is that number plus extended warranty, delivery, installation and removal, whenever they aren't included. Compare the whole package, not just the tag, and you'll end November with a deal that's actually good.
