A few days ago Cell Now, OnePlus's official importer, started selling the OnePlus Buds Pro 4 in Israel. These are the company's flagship earbuds, and they're entering one of the most crowded categories there is: noise-cancelling in-ear buds, where Samsung, Sony and Apple are all competing too.

I didn't live with them for a month. I went through the official specs, the importer's terms here, and the first published testing, including a battery run-down and a test of the OPPO Enco X4, which that testing describes as almost identical in hardware. The picture is fairly clear: excellent sound for the money, a battery that keeps its promise, and noise cancelling that improved a lot but still isn't at the top.

## The price in Israel

The list price is ₪649 (about $203), in two colors: Titanium Shadow grey and Polar White. You can buy them on OnePlus Israel's site in up to 12 interest-free payments, with the importer's warranty and service. According to Cell Now, the model sold here is the European one, CE-certified and with over-the-air updates. That's not a minor detail. Chinese-market earbuds sometimes ship with different firmware, and some features don't work on them.

AliExpress sellers already list them for around $135 to $150 (about ₪432 to ₪480), which is cheaper, but with no local warranty and no certainty about which version you'll get. For comparison, the company's clip-on Buds Clip cost ₪499 (about $156) here.

## Design and comfort

From the outside, almost nothing changed from the last generation: short, glossy stems and a round clamshell case that grew by a millimeter in length and three in width. Each earbud weighs 5.6 g and the case 41.8 g. There are four pairs of tips in the box, from XS to L, so most ears will find a fit. Controls are still a pinch on the stem.

The earbuds are rated IP55, so they handle dust and splashes, and a run in the rain is fine. The case has no rating at all, so don't leave it open next to the sink.

## Sound: where they win

This is why you'd buy them. Each earbud has two drivers, an 11mm dynamic woofer and a 6mm planar tweeter, each with its own DAC, tuned by Danish speaker maker Dynaudio. OnePlus claims 50% stronger bass drive than the Buds Pro 3 and a 10Hz to 40kHz frequency response.

According to the published testing, the change you'll notice is in the highs: sharper and more detailed, with less distortion and grain than before. The bass is strong without bleeding into the rest of the range, and vocals sit up front. There's also a caveat that comes up more than once: on tracks heavy with cymbals and drums, the highs can turn harsh and tiring. Luckily, the app gives you plenty to work with: five Dynaudio presets, dynamic bass boost, a manual 10-band EQ, Golden Sound, which calibrates the sound to your ears, and spatial audio with head tracking. A few minutes of tweaking takes the edge off.

High-quality wireless audio comes through LHDC 5.0, up to 24-bit/192kHz. Note that it's capped at 48kHz by default, and you have to raise it in the app. And here's the big asterisk: iPhones don't support LHDC, and neither do most Samsung phones. There you'll be on AAC, which is still very good, but not what's on the box.

## Noise cancelling

OnePlus says the ANC blocks up to twice as much noise as the Buds Pro 3, without giving a decibel figure. Testers agree the improvement is real and noticeable, and that on the strongest setting you can work comfortably in an open-plan office or next to a noisy PC. They also agree it's not in the same league as Bose, Sony or Apple. On the train to Haifa, it'll do the job. On a long flight, the pricier rivals are quieter.

There's an adaptive mode that reads your surroundings and adjusts the strength, a fixed mode, and transparency. In testing of OPPO's twin earbuds, automatic transparency during calls didn't work properly, and it's not yet clear whether OnePlus has fixed that.

## Calls

Each earbud has three microphones plus a VPU sensor that picks up your voice through bone vibration. In testing, background noise isolation was very good: a handheld vacuum at around 75 dB nearly vanished from the recording. The trade-off is that your voice sounds a little muffled, though perfectly clear. In a quiet room there's no noticeable gain over the last generation.

## Battery and charging

This is where OnePlus actually lives up to its numbers. The company quotes up to 13 hours from the earbuds and 53 with the case on AAC with ANC off. A published test at 50% volume got 12 hours, very close. With ANC on, that drops to about 6 hours and 26 with the case, and with LHDC plus ANC to 5.5 and 22.

![Battery chart from OnePlus's figures: 13 and 53 hours with ANC off, 6 and 26 with ANC on, 5.5 and 22 on LHDC](img/posts/oneplus-buds-pro-4-battery-en.webp "OnePlus's claimed battery life. Graphic: TechSite")

Ten minutes of charging gives up to 14 hours of listening with the case, and a full charge of everything takes about 65 minutes. The case charges over USB-C, on a wireless pad, or by reverse charging from a OnePlus phone.

## The features, and who gets them

The list includes Bluetooth 6.1 with two devices at once, Google Fast Pair and Microsoft Swift Pair, Gemini by voice, and a new neck-health feature that notices when your head has been tilted for too long. But some of the more interesting features are reserved for the company's own phones. Live translation only works on OnePlus phones running ColorOS 17 or later and is capped at 5,000 minutes a month. Game mode with 39ms latency is only supported on select models, and Dolby-processed audio recording needs a OnePlus 10 or newer.

In short: with a OnePlus or OPPO phone you get the full package. With other Android phones you get excellent earbuds. With an iPhone you get good earbuds, without LHDC and without some of the features.

:::score
## The bottom line

7/10

The OnePlus Buds Pro 4 sound like they cost more than they do, and their battery lands almost exactly on the manufacturer's numbers. At ₪649 with the importer's warranty, they're a good buy for Android owners, especially OnePlus owners. If your priority is total silence on flights, or you use an iPhone, Sony, Bose or Apple will serve you better, even if you pay more.
:::

**What I liked**

- Detailed sound with strong, clean bass
- Lots of tuning tools: Dynaudio presets and a 10-band EQ
- Battery life that nearly matches the claims
- Wireless and fast charging
- European model with updates and a local warranty

**What I didn't**

- Good ANC, not great
- Highs that can get harsh without tuning
- Translation, game mode and enhanced recording only with OnePlus phones
- No LHDC on iPhones and most Samsungs
- A case with no water resistance at all
