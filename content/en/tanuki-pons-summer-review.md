Some games slip into your life quietly, without giant trailers or marketing blitzes, and just make you feel good. TANUKI: Pon's Summer is one of those. It's out today, November 10, with timing that's hard to explain: right after the Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake and right before GTA 6. Give it a chance anyway and you'll find a small, sweet game made with a lot of care.

It comes from Denkiworks, a Kyoto studio founded by three developers who previously worked together on Cursed to Golf. This is their debut. The publisher is Critical Reflex, best known for much darker games like Buckshot Roulette and Mouthwashing. This time they went the opposite way.

![TANUKI: Pon's Summer at a glance: ₪70 on the Israeli Xbox Store, $19.99 in the US and included with Game Pass](img/posts/tanuki-pons-summer-overview-en.webp "What you get, and what it costs. Graphic: TechSite")

## The story: a tanuki in debt

Pon is a tanuki, the raccoon dog of Japanese folklore, who has let himself go a bit. He lives in a little wooden apartment at the island's sacred shrine and has spent most of the shrine's savings on video games and snacks. Life is good, until he realizes the Great Tanuki Festival, which only comes around once every 50 years, is a month away. The shrine is falling apart, and the money is gone.

So Pon takes a summer job at the local post office. The goal is to restore four key parts of the shrine: the torii gate, the water purification pavilion, the guardian statues and the prayer plaque stand. You earn money and items from deliveries and activities around the island, and you get to choose how the shrine looks in the end.

The festival countdown isn't meant to stress you out. Each in-game day is really a chapter of the story, and you have as long as you need to finish that day's tasks before going to bed. There's no ticking clock punishing you for wandering off.

![Pon riding a bike with a box on his back, with a tall pagoda and stairs in the background](img/posts/tanuki-pons-summer-pagoda-ride.webp "Around the island, on the way to the next delivery. Image: Critical Reflex / Denkiworks")

## The loop: mail, and more mail

Most days look similar. Wake up, ride to the post office, pick up the day's parcels and deliver them to the locals. Some parcels are routine, just drop them off and get paid. Others matter more and open up a side quest. Parcels vary in size, weight and shape, and part of the challenge is figuring out how to get them there at all.

This is where the game earns its keep. The side quests are mostly fetch quests, but many end in a short mini-game. You pour pints for the crowd at a bar, arrange sushi for a chef, step into a sumo ring, fish crates out of the sea with a crane, drum with a band at an open mic night, and there's a baseball game too. Not every mini-game is brilliant, but the game doesn't keep throwing the same one at you, so it never gets old.

## The BMX

To get around the island, Pon has his trusty BMX, which navigates better than he does: you can only jump while riding, not on foot. The controls are simple and pleasant, and mid-air tricks are a direction plus a button. One thing bugged me: accelerate and grind share a button, and I'd rather they were split.

More than 100 BMX challenges are scattered around the map, across five difficulty tiers. There are checkpoint races, runs to a distant point, score attacks and combo challenges. Most are addictive, the kind that makes you stop halfway to a delivery and say "fine, one more." The combo challenges are the exception. Chaining tricks together with the wheelie move isn't reliable enough, and this is no Tony Hawk.

![Pon grinding along a yellow rail above the rooftops, with sparks under the wheels and a bridge in the background](img/posts/tanuki-pons-summer-bmx-trick.webp "Grinding over the rooftops. Combos are a different story. Image: Critical Reflex / Denkiworks")

## The little things

What wins you over is how much thought went into the details. The soundtrack, for instance, is always playing in two versions at once: a calm one and a rock one with electric guitar. Hop on the bike and the music slides seamlessly into the rock version, hop off and it settles back down. It sounds small, but it's one of the things that makes the game.

There's also fishing and bug catching for an encyclopedia, straight out of Animal Crossing and just as compulsive. Finish a BMX challenge and the game drops you right back where you left off and points you back to your delivery. Pray at small shrines to unlock a network of fast-travel points, and at the start of each day you can skip straight from Pon's bedroom to the post office. It's a game that respects your time.

And for anyone into cosmetics, Pon gets special hats inspired by games like Hollow Knight: Silksong, PEAK and Shovel Knight.

![Pon standing on a wooden dock with a fishing rod, an orange float on blue water and a wooden house and trees behind him](img/posts/tanuki-pons-summer-fishing.webp "A fishing break between deliveries. Image: Critical Reflex / Denkiworks")

## What didn't work as well

At the end of the day, this is a game of fetch quests. The mini-games do everything they can to hide that, and mostly succeed, but if you want depth or a real challenge you won't find it here. The bike handling, great for cruising, isn't tight enough for the hardest combo challenges either.

## Price and platforms

It's out on PC via Steam, Switch and Switch 2, PS4 and PS5, and Xbox Series X|S. On the Israeli Xbox Store it costs ₪70 (about $22), and in the US it's $19.99 (about ₪63). Game Pass members get it from day one on console and PC. Buy it on Switch and you get the Switch 2 version free, and vice versa. At the time of writing Steam hadn't listed a shekel price yet, but there's a free 2-3 hour demo covering the first three days, and your progress carries over to the full game.

There's no Hebrew, but the English is simple, and it works for kids who read a little English. It's cute enough to play with them, too.

## The bottom line

7/10

TANUKI: Pon's Summer is a little hug of a game. Riding around is fun, the island is charming, the dynamic soundtrack is lovely, the dialogue is light and funny, and the mini-games keep things fresh. It loses points for a loop built mostly on fetch quests and for controls that aren't precise enough for the combo challenges. At ₪70, or free on Game Pass, it's an easy recommendation if you want something relaxing between this month's two giants.
:::

**What I liked**

- Riding a BMX all over the island
- A wide variety of mini-games
- A soundtrack that changes style when you get on the bike
- Most of the BMX challenges are addictive
- A game that respects your time

**What I liked less**

- Mostly fetch quests
- Controls aren't tight enough for combo challenges
- Accelerate and grind on the same button
