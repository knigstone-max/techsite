Israelis bake a lot. Challah for Friday, sourdough, holiday cakes, pizza dough with the kids. If you bake every week, at some point your old mixer starts running hot, walking across the counter, or just giving up on heavy dough. Bosch built the Series 8 for exactly those people. It's the brand's most powerful mixer, and the silver MUMS8ZS00 is the base version sold in Israel.

It isn't cheap. In early November I found it at Bug for ₪3,548 (about $1,131), at BetterShop for ₪3,549, at Hameir for ₪3,590, and on Zap for ₪3,589 with delivery. So roughly ₪3,550, with hardly any difference between stores.

## Power is the main reason to buy it

The motor is rated at 1,800W, and Bosch calls it the most powerful stand mixer made in Europe. It's air-cooled, and Bosch says it can handle eight kneading cycles in a row without overheating. The company also tested it for 420 hours of use, which by its own maths is 15 years in a home kitchen.

In practice, heavy yeast dough, like a big challah or a family-sized batch of pizza dough, shouldn't worry it. Bosch talks about a range from two egg whites up to dough for ten baguettes. The power isn't there just for the spec sheet. It's what lets the mixer work a large bowl without struggling.

## A big bowl, and a motion that covers all of it

The bowl is 6 litres of stainless steel, with two handles. Bosch rates it for 3.6 kg of cake mix, 3 kg of yeast dough or 2.8 kg of bread dough, with up to 1.7 kg of flour. That's several large challahs in one go, a real plus for big families or anyone who also bakes for the neighbours.

The tools move in what Bosch calls 3D PlanetaryMixing. They spin on their own axis while tracing a figure eight around the bowl. The idea is to reach the sides and the bottom so there's no dry flour stuck at the edge, and you don't have to stop and scrape every few minutes.

## Built-in scale and auto programs

The most useful everyday feature is the scale built into the base. You can weigh straight into the bowl as you add ingredients, or on the top of the machine. If you bake from recipes in grams, you know what that saves: fewer dishes and less shuffling between a scale and the bowl.

![Close-up of the silver Bosch Series 8 mixer's display, with the built-in scale and time and speed readouts](img/posts/bosch-series-8-mums8-display.webp "The display and the scale built into the base. Image: Bosch")

On top of that you get seven speeds plus pulse, a timer you can set in advance, and sensor-based auto programs. The machine recognises which tool is attached and adjusts its settings, and it starts slowly so flour doesn't end up all over the kitchen. The programs suit people who'd rather not think about speed and time, though experienced bakers will probably stick to manual most of the time.

## What's in the box, and what costs extra

You get the stainless bowl with a lid, a professional kneading hook, a professional whisk, and a flexible beater Bosch calls the "guitar", which is good for soft batters and creams. That covers most jobs. Accessories like a meat mincer or a chopper are sold separately.

The whole body is metal, in pearl silver. It measures 33.9 by 22.3 cm at the base and 45.7 cm tall, and weighs 11.6 kg. This isn't something you'll want to lift out of a cupboard every day. If you don't have a permanent spot on the counter for it, think twice. The power cord is 120 cm long.

## Warranty and service

The importer is BSH Home Appliances, with service on *2220. According to the importer's terms, the motor has a 10-year warranty. That matters on an appliance at this price, so keep the receipt and make sure you buy from a store selling the official import.

## Against its smaller siblings

Bosch's lower series have weaker mixers: the Series 6 with 1,600W and a 5.5-litre bowl, and the Series 4 with 1,000W and a 3.9-litre bowl. If you bake a cake every other week, you don't need a Series 8. The premium makes sense mainly if you work a lot with heavy dough, or if the built-in scale and programs matter to you.

## The bottom line

8/10

The Bosch Series 8 is a serious mixer for people who bake a lot and in quantity. The strong motor, the big bowl and the built-in scale make the work easier, and the 10-year motor warranty is reassuring. It loses points on price, weight and size, and for charging extra for basics like a mincer. If you bake once a month, a cheaper model will do.
:::

**What I liked**

- 1,800W air-cooled motor
- 6-litre bowl that handles big batches
- Built-in scale that weighs right in the bowl
- Metal body and a 10-year motor warranty
- Automatic tool recognition and a gentle start

**What I didn't**

- High price, about ₪3,550
- Heavy and big, it needs a permanent spot
- Accessories like a meat mincer cost extra
