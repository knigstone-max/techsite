Late November is best-of-the-year season, and the app stores join in. On November 17 Google announced its **Best of Play 2026** winners, and a day later Apple published its **45 App Store Award finalists**. Apple's winners will be named in the coming weeks.

The catch with these lists is that they're written for an American audience. Some apps aren't available here, some don't understand Hebrew, and some cost money without saying so up front. So we went through both lists, looked up each app in Apple's Israeli store, and picked ten worth downloading.

![Table: ten apps of 2026, with the award or nomination, what each does and its price in the Israeli store](img/posts/best-apps-2026-list-en.webp "Our ten. Graphic: TechSite")

## Reading, writing and work

**Wispr Flow.** Google's Best Innovative App. You talk, it hands back tidy text with punctuation and without the "umm"s, in any app: email, WhatsApp, a document. It runs on Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android. **And yes, it supports Hebrew**, with one catch: according to Wispr's help page, Hebrew is one of the languages it can't identify from speech on its own. You have to go into settings and pick Hebrew explicitly. And if you also pick another language it can't auto-detect, say Russian or Arabic, it may mix them up, so choose only one of those. Free to download, with a paid subscription.

**Notability.** Google's Best App of the year and its productivity winner too. It's a long-running iPad notebook that was rebuilt natively for Android this year. According to Google, its AI lets you chat with your materials, build study organisers and generate custom quizzes. For students, and for Israeli high schoolers heading into bagrut exams, it's worth a try. On iPhone it also has a Hebrew interface.

**Things 3.** A Mac App of the Year finalist. A simple, good-looking to-do list. It's the only pick here that costs money up front: **₪20.90 in the Israeli store** for iPhone (about $6.25; $6.99 in the US store).

**ElevenReader.** An iPhone App of the Year finalist. It reads any text aloud, books, PDFs or documents, in AI voices that sound surprisingly natural. Free in the Israeli store. Per its store listing, Hebrew isn't among its interface languages, so test how it handles Hebrew text before you rely on it.

## Phone, watch and TV

**(Not Boring) Camera.** Another Apple iPhone finalist: a camera that looks and feels like an old one, with settings you can tweak and a natural look to its photos. Free to download.

**Raylie.** An iPhone finalist, and maybe the most fun on the list. Enter a flight and it tells you what you'll see from the window along the way: sunrise, the northern lights, landmarks. If you fly out of Ben Gurion and always wonder which side to sit on, it's a good place to start. Free.

**Pocket Casts.** The only app on both lists: Google's pick for watches, and an Apple TV App of the Year finalist. A veteran podcast player with good apps for both the watch and the TV. Free to download.

**Strava.** Google's Best Cross-device App. If you run or ride, you probably have it already. Google singled out hands-free logging from a Wear OS watch and the lock-screen widget that shows your weekly progress.

## Everyday life

**Hearing Buddy.** An Apple Cultural Impact finalist that helps people who are deaf or hard of hearing follow a conversation by turning speech into text. Free in the Israeli store. Check that it handles Hebrew before recommending it to your grandmother, because the listing doesn't say.

**Sofascore.** Google's winner for sports fans. Live scores as they happen. Free to download, with ads.

## What you won't find here

Two apps from the lists aren't in Apple's Israeli store, based on our check this morning: **Qobuz**, the high-resolution music service that won Google's entertainment award, and **Karrot**, a neighbourhood second-hand marketplace on Apple's list. Letterboxd, the film recommendations network nominated for Apple's TV award, is available here.

## How to choose

- **"Free" usually means the download.** Almost every app on this list has a subscription or in-app purchases. Check before you get hooked.
- **Hebrew is the first question.** Most of these have no Hebrew interface. That matters less for a camera, and a lot more for an app that reads or takes dictation.
- **An award isn't everything.** These lists reflect American editors' taste. If you only take one app from here, Wispr Flow with Hebrew set up properly is the one that'll save you the most time each day.
