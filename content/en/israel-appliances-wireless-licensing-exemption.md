Most washing machines, air conditioners and fridges sold today ship with Wi-Fi or Bluetooth for an app, remote control or software updates. In Israel, that small radio has also been the reason an ordinary appliance could end up waiting on the Ministry of Communications. Communications Minister Shlomo Karhi has now signed an amendment to the Wireless Telegraphy (Licensing Exemption) Regulations, 5787-2026, that takes 13 categories of home products out of that queue, provided they carry a CE mark.

According to the ministry's October 19 announcement, the rules take effect once they're published in Reshumot, the official gazette. The industry site Zman Avir reported that they kick in five days after publication.

![A washing machine with a Wi-Fi logo, annotated to show that the wireless module used to need approval and a CE mark is now enough](img/posts/appliance-wifi-exemption-annotated-en.webp "That little logo is the whole story: a Wi-Fi module pulled white goods into a communications licensing process")

## What's covered

The list comes in three groups, all conditional on a CE mark:

- **White goods and air conditioning:** air conditioners, fridges or freezers, washing machines, dryers, dishwashers and air purifiers.
- **Kitchen:** coffee machines, water bars, ovens or microwaves, induction hobs and cooker hoods.
- **Other:** home sound projectors and passive RFID plastic cards.

It applies to both commercial and personal imports. The CE mark shows a product meets European rules, including the EU Radio Equipment Directive, and that's the ministry's logic: if a device has already passed the European checks, there's no reason to test it again in Israel.

## How it worked until now

The ministry says appliances with a wireless component sometimes needed their own licensing process. That meant an online application in the ministry's system, specs and lab reports, special marking on the packaging, and renewing the approval every two years. For an importer bringing in dozens of A/C and washer models, that's one more layer of paperwork and time before stock leaves the port.

## The car part

There's a second track that has nothing to do with kitchens. Anyone importing up to five CarPlay adapters (the rules call them screen-mirroring devices for cars) or car multimedia systems per shipment for personal use is exempt, even without a CE mark. Commercial imports of those products still need a conformity approval.

## Will prices drop?

The ministry talks about faster port clearance, lower regulatory costs for official and parallel importers, more choice and more competition. Nobody has promised specific price cuts, and the exemption only removes the Ministry of Communications requirement. Customs, safety and standards rules still apply, so a fridge shipped from Europe still has to meet everything else Israeli law requires of it.

What you can say is that parallel importers of white goods, and anyone ordering a smart coffee machine from a European store, have one less hurdle. In a market where app-connected A/Cs and washers are now the default, that matters.

## The numbers behind it

This isn't the first exemption. Over the past two years the ministry also exempted security cameras and baby monitors for personal import, and game consoles for commercial import. It says the number of parcels it had to handle and inspect at the post office and shipping companies fell from 125,000 in 2024 to about 60,000 in 2025, and it expects a further drop in 2026.
