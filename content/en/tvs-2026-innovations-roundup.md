Our year-end series has been through [the laundry room](/techsite/en/posts/washers-dryers-2026-innovations-roundup/), [floor cleaning](/techsite/en/posts/floor-cleaning-2026-innovations-roundup/), [fridges](/techsite/en/posts/fridges-2026-innovations-roundup/) and [the kitchen](/techsite/en/posts/kitchen-appliances-2026-innovations-roundup/). Now it's the living room, and the TV.

If you had to sum up 2026 in TVs in one line: the colour moved into the backlight. Almost every big brand launched a TV where red, green and blue LEDs sit behind the panel instead of plain white or blue ones. Alongside that, OLED took a real step up in brightness, Mini LED got cheap enough to be the default, and a new version of Dolby Vision started rolling out.

![Six things that changed in TVs in 2026: RGB backlights, brighter OLED, cheaper Mini LED, Dolby Vision 2, a cyan LED and giant screens](img/posts/tvs-2026-trends-en.webp "The year in six panels. Graphic: TechSite")

## 1. RGB backlights: the idea of the year

A regular LCD TV, including most Mini LED models, lights the panel with blue or white LEDs and gets its colours from filters and a quantum dot layer. In an RGB TV the backlight itself is made of separate red, green and blue LEDs, and the set controls each colour in each zone. The promise is purer colour at very high brightness. Several of these TVs cover 100% of BT.2020, the widest colour space used in video, which ordinary LED TVs couldn't do.

Everyone has their own name for it:

- **Samsung, Micro RGB.** In 2025 there was one 115-inch model. For 2026 Samsung expanded the range to 55, 65, 75, 85, 100 and 115 inches, with LEDs smaller than 100 microns and VDE certification for 100% of BT.2020. In Israel, the 55-inch R85H starts at ₪4,678 (about $1,406) on Zap, with offers going up to ₪7,490 (about $2,252).
- **TCL, RGB-Mini LED.** In Israel it's sold as the Q7D RGB and as the RM7L, the same hardware through two importers, in 65 to 98 inches. It has up to 2,000 nits, 1,344 to 2,880 dimming zones depending on size and 144Hz, and starts at ₪4,990 (about $1,500) for 65 inches.
- **Sony, True RGB.** The BRAVIA 9 II and BRAVIA 7 II, announced in May, are Sony's first BRAVIA TVs with independently driven RGB LEDs. The 7 II comes in 50 to 98 inches, at $2,099.99 (about ₪6,984) for 55 inches in the US. We didn't find Israeli prices yet.
- **Hisense, RGB MiniLED evo.** Hisense, which showed the first RGB LED TV at CES 2025, added a fourth colour this year: a cyan LED that helps with the transition between blue and green. We didn't find its RGB models in Israeli stores.
- **LG** also showed a Micro RGB evo TV at CES, alongside its OLEDs.

Before you get excited, two caveats. First, an RGB TV still works in zones, not pixel by pixel like OLED, so you can still get blooming around bright objects on a dark background, and The Verge and WIRED both point out the risk of colours bleeding between neighbouring zones. Second, the names are confusing on purpose: Micro RGB is not Micro LED. Real Micro LED, where each pixel is its own LED, still starts at around $100,000.

## 2. OLED got a lot brighter

OLED's weak spot was always brightness in a sunny room, which in an Israeli living room is most of the day. This year it closed a good part of the gap.

The **LG OLED evo G6** uses what LG calls Brightness Booster Ultra and claims up to 3.9 times the brightness of a regular OLED, with an anti-reflection screen and Perfect Black and Perfect Color verification from UL. It comes in 55 to 97 inches, from $2,499 (about ₪8,312) in the US. In Israel the 65-inch starts at ₪7,442 (about $2,238) on Zap.

The **C6**, the model most people actually buy, got the same processor as the flagships, 4K at 165Hz and four HDMI 2.1 ports. We [reviewed the 55-inch](/techsite/en/posts/lg-oled-c6-55-review-israel/), which sells here for about ₪4,489 (about $1,350). Samsung's **S95H** QD-OLED also raised its brightness and kept its Glare Free screen.

## 3. Mini LED became the default

Two years ago Mini LED was a premium feature. In 2026 it's in the cheapest TVs on the shelf. [Xiaomi TV X Mini LED](/techsite/en/posts/xiaomi-tv-x-mini-led-review/) sells in Israel from ₪1,549 (about $466) for 43 inches up to ₪3,490 (about $1,049) for 75. The [TCL Q7D PRO](/techsite/en/posts/tcl-q7d-pro-mini-led-review-israel/) starts at ₪3,990 (about $1,200) for 55 inches, and it's brighter than TCL's own RGB model, at up to 2,200 nits.

At the top end, TCL's X11L uses what it calls SQD Mini LED with up to 20,000 dimming zones and 10,000 nits of peak brightness, according to TCL's CES figures. Those are numbers that, two years ago, belonged in a lab.

## 4. Dolby Vision 2

On August 6 Dolby and Hisense announced that Dolby Vision 2 had started rolling out, first to Hisense's 2026 UX, UR9, UR8 and U7 lines through a software update. Three features matter in day-to-day use:

- **An intensity slider.** You can turn the Dolby Vision effect up or down, for example to make up for a reflective room.
- **Content Intelligence.** The TV adjusts the picture to what you're watching, from dark films to sport, using information embedded in the title.
- **Authentic Motion** (in the premium Dolby Vision 2 Max tier). It's meant to remove judder without the "soap opera" look, but only on titles that are authored for it.

TCL and Philips have also committed to Dolby Vision 2. Samsung, as before, doesn't support Dolby Vision at all and sticks to HDR10+. If most of what you watch is Netflix or Disney+ in Dolby Vision, that's worth checking before you buy.

## 5. Bigger screens, and screens that move

98 inches stopped being a curiosity. Sony's BRAVIA 9 II goes up to 115 inches, Samsung's Micro RGB range goes to 115, TCL's flagship RM9L goes to 115, and Hisense showed a 116-inch RGB model and a 163-inch Micro LED. At the other end, LG's [StanbyME 2 Max](/techsite/en/posts/lg-stanbyme-2-max-israel/) is a 32-inch 4K screen on wheels with a battery, which is a different answer to the question "where does the TV go".

## What this means if you're buying now

- **Bright living room, mostly daytime viewing:** a good Mini LED or one of the new RGB models. You get brightness OLED still can't match on full-screen content.
- **Films in the evening, in a dark room:** OLED is still the safest choice. The C6 gives you most of what matters, and the G6 is for people who want the brightest OLED there is.
- **Tight budget:** Mini LED from Xiaomi or TCL gives you a big step over a basic LED TV for not much more money.
- **Check the HDR formats:** Dolby Vision for most streaming services, HDR10+ if you're buying Samsung.
- **Check who imports it:** with TCL, for example, the same RGB TV comes in through two different importers under two model names. Warranty and service are the importer's.

Prices on Zap move from week to week, and the gap between the cheapest and most expensive store for the same model is often more than ₪1,000. Compare before you pay, and make sure the listing says official import.
