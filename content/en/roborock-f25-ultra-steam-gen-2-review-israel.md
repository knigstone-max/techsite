Over the past year almost every wet-dry vacuum brand added steam to its flagship. We've reviewed the [Dreame H16 Pro Steam](/techsite/en/posts/dreame-h16-pro-steam-review-israel/), the [Tineco S9 Artist Steam Pro](/techsite/en/posts/tineco-s9-artist-steam-pro-review-israel/) and the [Xiaomi Wet Dry Vacuum 5 Pro](/techsite/en/posts/xiaomi-wet-dry-vacuum-5-pro-review-israel/). They share one weakness: the steam runs out after about 20 minutes, which isn't enough to steam every floor in a typical four-room Israeli flat.

The **Roborock F25 Ultra Steam Gen 2** is the first to promise 30 minutes. [We covered its Israeli launch](/techsite/en/posts/roborock-f25-ultra-steam-israel/) in early November at ₪3,290. The price has dropped since, so it's a good moment for a proper look. I read Roborock's global product page, including the footnotes at the bottom where the interesting details hide, the full spec sheet, prices here and abroad, and what's already known from European testing. Then I set it against the three steam models we've reviewed.

## What it costs in Israel

At launch on October 6 it was priced at **₪3,290 (about $999)**. Today:

- JOY lists it at **₪2,831 (about $860)** with an official importer warranty.
- Kravitz sells it for ₪2,890 (about $878).
- Some shops still ask ₪3,190 to ₪3,290 (about $969 to $999).

For comparison, the German list price is €799 (about ₪2,884), and Roborock's German store has it at €649 (about ₪2,342). After the drop, the gap with Europe isn't the usual chasm you see with robot vacuums. Before you buy, make sure the listing says "official importer" and comes with a warranty card. On a machine with a steam boiler, a pump and two wheel motors, that matters.

## In the box

The vacuum, a charging and drying dock, **two rollers** (one fitted, one spare), a spare filter, a cleaning brush, a bottle of floor cleaner and a power cord. A spare roller is a small thing rivals don't always include, and it's welcome. A roller that gets dried in the dock after heavy kitchen duty works hard.

## Steam: 30 minutes, and two numbers to read carefully

This is where it stands apart. Steam mode runs for **up to 30 minutes** per charge, against 20 on the Dreame and the Xiaomi. Pull the trigger and it switches to **Turbo Steam**, which Roborock rates at **5,000 mg of steam per minute**, 2.5 times the previous F25 Ultra.

Now the two figures that need context:

- **180°C.** Roborock's own footnote says this is measured at the internal heater output, not at the steam outlet. Same story as Dreame (200°C at the heater) and Tineco (160°C at the boiler). What actually reaches the floor on all of them is steam at around 100°C. The big number doesn't tell you which one cleans better.
- **30 seconds to heat up.** Another footnote: that's a "warm start", measured after the machine had already been in steam mode, stopped for three minutes and restarted. The first run of the morning will take longer. The boiler is rated at up to 285 W.

There's also a **WaveFlow** mode that runs 90°C water across the whole roller for kitchen grease. In Israeli homes I'd argue it's as useful as the steam. Most of the stubborn dirt here is grease by the hob and under the dining table, not mud.

German magazine connect found that the steam lifted dried ketchup, mustard and toothpaste, and that Turbo handled cooking oil well. That lines up with Roborock's claims. Roborock also cites TÜV SÜD testing showing no damage to solid wood flooring after a simulated five years of steam use. Still, if you have delicate parquet or cheap laminate, start in normal mode and test a corner.

## Suction, roller and hair

**25,000Pa** of suction, a roller spinning at **450 rpm** and pressing down with **33 newtons**. Those are numbers from Roborock's top tier, close to the cheaper [F25 Pro Turbo](/techsite/en/posts/roborock-f25-pro-turbo-review-israel/). Against Dreame's 28,000Pa and Xiaomi's 26,000Pa it trails slightly on paper, but the difference is too small to notice on ceramic tiles.

For hair there are **JawScrapers**: shark-tooth blades that steer hair away from the roller, plus a constant-pressure scraper that squeezes water out of it. connect reported no tangling during testing, with one caveat: pick up big clumps by hand first. In a home with a shedding dog or cat, that's worth a lot.

A **DirTect** sensor reads how dirty the floor is and adjusts suction and water flow on its own, and a blue light at the front shows up dust and hair.

## The edge arm: my favourite part

The classic wet-dry vacuum complaint is the dirty strip along the wall and skirting. The F25 Ultra Steam Gen 2 has an arm called **SensEdge** that lifts as you push forward, so it doesn't shove water and debris ahead of it, and drops back in 0.18 seconds when you stop. Roborock says it reaches right up to the wall on three sides of the head.

connect said it ran very close to walls without leaving streaks, and only struggled with very deep, narrow gaps such as the joint where parquet meets the skirting. That sounds fair. No vacuum is going to clean that crack.

## Pushing 6 kg

The vacuum weighs **6.0 kg**, or 7.6 kg with the dock. That's a little lighter than the Dreame (6.8 kg) and about the same as the Xiaomi and Tineco (6.1 kg). What helps is **SlideTech 2.0**: two separate motors driving the wheels, and a clean-water tank mounted low to bring the centre of gravity down. The head swivels up to 70 degrees.

It lies flat to **180 degrees** to reach under furniture, and the body is **12.5 cm** tall. Get a ruler out: in connect's test it couldn't fit under a 10 cm TV unit. If your sofa or bed sits lower than about 13 cm, it won't go under.

On stairs, 6 kg is still 6 kg. If you live in a duplex and carry it up a floor every day, you'll feel it.

## Battery: 100 minutes, but not in every mode

Roborock quotes **up to 100 minutes** and up to 780 m² per charge. The footnote makes clear the 100 minutes are in Auto mode with ECO suction. In steam mode it's up to 30 minutes. The battery is 6,300mAh and a full charge takes around four hours.

In practice: for a 100 to 120 m² home, a full pass in Auto mode shouldn't be a problem. If you want steam on every floor, 30 minutes will cover most flats, and that's the main advantage over the competition.

Tanks: **1 litre** of clean water, **720 ml** of dirty water, and a separate 100 ml compartment for detergent that the vacuum doses itself. Handy. No measuring capfuls every time.

## Dock, drying and smell

Back in the dock, it washes the roller with 95°C water and steam, then dries it with hot air. Two options: **5-minute fast drying**, which connect found fairly loud, and **30-minute quiet drying** at 45 dB(A). Roborock measured 0.88 g of water left in the roller after fast drying and 0.55 g after quiet drying. Either way, it should prevent the wet-rag smell.

If you've moved to a time-of-use electricity plan, schedule the self-clean in the app for off-peak hours. [We covered the winter peak hours here](/techsite/en/posts/winter-tou-electricity-peak-hours-appliances/).

The dirty tank has a module that separates solids from water to make emptying less grim. That's where connect found its only real complaint: the mesh is too coarse, so oat flakes and similar bits pass into the water instead of being caught. Not a deal-breaker, but you'll still need to rinse the tank properly after every use. [Our care guide](/techsite/en/posts/wet-dry-vacuum-care-guide-smell/) covers what to do and when.

## The app

You can ignore your phone entirely: three buttons on the handle (power, mode, self-clean) and a colour screen showing the four modes: Auto, Sponge (for big spills, no clean water), Hot Water and Steam. In the Roborock app you can tune suction and water, set the self-clean intensity and schedule it, and see a cleaning history.

## How it stacks up

![Comparison table: Roborock F25 Ultra Steam Gen 2 vs Dreame H16 Pro Steam and Xiaomi Wet Dry Vacuum 5 Pro, covering price, suction, steam time, runtime, weight and drying](img/posts/roborock-f25-ultra-steam-gen-2-vs-rivals-en.webp "Three steam wet-dry vacuums sold in Israel. Graphic: TechSite")

- **Against the Dreame H16 Pro Steam** (about ₪2,533, roughly $769, from the importer): the Dreame has more suction and two arms, and costs about ₪300 less. The Roborock is 800 g lighter, steams for 10 minutes longer and runs up to 100 minutes against 72.
- **Against the Xiaomi Wet Dry Vacuum 5 Pro** (₪2,490, about $756, from Hamilton): ₪340 cheaper, but it runs for only 40 minutes and steams for 20. No edge arm.
- **Against the Roborock F25 Pro Turbo** (about ₪1,529 to ₪1,690 today, roughly $464 to $513): if steam isn't essential, the cheaper sibling gives you the same suction, weighs just 4.7 kg and costs roughly ₪1,100 to ₪1,300 less.

## Who it's for

**Yes:** four- and five-room flats with ceramic or porcelain tiles, a kitchen that produces grease, kids or pets who leave dried-on stains. Anyone who wants to steam the whole home on one charge.

**Less so:** homes with lots of stairs, sofas and beds lower than 12.5 cm, and anyone happy with hot water and no steam. For that last group, the F25 Pro Turbo makes more sense.

## The bottom line

8.5/10

The Roborock F25 Ultra Steam Gen 2 is the most complete steam wet-dry vacuum on sale in Israel right now. Thirty minutes of steam, an arm that cleans tight to the wall, powered wheels, a spare roller in the box and a very long battery. The downsides: 6 kg that you feel on stairs, a 12.5 cm body that won't fit under every piece of furniture, a dirty-tank mesh that's too coarse, and marketing figures (180°C, 30 seconds) that need their footnotes. At ₪2,831 on official import it's worth the premium over Xiaomi and Dreame. At the ₪3,290 launch price it was a harder sell.
