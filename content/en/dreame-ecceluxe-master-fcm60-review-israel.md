In Israel, Dreame is mostly known for robot vacuums. Early this month it moved into the kitchen with two bean-to-cup machines that grind and brew at the touch of a button. We covered the launch already. This time I'm looking at the bigger one, the Ecceluxe Master FCM60, and whether it's worth the money.

Dreame's Israeli site currently has it at ₪2,110 (about $668) on sale, down from ₪2,490 (about $788). It comes in a cream white.

I haven't been drinking from it for a month. I went through the official specs, prices here and in Europe, and what published lab testing in Europe has found so far. The short version: good espresso, a cold brew mode that really is different from what rivals offer, and one problem worth knowing about upfront, which is the milk.

![Dreame Ecceluxe Master annotated: a 450 g bean hopper, an icon touch panel, an adjustable spout and a 500 ml milk carafe](img/posts/dreame-master-annotated-en.webp "What's on the machine. Graphic: TechSite")

## Design and size

The Master is a full-size machine: 40 cm tall, 27.5 cm wide, 41 cm deep and 9.8 kg. The body is almost entirely plastic, with a metal drip grate. It doesn't look cheap, but it doesn't feel like a ₪4,000 machine either.

One small detail matters a lot in Israeli kitchens, where wall cabinets often hang low over the counter. The bean hopper comes in two parts: a 250 g base and a 200 g extension. Take the extension off and the machine drops to 34 cm. You lose some capacity, but it fits under the cabinet.

The 2-litre water tank sits at the back. You can fill it in place, and a guide rail lets you slot it back in from the front. The drip tray, grounds bin and brew unit live behind a front door.

## Using it

There's no colour screen with menus here. There's a touch panel of backlit icons, and you tap what you want. Espresso, lungo and cold brew are always available. Attach the milk carafe and flat white, cappuccino and latte macchiato light up, plus a button for hot milk or hot water. Each drink has three strength levels, and you set the volume by holding the button until you have enough. The machine remembers it for next time.

It's simple, and anyone at home will get it without a manual. If you like to tweak everything, you'll miss one thing: you can't change the brewing temperature.

It's ready to go in under half a minute from switch-on, including a short rinse. A 120 ml coffee takes about 73 seconds, and a 180 ml cappuccino about 104 seconds.

## The grinder and the espresso

Inside is a steel conical burr grinder with 9 settings, 19 bar of pressure and a pre-infusion step you can switch off. It uses 8 to 12 g of coffee per cup. Dreame advises changing the grind only while the grinder is running, and pulling 3 or 4 espressos before judging the taste.

Published testing agrees on what matters most: the espresso is balanced, neither bitter nor sour, with good crema, even on default settings. The lungo comes out just as rounded.

There's one catch. The first cup of the morning measured about 62°C in testing, which is lukewarm for espresso. The second was hotter, since the machine had warmed up. Dreame's own advice, if the coffee isn't hot enough, is to run hot water three times before brewing. It works, but it's not elegant.

The grinder is also loud: about 71 dB while grinding. Brewing itself is very quiet, around 45 dB. If you're the first one up at home, you'll notice.

## Cold brew: what it actually is

This is the feature that sets the Master apart from most machines at this price, so it's worth being clear about what it does. It isn't classic cold brew, steeped in cold water for 12 hours. Dreame says the machine brews espresso at a lower temperature than usual to get a cold-brew-like flavour.

In practice, testing shows it works better than it sounds. You're not pouring hot coffee over ice and watering it down. The coffee is brewed cool from the start. It tastes rounded, with no bitterness or sourness. But it doesn't come out cold. Testing measured about 42°C, so lukewarm, and you need ice for a properly cold drink. For an Israeli summer, with a splash of milk and ice, it's probably the best reason to pick this machine over others.

## Milk: the weak spot

The milk carafe holds 500 ml, enough for several drinks in a row. It clips on at the front behind a small flap, and a dial on it sets how fine the foam is. Afterwards you can unclip it and put it in the fridge, and Dreame says it's dishwasher safe.

The cappuccino tastes good, the milk comes out sweet, and the temperature is even, about 65°C in both foam and coffee. The problem is texture. In testing the foam was uneven: partly fine, partly coarse, and piled higher on one side of the cup than the other. The milk system is also sensitive to cleaning. By the third or fourth cappuccino of the day, if you don't rinse in between, the foam changes noticeably.

The fix is simple, you just have to remember it: press the clean button after every milk drink, and always before the carafe goes back in the fridge. Dreame recommends cleaning the milk parts within 30 minutes of use.

## Cleaning and upkeep

Some good, some less good. The good: the brew unit comes out by hand in seconds and only needs a rinse under the tap, no soap. The machine rinses itself at switch-on and switch-off, there's a 20-second quick clean for the spouts and a one-minute deep clean, and it reminds you to empty the grounds bin every 10 cups. The pucks come out dry.

The less good: very few parts are dishwasher safe, and there's a fixed area above the coffee spout that gets grubby over time and can only be wiped with a cloth. Descaling is due roughly every 20 litres of water and takes about an hour. With the hard water in most of Israel, use filtered water as Dreame itself recommends, and keep up with descaling.

## The price, and the price abroad

₪2,110 on sale is a fair price for an automatic machine with a milk system. But in Germany it has lately been selling for around €425 (about ₪1,507, about $477), well under its official €649 there (about ₪2,302). So even on sale, you pay roughly ₪600 more here. Some of that is VAT and import costs, some of it is the local warranty.

Which brings me to the question I'd ask before buying: who provides warranty and service here, and for how long. On a coffee machine, with a pump, a grinder and milk lines, that matters as much as the price. Dreame is new to this category and doesn't yet have a service track record for coffee machines in Israel.

## What it's up against

Its little sister, the Ecceluxe Slim FCM30, is ₪1,686 (about $533) on sale. It's very narrow, 18 cm, but has a 240 ml milk container and no cold brew. If you mostly drink espresso in a small kitchen, it's enough. If you drink several cappuccinos a day or want cold brew, the Master makes more sense.

Against long-established brands like De'Longhi and Philips, which sell automatic machines in Israel in the same price range, Dreame's edge is cold brew and the hopper that shortens to fit under cabinets. Theirs is years of service and spare parts.

:::score
## The bottom line

7/10

The Dreame Ecceluxe Master makes balanced espresso with no effort, offers a cold brew mode that really is different from what other machines at this price do, and is simple enough for everyone at home. It loses points for uneven milk foam, a milk system that needs constant rinsing, a lukewarm first cup and a loud grinder. And for being much cheaper abroad. If you mostly drink black coffee and like it iced in summer, it's a good pick. If you live on cappuccinos, shop around.
:::

**What I liked**

- Balanced espresso and lungo, no bitterness
- Cold brew made at low temperature, not watered down
- A hopper that shortens to 34 cm to fit under cabinets
- Simple icon-based controls
- A brew unit that comes out and rinses in seconds

**What I liked less**

- Uneven milk foam
- A fussy milk system that needs rinsing after every drink
- A lukewarm first cup, and no temperature control
- A loud grinder
- About ₪600 more than in Germany
