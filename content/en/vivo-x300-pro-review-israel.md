Vivo is trying Israel again, and this time the pitch is clear: camera first. The X300 Pro arrives through importer Bandai at about ₪4,449 in their store (roughly $1,405). On Wisebuy, the 512GB / 16GB RAM listing ranges from ₪4,290 to ₪4,489. That's flagship money, not "almost flagship," and the question is whether the package around those lenses is complete enough to justify it against Galaxy and iPhone.

I didn't live with the phone for weeks as a lab reviewer. I went through the official specs, what Bandai sells locally, and what you give up versus brands already dug into Israel. Short version: if zoom and close-up photography are the main reason you buy a phone, this is one of the strongest options around. If you want a familiar ecosystem and service in every mall, think twice.

![Vivo X300 Pro specs in Israel: 6.78-inch display, Dimensity 9500, 16+512GB, Zeiss cameras and about ₪4,449](img/posts/vivo-x300-pro-specs-en.webp "The numbers in one place. Graphic: TechSite")

## Design that screams camera

The back is dominated by a large round camera island with a knurled metal ring that looks more like a lens barrel than a phone. It's the same visual language we've seen on Huawei, Xiaomi and OPPO camera flagships. The phone weighs about 226 g on the spec sheet, with a matte glass back and rounded corners. It feels big and planted, not feather-light like some of this year's slim flagships.

There's a physical shortcut button on the side, in the spirit of Apple's Action Button, with two assignable actions: long press and double press. Durability is a real plus: IP68 and IP69, so hot high-pressure jets as well as immersion. On the other hand, without a strong brand cue on the front or a distinctive island shape, it looks generic if you want "wow" out of the box.

## A screen that survives Israeli sun

The display is a 6.78-inch LTPO AMOLED, 1260×2800, 120Hz, with a claimed peak of up to 4,500 nits. That's enough to read outdoors at noon. The under-display ultrasonic fingerprint sensor is faster and more consistent than most cheap optical ones.

Default colour tuning is the weaker spot. It pushes reds and oranges. You can fix that in settings, but side-by-side with Samsung or OPPO Ultra panels some people will find the colours a bit less "clean." Vivo saved the more aggressive calibration for the Ultra model in the series, which we still haven't seen locally.

## Cameras: the reason to buy

This is the whole story. The Zeiss partnership includes T* lens coating that cuts reflections and flare before software gets involved. The array: 50MP main, 50MP ultrawide, and a 200MP periscope telephoto with about 3.7× optical zoom. There's also an optional external telephoto kit that reaches an effective focal length of about 200 mm, sold separately for around ₪800 according to market reports.

![Close-up of the Vivo X300 Pro's round camera island with the Zeiss T* logo](img/posts/vivo-x300-pro-camera-module.webp "The camera island, the phone's reason to exist. Image: vivo")

Day-to-day, the main sensor turns out sharp frames with natural depth. The zoom lens is where it pulls ahead: short-range macro through the telephoto, and zoom that stays crisp around 10×. Beyond that, processing starts guessing more. Night and ultrawide are solid, with the classic Chinese tendency toward slightly oversaturated processing.

If you don't shoot much, you're paying for hardware you won't use. If you do, it's one of the best reasons to pick Vivo over a more familiar badge.

## Performance, battery and software

Under the hood sits a MediaTek Dimensity 9500, with 16GB RAM and 512GB storage on the model sold here. That's enough for heavy games and multitasking without feeling choked. OriginOS on Android 16 is cleaner than the old Funtouch skin, with less visual noise than OPPO's interface. Vivo promises five years of OS updates and seven years of security patches.

The battery is 6,510 mAh on the global unit Bandai brings in. Important: the European version is about 1,000 mAh smaller, so if you're comparing overseas prices, check which SKU. Wired charging up to 90W, wireless up to 40W. In practice that's a full charge around 50 minutes, with about 50% after roughly 20 minutes on a cable.

What's missing: no 3.5 mm headphone jack, and some built-in AI tools are still weak in Hebrew. Google's apps fill most of that gap.

## Price and service in Israel

About ₪4,449 at Bandai puts it against Galaxy S26 Pro and iPhone 17/18 Pro Max more than against mid-flagships. Wisebuy shows other shops dipping to ₪4,290. Having an official importer is better than the years when Vivo mostly arrived through small parallel channels. Service reach and shop presence are still thinner than Samsung, Apple or even Xiaomi.

:::score
## Bottom line

8/10

The Vivo X300 Pro is a camera monster at Israeli flagship money: 200MP Zeiss telephoto, bright screen, big battery and a fast chip. It loses points for a brand that still isn't rooted here, generic looks and software that needs a learning curve. For photographers and anyone willing to learn OriginOS, it's a strong buy. For people who want Samsung or Apple quiet ownership, stay with what you know.
:::

**What I liked**

- Zeiss array with a 200MP telephoto that justifies the price for shooters
- 6,510 mAh battery and 90W fast charging
- IP68 and IP69 durability
- Bright screen that holds up in the sun
- Five years of OS updates and seven years of security patches
- Official importer (Bandai) instead of parallel-only

**What I liked less**

- Flagship price against a brand still thin on service and shelf space
- Generic design that doesn't stick in memory
- Default colour calibration runs warm
- Some AI tools are weak in Hebrew
- External telephoto kit is pricey and separate
