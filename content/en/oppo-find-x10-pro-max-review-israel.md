When the Find X10 Pro Max reached Israel at the end of October, it came with a price that puts it in the same bracket as the most expensive iPhones: ₪6,499 (about $2,049) at Ronlight, OPPO's official importer, in a single 12GB/512GB configuration. That's a lot of money for a brand that most Israelis still don't think of first when they buy a flagship.

So the question in this review is simple. Is it worth that much, compared with an iPhone 18 Pro Max, a Galaxy S26 Ultra or a Pixel 11 Pro XL? I haven't carried it for a month. I went through the official specs, the Israeli and European prices, and the published lab and field testing so far. The short version: the cameras are the best you'll find on an Android phone right now, the battery is excellent, and the price and a few software choices hold it back.

## Price in Israel and abroad

Ronlight sells it in Titanium Grey or Lunar White for ₪6,499, and it's also available through authorised retailers such as KSP. At launch the first 150 buyers got Enco Buds 3 Pro earbuds, a 50W magnetic wireless charger and a case, plus a cheap upgrade on the Hasselblad Teleconverter. That bundle was limited, so check what's on offer now.

In Europe it costs €1,799, about ₪6,356 ($2,004). So the Israeli price is close to Europe's, and there's one detail that works in our favour: the model sold here has an 8,000mAh battery, while the European version gets a 7,150mAh one. If you're buying abroad or through a grey importer, look at the box.

For comparison, iStore lists the iPhone 18 Pro Max from ₪5,899 for 256GB, and Electra sells the Pixel 11 Pro XL from ₪4,899 for 256GB. The Galaxy S26 Ultra with 512GB was down to ₪3,999 at Samsung's importer last month. Even with twice the base storage of some of these, the OPPO is the most expensive of the group.

## Design and feel

This isn't a light phone. It's 8.4 mm thick and weighs 233 g, which makes it heavier than most Android flagships but still lighter than the 249 g iPhone 18 Pro Max. The frame is matte aluminium, the front is Gorilla Glass Victus 2, and the back is a single glass panel. On the white model the glass sits over a laser-etched pattern that catches the light nicely. The grey one is plain matte.

The camera block is large and sits in the top corner, with a second smaller pill below it for the flash and a colour sensor. Put it on a table and it rocks. Most people will put a case on it anyway.

It's rated IP66, IP68 and IP69. In plain terms: dust, a dunk in water and a high-pressure jet are all covered. There's also a Snap Key on the frame that you can set to switch between silent and vibrate, turn on the torch or open AI Mind Space, which collects notes and screenshots.

## The display

The 6.78-inch AMOLED runs at 2772x1272, which works out to about 450 pixels per inch. That's sharp enough that you won't see pixels. The refresh rate changes from 1Hz to 120Hz, and up to 144Hz in supported games. The bezels are very thin, about 1.13 mm.

It isn't the brightest screen around. In published lab testing it reached about 750 nits with manual brightness and close to 1,000 nits in automatic mode, with HDR peaks of about 1,500 nits. Outdoors in an Israeli summer it's readable, but the iPhone and Galaxy go brighter. Colour accuracy is excellent in the Natural profile. If flicker bothers you, the 3840Hz PWM dimming should help in a dark room.

## Performance and heat

Inside is MediaTek's Dimensity 9600 Pro, made on a 2nm process and clocked at up to 4.55GHz, with 12GB of RAM and 512GB of UFS 4.1 storage. Day to day it's fast, with no stutter. In benchmarks it sits a step behind the best. Published Geekbench results put the iPhone 18 Pro Max about 28% ahead in single-core and 19% ahead in multi-core. In graphics the picture is better, with results on par with or ahead of the Galaxy S26 Ultra in some tests.

By default the phone runs in a balanced mode. There's a performance mode, but it adds little and drains more power. One detail matters here specifically: published testing found the phone holds its internal temperature to a hard limit of 40°C. That's sensible for comfort, but on a long gaming session on a hot August day on the balcony, expect it to throttle earlier than you'd like.

## Battery and charging

This is where the Israeli version really shines. With 8,000mAh and an efficient chip, two days of normal use is realistic, and published testing with the same battery size often ran into a third day. In standard screen-on tests it delivered more than double the time of the Galaxy S26 Ultra in some tasks. Even the European 7,150mAh model lasted close to 38 hours in a looping video test. OPPO says the battery keeps 80% of its capacity after five years of typical charging.

The weak spot is charging. The box says 80W, but in practice power hovers around 30W for much of the cycle, and a full charge takes about 80 minutes. That's slow for a phone at this level. The upside is that it reaches that speed with standard USB PD PPS and UFCS chargers, so you don't need OPPO's charger. Around 25 minutes on the cable is enough for a busy day. Wireless charging goes up to 50W with a compatible charger.

## The cameras

This is why you buy this phone. There are three 200MP cameras on the back:

- **Main:** 200MP, a large 1/1.3-inch sensor, f/1.5 aperture and optical stabilisation.
- **Telephoto:** 200MP, 3x optical zoom (70 mm equivalent), f/2.1, stabilised, and it doubles as a macro camera from 10 cm.
- **Ultra-wide:** 200MP, 1/1.56-inch sensor, f/2.2, 124°.

On the front there's a 50MP selfie camera with autofocus. Next to the flash is a True Color sensor that measures colour in the scene to help get skin tones and white balance right.

By default the phone saves 26MP photos, and you can switch to 50MP or 200MP when you want every detail. What stands out in every published sample set I went through is consistency. Usually a phone's ultra-wide and zoom cameras are noticeably weaker than the main one. Here all three look nearly the same in colour, detail and low-light performance. The ultra-wide shows a little colour fringing and is slightly softer, but the gap is small.

![The back of the white Find X10 Pro Max with labels for the three 200MP cameras, the LED flash and the True Color sensor](img/posts/oppo-x10-pro-max-camera-annotated-en.webp "What's on the back. Image: OPPO. Graphic: TechSite")

The telephoto is the highlight. Because the sensor is so large, you can crop to 6x and 13x and keep near-lossless detail, which makes it great for portraits and landscapes. Up to about 30x, photos are good in daylight. Beyond that the phone switches to AI upscaling, and the results look artificial: smoothed textures, sharpened edges and detail that doesn't quite feel real. If you shoot far away a lot, the Hasselblad Teleconverter clips over the telephoto and gives 10x optical zoom. In Israel it costs ₪1,499 (about $473) on its own.

Two more things are worth knowing. There's noticeable lens flare on the main and ultra-wide cameras when you shoot towards the sun, which is common in Israeli light. And the 18 Hasselblad colour palettes are well done: you pick a look before or after the shot and can change it later without losing anything.

For video, all three rear cameras shoot 8K at 30fps and 4K at 120fps, with Dolby Vision, O-Log2 for colour grading and Open Gate for using the full sensor area. The phone also works well with the Blackmagic camera app. For anyone who films seriously, this is one of the strongest setups on any phone.

## Software

It runs ColorOS 17 on Android 17, with a new semi-transparent design that looks a lot like Apple's Liquid Glass. Some people will like it, some won't. You can turn some of the blur effects off, but not everywhere. There's more pre-installed bloatware than there should be at this price.

The AI tools include text-based photo editing in the gallery and an agent called AI Mind Pilot that can sort files and help with plans and documents. Gemini is available as usual on Android. None of these is a reason to buy the phone, but nothing important is missing either.

On updates, published reports differ, with some citing five years of Android updates and others seven. Before you pay ₪6,499 for a phone you plan to keep for years, ask Ronlight exactly what it commits to.

## The bottom line

8/10

The OPPO Find X10 Pro Max has the most consistent camera system on any phone right now, a battery that easily runs for two days in the Israeli version, and excellent video features. It loses points for slow charging for its class, a screen that isn't the brightest, a hard heat limit that may bite in an Israeli summer, software that copies iOS a bit too eagerly and, above all, its price. If you photograph a lot and want the best camera on Android, it's the phone to buy. If not, the regular flagships do almost everything for less.
:::

**What I liked**

- Three 200MP cameras that look almost identical in quality
- An excellent 3x telephoto with sharp crops up to 13x
- 8,000mAh in the Israeli model, two days of real use
- Professional video: 8K, 4K 120, O-Log2, Open Gate
- IP66/68/69 and a solid build

**What I liked less**

- ₪6,499, more than an iPhone 18 Pro Max
- Charging that's slow for 80W, about 80 minutes to full
- Not the brightest screen
- AI upscaling beyond 30x looks artificial
- Bloatware, and an unclear update commitment
