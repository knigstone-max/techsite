Wet-dry vacuums have one problem nobody puts on the box: the smell. Dirty water travels up a pipe into a tank, some of it stays in the pipes and the filter, and after a few weeks the machine starts to smell like a mop that was left in a bucket. Every brand fights it with self-cleaning cycles and hot-air docks. Dyson took a different path. In the **Clean+Wash Hygiene** (model WR03) there's no suction at all, and no exhaust filter.

Dyson first showed it in Berlin in September 2025, and it's now sold in Israel on Dyson's Israeli site and at electrical retailers. Over the past month we reviewed four wet-dry vacuums that all suck up dirty water: the [Tineco S9 Artist Steam Pro](/techsite/en/posts/tineco-s9-artist-steam-pro-review-israel/), the [Dreame H16 Pro Steam](/techsite/en/posts/dreame-h16-pro-steam-review-israel/), the [Xiaomi Wet Dry Vacuum 5 Pro](/techsite/en/posts/xiaomi-wet-dry-vacuum-5-pro-review-israel/) and the [Roborock F25 Pro Turbo](/techsite/en/posts/roborock-f25-pro-turbo-review-israel/). The Dyson is built on a different idea, so I went through its specs, Dyson's footnotes and what owners say, and lined it up against them.

## What it costs

- **Dyson Israel**: ₪1,979 (about $595), down from ₪2,199 (about $661)
- **Big Electric**: ₪2,199, with a two-year importer warranty and the option of two more years for ₪199 (about $60)
- **US**: $399.99 (about ₪1,330) on Dyson's site, down from $499.99 (about ₪1,663), before sales tax
- **UK**: £259.99 (about ₪1,134, or $341)

The gap with the US and UK is large, as it usually is with Dyson. Still, at ₪1,979 it's cheaper than most of the steam models we reviewed, and more expensive than the Roborock F25 Pro Turbo, which has dropped to ₪1,529 (about $460).

## How it cleans without suction

This is the part to understand before buying. A regular wet-dry vacuum sprays water, scrubs with a roller and sucks the dirty water up through a pipe into a tank in the body. The Dyson doesn't suck. The motor spins a microfibre roller with 84,000 filaments, and with every turn, dirty water and debris are scraped off the roller and it's wetted again with clean water. Dyson says the liquid is drawn off by capillary action, and the dry dirt is caught by filaments that lock together into tiny hook shapes.

Where does the dirt go? It stays down in the cleaner head. There's a 0.52-litre dirty water tank and a separate tray for dry debris, both in the head itself. Nothing goes up the machine, so there are no internal pipes to clog and no filter to go mouldy. You pull both out together in one move, and Dyson says emptying takes 20 seconds.

The clear benefit: Dyson claims there's no drop in pick-up over time, even after the equivalent of five years of use, because there's no suction to lose. The question, which Dyson's marketing doesn't really answer, is how it copes with bigger, heavier dirt. On crumbs, hair and liquid spills Dyson shows it working. On a pile of sand from the beach or rice that spilled on the floor, I'd want to see it first.

## The roller and hair

On the roller there are nylon bristles among the microfibre, and behind it there's an anti-tangle comb that Dyson says handles hair up to 35 cm long without wrapping. That's a real problem with long hair or a dog that sheds, and competitors attack it too, like Dreame with its TangleCut roller.

Dyson also promises cleaning along the edges on three sides of the head, and leaving less than one gram of water per metre of floor, so the floor dries within about 60 seconds. On Israeli porcelain tiles, that's exactly what you want: no streaks and no footprints from the kids walking across the wet floor.

## Self-cleaning and drying

This is the biggest improvement over the previous generation. The [WashG1](/techsite/en/posts/wet-dry-vacuum-buying-guide-israel/), which we didn't recommend in our buying guide, rinsed its rollers in the dock but didn't dry them, and that's exactly where smell and mould start. The Clean+Wash Hygiene comes with a dock that runs a self-clean cycle through the whole system and then dries the roller with 85°C air. Dyson still recommends emptying both tanks and the debris tray after every clean, which is good advice for any machine of this type.

## Weight, size and battery

- **Weight: 3.82 kg.** Dyson says it's the lightest in-hand of its kind. Of the machines we looked at, the Roborock weighs 4.7 kg, the Tineco and Xiaomi 6.1 kg and the Dreame 6.8 kg. If you have stairs, that's a big difference.
- **Lies flat to 113 mm**, so it gets under fairly low sofas and beds.
- **Runtime: up to 70 minutes**, and Dyson says one charge covers up to 350 square metres. Dyson doesn't say which of the four power modes that figure is for, so expect less in Max.
- **Clean water tank: 0.75 litres**, on the outside of the body, where you can see how much is left. That's smaller than most suction models. In a large apartment you may need to refill once.

## Four power modes, and what goes in the water

There are four modes, from everyday cleaning up to Max for dried-on stains. Dyson asks for gentle, low-foaming household floor cleaners, diluted as directed, and no bleach. It also sells its own 02 Probiotic solution. If you're used to pouring a generous splash of floor cleaner into the bucket, you'll have to break the habit.

## What it doesn't do

- **Hard floors only.** Tiles, marble, vinyl, laminate and sealed wood. No carpets and no rugs.
- **No steam.** If you're after hot cleaning against kitchen grease, the Tineco, Xiaomi and Dreame all have it.
- **No real vacuum.** It picks up dry dirt with the roller, but it doesn't replace a vacuum for corners, sofas and the car.

## What owners say

On Dyson's Irish site, the Clean+Wash Hygiene has an average of 3.7 out of 5 from 804 ratings, and only 49% of reviewers say they'd recommend it to a friend. That's a weak number for a Dyson product. One five-star reviewer wrote that they decided to write a review precisely because of the many negative ones, and suggested that some people may not be using it properly. That's a signal to try it in a store if you can, and to check the return policy before you buy.

## Who it's for

It fits a home with tiles or laminate throughout, a lot of small daily messes, and someone who gets tired of carrying a 6 kg machine around, or who has already been put off by the smell of a wet-dry vacuum. Someone who wants steam, or one machine that does everything including heavy dirt, will be better served by a suction model.

## The bottom line

7/10

The Dyson Clean+Wash Hygiene is a smart approach to a real problem: no suction and no filter, the dirt stays in the head, and a dock dries the roller with hot air. At 3.82 kg it's the lightest of the wet-dry cleaners we've looked at, and the clear water tank and 70-minute runtime are good for an apartment. But it's hard floors only, has no steam, the clean water tank is small, and the owner ratings on Dyson's own site are mediocre. At ₪1,979 it's a reasonable buy for the right home. At ₪2,199 the Roborock gives you more for less.

**What I liked**

- 3.82 kg, much lighter than the competition
- No suction and no filter: no clogged pipes and no musty smell
- Dirty water and dry debris separated in the head, emptied in one move
- Hot-air drying dock at 85°C, something the WashG1 didn't have
- Anti-tangle comb for hair up to 35 cm

**What I liked less**

- No steam
