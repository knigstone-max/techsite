Last week we looked at the [Dreame H16 Pro Steam](/techsite/en/posts/dreame-h16-pro-steam-review-israel/), and before that the [Xiaomi Wet Dry Vacuum 5 Pro](/techsite/en/posts/xiaomi-wet-dry-vacuum-5-pro-review-israel/). Now it's **Tineco**'s turn, the brand that more or less created the cordless wet-dry vacuum. The **Floor One S9 Artist Steam Pro** is the steam model it sells in Israel today, and it's part of the same "who runs hotter" race every brand joined this year.

I went through the product page, the user manual and what Israeli stores are offering, and lined it up against its three direct rivals.

![Tineco S9 Artist Steam Pro with callouts: steam from the head, edge cleaning, runtime, weight and tank sizes](img/posts/tineco-s9-artist-steam-pro-annotated-en.webp "What's in it, according to Tineco. Graphic: TechSite")

## What it costs in Israel

This is where it gets interesting, because the spread between stores is huge:

- **Tineco Israel** (Unico, the official importer): ₪2,950 (about $887), with a **two-year warranty**
- **Zap**: offers from ₪1,889 (about $568) up to ₪3,625 (about $1,090), across 12 stores

A ₪1,000 gap on the same machine is a lot. Before you grab the cheapest listing, check two things: that it's an official import (several stores say so on the product page), and that the two-year warranty comes from the importer, not the shop. A wet-dry vacuum has a steam boiler, a pump, a battery and a roller motor, so a proper service network is worth something.

## Steam: 160° in the boiler, 99° on the floor

Tineco calls it HyperSteam. According to the company's European product page, the boiler heats steam up to **160°C** and the steam leaves the head at **no less than 99°C**. Tineco Israel's site also says 160.

A small correction on our side: in our [floor-cleaning year in review](/techsite/en/posts/floor-cleaning-2026-innovations-roundup/) we wrote 140°C, as some Israeli retailers list it. The official figure is 160.

What does it mean in practice? Just like with the Dreame, the big number is the boiler, and what hits the floor is steam at around 100°C. That's what softens dried grease by the stove and sticky spots under the kids' table. So comparing Tineco's "160" with Dreame's "200" doesn't tell you which one cleans better.

Two details from the manual worth knowing:

- **Steam takes 30 to 65 seconds** to get ready after you switch to Steam mode. Not a big deal, but not instant either.
- **No cleaning solution** in the tank when you use steam. If you're used to adding a capful to every wash, remember to empty it first.

## Battery: where it leads

Tineco quotes **up to 90 minutes in Quiet mode** and **up to 75 minutes in Auto**. The battery is 25.2V and 6,250mAh, and a full charge takes under four hours.

That's more than any of the direct rivals in the table below, apart from Roborock's ECO-mode figure. In a typical four-room Israeli apartment, that means the whole place on one charge, coffee break included. Keep in mind these are the maker's lab numbers, and steam uses more power.

## Weight and build

**6.1 kg**, the same as the Xiaomi and lighter than the Dreame's 6.8 kg. You feel those 700 grams carrying it upstairs or holding it up to get under a bed.

It lies flat to **180°**, so it fits under a sofa or a low bed. One detail from the manual: lying flat, the dirty tank holds only 0.63 litres instead of 0.8, so empty it before a long stretch under furniture.

## How it deals with dirt

- **iLoop**: a sensor that reads how dirty the floor is and adjusts suction and water. Water flow ranges from 20 to 75 ml per minute.
- **ReverseScrub**: one press spins the roller backwards to scrub a stubborn spot instead of gliding over it.
- **DualBlock**: two layers of scrapers that keep hair from wrapping around the roller. With long hair or a dog in the house, that's the difference between a clean roller and scissors once a week.
- **Three-sided edge cleaning**: the head cleans at the sides and the front, so you can get right up to the skirting.

Suction: Tineco doesn't list a figure on its European page, and Israeli retailers quote **22,000Pa**. That's below the Dreame's 28,000Pa and the Xiaomi's 26,000Pa. On a wet-dry vacuum this matters less than on a regular vacuum, since most of the work is pulling water and wet dirt off the roller, but large crumbs and hair on tiles with deep grout lines will notice it.

## The dock: cleaning and drying

When you're done, it goes back on the dock and flushes the pipes and the roller. Then comes **FlashDry**: about five minutes of 85°C hot air. There's also a quieter SilentDry mode, up to 45 dB according to Tineco, for evening cleaning when the kids are asleep.

Fast drying isn't a luxury. A roller that stays wet until tomorrow and starts to smell is the most common complaint about wet-dry vacuums, and Tineco's answer is good enough. What it lacks compared with the Dreame is a **boiling-water wash** in the dock: Tineco doesn't list a hot-water wash for this model, only heated drying.

## Which floors

Per the manual, it's for hard floors: **tile, vinyl and sealed wood**. Not carpet. In Israel, where most homes have porcelain or ceramic tiles, that fits almost everyone. If you have untreated natural wood, think twice before aiming steam at it, and that goes for every machine in this category.

## Against the competition

- **Xiaomi Wet Dry Vacuum 5 Pro** (₪2,490, about $749): same claimed steam, same weight. The Tineco has more than twice the battery life, the Xiaomi has stronger suction and a lower importer price. Big home, Tineco. Small home, the Xiaomi is enough.
- **Dreame H16 Pro Steam** (₪2,533, about $762): the Dreame gives you more: two arms, stronger suction, a three-year warranty and a boiling-water wash. It's also heavier, and its steam runs for 20 minutes at a stretch.
- **Roborock F25 Ultra Steam Gen 2** (₪3,290 list, about $989, and ₪2,890 on sale at Machsanei Hashmal): the priciest of the four, with three years of warranty and a robotic arm. Pay more, get more.

At the importer's ₪2,950, the Tineco is hard to justify against the Dreame. At ₪1,900 to ₪2,200 as an official import, it becomes a good deal, mainly because of the battery and the weight.

## The bottom line

7.5/10

The Tineco S9 Artist Steam Pro is a relatively light steam wet-dry vacuum with a very long battery, quick hot-air drying and scrapers that really do stop hair from tangling. Suction is weaker than its rivals, the dock doesn't wash with boiling water, and the official price is too high for what Dreame and Xiaomi offer. At around ₪2,000 as an official import, it's a good pick for a larger home.
:::

**What I liked**

- Up to 90 minutes on one charge
- 6.1 kg, lighter than the Dreame
- Steam for the greasy corners of the kitchen
- Dual scrapers against hair
- Five-minute hot-air drying, plus a quiet drying mode
- Lies flat to 180°

**What I liked less**

- 22,000Pa suction, below the competition
- Tineco doesn't list a hot-water wash in the dock
- Half a minute to a minute of waiting for steam, and no cleaning solution in that mode
- Two-year warranty, versus three on the Dreame and Roborock
- High importer price and a gap of over ₪1,000 between stores
