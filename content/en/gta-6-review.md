Let me get this out of the way so you don't have to scroll: GTA VI gets 11 out of 10 from us. Yes, I know that isn't supposed to be possible. But after dozens of hours in Leonida, the usual scale just looks too small. There are great games, and there are games that move the bar for everything that comes after them. GTA VI is the second kind, and I can't remember the last time I felt this way.

It came out yesterday, 19 November, on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. I played it on a PS5 and a PS5 Pro, with a DualSense in hand and far too much coffee. Before we dive in, here are the key numbers in one image.

![GTA VI at a glance: an 11 out of 10 score, release date, platforms, Israeli and US prices, frame rate, languages, story length and age rating](img/posts/gta6-review-glance-en.webp "Everything at a glance. Image: Rockstar Games. Graphic: TechSite")

## Why 11 and not 10

We keep a 10 for games with almost nothing left to fix. GTA VI does something else. It takes nearly everything open-world games have done in the last decade and does it better, and then it adds things I haven't seen in any game. The city feels genuinely populated. The leads are people, not puppets. The driving, the physics, the lighting, the music, the radio, the heists, even the grocery runs. Any one of those layers could have been a game of its own.

It has one weakness, and I'll get to it properly: the story builds slowly. Really slowly. But even that weakness, once it's behind you, turns out to be part of why the ending lands so hard. So, 11. If you're looking for a reason not to buy it, you'll have to look somewhere else.

## Leonida: a state built from scratch

GTA VI is set in Leonida, Rockstar's take on Florida. Vice City is only part of it. Leonida is a whole state, with six big regions that each look, sound and feel different. Most importantly, the entire map is open from the start. No blocked bridges, no "second island unlocks after mission ten." If you want to drive from the Keys to the northern forests in your first hour, nobody will stop you.

![Six regions of Leonida, each with a photo and a short description: Vice City, Leonida Keys, Grassrivers, Port Gellhorn, Ambrosia and Mount Kalaga](img/posts/gta6-review-regions-en.webp "Six regions, and none of them feels like a copy of another. Images: Rockstar Games. Graphic: TechSite")

You feel the size. Driving from the top of the forests to the bottom of the Keys takes about ten minutes in a fast car, on a decent traffic day. And the scenery changes every few minutes along the way: gated rich neighborhoods, a downtown full of glass towers, rough neighborhoods, swamps, fishing villages and beach towns. It doesn't feel like a map someone drew. It feels like a place.

![A yellow and white seaplane flying low over green islands, a long bridge and turquoise water in the Leonida Keys](img/posts/gta6-review-keys-seaplane.webp "The Keys from above. Water in a color I didn't think a game could make. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

What surprised me most is how many places you can walk into. Not every building, of course, but far more than I expected: clubs, gyms, flea markets, malls, restaurants, clothes shops, basketball courts, shooting ranges, aircraft hangars, gas stations. Each one has its own look. There isn't one clothes shop copy-pasted twenty times.

## Outside the city: Leonida's less glossy side

Vice City gets most of the attention, but the parts that surprised me most were outside it. Port Gellhorn is a beach town that has seen better days, with cheap motels, half-dead neon signs and people who look like they got stuck there on the way somewhere else. There's a sadness to it, and Rockstar doesn't try to pretty it up.

![A town at orange sunset from above: low houses, power poles, trees and a road leading to the horizon in Port Gellhorn](img/posts/gta6-review-port-gellhorn.webp "Port Gellhorn at sunset. The beauty of a place everyone forgot. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

Ambrosia is something else again: a sugar refinery, dusty roads, heavy trucks and biker gangs riding in packs. And Grassrivers, the swamp region, is wild Florida in full, with airboats, alligators and fishing shacks on the water. In those three regions I felt like I was discovering Leonida, not just driving through it.

![A pack of bikers in black vests riding down a road in Ambrosia, past signs and power poles](img/posts/gta6-review-ambrosia-bikers.webp "A biker pack in Ambrosia. Best to let them pass. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

## Vice City: same city, completely different league

If you remember the 2002 Vice City, you'll recognize the spirit: neon, palm trees, sports cars and Hawaiian shirts. But the 2026 city is a different beast. Curved residential towers reflected in the water, marinas full of yachts, packed beaches, highways across the bay and neighborhoods that each feel like their own world.

![Two curved residential towers and a row of skyscrapers on the Vice City waterfront, with boats and blue sky](img/posts/gta6-review-vice-skyline.webp "The Vice City skyline by day. Every window reflects something different. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

And at night it lights up. My first drive along the coast after dark, radio on, was one of those moments where I dropped the mission and just drove. The Ferris wheel glowing, the towers lit, cars leaving streaks of light on wet asphalt.

![Vice City at night from above: a lit Ferris wheel, towers, roads and city lights to the horizon](img/posts/gta6-review-vice-night.webp "Vice City at night. This is where I realized I wasn't going to bed. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

The beach works like a real beach, too. Lifeguards in their towers, sunbathers, tourists getting burned and people just strolling the sand. In most games the beach is a backdrop. Here it's somewhere you can spend half an hour just people-watching.

![A Vice City beach with a colorful lifeguard tower, a woman sunbathing on a towel and people in hats walking on the sand](img/posts/gta6-review-vice-beach.webp "The Vice City beach. Everyone here is busy with their own story. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

## Jason and Lucia: the best duo the series has had

The heart of the game is the two people at its center. Jason Duval is ex-military, quiet, steady, sparing with words. Lucia Caminos has been boosting cars since she was 14, has a sharp sense of humor and takes zero nonsense. Within a few hours I'd worked out that Lucia is the scarier of the two. When I played as her, I reached for a gun a lot faster.

![Lucia and Jason embracing against an orange sunset, with a helicopter in the sky behind them](img/posts/gta6-review-couple-sunset.webp "Jason and Lucia. Not just partners in crime, a real couple. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

What makes them special is that they aren't caricatures. They're two people trying to build a life in a world that's rigged against them. Their goal is simple: make enough money to get out of this life and settle down somewhere quiet. Naturally, it doesn't go to plan.

You feel their bond all the time. When they're together, you can hold a shoulder button and they walk hand in hand. At first I used it as a tactic: a couple holding hands outside a jewelry store looks a lot less suspicious. A few hours later it was just a habit. They're in love, so of course they hold hands.

![Jason and Lucia leaning on a red sports car in a parking lot in the afternoon sun](img/posts/gta6-review-trailer-couple.webp "A quiet moment before the chaos. From the official trailer. Rockstar Games")

There's a real relationship system too, with a little heart meter that rises and falls with what you do. Making coffee in the morning? You can hand it to your partner instead of drinking it yourself. Building a flat-pack coffee table together, step by step from the instructions? The heart goes up. Texting a selfie from the beach? That too. These things affect how the two treat each other and some of the story beats. And if Jason skips the shower for a few days, Lucia will make sure to mention it.

## Lucia: the lead the series was waiting for

Lucia is GTA's first female protagonist, and she changes more than I expected. She isn't a poster-style "strong heroine." She's a person with a past. The game spends time on her rough childhood and her complicated relationship with a mother who always promised and never delivered. All of that gives weight to her hunger to get ahead, make money and never go back to where she came from.

![Lucia in the driver's seat of an open-top car, in the pink and purple light of a city night](img/posts/gta6-review-lucia-car.webp "Lucia. She isn't waiting for anyone to save her. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

It affects the whole world. GTA has always been a very male world, and in past games women were mostly the punchline. Here, Lucia is surrounded by women with depth: up-and-coming rappers, her mother, her friends from prison, the drug runner with the purple rifle. They can be as violent, tough and messed up as the men. Leonida is still crude, but now there's someone reacting to it with an eye-roll and a comeback.

Jason is the quiet half. At first he looks like another muscular guy who doesn't say much. But through Lucia's eyes, her teasing and her affection, you see his softer side. The two of them together are what makes the story. They're a couple you want to see make it, even while they're doing things they really shouldn't.

## Starting out in the Keys: a house on stilts and a pet snake

The game opens with Lucia in prison. You play only as Jason, who lives in a run-down house on stilts in the Keys, with a jetty out back and a pet snake, working for a shady guy down at the marina. The early jobs are small: odd jobs, deliveries, a bit of violence. Meanwhile, you fix up the house. Actually fix it: hammering in new stairs, rolling paint onto the walls.

![Lucia in an orange prison jumpsuit with a hard stare, another inmate beside her](img/posts/gta6-review-lucia-prison.webp "Lucia starts the game behind bars. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

![Jason in a tank top sitting in a car in the shade of palm trees, one hand on the wheel, looking tired](img/posts/gta6-review-jason-keys.webp "Jason in the Keys, waiting for Lucia and building her a home. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

This stretch is a lesson in what Rockstar does well. It's quiet, it's small, and it lets you get to know Jason before the noise starts. It's also where the game's one weakness begins. We'll get there.

## A world that lives even when you're not looking

I've been playing open-world games for over twenty years and I've never seen a city that feels like this. Something is always happening in the corner of your eye. Cops chasing someone. A car broken down at the roadside, and the driver either asks for help or swears at you to leave her alone. Two people arguing outside a shop. Someone filming a video for social media.

![An annotated screenshot from the Keys: an iguana crossing the road, a woman on a mobility scooter, a minibus at the curb, a billboard, a chat by a door and a cyclist](img/posts/gta6-review-keys-annotated-en.webp "One frame from the Keys, six reasons to stop and look. Screenshot: Rockstar Games. Labels: TechSite")

You can talk to anyone on the street, with two options: greet them or taunt them. Most of the time you get a "hey," or someone asking for a few bucks. Sometimes it ends in a fistfight. And sometimes, and this is the wild part, it opens up a whole storyline. Stopping to help a delivery driver whose car broke down can turn into a string of missions where you deliver her parcels for her, so her AI chatbot manager doesn't fire her. Along the way there are porch pirates, and there's a tiger. Yes, a tiger.

![A street in the Keys: people outside shops, parked cars and characters walking along the sidewalk](img/posts/gta6-review-keys-street.webp "An ordinary street in the Keys. Nobody here is just standing around. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

The crowds are a thing of their own. Clubs and events can pack more than a hundred people into one space, and no two look or move the same. You can get close and listen in on conversations. Walk away or into another room and the voices go muffled, like in real life.

![Lucia standing on a plaza by the marina, next to a man in a racing suit with his arms in the air, a camera on a tripod and a stand for a brand called AudaCity](img/posts/gta6-review-trailer-street.webp "A content shoot by the marina. Everyone here wants to go viral. From the official trailer. Rockstar Games")

Social media is part of the world. In Vice City even gang members hustle online, and the dealers fret about their follower counts. The characters' phones have a social app with videos and comment sections. It's funny, it's sharp, and sometimes it's alarming how much it looks like your own feed.

![A rap duo in Vice City, one filming the other on a phone in purple club light](img/posts/gta6-review-real-dimez.webp "Real Dimez. In Leonida, if it wasn't filmed, it didn't happen. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

## The phone: the most natural interface I've seen

In most games the phone is a menu in disguise. In GTA VI it's part of the characters' lives. Bosses text you a location and you can add it to your GPS. Lucia sends Jason a link to a funny video, and you can open it in the app and read the comments. The fitness app reminds you you're short on protein.

And the game doesn't pause when you pull out the phone. If a message comes in mid-drive, you can pull over and read it, or try reading while driving. It's exactly as bad an idea as it sounds.

## Everyday life: yes, it's GTA, and yes, you can eat crisps

GTA VI adds plenty of small everyday details and manages not to turn them into chores. Food and drink boost your physical abilities. Your car occasionally needs fuel. Changing clothes helps shake the cops when you're wanted. You can ride a bus that runs a fixed route and stops at stations, or rent an e-scooter and block the sidewalk like every other tourist.

![Shirtless Jason lifting a loaded barbell in a gym](img/posts/gta6-review-jason-gym.webp "Leg day or chest day? In Leonida, working out is part of the routine. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

The clever part is that you can ignore almost all of it. No hunger meters flashing every fifteen minutes. This isn't The Sims. The details are just there, and if you want to live the routine, you can. For me it came down to coffee every morning, hand in hand on the way to the car.

And some moments are more routine than crime, and they're among the best in the game: a cookout with friends by the water, playing fetch with a dog you met on the street, helping a neighbor tune up a car, 18 holes of mini golf full of jokes. One minute you're shooting your way out of a deal gone wrong, the next you're sitting on the living room floor assembling furniture. That contrast is exactly what makes Jason and Lucia feel real.

## Missions and heists: a Christopher Nolan movie without the sci-fi

The broad goal of the game is to make money. As much as possible. Outside the big story missions, you choose how. Rob jewelry stores, steal cars, raid drug dens, or dive for treasure on the seabed. Along the way you meet fences who buy your loot, and sometimes they text you especially juicy targets.

![Jason in a mask and Lucia with guns drawn inside a shop, a worker behind the counter with his hands up](img/posts/gta6-review-couple-robbery.webp "A shop robbery. It doesn't always go to plan, which is the fun of it. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

I personally prefer chases to armed robbery, so stealing cars became my main income. What I loved is that no small job is fully predictable. The pawn shop cashier might have a shotgun under the counter. You might slam into a truck mid-getaway. The world keeps forcing you to improvise.

Then come the big heists. They return from GTA V and are far more elaborate this time. Each one is built from several prep missions: getting gear, gathering intel, casing the place. Some of the prep jobs are huge on their own, including one where you go undercover for weeks of in-game time. When the big day comes, it plays out like a Hollywood action sequence, cutting quickly between Jason and Lucia, each bringing their own skills.

![Jason firing from behind a car while Lucia covers him, with a police car behind, a helicopter overhead and palm trees](img/posts/gta6-review-couple-shootout.webp "When it goes wrong, it goes wrong in a big way. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

One small change makes a huge difference: a new checkpoint system. Fail, and you restart nearby rather than from the beginning. Fail enough times and you can skip the mission altogether. After years of GTA missions that made me want to throw the controller, it's a real relief.

## Combat: simple, quick and loose

Gunplay will feel familiar to series fans. Get behind cover, pop up, shoot. The arsenal is huge, from small pistols to heavy weapons that turn any street into a war zone. Jason and Lucia can take a beating, which lets you jump into fights without worrying that every bullet will end the story.

What makes the fights special is where they happen. The game loves taking you somewhere gorgeous, a mansion, a yacht, a club, and then making it go sideways. I was surprised again and again by the excuse the writers found to turn an ordinary visit into a firefight. And thanks to the new checkpoints, even long fights don't wear you down.

## Side stories: full plots hiding at the edges

GTA VI has side stories so big that I forgot about the main plot for hours. Jason has an old friend trying to start a record label in the back room of a strip club, and it escalates into a feud with a famous rapper and a few life-or-death situations. Lucia deals with her mother, who has decided to become a real estate agent and gets tangled up with a violent gang, and with her best friend, who tries to recruit her into something that looks like a pyramid scheme and turns out to be far more ridiculous.

![Boobie Ike in pink shorts holding a phone outside a club called Jack of Hearts, with people holding drinks around him](img/posts/gta6-review-boobie-club.webp "Boobie Ike outside his club. Business in Leonida always starts at night. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

Then there's the supporting cast: Florida-flavored lunatics, biker gangs, friendly conspiracy theorists, a shady doctor, Lucia's friends from prison and their useless boyfriends. One of them, a tough middle-aged drug runner who treats her purple rifle like a child, is one of my favorite characters in the whole series.

![Cal Hampton lying on a pink flamingo float in a blue pool, can in hand and sunglasses on](img/posts/gta6-review-cal-pool.webp "Cal, Jason's buddy, on a typical workday. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

## The satire: proper GTA, just smarter

GTA has always mocked America, but sometimes it slid into punching down. In GTA VI the jokes are aimed at people who deserve them: men's rights activists, crypto and NFT bros, MLM hustlers, conspiracy theorists and people spending millions trying to live forever. The billboards, TV shows and radio stations are full of it.

To be clear, this is still GTA, and it is absolutely not for kids. It's crude, filthy and unhinged. There's a TV reality show I'd rather not describe here. There were scenes that made me laugh out loud and others that made me check whether anyone at home could hear. It's rated PEGI 18, and rightly so.

## Driving: the purest fun in the game

The driving in GTA VI is the best the series has had. Cars feel heavy when they should and light when they should. A sports car is fast and fragile, a truck drives like a tank and shoves everything aside, a motorbike threads through any gap in traffic, and off-roaders take you into swamps and dirt tracks.

![Jason riding a motorbike at night, with more riders behind him and city lights](img/posts/gta6-review-jason-bikes.webp "A night ride through Vice City. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

![A rider on a dirt bike in mid-air, high above palm trees and a tower](img/posts/gta6-review-trailer-bike-jump.webp "The jumps are back, and bigger. From the official trailer. Rockstar Games")

The vehicle physics are especially impressive. Clip a curb at speed and the car flips the way a real car would, setting off a pile-up. Cars have real weight, and every crash feels heavy. It makes every police chase feel genuinely dangerous.

![A white police car sliding across a wide road between palm trees and buildings, with people on the sidewalk](img/posts/gta6-review-trailer-police.webp "Leonida's cops don't give up easily. From the official trailer. Rockstar Games")

There's car customization too, and I promise you'll sink more hours into it than you plan to. Paint, rims, lowered suspension. And when the car is finally done, parking it by the beach is a proud moment.

![A lowered white sports car with pink livery parked on a palm-lined boulevard, a man in a button-up shirt sitting on it](img/posts/gta6-review-vice-custom-car.webp "Vice City-style customization. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

And not just cars. There are airboats in the swamps, seaplanes, helicopters, jet skis and yachts. Leonida is built around water, and a big part of the experience is just navigating between the islands.

![An airboat from above, gliding through green swamp plants and dark water in Grassrivers](img/posts/gta6-review-grassrivers-airboat.webp "An airboat in Grassrivers. Keep your hands out of the water. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

## The police: more than stars on a screen

Leonida's police feel like part of the world rather than a game rule. Crimes get reported: a shopkeeper who sees you causing trouble will call it in. Changing your clothes helps you slip away. And a police helicopter overhead is your cue to think fast.

Here's how it can play out. You head out to buy a shirt, accidentally bump into some gang members, and they follow you into the shop. A fight breaks out, the owner calls the cops, and since you're wanted anyway, why not empty the register? A hunt for a T-shirt ends in a police chase across half the city. That's GTA at its best: nobody scripted that scene. It just happened.

## Physics and small details: the crisp packet moment

There's one moment that sums up the whole approach. Lucia is eating a bag of crisps to top up her health before a fight, and halfway through she also picks up a can of soda. Until then she'd held the bag in one hand and picked crisps out with the other. Once that second hand is full, she starts tipping the bag over her mouth. Someone at Rockstar sat down and made that. For a situation maybe one percent of players will ever see.

And it isn't alone. A character who falls in the mud gets dirty only where their clothes hit the ground. Older people have wrinkles and veins showing through their skin. Fabric and hair move with the wind and with motion. In the rain, drops bead on car roofs and drip from gutters. In bright sun you can see finger smudges on your phone screen. And sometimes a laptop screen is reflected in a character's glasses.

![A large green iguana on a road in the Keys, with a red car and bridge pillars behind it](img/posts/gta6-review-trailer-iguana.webp "An iguana, a road, and nobody in a hurry. From the official trailer. Rockstar Games")

Animals are part of the detail too. Iguanas on the road, alligators in the canals, birds, dogs you can play with, and in the wild regions animals you can watch or hunt.

![Wild animals standing on a shaded riverbank in Mount Kalaga, among green trees](img/posts/gta6-review-kalaga-wildlife.webp "Mount Kalaga's wildlife. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

## Graphics and lighting: the best-looking game I've seen

I don't like saying "the best," but there's no way around it here. GTA VI is the best-looking game ever released on a console. The lighting does things I didn't think could be done in real time: headlights sweeping through a window, a squad car's red and blue painting a whole street, leaf shadows flickering at sunset.

![The Vice City skyline at a pink sunset, with palm trees, water reflecting the sun and a Ferris wheel](img/posts/gta6-review-trailer-skyline.webp "Sunset over Vice City. Not a movie, the game. From the official trailer. Rockstar Games")

![A lone tree in an open field against a golden sunset, light rays passing through its branches](img/posts/gta6-review-couple-tree.webp "Golden hour in Leonida. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

The nights are the real stars. Vice City after dark, with thousands of lit windows, neon signs and headlights on the highway, looks like a photograph. More than once I stopped the car just to open photo mode.

![Vice City at night from above, thousands of building lights, lit roads and a purple sky](img/posts/gta6-review-vice-lights.webp "Thousands of windows, and none of them painted on. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

Underwater too. I dove in the Keys looking for treasure and found a whole world of fish, reefs and light rays coming down from the surface. Most games give up on what happens below the waves. Here it's a full play space.

![A diver underwater in the Keys, a coral reef, fish and light rays from the surface](img/posts/gta6-review-keys-underwater.webp "Under the water in the Keys. There's stuff to find down here too. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

## Activities: I have no idea how many hours went into drone racing

If you think you'll finish the story and be done, think again. Even after the credits, the game showed me at under 60 percent completion. There are drone races you can't stop playing, mud-soaked rally races with gold medals to chase, meditation spots tucked into remote corners, hunting, fishing, kayaking, mini golf, basketball and shooting ranges. Plus collectibles, rare cars and places to rob on every corner.

![A kayaker in an orange kayak paddling down a green river, under an old iron bridge next to a yellow depth marker](img/posts/gta6-review-kalaga-kayak.webp "Kayaking on Mount Kalaga. Yes, this is GTA too. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

![Jason standing on a boat with a fishing rod, a friend in a colorful shirt sitting beside him, blue water behind](img/posts/gta6-review-jason-fishing.webp "A fishing day with a buddy. Quiet scenes like this are half the magic. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

![Lots of boats and jet skis in a blue lagoon in the Keys, people in swimwear sunbathing and swimming](img/posts/gta6-review-keys-boats.webp "A boat party in the Keys. In Leonida, the weekend starts on Wednesday. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

There's entertainment inside the entertainment, too. The in-game TV has full shows, there are books, and there are even video games inside the game. Including, as I've already told friends, a wildly pretentious indie game that's one of the best jokes of the year.

Shopping is an activity of its own, too. There are malls, flea markets and clothes shops, each selling a different look. We spent far too long dressing Jason and Lucia, and honestly, no regrets. In Leonida, how you look is part of the game, and not only for the photo you post. And after a long day, nothing beats an ice cream on the promenade at sunset.

## Music, radio and clubs

In GTA the radio has always been half the experience, and GTA VI delivers. There's a ridiculous music selection with real-world artists, and there are the talk stations, some of the funniest things in the game. I once drove the highway at sunset with Bad Bunny on the radio and realized this was exactly the moment I'd been waiting 13 years for. An official album with 34 original tracks came out alongside the game.

![A huge crowd dancing in a nightclub with green and pink lights and a DJ booth above](img/posts/gta6-review-vice-club.webp "A Vice City club. Hundreds of people, and nobody looks the same. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

And the clubs. This is where Rockstar shows off everything it has. Hundreds of people under the lights, all dressed up for a night out, dancing to a carefully picked soundtrack. On PS5, the DualSense pulses in your hands with the bassline. And in the middle of it all, Jason and Lucia dance together, focused only on each other.

![Lucia and Jason dancing close in a club in purple and blue light](img/posts/gta6-review-couple-club.webp "Jason and Lucia on the dance floor. This moment sums up the whole game. Screenshot: Rockstar Games")

## The only con: the story takes its time

Now for the weakness, as promised. GTA VI is in no hurry. None at all. In the opening hours you play only as Jason, with Lucia in prison. Even after she gets out, it takes a while before the two work as a team. For me, switching freely between them opened up only after about 17 hours. And Vice City itself, the city on the cover, I reached only after about 30 hours.

![A story timeline: from the first hour to roughly hour 30 is the slow build, then the plot takes off until the ending around hour 70](img/posts/gta6-review-pacing-en.webp "Here's what the pacing looks like. Hours are approximate. Graphic: TechSite")

In this first stretch, Jason and Lucia work for a long line of small-time crooks, and some of their jobs feel like errands: drive here, grab that, lose the cops, repeat. Some of the characters at this stage are two-line caricatures. If you come in expecting explosions from minute one, like GTA V's opening, you might be let down.

But, and it's a big but, once the story takes off it doesn't stop. In the second half Jason and Lucia join a ring of professional criminals, the missions build on each other, the heists get bigger and crazier, and the story becomes one of the best Rockstar has written. By the final missions I was pacing the living room in front of the TV because I couldn't sit still. All those slow hours getting to know Jason and Lucia are exactly why I cared so much about what would happen to them.

So yes, it's a weakness. If you're the kind of player who wants action right away, know that the game asks for patience. My tip: don't rush the story early on. Wander, do activities, steal cars, get to know the Keys. That time goes much faster when you're enjoying the world.

## No spoilers: the ending and what comes after

I won't reveal anything about the ending, and I'd tread carefully on social media for the next few weeks. What I can say: the final missions are tenser, bigger and more emotional than anything the series has done. I didn't expect GTA to make me genuinely worry about two characters. It did.

And the end isn't really the end. After the credits, Leonida stays open, with all the activities, side stories, small jobs and places you haven't found yet. There are rare cars to collect, treasure underwater, races you haven't won and whole neighborhoods you haven't set foot in. It's a game you'll come back to on an ordinary weeknight just to drive the coast with the radio on.

## PS5 and PS5 Pro: 30fps, but what a 30

There's no performance mode in GTA VI. It runs at 30 frames per second on every console, PS5 Pro included, and Rockstar has said that won't change. I know that disappoints some of you. In practice, though, the decision makes sense: all that simulation, the crowds, the lighting and the physics, takes everything the CPU has.

![PS5 vs PS5 Pro in GTA VI: both at 30fps, with the Pro steadier and sharper](img/posts/gta6-review-ps5-vs-pro-en.webp "The console difference in brief. Graphic: TechSite")

On PS5 Pro the game is locked to 30 with near-perfect frame pacing, and I played long sessions without seeing a single hitch. The image is sharper thanks to PSSR, and the night lighting is just stunning. On a standard PS5 it still looks amazing, but scenes with lots of explosions at once bring small dips. They don't get in the way of playing, but if you're sensitive to that, you'll notice.

And the controls? Despite the 30, it responds much better than Red Dead Redemption 2. Character movement is snappier and driving feels smooth. After half an hour I'd forgotten about the number.

On the DualSense: the moment the controller pulses to the club's bassline is the one that stuck with me. It's a small touch, and it adds something.

## Against GTA V and Red Dead Redemption 2

The question everyone asks: is it better than GTA V? My answer is yes, by a distance. GTA V was a huge, fun, chaotic playground. Red Dead Redemption 2 was the most convincing world I'd seen, but also heavy and slow. GTA VI takes the freedom of the first and the depth of the second and joins them. It's as loose as GTA and as alive as Red Dead.

It's also the most grown-up game in the series. The jokes are still there and so is the mayhem, but there's a real love story that gives you a reason to keep going. In GTA V I followed Michael, Franklin and Trevor because I wanted to see what they'd do. In GTA VI I kept going because I cared what would happen to Jason and Lucia. That's a huge difference.

## Tips for the first hours

- **Don't rush the story.** The early hours are slow on purpose. Use them to learn the Keys, make money and find activities.
- **Hold hands.** It's cute, and it's useful. When you're casing a place before a robbery, a couple in love draws far less attention.
- **Eat and drink.** It boosts your physical abilities, and you'll feel it in fights.
- **Change clothes when you're wanted.** It's one of the most useful tools for losing the cops.
- **Answer your texts.** Many of the juiciest targets arrive by phone, from the fences who buy your loot.
- **Stop for people at the roadside.** Some of the best stories in the game start with a broken-down car.
- **Watch your fuel.** It doesn't come up often, but nothing's more embarrassing than a car dying mid-chase.

## Who it's for, and who it isn't

If you love open worlds, this is your game. If you love good stories, also yes. If you're the kind of player who drops into a city and just wanders, drives, looks around and finds trouble, this is the biggest playground ever built. And if you played GTA V and loved it, there's no question at all.

Who's it less for? Anyone after a short game they can finish over a weekend. GTA VI is a commitment. Even if you only chase the story you'll spend dozens of hours, and if you poke into every corner you'll still be here in spring. And of course, anyone sensitive to crude content, violence, drugs and sex should know the game doesn't apologize for any of it.

One more word for parents: the rating is 18, and it's fully deserved. This isn't the "a bit of shooting and cars" GTA. There's content here I wouldn't want a 12-year-old to see, and it's not something you can switch off in the settings.
