In September, Apple did something it hadn't done in years: it launched a new iPhone generation without the regular model. The iPhone 18 Pro, Pro Max and the foldable iPhone Duo all reached Israeli stores, but a plain iPhone 18 never showed up. This week a new explanation surfaced, and it involves Samsung.

## The report

According to Semicon Portal, a Japanese site that covers the chip industry, Apple pushed back the base iPhone 18 to give Samsung time to make enough camera sensors for it. The report says Samsung began prototyping new iPhone sensors in the spring at its plant in Austin, Texas, with mass production planned for the fall. On that schedule, there was no way to build launch stock for September.

It fits with Apple's announcement last August of a partnership with Samsung's Texas fab on a new chipmaking technology. Apple didn't say what the chips were for, and later reports tied them to future iPhone cameras. There's a hint from Sony's side too. Sony has supplied iPhone camera sensors for years, and on its November 5 earnings call, CFO Lin Tao was asked about losing sole-supplier status with a major customer. She wouldn't talk about any specific customer, but said it has to be assumed that customers buy from several suppliers as normal business practice.

## How seriously to take it

With some caution. Neither Apple nor Samsung has confirmed anything. Reports that Apple planned to split its launches, with the pricier models in the fall and the regular ones in the spring, go back to May 2025, long before Samsung started on these sensors. So the sensors may be part of the story without being all of it. Expensive memory and component shortages have been mentioned as reasons before.

What is taking shape is the timing. Reports now expect the regular iPhone 18 in the first half of 2027, possibly alongside an iPhone 18e and iPhone Air 2. The rumors also point to two 48MP cameras, like the iPhone 17. Whether Sony or Samsung will make those sensors is still unknown.

## What to do in Israel meanwhile

If you've been holding out for "the next regular iPhone," expect a long wait, probably until spring at the earliest. The real question is whether waiting makes sense. Here's what iDigital, Apple's official reseller in Israel, sells today:

![Price table: the iPhone models on sale in Israel today, from the iPhone 15 at ₪2,869 to the iPhone Duo at ₪9,249](img/posts/iphone-israel-lineup-prices-en.webp "\"Starting at\" prices at iDigital. Graphic: TechSite")

- **iPhone 17** is the obvious comparison. The 256GB model costs ₪4,249 (about $1,328), or ₪3,601 (about $1,126) at the VAT-free Eilat stores.
- **iPhone 17e** starts at ₪3,299 (about $1,031), and the **iPhone Air** at ₪3,199 (about $1,000). If an older model is fine, the iPhone 15 is ₪2,869 (about $897).
- **iPhone 18 Pro** starts at ₪5,649 (about $1,766), ₪1,400 more than the iPhone 17.

If your current phone works and its battery holds up, there's no reason to rush. You can wait and see what arrives in the spring and at what price. But if your phone is on its last legs, waiting half a year for a model that hasn't even been announced doesn't make sense. The iPhone 17 will get updates for years, and its price will only drop further once the next one lands.

One more thing to keep in mind: memory prices across the industry climbed sharply this year. Even if the iPhone 18 arrives with a better sensor, nothing says it will cost less than the 17. If anything, the opposite.
