If you've ever played Pico Park with friends, you know how it goes. It starts with "this looks easy", moves on to "who pressed the button?!", and ends with everyone laughing on the couch. The series from Japanese studio TECOPARK has sold more than 8.5 million copies, and today, November 12, it gets a new entry with a very well-known guest: Sonic the Hedgehog, who turns 35 this year.

Sonic Pico Park is out now on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Xbox One, Switch, Switch 2 and PC via Steam, for $9.99 (about ₪32). On Switch 2 you play the Switch version. Here's whether it's worth those ten dollars, and who will enjoy it most.

![Sonic Pico Park at a glance: $9.99, 2 to 8 players, 15 worlds of 4 levels, a Battle mode and cross-play](img/posts/sonic-pico-park-overview-en.webp "The short version. Graphic: TechSite")

## How it works

The rules are as simple as ever. Each stage is a small 2D level, and the goal is to get everyone to the goal plate on the right. Not one player. Everyone. If even one character dies, the whole team goes back to the start of the stage. That's what makes it work: you can't carry the team on your own, and every mistake by one person costs everyone.

Every stage has one or two ideas. A button that has to stay pressed while someone else crosses. A wall too high to jump, so you stack on each other's heads to make a living ladder. A key that has to pass from hand to hand. Nothing lasts more than a few minutes, and that's on purpose. It's a game of quick wins and quick failures.

The clever part is how stages adapt to the number of players. A puzzle with two players looks different from the same puzzle with four or eight. With more players there's more to coordinate, and more chances for someone to get in the way at exactly the wrong moment.

![A stack of small pixel characters climbing on top of each other up a blue wall, with Sonic at the top](img/posts/sonic-pico-park-stack-climb.webp "Nobody gets up alone: a human ladder of characters. Image: PICO PARK Inc. / SEGA")

## What Sonic brings

This isn't just a skin. Every character can do Sonic's Spin Dash, which you need to build up speed, smash through cracked walls and take out enemies. Rings let you absorb one hit, for example from spikes, so sometimes one player has to grab a ring and walk over the hazard to open the way for everyone else. In some stages you have to hold on to a ring to get past a gate. Springs, loops, Item Box Monitors and even Chao all appear as stage mechanics.

You can play as the classic Pico Cats or as Sonic and friends: Sonic, Tails, Knuckles, Amy, Shadow, Cream, Big, Silver, Blaze, Espio, Charmy and Rouge. Everyone jumps the same height, but there are differences. Tails, Cream, Charmy and Rouge can fly, carrying one friend easily or two for a short moment. Knuckles glides and can push certain blocks. If a stage needs an ability nobody has picked, a door appears that lets one player swap characters on the spot, without going back to the menu.

The worlds vary nicely. Some, like Rules, are pure puzzles. Others, with names like Go! and Go! Go!, lean into speed and loops. And a few are built around specific Sonic characters and nods to older games. Fans will spot plenty of small touches.

## With 2, with 4, with 8

You can play with 2 to 8 players, on the same screen or online, and online play supports cross-play between all platforms. On PC, up to 7 local players can join an online session from a single computer. Every stage is open to any number of players.

It's fun even with two. Small misjudgements create funny moments without anyone planning them. But the sweet spot seems to be four: enough people to make the stages feel full, and enough room to pick two flyers and two who can't fly. With eight it's pure chaos, which is exactly what some groups want.

The Battle mode is the competitive side, mostly about collecting rings while avoiding obstacles, with another mode where you collect orbs. With four or more it gets lively. With two or three, it's flat.

## What doesn't work as well

**Balance between characters.** In small groups, picking a character who can't fly puts you at a disadvantage. With two players there's almost no reason not to take Tails or Cream. The swap door helps, but it's a patch rather than a fix.

**Where's the speed?** This is a Sonic game where you're not really supposed to go fast. The loops are there, but the characters move at Pico Park pace. When you do hit a sideways spring, it launches you at full speed, often straight into a hazard. Patience wins here, not reflexes.

**It ends too quickly.** This is the biggest complaint. Fifteen worlds of four stages sounds like a lot, but each stage is short, and a group that clicks can see most of the content in a few hours. Worse, some of the cleverest mechanics appear once or twice in a world and never come back. You finish a stage thinking "that was brilliant, give me five more like it", and the game moves on.

**No Hebrew.** The game supports 14 languages, Hebrew isn't one of them. In practice there's very little text, so even young children will manage, but it's worth knowing.

## Who it's for

For families with kids, Sonic Pico Park is close to an ideal purchase for Hanukkah break or a rainy Friday night: cheap, easy to explain, and works with several controllers on one screen. It also suits groups of friends who already enjoy Pico Park and anyone who grew up on Sonic.

It's less suitable for solo players, because there's no single-player mode at all. And if you're looking for real Sonic-style speed, you won't find it here.

:::score
## The bottom line

7/10

Sonic Pico Park blends the Pico Park formula with Sonic smartly and with a lot of affection: Spin Dash, rings, loops and characters that really change how you solve the stages. It's funny with two and wild with eight, and $9.99 is a fair price. It loses points for short content, clever ideas that are used once and dropped, imbalance between flying and non-flying characters, and a lack of real speed. Get it for your next game night with friends or family. Just don't expect it to last all winter.
:::

**What I liked**

- Short, clever stages that adapt to the number of players
- Sonic mechanics that really change the puzzles
- 2 to 8 players, on one screen or online, with cross-play
- A low price
- Easy for children and people who don't usually play

**What I liked less**

- Ends quickly, with ideas that don't come back
- Flying characters are clearly more useful
- Very little real speed for a Sonic game
- A Battle mode that only works with a crowd
- No single-player mode, and no Hebrew
