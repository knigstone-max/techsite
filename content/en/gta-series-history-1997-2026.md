Nine days to go. GTA VI lands on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S on November 19, and as of today, November 10, you can already pre-load it. If you haven't cleared at least 212GB yet, now is a good time to delete the games you keep telling yourself you'll finish. Sony says it is the most pre-ordered game in the history of the PlayStation Store. Take-Two told investors that pre-orders are "tracking far in excess" of its own expectations. Yes, the same company that delayed the game twice.

Before the whole planet disappears into Leonida for a fortnight, it's worth remembering how we got here. This series wasn't born a billion-dollar monster. It started as a stuttering side project at a small Scottish studio, with cars the size of postage stamps and a publisher that nearly pulled the plug more than once. What happened next changed how games are built, sold and argued about.

If you just want Israeli prices and the cheapest place to pre-order, that's [in a separate piece](/techsite/en/posts/gta-6-israel-price-preorder/). This one is about the big story.

## 1997: the game nobody wanted

Start in Dundee. In 1997, DMA Design was mostly known as "the Lemmings people", the studio behind those little green guys who march cheerfully off cliffs. Late that year it put out Grand Theft Auto on PC and PlayStation through publisher BMG Interactive. According to the Guardian, the project was so unloved internally that BMG almost cancelled it several times.

What did we get? A top-down view, like a city map someone forgot to switch off. Three cities: Liberty City (New York), San Andreas (San Francisco) and Vice City (Miami). Every name you know today was already there in 1997, drawn in crayon. You picked one of eight characters, answered ringing payphones, took jobs from local crime bosses and, above all, scored points. A million to clear a level, and pretty much anything counted: a job, a stolen car, or plain old mayhem.

![A screenshot from the original GTA seen from above: police cars box in a car on a road under the yellow word BUSTED](img/posts/gta-history-gta1-busted.webp "The original GTA, 1997. BUSTED, years before it was a meme. Image: Rockstar Games, from DMA Design's press kit")

It looks like it runs on a calculator now. But the feeling was new: here's a city, it moves, and you can do roughly whatever you like in it. The game doesn't judge you. It just keeps score.

Then there's the detail that explains the series better than anything. When the British press started howling about the violence, BMG hired celebrity publicist Max Clifford. Clifford didn't calm anyone down. He poured petrol on it, and the row went all the way to the House of Lords, where a former Scottish secretary condemned the game. GTA learned on day one what half the industry still hasn't: outrage is free marketing.

### London, then Anywhere City

1999 brought two expansions, London 1969 and London 1961, still the only GTA games set outside the United States. You drive on the left, the slang is British and there's a very familiar James Bond spoof. That October came GTA 2, set in a nameless retro-future metropolis called Anywhere City. Its big idea was rival gangs: work for one and the others remember. In fairness, it looked almost identical to the first game. What people actually remember is the live-action intro film and the fact that it's the only GTA ever released on Sega's Dreamcast.

## 2001: the day the camera hit the street

Then came October 2001, the PlayStation 2, and Grand Theft Auto III. For the first time Liberty City wasn't a map you looked down on. It was a place you stood in. A camera over your shoulder, buildings that blocked out the sky, rain, an elevated train, and radio stations running ads dumber than the real ones.

One for the history buffs: GTA III still shipped under the DMA Design name. By then the studio was part of the Take-Two and Rockstar family, and in 2002 it took the name it still carries, Rockstar North. It has led the series from Edinburgh ever since.

Claude, the hero, never says a word. It didn't matter, because the city was the star. Around him was a supporting cast voiced by people like Kyle MacLachlan and Debi Mazar, which in 2001 was a rare thing in a video game.

![Claude, the hero of GTA III, walks down a Liberty City street between skyscrapers in a black jacket and green trousers](img/posts/gta-history-gta3-liberty-city.webp "GTA III, 2001. Claude doesn't talk. The city talks for him. Image: Rockstar Games")

Let's be precise: GTA III didn't invent the open world. Open worlds existed before it. What it did was turn the idea into a formula the whole industry copied: one big city, story missions scattered across it, and endless nonsense in between. Almost every open-world game since, from heist sims to superhero games, uses that skeleton. That's the gap between a good game and one that rewrites the rules.

The outrage arrived on schedule, too. The press discovered you could pick up a sex worker, get a health boost, then kill her to take your money back. The game never makes you do it, but that didn't trouble the headlines. GTA's philosophy has always been that players can do what they want, even when it's ugly. And ugly sells.

## 2002 to 2004: the years Rockstar couldn't miss

### Vice City: neon, synths and Scarface

A year after GTA III, in October 2002, came Vice City. Tommy Vercetti gets out of prison, lands in a fictional 1986 Miami and wants one thing: to get to the top of the pile as fast as possible. If that sounds like Scarface with a dash of Miami Vice, you're not alone. The Guardian put it exactly that way, and plenty of fans still call it the best game in the series.

![Tommy Vercetti in a Hawaiian shirt rides a motorbike and fires a gun beside pink art deco buildings in Vice City](img/posts/gta-history-vice-city-2002.webp "Vice City, 2002. If you own a Hawaiian shirt, this is your game. Image: Rockstar Games")

Setting it in the 1980s was a stroke of genius. It gave Rockstar an excuse to stack the radio with period hits, blow up speedboats to synth-pop and dress everyone in pastel suits. It was the first GTA with a look you could recognise from a single screenshot. Other games did open worlds. Vice City did them with style.

A word on the radio while we're here. From GTA III onwards, the stations are half the atmosphere: a licensed soundtrack of real hits wrapped in talk shows, unhinged DJs and ads for products that wouldn't last a day in the real world. Lots of players remember Vice City's stations better than its missions. Rockstar figured that out early, and every GTA since has shipped with a soundtrack that's practically a product of its own. GTA VI already has an official album.

### San Andreas: three cities, CJ and one very hot coffee

In 2004 came San Andreas and Carl "CJ" Johnson, heading home to Los Santos in 1992 after his mother is killed. This time the inspiration was the '90s hood movie, and Rockstar didn't hide it. There are three cities, Los Santos, San Fierro and Las Venturas, with desert, mountains, lakes and forest in between. You can cycle, swim, fly planes, hit the gym and eat until CJ gets fat. It also has one of the series' best villains, the crooked cop Frank Tenpenny, voiced by Samuel L. Jackson.

![CJ in a green shirt stands with his gang on Grove Street at sunset, among low houses and palm trees](img/posts/gta-history-san-andreas-grove.webp "San Andreas, 2004. Grove Street. Home. Image: Rockstar Games")

San Andreas is also remembered for "Hot Coffee". A sex scene that had been cut was still sitting in the code, and modders found a way to switch it on. It went all the way to the US Congress, and the Federal Trade Commission questioned founders Sam and Dan Houser. That was no longer the kind of scandal that helps sales. It was the moment Rockstar learned there was a line.

Along the way the series went pocket-sized. Liberty City Stories (2005) and Vice City Stories (2006) brought the 3D era to the PSP. In hindsight they were a dress rehearsal for everything that came later: more content for the same worlds, and more ways to sell you the same city.

## 2008: GTA IV tries to grow up

In 2008, on PS3 and Xbox 360, came GTA IV. Niko Bellic, an Eastern European ex-soldier, arrives in Liberty City chasing the American dream and quickly finds that the dream starts and ends with organised crime. The tone was darker and much more sober. The city was grey, wet and believable. The driving was heavy, the physics behaved like physics, and for the first time your mobile phone became part of the interface: call a cab, ring your friends, pay for every mistake.

![Niko Bellic in a leather jacket stands in front of a lit-up diner at night in Liberty City, gesturing with one hand](img/posts/gta-history-gta4-niko.webp "GTA IV, 2008. Niko Bellic and the grey version of the American dream. Image: Rockstar Games")

And then there's cousin Roman, calling every half hour to ask if you fancy bowling. It's a running joke to this day, but it also showed how far Rockstar would go on details nobody else would bother with. In 2009 came two add-ons, The Lost and Damned and The Ballad of Gay Tony. Looking back, that was the first sign of the strategy Rockstar would later perfect: don't walk away from a world once you've built it. Keep selling inside it.

## 2013: GTA V, the biggest cash machine in entertainment

Then, in September 2013, came GTA V, and nothing was the same again. Three leads instead of one: Michael, a retired bank robber bored to death in his mansion; Franklin, a young guy trying to get out of the neighbourhood; and Trevor, who is... Trevor. The Guardian summed it up nicely: the three of them split the series into its three personalities, cinematic ambition, crime drama and pure violent chaos.

According to Take-Two, the game passed $1 billion in retail sales in just three days, the fastest any entertainment product had reached that mark, films included. As of November 2026, GTA V has sold nearly 235 million units. That's almost half of the whole series, which stands at nearly 480 million. And to settle the argument for good: in the last quarter alone, 13 years after launch, that number went up by several million. Remember that next time someone tells you old games don't sell.

![A comparison of Los Santos at night in GTA V and Vice City at night in GTA VI, with labels on each game's platforms and upgrades](img/posts/gta-history-v-vs-vi-en.webp "Los Santos in 2013 against Vice City in 2026. Images: Rockstar Games. Graphic: TechSite")

### GTA Online, or how to sell one game three times

What really made GTA V a money printer was GTA Online. It launched in autumn 2013 in such a mess that servers buckled and characters simply vanished. Over the years, though, it became one of the most profitable online games ever made: free updates, heists, supercars and, of course, Shark Cards for the impatient. Since 2022 there's also a GTA+ subscription.

That's also where the fans' most justified complaint begins. Because the machine kept printing cash, the gap between GTA V and GTA VI stretched to almost 13 years. GTA V ended up on three console generations. An enhanced version came to PS5 and Xbox Series X|S in March 2022 and to PC in March 2025, as a free upgrade for existing owners. Still not convinced it works? The Israeli PS Store still sells GTA V today for ₪152 (about $49).

## 29 years of graphics: what actually changed

Saying "the graphics got better" is true of any series that's lasted a decade. With GTA, each visual leap also changed how you play.

![A strip of eight screenshots from the original GTA to GTA VI, each with its year and a short note on what changed](img/posts/gta-history-graphics-evolution-en.webp "From 1997 to 2026, every big leap in one image. Images: Rockstar Games. Graphic: TechSite")

- **1997 to 1999, overhead:** 2D sprites and a CCTV-camera view. The city is a game board, and the fun is in the traffic.
- **2001 to 2004, eye level:** chunky polygons, fog hiding whatever the console couldn't draw, and square faces. But for the first time there's a skyline, a sunset and a sense of place. Vice City and San Andreas proved that strong style beats technical limits.
- **2008, weight:** GTA IV gave the series mass. Cars that crumple convincingly, people who lose their balance, lighting that's sometimes downright oppressive. Not everyone loved it, but it was a necessary step.
- **2013, scale:** GTA V took all that and added a huge map, from the mountains down to Michael's tennis court, plus three characters you swap between at the press of a button.
- **2026, density:** going by what Rockstar has shown, GTA VI isn't only about size but about how much detail sits in every corner. More on that shortly.

![The original GTA's top-down view next to Jason and Lucia leaning on a green sports car on a Vice City beach in GTA VI](img/posts/gta-history-2d-vs-vi-en.webp "Same series, same Vice City, 29 years later. Images: Rockstar Games. Graphic: TechSite")

## The faces: from a silent hero to a couple on the cover

Line up the leads over the years and you see the whole series grow. Claude never spoke. Tommy was a classic gangster in a Hawaiian shirt. CJ was the first who felt like a person with a family and a neighbourhood. Niko was the first with a heavy past and a conscience. GTA V split the hero three ways, and GTA VI, for the first time since the series went 3D, has a female lead: Lucia Caminos, alongside Jason Duval.

![A collage of six images: Claude from GTA III, Tommy Vercetti from Vice City, CJ from San Andreas, Niko Bellic from GTA IV, Michael, Franklin and Trevor from GTA V, and Lucia and Jason from GTA VI](img/posts/gta-history-characters-en.webp "Six generations of leads, 2001 to 2026. Images: Rockstar Games. Graphic: TechSite")

Trivia fans, take note: the 1997 original already let you pick a female character from its eight options. But that was a tiny face on a menu, not a lead with a story.

## The impact: how GTA changed games, and what's outside them

### The open-world template

The most obvious legacy is structure. "Big city, story missions, loads to do on the side" is now the default for half the big games on the market. Even games nowhere near crime, with knights or superheroes, lean heavily on the skeleton GTA III set in 2001.

### Adult games are big business

GTA proved that an adults-only game full of violence, drugs, political satire and swearing could also be the best-selling product in the world. Once GTA V made a billion dollars in three days, nobody in the business could claim games were kids' stuff any more. GTA VI is rated PEGI 18 as well. Don't buy it for a ten-year-old just because he begs.

### Satire hiding on the radio

What really sets GTA apart is that its jabs at America don't come as speeches. They come in radio ads, idiotic talk shows, billboards and fake company names. It's a whole pop culture inside a game, and it changed how other studios build worlds: not just a place, but its own media, brands and politics.

### The outrage machine

From the House of Lords in 1997, through lawsuits by victims' families in the Vice City era, to Congress over Hot Coffee, GTA was the favourite target of every politician hunting for a "violent video games" headline. It fuelled a decade of public fights about games, ratings and censorship. Most of them ended with nothing, apart from more publicity for the game.

### The business model

And perhaps the deepest influence, and the least romantic: GTA Online showed the industry how much money one game can make if you just keep feeding it. Live-service games existed before, but few proved the model at that scale, and ever since, every CEO has wanted their own GTA Online. Most don't get one.

## GTA VI: what we actually know

### The delays

GTA VI was officially revealed with a trailer in December 2023. Guinness says it drew 90.4 million YouTube views in 24 hours, a record for a video game trailer. Rockstar was aiming for 2025 back then. Then the delays came: first to May 26, 2026, and then, on November 6, 2025, to November 19, 2026. "We are sorry for adding additional time to what we realize has been a long wait," Rockstar wrote, "but these extra months will allow us to finish the game with the level of polish you have come to expect and deserve." The second trailer, from May 2025, pulled in around 475 million views across all platforms in 24 hours, according to Rockstar.

### Where, who and what

It all happens in present-day Vice City and the fictional state of Leonida, which is basically Florida under Rockstar's magnifying glass. Jason and Lucia have always known the deck is stacked against them. When an easy score goes wrong, they end up on the darkest side of the sunniest place in America, in the middle of a criminal conspiracy that stretches across the state. That's the official blurb, and it's all Rockstar is willing to say.

![Jason and Lucia lean against each other in the middle of a robbery, with a skull mask, gloves and cash flying through the air](img/posts/gta-history-vi-heist.webp "Lucia and Jason, on the job. Image: Rockstar Games")

![Vice City by day in GTA VI: skyscrapers, a turquoise bay, bridges, boats and a beach with umbrellas](img/posts/gta-history-vi-vice-city-day.webp "Vice City, 2026. Image: Rockstar Games")

### How big is the map?

The internet has been saying for months that "the map is twice the size of GTA V". Rockstar never said that. What it did say, through co-studio head Aaron Garbut in an interview with Edge magazine, is that Leonida is almost as big as the maps of GTA IV, GTA V and Red Dead Redemption 2 combined, with "an order of magnitude more interiors" than any of its previous games. If that holds up, it's the most important change here: less empty desert, more buildings you can actually walk into.

![Vice City in 1986 in GTA Vice City against present-day Vice City in GTA VI, with labels for each version](img/posts/gta-history-vice-city-then-now-en.webp "The same city, 24 years apart. Images: Rockstar Games. Graphic: TechSite")

### What it means if you play on PS5

According to the PlayStation Blog, GTA VI was built from the ground up for PS5. It uses the DualSense speaker, haptics and adaptive triggers throughout, all adjustable in the accessibility settings. There's Tempest 3D Audio and faster loading thanks to the SSD. PS5 Pro adds PSSR upscaling and ray-traced shadows. Xbox Series X|S owners get exactly the same game, minus the controller toys. And PC? No official date at all. Rockstar hasn't said a word.

- **Ultimate Edition:** $99.99 in the US and ₪399 (about $128) in the Israeli PS Store. It adds premium vehicles, weapons, outfits and extras woven through Jason and Lucia's story.

- **Launch:** November 19, at midnight in each country. New Zealand goes first.

## The bottom line

Between 1997 and 2026, GTA went from a game its own publisher nearly shelved to a series that has sold close to half a billion copies. Along the way it defined what an open world should look like, proved adult games are huge business and taught the industry how to sell the same world for more than a decade. Not all of it is pretty. There were pointless scandals, a mountain of Shark Cards and a wait that went on far too long. But no other series has shaped how we play quite like this one.

Now we find out whether GTA VI justifies 13 years of hype. The bar Rockstar set for itself is ridiculously high, and if anyone can clear it, it's them. In the meantime, free up some space, charge your DualSense and warn your boss you'll be "ill" on November 20. See you in Leonida.
