Fridges with screens aren't new, but Hisense has pushed the idea further. The PureSmart Duo series, announced in October and now sold by big UK retailers like Currys, has a **21-inch** touchscreen in the door. That's close to a computer monitor, and it does a lot more than show the temperature.

## What the screen does

Hisense splits the screen's job three ways:

- **Fridge controls.** Temperature settings, filter reminders and a timer.
- **Food management.** You log what's in the fridge, set expiry dates, and it reminds you when something is about to go off. There are more than 80 recipes and a shopping list, and you can send ingredients from a recipe straight to the list.
- **Entertainment.** Through Hisense's VIDAA platform you get TV, music, a browser and apps. When nobody's using it, the screen shows artwork, wallpapers or AI-generated screensavers.

The fridge also links to Hisense's ConnectLife app and, through it, to compatible smart home devices. There's a voice assistant too.

## Three cameras inside

The feature I find most interesting is Smart Food Vision: three built-in cameras that capture what's on the shelves. The idea is simple. Instead of standing in front of an open fridge trying to remember what's missing, you check the contents on the screen or your phone. At the supermarket, that could save you the "do we still have milk?" call home.

How useful it is depends on discipline. An inventory with expiry dates only helps if you keep it up to date, a point Australian outlet ChannelNews raised in its own launch coverage.

## Still a fridge

Under all the tech is a four-door fridge. Hisense's official announcement puts this model in the series at **611 litres**. It has Dual-Tech Cooling, three temperature zones with a flexible compartment (My Fresh Choice), a sliding shelf and a Cool Fresh Box. Hisense also claims its Anti-Bacterial Guard eliminates up to 99.9% of bacteria inside.

The door dispenser, called Refresh Hub Pro, gives you crescent ice, crushed ice and chilled water without opening the fridge. The water is filtered and UV-treated, and Hisense claims 99.9% sterilisation. No independent test results were published for either claim. Design-wise, you get flat doors, recessed handles and a black stainless steel finish.

## What about Israel?

Hisense refrigerators are imported to Israel by the **H.Y. Group (קבוצת ח.י)**. So far it hasn't announced the PureSmart Duo, and there's no local price. The UK launch announcements didn't include a price either.

For a sense of scale: the Hisense four-door fridge H.Y. sells on its site today, the RQ72-SK with 609 litres net, is listed at **₪7,390** (about $2,290). The PureSmart Duo is similar in capacity and adds a screen, cameras and an ice dispenser, so it's hard to see it arriving for less.

If it does come, check a few things first:

- **Water line.** The ice and water dispenser needs a permanent plumbed connection. Plenty of Israeli kitchens have no water point near the fridge, which means calling a plumber.
- **Shabbat mode.** With a big screen, cameras and sensors, this is worth asking the importer about. Hisense didn't mention it.
- **Hebrew and apps.** VIDAA and the recipe library were built for other markets. It's not known what will work in Hebrew.

For now, it's mostly a sign of where things are heading: manufacturers want the fridge to be one more screen in the house, right alongside the TV and the phone. Whether you want TV on your fridge is a matter of taste.
