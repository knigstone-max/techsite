In August, Corma came out of stealth with $60 million. Yesterday, November 16, it announced a **$125 million** Series A (about ₪403 million) at a **$1 billion** valuation (about ₪3.2 billion). Three months, and a company of roughly 25 people is a unicorn.

## Who put in the money

The round was led by US venture firm NEA, with Corma's announcement thanking NEA's Aaron Jacobson. Existing backers Sequoia Capital, Khosla Ventures and Coatue came back in. Total funding to date: **$185 million** (about ₪596 million).

## What Corma does

Corma was founded in 2025 by CEO Alon Pluda, a graduate of the Psagot program and a Unit 8200 veteran. It's based in Tel Aviv and San Francisco. According to Calcalist, the team mixes researchers from Google and DeepMind with 8200 security people.

The pitch: instead of taking a general-purpose AI model, trained mostly to write code, and bending it toward security work, Corma trains **its own foundation model built only for defense**. On top of it sits a "workforce" of autonomous agents that work alongside a company's security team, using the tools it already has. They investigate threats, look into suspicious activity and carry out defensive tasks from start to finish.

## Why now

Corma's argument is that AI's attack capabilities are growing very fast while defense lags behind. In the blog post that came with the funding news, the company points to a wave of critical vulnerabilities and breaches in widely used software in October, which the industry took to calling Black October.

The company says demand is coming from Fortune 100, Fortune 500 and Fortune Global 500 organizations, and that it has grown its server capacity 24-fold. It also admits that getting enough compute is one of its biggest problems, and that there's a waitlist. The new money will mainly go into scaling its models and compute, with some selective hiring.

## Not just Corma

The same day brought another big cyber round. Daylight, founded by Israelis Hagai Shapira and Eldad Rudich and based in New York, raised **$80 million** (about ₪258 million) in a Series B led by Goldman Sachs' growth equity arm. It's reportedly valued at around $400 million, and 43 of its 80 employees are in Israel. Daylight also uses AI agents, but with human analysts supervising them.

Together, the two rounds show where the money in Israeli cyber is going right now: companies building defense around AI, and growing fast. The big question for Corma is whether that pace holds. A $1 billion price on a year-old company with about 25 people is a large bet on what its models will be able to do over the next few years.
