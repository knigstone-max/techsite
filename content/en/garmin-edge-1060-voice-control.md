Garmin announced the Edge 1060 on October 20. It is the company's new premium bike computer, and the main change is a built-in microphone and speaker, so riders can keep both hands on the bars.

## What the voice control does

After "Okay, Garmin" you can give commands like "Report a pothole", "Route me to the closest coffee shop" or "What is my heart rate?". The device changes the screen, reads out stats or suggests routes. With a connected phone and the Garmin Messenger app you can send voice messages and hear incoming ones through the speaker. The speaker also plays a digital bike bell. Garmin notes that voice control works in select languages only and that performance depends on riding conditions.

## Navigation

- Route Options: several routes to the same place, compared by ride time, climbing and bike type
- Route Previews: distance, estimated time, traffic and reported hazards before you start
- Up Ahead: the nearest coffee shops, restrooms or gas stations along the route
- Road closure alerts reported by other Garmin riders, when paired with a phone
- Free Flow Routing: routes with the fewest stops and intersections, for training

## Training and battery

The Edge 1060 works with Garmin Cycling Coach plans and syncs TrainingPeaks workouts. It can join a nearby GroupRide session with messaging and live locations. It can also reverse-charge a Varia light or a phone over USB-C, though not while riding. The touchscreen is joined by extra buttons for use with gloves or in bad weather.

Garmin quotes up to 20 hours of battery in the most demanding use and up to 60 hours in battery saver mode. It is available now for $799.99.
