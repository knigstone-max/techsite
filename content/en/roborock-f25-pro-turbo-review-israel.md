Most of the wet-dry vacuums we reviewed this month had one thing in common: steam. The [Tineco S9 Artist Steam Pro](/techsite/en/posts/tineco-s9-artist-steam-pro-review-israel/), the [Dreame H16 Pro Steam](/techsite/en/posts/dreame-h16-pro-steam-review-israel/) and the [Xiaomi Wet Dry Vacuum 5 Pro](/techsite/en/posts/xiaomi-wet-dry-vacuum-5-pro-review-israel/) all compete on who runs hotter, and all of them cost ₪2,000 and up.

The **Roborock F25 Pro Turbo** goes the other way. No steam at all. Instead there's a trigger on the handle: hold it, and the machine pushes more water, more suction and a faster roller for as long as you keep your finger down. When it arrived in Israel in October, alongside the [F25 Ultra Steam Gen 2](/techsite/en/posts/roborock-f25-ultra-steam-israel/), it cost ₪2,190. A month and a half later, it's far cheaper.

I went through Roborock's product and support pages, the footnotes (where the interesting details are), and current Israeli prices, and lined it up against the machines it actually competes with.

## What it costs in Israel

The launch price was ₪2,190 (about $658). This week I found:

- **GoMobile**: ₪1,529 (about $460), listed with an official warranty
- **iCell**: ₪1,609 (about $484)
- **Kravitz**: ₪1,690 (about $508)

In the US, Roborock's own store lists it at $549.99 (about ₪1,829), and right now it's on sale there for $379.99 (about ₪1,264), before sales tax.

The drop from ₪2,190 to around ₪1,550 changes the whole story. At the launch price it sat a few hundred shekels below steam machines with more features. At today's price it competes with the mid-range, and that's where it makes sense. Roborock and the stores don't spell out the warranty length on every listing, so ask before you pay, and make sure it's an official import.

## HyperScrub: a trigger instead of steam

This is the main feature. In normal use the F25 Pro Turbo works like any wet-dry vacuum: it sucks, washes with a roller that keeps getting clean water, and pulls the dirty water back into a separate tank. When you hit a stain that won't budge, you hold the trigger on the handle, and three things happen together:

- **Water delivery** goes up by 245%
- **Scrubbing density** goes up by 24%
- **Suction** goes up by 36%

All three are compared with AUTO mode and come from Roborock's internal testing, so treat them as direction, not a promise. In scrub mode the roller spins at **550 RPM**, and Roborock quotes **20 N** of downward pressure on the floor.

What does that mean at home? A dried coffee ring by the sofa, a dried drip of ketchup under the kids' chairs, the sticky film in front of the stove. Steam softens these with heat. HyperScrub tries to do it with more water and more friction. For most everyday stains that's enough. For old, thick grease on kitchen tiles, steam still has an advantage. That's physics.

One detail I liked: Roborock put HyperScrub through a 60-minute test by TÜV SÜD on solid wood flooring, pushing and pulling with scrub mode on, and the floor showed no swelling, warping or cracking. Even so, if you have real wood floors, check what the flooring maker allows. One lab test isn't approval for every floor.

## Suction, modes and the dirt sensor

**25,000Pa** of maximum suction. That's the same as the F25 Ultra Steam Gen 2, a bit below the Dreame H16 Pro Steam's 28,000Pa, and above the 22,000Pa that Israeli retailers quote for the Tineco. On a wet-dry vacuum the number matters less than on a dry stick vacuum, but you'll notice it with crumbs, sand from the beach bag and hair.

Four modes:

- **AUTO**: the DirTect sensor reads how dirty the floor is and adjusts suction and water
- **MAX**: everything at full power
- **ECO**: the quiet, economical mode for a daily once-over
- **Sponge**: for picking up spilled water and liquids

The sensor works in AUTO and Sponge, and there's a display on the body that shows the dirt level and status.

## Battery: 40 minutes in practice, 60 in the brochure

The battery is 21.6V and 4,000mAh. According to Roborock's support page:

- **AUTO**: about 40 minutes, up to about 300 m²
- **ECO**: about 60 minutes, up to about 460 m²

A full charge takes about four hours. The coverage figures were measured at the lowest suction and at 0.5 metres per second, which is faster than most people walk with a wet-dry vacuum. In a typical four-room Israeli apartment of 100 to 120 m², 40 minutes in AUTO is enough for one full pass with a margin. It won't get you through a house and a balcony and a stubborn stain session with the trigger held down.

That's the main weakness compared with the competition. The Tineco promises up to 90 minutes and the Dreame up to 72. The F25 Pro Turbo is closer to the Bissell's 40.

## Weight and getting under furniture

**4.7 kg** for the vacuum alone. That's lighter than the Roborock F25 Ace Pro (5.1 kg), the Tineco and Xiaomi (6.1 kg) and the Dreame (6.2 to 6.8 kg). Only the Bissell CrossWave Reach Plus Select, at 3.99 kg, is lighter in this group. With the dock it all comes to 6.3 kg.

The **SlideTech 2.0** wheels have two brushless motors that drive each wheel separately, so the machine helps you both forward and back. The head swivels up to 70°: Roborock explains in a footnote that this is the combined rotation of both sides, at least 35° each. That's enough to get around table and chair legs without lifting.

**FlatReach 2.0** lets the body lie flat to 180° and keep sucking. Roborock says it's 4.9 inches high, about 12.4 cm, so it fits under most beds and sofas. A note from the spec sheet: lying flat, the dirty tank holds only 600 ml instead of 720, so empty it before a long stretch under the bed.

## Edges and hair

Roborock says it cleans **0 mm from the wall** on both the left and right sides of the head. In a home with skirting boards and kitchen plinths, that's where the grey line of dirt builds up, so it matters.

Against hair there are **JawScrapers**, teeth that strip hair off the roller. Roborock cites an SGS test with 2,000 strands of 50 cm hair, dry and wet, with zero tangles in all four modes. With long hair or a dog in the house, that's the difference between a roller you rinse and a roller you cut free with scissors.

## Tanks and detergent

- **Clean water**: 740 ml
- **Dirty water**: 720 ml, with a removable module that separates solids from liquid, so you don't pour hair and crumbs down the sink with the water
- **Detergent compartment**: 100 ml, dosed automatically. Roborock says one fill lasts up to 30 days of daily cleaning

Automatic dosing is a small thing that makes a difference. Most people either forget the detergent or pour in too much, and too much leaves a sticky film on porcelain tiles.

## The dock: 95°, but not where you think

Back on the dock, it washes the roller and pipes and then dries them with hot air. Roborock advertises "95°C" in large letters, and here a footnote is worth reading: the 95° is **the temperature in the air duct, not the water temperature**. In other words, the drying air is hot. Roborock doesn't publish the water temperature for the wash.

Quick drying takes **5 minutes**. That's a good figure, in line with Tineco's FlashDry, and it addresses the most common complaint about wet-dry vacuums: a roller that stays damp until tomorrow and starts to smell.

You also get voice alerts (tank full, water low) and app control.

## Against the competition

- **Roborock F25 Ace Pro** (₪1,190, about $358): the pick in our [wet-dry vacuum buying guide](/techsite/en/posts/wet-dry-vacuum-buying-guide-israel/), with strong reviews. It's ₪340 cheaper and a bit heavier, and comes with a one-year warranty. The Pro Turbo adds the trigger, the motorised wheels and automatic detergent. If you have a lot of stubborn stains, the extra money buys you something. If the floor just gets everyday dirt, the Ace Pro is enough.
- **Bissell CrossWave Reach Plus Select** (₪1,490, about $448): lighter at 3.99 kg, a similar 40-minute runtime and a 24-month warranty. No scrub mode like HyperScrub.
- **Dreame H16 Pro Steam** (from ₪2,068, about $622): steam, stronger suction, a longer battery and a three-year warranty. Also 1.5 to 2 kg heavier and ₪500 more. If greasy kitchen floors are your main problem, it's worth the extra.
- **Roborock F25 Ultra Steam Gen 2** (₪3,290, about $989): the big brother, with steam up to 180°C, an edge arm and up to 100 minutes in ECO. Double the price.

## Who it's for

A home with porcelain or ceramic tiles, kids or pets, and everyday dirt with the occasional dried stain. Someone who finds a 6-kg machine too heavy, or carries the vacuum up and down stairs. Someone who wants a wet-dry vacuum from a brand with a presence in Israel without paying for steam.

Less for: large homes where 40 minutes won't cover everything, and kitchens where grease is the main enemy.

## The bottom line

8/10

The Roborock F25 Pro Turbo is a relatively light wet-dry vacuum with a smart idea: instead of steam, a trigger that boosts water, suction and roller speed only where you need it. Add effective scrapers against hair, cleaning right up to the wall on both sides, automatic detergent and five-minute hot-air drying. The battery is short compared with the competition, and there's no steam for heavy grease. At ₪1,529 to ₪1,690 it's a good buy. At the original ₪2,190 it wasn't.
:::

**What I liked**

- HyperScrub: extra scrubbing power on demand, without wasting the battery on every floor tile
- 4.7 kg, lighter than most of the competition
- Motorised wheels and a 70° swivel
- JawScrapers against hair, with an external test behind the claim
- Automatic detergent and a module that separates solids from dirty water
- Five-minute hot-air drying
- The price has dropped by ₪500 to ₪660 since launch

**What I liked less**

- About 40 minutes in AUTO, well below the Tineco and Dreame
- No steam, so thick grease needs more effort
- The "95°" in the ads is air temperature, not water temperature
- Lying flat, the dirty tank shrinks to 600 ml
- The warranty isn't clearly stated on every listing
