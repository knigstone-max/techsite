Sonos has had a rough few years. The disastrous 2024 app update dented its reputation, cost a CEO his job, and the company released no new hardware at all in 2025. The Play, announced in March, is in a sense its reset button: a speaker meant to remind people what Sonos does best. It's also the easiest speaker in its portable range to recommend.

I didn't live with it for a month. I went through the official specs, the terms here in Israel, and the first published testing, including battery measurements both here and abroad. The picture is fairly clear: an excellent speaker for the house and the yard that occasionally comes along on an outing, but not one you toss in your bag every morning.

## The price in Israel

The Play sells here for ₪1,169 (about $365), with a two-year warranty through Bug Multisystem. In the US it's $299 (about ₪957). For comparison, the larger Move 2 costs ₪1,999 (about $625) here, so the Play saves you nearly ₪830 against its big brother. Colors: black, white and sand.

## Design and build

The Play looks like an Era 100 that someone squeezed from the sides. It's tall and oval, 19.2 cm (7.57 in) high, 11.25 cm wide and 7.67 cm deep, in a matte finish. On the back there's a mint-green silicone carry loop you can remove if it's not your thing. The top has touch controls for play, skip, volume and grouping. Around back: a power button, a Bluetooth button, a physical switch that cuts power to the microphones, and a USB-C port.

It's IP67 rated against dust and water, meaning it survives a 1 m dunk for half an hour, and Sonos also says it's drop-resistant. Pool, beach, kitchen sink, all fine.

## The sound: where it wins

Inside are two angled tweeters, one midwoofer and three amplifiers, and according to published testing, two passive radiators added specifically to keep the bass full outdoors, where there are no walls to bounce sound off. The result, by every account, is balanced, neutral sound that doesn't pump one frequency at the expense of the rest. Rock, pop, orchestral scores, it all sounds good.

The angled tweeters give a bit of separation, but it isn't true stereo. Side by side with an Era 100, the Era is louder with a more forward midrange, and the Play loses some clarity in the top quarter of its volume range. In everyday listening, without swapping speakers back and forth, you're unlikely to notice. And in a typical Israeli apartment, 50 to 60 percent volume is roughly where the neighbors start reaching for the phone.

Automatic Trueplay tunes the sound to the room using the built-in mics, no more waving your phone around. The improvement is real but subtle. There's no reason to turn it off.

## WiFi, Bluetooth and everything in between

This is what sets the Play apart from every Bluetooth speaker at this price. At home it's a full WiFi speaker: dozens of streaming services through the Sonos app, Spotify Connect, AirPlay 2, grouping with other Sonos speakers in the house, and stereo pairing with a second Play. When you leave the house, one press switches it to Bluetooth 5.3.

There's a genuinely nice new trick, too. Even without WiFi, you can connect your phone to one Play over Bluetooth, then long-press Play/Pause on up to three more Play or Move 2 speakers to sync them. For a trip with a few families, that's handy.

The USB-C port does three jobs: it charges your phone when it runs flat, it takes a turntable or CD player through a line-in adapter, and it connects to wired Ethernet through another adapter. Both adapters are sold separately.

## Battery and charging

Sonos claims up to 24 hours from the 4,800mAh battery, and here the testing is a bit split. A test in Israel got 14 to 16 hours. Abroad, workdays of eight-plus hours drained only about 30 percent each day, which comes close to the claim. The difference is probably volume and music type. Either way, it's more than enough for a weekend outside, and well beyond the Roam's 10 hours.

The box includes a charging base and a cable, but no power brick. You need a USB-C PD charger of at least 18W, which is irritating on a ₪1,169 speaker. On the plus side, the base works nicely: set the speaker down and it's always charged. And credit where it's due: the battery is replaceable, so the speaker shouldn't turn into e-waste after a few years.

## Portability: the honest answer

1.3 kg (2.87 lb). That's less than half the Move 2's 3 kg, but still about the weight of a big bottle of water. For the yard, the balcony, the kids' room or a picnic you drive to, it's great. For a daily bag, not really. If you need something that slips into a backpack pocket, the Roam 2 fits better.

## What's missing

- **No phone calls.** It has microphones, but you can't take calls through it.
- **No Hebrew.** The Sonos app is in English, and Sonos's voice assistant speaks English. Alexa is supported on paper but not officially available in Israel.
- **The full experience needs WiFi.** Without a network, it's a very good Bluetooth speaker, nothing more.
- **The app.** After 2024, some suspicion is fair. Sonos announced its Sonos 27 platform and an updated app in September, which is the right direction, but trust takes time.

:::score
## The bottom line

8/10

The Sonos Play is the most balanced portable speaker Sonos sells: sound that embarrasses similarly priced rivals, real WiFi, Bluetooth with clever grouping, IP67 and a replaceable battery. At ₪1,169 it isn't cheap, and leaving out a charger and call support feels a bit stingy. But if you already have Sonos at home, or you want one speaker for the living room, balcony and yard, it's one of the most sensible buys in the category.
:::

**What I liked**

- Balanced, rich sound for its size
- Full WiFi, AirPlay 2 and Spotify Connect, plus Bluetooth
- Grouping several speakers even without WiFi
- IP67 and drop resistance
- Charging base, phone charging and a replaceable battery

**What I didn't**

- 1.3 kg: portable mostly between rooms
- No phone calls
- No charger in the box
- No Hebrew in the app or voice assistant
- Pricier in Israel than in the US
