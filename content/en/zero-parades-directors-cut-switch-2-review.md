How do you follow a game that felt one of a kind? ZA/UM, the studio behind Disco Elysium, answers with another cracked city, another lead with a hole in their memory, and more conversations that pull you in when you only meant to check a menu. Zero Parades: Director's Cut launched on November 3, 2026 for Nintendo Switch 2, PS5, Xbox and PC. On Israel's PlayStation Store it costs ₪159 (about $50), with a temporary launch price of ₪143.10. On the US Switch 2 eShop it is $39.99.

After hours in Portofiro, the game's fictional city, I can say this is still ZA/UM at their best on writing and mood. I can also say the feeling that I have been here before never really leaves.

![Zero Parades Director's Cut overview: narrative RPG, Switch 2 and more, ₪159 in Israel](img/posts/zero-parades-overview-en.webp "What the expanded edition adds. Graphic: TechSite")

## A spy named CASCADE

You play Hershel Wilk, a spy whose code name is the cooler-sounding CASCADE. After a botched mission she was put out in the cold, and she wakes in her apartment with a hazy memory and her partner, Pseudopod, passed out in a chair. From there the investigation starts: why is the partner like this, and what do her shadowy handlers, the Operant, actually want.

Portofiro is a city after the Latour-Woolgar Treaty, with the social clock reset to zero. On one side a communist bloc that runs intelligence; on the other, propaganda from a techno-fascist group called La Luz that seeps into every conversation. The game does not hold your hand. Most of the plot comes from talking to odd, precise NPCs, not from cutscenes.

![Zero Parades screenshot: Portofiro streets in a moody isometric view](img/posts/zero-parades-portofiro.webp "The city is the second lead character. Image: ZA/UM")

## Skills, anxiety and dice

This is not a classic combat RPG. You navigate dialogue trees, and passive and active skill checks decide when information opens up. Skills come in three colors: yellow for empathy and personality, pink for physical spycraft, purple for technical aptitude. Alongside them are three meters to balance: fatigue, delirium and anxiety. Bad choices inflate them, and when they fill you lose points in the matching skills.

There is also Conditioning: thought patterns you lock in along the way that buff clusters of abilities. Outfits affect stats, and dramatic encounters are the closest the game gets to action, chains of dice rolls that punish those meters. It adds tension to a game that is basically investigation without gunfights.

Moving through Portofiro feels smoother than I remember from Disco Elysium. There is a map, a scan tool, and less blind pixel hunting. You will still wander without a clear objective until the story clicks again, but the new tools shorten that stretch.

## Director's Cut on Switch 2

The Director's Cut is more than a console port. Per the studio it adds about 40,000 words to the script, new outfits and a Backstage mode that peeks behind the curtain. On Switch 2 you get mouse support, touchscreen and HD Rumble 2. The mouse feels more natural for poking at the environment than the controller alone, especially handheld.

![A dramatic encounter in Zero Parades with characters and dialogue on screen](img/posts/zero-parades-dramatic-encounter.webp "The moments when the dice decide if you are a hero or a casualty. Image: ZA/UM")

The clear downside on Switch 2 is the 30fps target. In crowded streets it shows, especially on a big docked screen. In an isometric, UI-heavy game that matters less than in an action title, but it is hard to ignore when the console can run heavier games at 60. Handheld feels better. Accessibility options are generous: color balancing, contrast and dyslexia settings.

## Who it is for in Israel

₪159 on PS5, or about $40 on the US Switch 2 eShop, is a fair price for a long narrative game. If you loved Disco Elysium and still want heavy political conversations in a collapsing city, you will find exactly that here, sometimes a little too exactly. If you have never touched the genre, this is a gentler entry thanks to the UI improvements and expanded content, and only then should you go back to the earlier game.

## Bottom line

8/10

Zero Parades: Director's Cut is a smart, beautiful narrative RPG packed with characters you will not forget. The writing, music and atmosphere carry long nights of wandering the city. It loses points for sitting too close to Disco Elysium and for 30fps on Switch 2. If you want spy craft of the mind instead of gunfire, it is one of November's stronger recommendations.
:::

**What I liked**

- Sharp writing and oddball characters that stick
- Art, mood and score that keep Portofiro alive
- Skills and status meters that add tension without combat
- Director's Cut extras and mouse support on Switch 2
- Fair price on Israel's PS Store (₪159)
- Broad accessibility options

**What I liked less**

- Sometimes feels like Disco Elysium with new wallpaper
- Less practical spycraft than the marketing hints
- 30fps on Switch 2 shows when docked
- Plenty of wandering before the next objective locks in
- No Hebrew, only English and other subtitle languages
