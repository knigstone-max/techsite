Shoppers browsing several big Israeli electronics sites this week may have spotted something odd. Instead of a product photo, they got a banner reading, in Hebrew, "We also choose to buy only at KSP." It wasn't a glitch. KSP discovered that rival sites were loading images and text directly from its servers, so it replaced those files with an ad for itself. According to Calcalist, the banner showed up on Lior Electric, Bug, Machsanei Hashmal, Shekem Electric, HitechZone, Walla Shops and bdk. Coming in the middle of an election season, the line doubled as a wink at the campaign slogans.

## From prank to lawsuit

KSP didn't stop at the joke. TheMarker reports it sent pre-suit warning letters to more than 200 chains. It has already filed a ₪700K (about $224K) suit against Lior Electric in the Haifa District Court, represented by attorney Assaf Ron.

![The KSP dispute in numbers: warning letters, the claim amount, the number of infringements and an earlier ruling](img/posts/ksp-lawsuit-numbers-en.webp "The dispute in numbers")

The suit lists 14 infringements and asks for ₪100K for each one. That comes to ₪1.4M, but the claim was capped at ₪700K to keep court fees down. KSP has been here before: Electra Consumer Products was previously ordered to pay it ₪50K (about $16K) in a similar case.

## What the rivals say

Bug said the issue covered about 32 products out of tens of thousands on its site, and that the images came from the importer. It says it has since switched to manufacturer images hosted on its own servers, and that its product text matches what appears on Best Buy and in a Lenovo document. Lior Electric, Machsanei Hashmal and HitechZone did not comment.

## Why shoppers should care

It looks like a spat between retailers, but there's a practical angle. When several sites show the exact same photo and description, there's no way to tell who wrote the spec sheet or whether anyone checked it. If what arrives at your door doesn't match the listing, the seller is responsible. Before a big purchase, it's worth checking the specs against the manufacturer's or official importer's site.
