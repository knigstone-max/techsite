Another big round for Israeli cyber. Sentra, which protects data inside large organisations, announced on November 4 that it has raised $150 million, about ₪474 million. The round, which Sentra calls its Series C, is led by Capital One Ventures, the US bank's investment arm, and a Capital One representative is joining the board. Existing investors Bessemer, Zeev Ventures, Standard Investments, Munich Re Ventures and Key1 Capital are in too.

That brings Sentra's total funding to more than $250 million (about ₪789 million). It hasn't disclosed a valuation, but reports say it has tripled since the last round.

## What Sentra does

Picture a big company. Its data is spread across the cloud, SaaS apps, data warehouses and servers in the office. Some of it is sensitive, and it isn't always clear who can get to it. Now add AI agents, each of which is effectively a new user reading data nonstop at machine speed. That's the problem Sentra is going after.

Its platform finds sensitive data, classifies it, and shows which employees, service accounts and AI agents can reach it, including inherited permissions nobody noticed. If an agent starts touching data in an unusual way, it raises an alert and can revoke the access. It rolls several categories that are usually sold separately into one product: data security posture management (DSPM), data detection and response (DDR), data access governance (DAG) and data loss prevention (DLP).

Sentra makes a point of the fact that customer data never leaves the customer's environment. Scanning happens in place, without copying anything to Sentra's servers, which matters a lot to banks and other regulated businesses.

CEO Yoav Regev said in the announcement that "AI agents are now the busiest consumers of enterprise data," and that many inherit access to data that hasn't been fully classified or governed. One customer named in the release is Lyft, whose CISO, Chaim Sanders, said the company needs to know what data its agents can reach before it turns them on.

## Who's behind it

Sentra was founded in 2021 by four people: CEO Yoav Regev, who used to head Unit 8200's cyber center; president Asaf Kochan, a former 8200 commander; CTO Ron Reiter, who came from Oracle; and VP of product Yair Cohen, who worked at Microsoft and Datadog. It's headquartered in both New York and Tel Aviv.

It has 130 employees today, 65 of them in Tel Aviv, and about 100 large customers in the US and Europe. Revenue is in the tens of millions of dollars and grew more than 300% over the past year.

## What's next

Sentra says the money will go into product development, doubling headcount in Israel and the US, a new London office, and US federal sales starting in the second half of 2027. It's aiming for $100 million in sales and isn't ruling out buying another company, either in data loss prevention or in identity management.

## Why it matters

Securing the data itself, not just the network around it, has become a crowded space. It includes another big Israeli player, Cyera, alongside veterans like Varonis. Both Israeli companies have grown fast, which shows how worried large organisations are about putting AI agents to work. It's also good news locally: a company planning to double its Tel Aviv team.
