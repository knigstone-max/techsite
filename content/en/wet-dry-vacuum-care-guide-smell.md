The wet-dry vacuum has changed how a lot of Israeli homes get cleaned. Tiled floors, plenty of dust, sand blowing in from the balcony: it's exactly what these machines are good at. But anyone who owns one knows the moment you open the cupboard and get hit by the smell of a mop left in a bucket.

The good news: it's almost never a fault. It's maintenance. We went to the sources, the **Roborock F25** user manual and **Tineco**'s FAQ, and sorted it by how often you need to do it. Models differ, but the principles hold for nearly any wet-dry vacuum with a dirty-water tank and a dock.

![Wet-dry vacuum care schedule: after every clean, when hair builds up, monthly, every three months, and what never goes in the tank](img/posts/wet-dry-vacuum-care-schedule-en.webp "What to do and when. Graphic: TechSite")

## Why it smells in the first place

Tineco puts it plainly: a bad smell usually comes from damp residue in the tank, the brush or its cover. Dirty water with hair and crumbs, sitting for a few days in a closed cupboard, is the whole story. So the fix is simple too: clean those parts and let them dry completely.

One more thing Tineco asks for explicitly: don't mask the smell with scented additives. That just hides the problem.

## After every clean

1. **Dock it and run self-cleaning.** On most models drying starts automatically afterwards. Don't skip it when you're in a hurry.
2. **Empty the dirty-water tank.** Roborock's F25 manual says to empty it promptly after self-cleaning and drying to prevent odours. Dirty water left in the tank until next time is the number one cause of the smell.
3. **Rinse the tank** and leave it open for a moment to dry before you put it back.

## When hair and dirt build up

Roborock calls this "as needed", and in a home with long hair or a pet that can mean every few days.

- **The roller:** take it out, clear the hair with the maintenance brush from the box, and rinse it in clean water.
- **The dirty-water channel:** brush out the roller slot and the channel. Roborock warns against rinsing the cleaner head directly under running water.
- **If it leaves streaks** or stops picking up water: Roborock says to check the channel for clogs and make sure the filter isn't blocked or wet.

## At least once a month: the filter

Roborock's manual says to rinse the filter **at least once a month** under the tap, then let it dry for **at least 24 hours** before putting it back. That's a long wait if you mop daily. If you have a spare filter, this is exactly what it's for.

One more note from the manual: don't touch the filter surface with your hands or a brush.

## Every three months

- **A new roller.** In the F25's maintenance table Roborock recommends replacing the roller every three months, adding that it depends on use. If the fibres look worn or it starts leaving streaks, it's time.
- **If it lives in a cupboard.** Roborock asks you to fully charge it at least once every three months so the battery doesn't run completely flat.

## What never goes in the tank

Tineco is very clear here: **no vinegar, no dish soap, no scented oils and no descaler**, unless the maker explicitly approves it. Use the maker's own cleaning solution, or plain water if the manual allows it. And **no boiling water**, even on a model with steam or hot water. That doesn't mean the tank can take any temperature.

With Israel's hard water, the urge to pour in a splash of vinegar against limescale is strong. Don't, without checking your model's manual first. If limescale is a problem, ask the importer what you're allowed to use.

## The bottom line

Most problems are solved in two minutes after each clean: self-clean, dry, empty the tank. The filter once a month, the roller once a quarter. And if you're still choosing a machine, a dock that dries with hot air does a lot of this work for you.
