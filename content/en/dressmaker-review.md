Some games teach you to aim better. Others teach you what a juliet sleeve is. Dressmaker is the second kind, and I mean that as a compliment.

It came out on September 21, but it's only now, with the late-November sales, that it's landing on a lot of players' radar. It's made by Cozy Lives, a new label from South African studio Free Lives. Yes, the people behind Broforce, GORN and Anger Foot. It's a bit like finding out the director of Fast & Furious made a film about baking bread.

## How it started

The story behind the game is part of its charm. Sabrina Becker wanted to be a costume designer until Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, a connective tissue disorder, put a stop to it. Her partner Jonathan Hau-Yoon, who worked on Fortnite and Terra Nil, wanted to give her a way to keep doing the craft. They built a small prototype, it blew up online before there was a real game, and Free Lives had to admit a game about dresses could be a hit. According to the publisher, the prototype passed 100,000 downloads, and the game had over 400,000 Steam wishlists before launch.

It shows. Becker is credited as the team's dressmaking expert, and throughout the game you can feel that someone who has actually made dresses decided what matters.

## A day at the shop

You're the new dressmaker in town, running the only clothes shop around. Customers arrive with requests that are sometimes precise and sometimes contradictory: a respectable funeral dress, but with something to catch the eye. Workwear for a farmer who loves bold colours. Something daring that still works for daytime.

![A piece of green fabric feeding through an old black sewing machine, with a dotted line showing where to stitch](img/posts/dressmaker-sewing-machine.webp "The sewing machine. The most stressful part of the game. Image: Cozy Lives / Free Lives")

Each order runs through several stages, and each is a little game of its own:

- **The sketchbook.** Draw the dress in pencil, pin fabric swatches and add appliqué. This is the creative bit, where you translate the customer's personality into colours, shapes and textures.
- **Shopping.** Cotton, linen, wool, silk and velvet, each at its own price per metre. Velvet workwear is a waste of money and a cotton ball gown is an insult. This is where the business side comes in: every choice adds to or eats into your profit.
- **Cutting.** Lay out the pattern pieces on the fabric and cut. What I loved: the game cares about grain. Cut along it or on the bias, and that's what shows up in the finished dress.
- **Sewing.** Feed the fabric through an old machine along a dotted line and try to stay on it. Straight seams are easy. Curves, sleeves and a mermaid skirt test your nerves, even with the machine speed turned down.
- **Finishing and delivery.** Buttons, bows, lace, brooches. Photograph the dress on the mannequin and hand it over.

Then comes the moment. The customer looks, and sometimes she just doesn't like it. After half an hour on a dress, a rejection over a missing brooch genuinely stings. But when it works and the five-star rating pops up, it's one of the most satisfying moments I've had in a game this year.

## More of a management game than it looks

Under the pastels there's a real business sim. You earn money and reputation, unlock new patterns and accessories, and you can make your own dresses and put them in the shop window to sell off the rack. So once the orders are done, you can sew whatever you like, which leaves room for creativity without a customer looking over your shoulder.

![Pattern pieces of different shapes laid out on floral fabric with scissors, with a quality score for each piece](img/posts/dressmaker-pattern-cutting.webp "Laying out the pattern. Every piece gets a quality score. Image: Cozy Lives / Free Lives")

The town's social calendar sets the pace: events approach, and each one brings a wave of orders. Customers get more demanding as you go, and a few have personalities you'll remember.

And there's a cat. You can pet it. It says so on the official page, and it's the most important feature in the game.

## What didn't work as well

**Repetition.** After a few hours, cutting and sewing your 18th sleeve of the day starts to feel like work. New patterns help, but if you want fast progression you'll get bored.

**Rejections that sting too much.** When a customer turns down a dress, it isn't always clear why. Wrong keywords? Wrong colour? A missing accessory? A slightly clearer hint would save some frustration.

**No Hebrew, no consoles.** The game is in English, Simplified Chinese and Japanese only, and there's a fair amount of text and sewing vocabulary. If your English is basic, expect to look up words like bodice and pleats. It's also PC-only: Windows and Mac, no Switch or PlayStation version, even though it feels made for a handheld on the sofa.

## Price and requirements

On the Israeli Steam store it costs **₪54.49 (about $16.55)**, and right now, 20% off, **₪43.59 (about $13.24)**. The US price is $14.99 (about ₪49), so Israeli players pay roughly 10% more. Not dramatic, but worth knowing.

Requirements are modest: Windows 10 or later, a Core i5 or Ryzen CPU, 8GB of RAM, Intel UHD 630-class graphics and 2GB of space. Almost any laptop from the last few years will run it.

## Who it's for

Anyone who loved Stardew Valley for the daily loop rather than the combat. Anyone who sews, wants to sew, or never understood why a good dress costs so much. It's also a great game to show someone who doesn't play games at all. No reflexes, no dying, no time pressure, apart from the machine.

If you want action, look elsewhere. If you want a Hanukkah gift for a PC player, it's one of the cheapest and most original you'll find.

:::score
## The bottom line

8.5/10

Dressmaker takes a real craft and turns it into a game without losing respect for it. The sketchbook, the fabric choices, cutting on the grain and the nerve-racking machine build a calming, satisfying loop, with a business layer that gives every decision weight. It loses points for repetition after a few hours, rejections that aren't always explained, and the lack of Hebrew or a console version. For under ₪55, it's one of the most original cosy games of the year.
:::

**What I liked**

- Real dressmaking: patterns, grain, fabric types
- The sketchbook gives genuine creative freedom
- A business layer that makes every choice matter
- The sewing machine, tenser than most action-game bosses
- A moving story behind the game
- Low price and modest system requirements

**What I liked less**

- Gets repetitive after a few hours
- Not always clear why a customer rejected a dress
- No Hebrew, and lots of sewing terms in English
- PC only, no console version
- A little pricier on Israeli Steam
