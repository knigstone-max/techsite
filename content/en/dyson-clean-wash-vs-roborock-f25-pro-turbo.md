Most wet-dry vacuums on the market weigh 6 kg or more. If you live in a duplex, carry the vacuum up the stairs or just get tired of pushing a heavy machine around, you want something lighter. Over the past month we reviewed two wet-dry cleaners that come in under 5 kg: the [Dyson Clean+Wash Hygiene](/techsite/en/posts/dyson-clean-wash-hygiene-review-israel/) and the [Roborock F25 Pro Turbo](/techsite/en/posts/roborock-f25-pro-turbo-review-israel/). Both cost less than ₪2,000, and they're built around completely opposite ideas.

So instead of jumping between two long reviews, here they are side by side, at today's prices.

![Comparison table: Dyson Clean+Wash Hygiene vs Roborock F25 Pro Turbo, with Israeli price, cleaning method, weight, battery, tanks, drying and warranty](img/posts/dyson-vs-roborock-table-en.webp "The two machines head to head. Graphic: TechSite")

## The big difference: suction or no suction

**Dyson dropped suction.** In the Clean+Wash Hygiene, the motor spins a microfibre roller with 84,000 filaments. With every turn the roller is wetted with clean water, and the dirt and dirty water are scraped off into the head itself: a 0.52-litre tank for liquid and a separate tray for dry debris. Nothing travels up a pipe, so there are no pipes to clog and no filter to go mouldy. It's Dyson's answer to the best-known problem with wet-dry vacuums: the smell.

**Roborock went for strong suction.** The F25 Pro Turbo pulls 25,000Pa, washes with a roller fed with clean water, and sucks the dirty water up into a 0.72-litre tank in the body. The tank has a module that separates solids from liquid, so you don't pour hair and crumbs down the sink with the water.

What does that mean in practice? On crumbs, hair and liquid spills, both methods work. The question is heavy, coarse dirt: beach sand spilled from a bag, rice on the floor, soil from a plant pot. Here real suction is an advantage, and Dyson itself doesn't show how its machine copes with that. If your home sees a lot of sand, the Roborock is the safer choice.

## Weight and handling

The Dyson weighs **3.82 kg**, the Roborock **4.7 kg**. A kilo doesn't sound like much, but you feel it carrying the vacuum up a floor.

On the other hand, the Roborock has driven wheels that help push it forward and pull it back, and a head that swivels up to 70 degrees (35 each way, as Roborock explains in a footnote). On the floor, the weight gap shrinks. The real difference is when you lift it.

Both lie flat under furniture: the Dyson to 113 mm, the Roborock to about 12.4 cm. One note on the Roborock: lying flat, its dirty tank holds only 600 ml instead of 720.

## Battery

The Dyson wins clearly here: **up to 70 minutes**, and Dyson says that covers up to 350 m². It doesn't say which mode, so expect less on max.

The Roborock gives **about 40 minutes in AUTO** and around 60 in ECO, per Roborock's support page. In a typical four-room apartment of 100 to 120 m², 40 minutes is enough for one full pass with a margin. It won't cover a big house with a balcony and a few stubborn stains that need the trigger.

## Roborock's trigger

HyperScrub is the feature the Dyson doesn't have. Pull a trigger on the handle and the machine boosts water, suction and roller speed all at once, up to 550 rpm, for as long as your finger stays down. It's exactly what a dried sauce stain by the hob needs, without burning battery on every tile. Add automatic detergent dosing (a 100 ml compartment Roborock says lasts a month) and you get a machine with more tools.

## The dock and the smell

Both dry with hot air, which is what matters most against odour. Dyson states 85°C, Roborock a 5-minute quick dry. The "95°C" Roborock advertises in big letters is, according to its footnote, the air temperature in the duct, not the water temperature.

Dyson's edge here is structural: no pipes and no filter means fewer places for water to sit. Still, Dyson recommends emptying both tanks and the debris tray after every clean, and that's good advice for any wet-dry vacuum.

## Price and warranty

- **Dyson Clean+Wash Hygiene:** **₪1,979 (about $586)** on Dyson's Israeli site, down from ₪2,199 (about $651). When we reviewed it, retailers sold it with a two-year importer warranty
- **Roborock F25 Pro Turbo:** **₪1,585 to ₪1,690 (about $469 to $500)** on Zap as an official import, and ₪1,599 (about $473) at GoMobile. Most Zap sellers list a one-year warranty, and a few offer more. Ask before you pay

Today's gap is about ₪300 to ₪400 in the Roborock's favour.

## One more thing: what owners say

On Dyson's Irish site, the Clean+Wash Hygiene has an average of 3.7 out of 5 from 804 ratings, and only 49% of reviewers say they'd recommend it to a friend. That's a weak number for a Dyson product, and some positive reviewers argue the negative ones come from people not using it properly. Either way, it's a reason to try it in a shop if you can, and to check the return policy.

## So which one?

**Get the Dyson if:** you have hard floors throughout (tiles, porcelain, laminate), lots of small everyday mess, stairs or a back that doesn't love 5 kg, and especially if a wet-dry vacuum has already put you off with its smell. The long battery and low weight are its strengths.

**Get the Roborock if:** you want one tool that also handles sand, rice and coarse dirt, you have stubborn kitchen stains the trigger can deal with, and you'd rather pay less. It gives you more features for ₪300 to ₪400 less, at the cost of a shorter battery.
