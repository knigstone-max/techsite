Tower Semiconductor, the chip foundry based in Migdal HaEmek, reported third-quarter results on Monday, and almost every line was a record. Revenue came to $526 million (about ₪1.65 billion), up 33% from $396 million a year earlier. Analysts had expected $522 million.

Net profit, excluding one-off items, was $120 million (about ₪376 million), more than double the $54 million of a year ago. Gross profit jumped to $179 million and operating profit to $127 million, two and a half times last year's figure.

## The outlook: $2 billion a year

Tower expects fourth-quarter revenue of $600 million (about ₪1.88 billion), give or take 5%. That would be 36% growth year on year, against analyst expectations of $580 million. If it lands, the company will finish 2026 with about $2 billion (about ₪6.3 billion) in revenue.

"We are entering the strongest growth phase in our history," CEO Russell Ellwanger said in the results release. He added that Tower will close the year at an annualized revenue run rate of $2.4 billion.

## What's driving it

The main engine is SiPho, or silicon photonics: chips that move data with light, mostly used for fast links inside data centres, AI ones included. Tower is aiming for a $1.1 billion annual run rate from SiPho alone in the fourth quarter, five times what the business brought in across all of 2025.

To keep up, it's expanding 300mm wafer production in Japan, and Ellwanger says that work is ahead of schedule. Customers are also extending contracts with Tower through 2029.

## 150 jobs in Israel

The most tangible news for Israel came after the numbers. Ellwanger told Globes that Tower is hiring 150 new employees in Israel, for operator and technician roles. "We wouldn't be hiring employees if we weren't so confident about expanding production capacity," he said. About 1,500 people already work at the Migdal HaEmek plant.

## And the stock?

The numbers didn't stop the swings. On Friday, before the report, the stock closed down 11.41%, leaving a market cap of $18.9 billion (about ₪59 billion). On Monday it opened 6.5% lower. Shareholders will probably be watching the Japan buildout and the SiPho ramp over the next few quarters.
