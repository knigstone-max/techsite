Hamilton, Xiaomi's official importer in Israel, announced a big vacuum launch yesterday: three product lines and seven models priced between ₪899 and ₪4,690. The headliner is the Robot Vacuum 6 Max, the most advanced robot Xiaomi sells here, joined by a cordless wet-dry vacuum with steam and three regular stick vacuums.

![All prices for Xiaomi's new vacuums in Israel, in shekels and dollars, with the main feature of each model](img/posts/xiaomi-vacuums-israel-prices-en.webp "Seven models, seven prices. Graphic: TechSite")

## Robot Vacuum 6 Max: a roller instead of mop pads

The interesting part of the flagship is how it mops. Instead of two spinning pads, it uses a roller that gets a steady feed of clean water while it works and cleans itself as it goes. The practical upshot is that it shouldn't drag kitchen grime into the living room, which anyone who has owned a cheap mopping robot will recognise.

Alongside the roller there are three arms: the roller itself extends into corners, there's a side brush, and a third arm carries a round mop pad for skirting boards and chair legs. Suction is rated at 35,000Pa.

![A black robot vacuum climbing a low step on mechanical legs, with a 6 cm marker](img/posts/xiaomi-robot-vacuum-6-max-climb.webp "The legs that lift it over ledges up to 6 cm. Image: Xiaomi")

## Thresholds, sofas and rugs

Israeli apartments are full of thresholds between rooms and aluminium balcony door tracks, and that's where a lot of robots get stuck. The 6 Max has two mechanical legs that lift it over obstacles up to 6 cm high. Its lidar turret retracts when needed, so it can also get under low sofas and beds.

According to the importer, three AI cameras recognise 280 kinds of objects and furniture and 47 kinds of stains, so it can steer around cables and socks and pick a cleaning method for each mess. The dock washes the roller with 85°C water, dries it with warm air and empties the dustbin into a bag. Xiaomi's spec page lists drying at up to 50°C and a 6,400mAh battery.

Price: ₪4,690 (about $1,470).

## The two cheaper robots

- **Robot Vacuum 6 Pro**, ₪3,990 (about $1,250): 30,000Pa suction, two arms for corners, climbs obstacles up to 4 cm.
- **Robot Vacuum 6**, ₪3,190 (about $1,000): also 30,000Pa, with an AI camera that recognises more than 220 object types.

Both use laser navigation and mapping, and the Xiaomi Home app handles virtual walls, no-go zones and multi-floor maps. If your flat doesn't have tall thresholds, the base model saves you ₪1,500 over the Max.

## A wet-dry vacuum with steam

The Mijia Wet Dry Vacuum 5 Pro vacuums, mops and steams at 160°C in a single pass. Suction is 26,000Pa, the roller spins at 400 rpm, and the body lies flat to 180 degrees to reach under sofas and cabinets. When you're done it rinses itself with hot water and dries with warm air. Runtime is up to 40 minutes, and it costs ₪2,490 (about $780).

Steam mostly helps with sticky kitchen spills. If you have wooden floors that don't like heat and moisture, check what the flooring maker recommends first.

## Three stick vacuums

The Mijia Vacuum Cleaner 4 line has five-stage filtration, a motorised brush designed not to tangle with hair, and a light that shows dust on the floor:

- **4 Pro**: 280AW, removable battery, up to 90 minutes, ₪1,590 (about $498)
- **4**: 230AW, ₪1,290 (about $404)
- **4C**: 170AW, ₪899 (about $282)

## Warranty and service

Every model comes with a two-year importer warranty. Buy the Robot Vacuum 6 Max or the Wet Dry Vacuum 5 Pro by December 31, 2026, and you also get a year of home pickup service, where they collect the device for repair and bring it back. The products are sold at Xiaomi stores, on Xiaomi Israel's website and through authorised retailers.
