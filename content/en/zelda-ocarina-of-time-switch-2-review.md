Ask anyone who played video games in the late '90s about the best game ever made, and Ocarina of Time will come up fast. The 1998 Nintendo 64 game set the rules for 3D action-adventure: locking on to enemies, a camera that follows the action, a big world that opens up as you go. Tomorrow, November 5, Nintendo releases a full remake exclusively for Switch 2.

This isn't a port with sharper textures. Nintendo has rebuilt the visuals, recorded voices for the characters, changed the controls and added a quest log. The game isn't out yet, so this review is based on what Nintendo has shown and announced, not on hours of play. Even so, it's clear what kind of package this is.

## Price and where to buy it in Israel

In the US, the game costs $59.99 (about ₪189) digitally and $69.99 (about ₪221) on a cartridge. The first print run of the physical edition comes in packaging with a textured foil finish, nice if you're a collector.

In Israel, KSP is taking pre-orders for 315 ₪ (about $100), and Bug and Arcadia list it at 319 ₪ (about $101). It's from the official importer, rated 12+, and single-player only.

## How it looks and sounds

The visuals have been completely redone. Hyrule Field, Kakariko Village and Death Mountain look like places now, not a collection of grey polygons. Castle Town, which was a fairly small square in the original, has been expanded with more detail. Loading is faster, and performance is smoother.

The sound got the same treatment. The original tunes, which many of us can still hum, have been re-recorded with a symphony orchestra, and the sound effects have been refreshed. The biggest change is that the characters speak. The cutscenes are fully voiced and expanded, with more dialogue than before. Purists may miss the silent text boxes, but for a story about growing up, losing your childhood and coming back seven years later, it adds a lot.

![Link riding Epona across a green field, with Death Mountain behind him](img/posts/zelda-ocarina-of-time-switch-2-epona.webp "Riding Epona across Hyrule Field. Image: Nintendo")

## Controls that finally make sense

Here's the change that matters most if you've ever tried to go back to the original. Link now has **a jump button**, a first for a traditional 3D Zelda. In 1998, Link jumped automatically when he reached a ledge, which worked but sometimes felt like the game was deciding for you. There's also a dash, on land by holding A, and in water with X.

The camera is now on the right stick, so you can look around freely. Bows and the slingshot support gyro aiming, which is much more precise than the stick. And swapping items takes a single button, instead of opening a menu every few seconds. Anyone who remembers the Water Temple, where you had to put the Iron Boots on and take them off again and again, will understand why that's a big deal.

## The world and the guidance

The day-night cycle now runs everywhere, including in towns, where time used to stand still. There's also a new quest log called Threads of Time. It's a tapestry that fills in as you progress, showing which threads of the story you've closed and what's still open.

That's good news for players who come back after a few days and can't remember where they were going. The original was a game where you could get stuck for an hour, mostly because you'd forgotten what an old man in a village said three dungeons ago. The quest log should solve that without leading you by the hand.

![Link playing the ocarina in front of Princess Zelda, with musical notes in the air](img/posts/zelda-ocarina-of-time-switch-2-ocarina.webp "Playing Zelda's Lullaby. Image: Nintendo")

## The ocarina, for real this time

The ocarina is at the heart of the game, and Nintendo had fun with it. Besides playing songs with the buttons, you can hum them into the Switch 2 microphone, or play a real ocarina in front of the console. Nintendo also sells a playable ocarina accessory, and HORI makes an electric ocarina with a free-play mode and an auto-play mode. It's a gimmick, but for anyone who grew up on Zelda's Lullaby and the Song of Storms, it's a pretty charming one.

There are extras around the game too. Young Link and Young Zelda amiibo are coming in 2027 and unlock retro-style masks. The Zelda Notes app adds adventure tracking, a map, challenges where you find the spot shown in a hint image, a virtual ocarina and a daily quiz with more than 2,000 questions.

## The dungeons

The dungeons were always the heart of Ocarina of Time. Dodongo's Cavern, the Forest Temple and the Shadow Temple are among the best-designed in the series, and Nintendo's marketing puts the new visuals, controls and guidance front and centre rather than promising new dungeons. With a free camera and a jump button, though, going back through them should feel noticeably different.

![Link facing King Dodongo, a huge reptile, in a lava-filled cave](img/posts/zelda-ocarina-of-time-switch-2-king-dodongo.webp "The King Dodongo fight in Dodongo's Cavern. Image: Nintendo")

## Who it's for

If you've never played Ocarina of Time, this is clearly the best way to do it. It's a full, long adventure, and it doesn't feel like homework the way the original sometimes does today.

If you played it 25 years ago, the question is mostly whether you want the same adventure again in better shape. It's the same story. The controls, the presentation and the quality-of-life features are what's new. For a lot of fans, that's exactly the remake they've been waiting for.

And if you don't have a Switch 2 yet, this is the first big exclusive that might push you to buy one.

:::score
## The bottom line

9/10

Nintendo has taken one of the most important games ever made and set out to fix much of what aged badly, while keeping the adventure people remember. A jump button, a free camera, quick item swapping and a quest log make it much more comfortable, and the new visuals, orchestral music and voice acting give it new life. It loses a point for the price of around ₪315 for a story many players already know, and because some extras depend on accessories and amiibo you'll have to buy separately.
:::

**What I liked**

- A full visual rebuild that respects the original
- Jump button, free camera and quick item swapping
- Orchestral music and voiced cutscenes
- A quest log that saves you from getting stuck
- Playing the ocarina with your voice or a real ocarina

**What I didn't**

- Veterans pay full price for a story they already know
- Around ₪315 in Israel, on the high side
- Some extras depend on accessories and amiibo
- Single-player only
