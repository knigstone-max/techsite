Most games that brag about destructible environments treat it as decoration. A building falls, dust flies, and you keep shooting like before. In VOID/BREAKER destruction is how you fight. Bring a structure down on a group of robots, they get pinned under the rubble, a green bar pops up over them, and that's your moment to finish them off. The whole trick is scanning the arena for the next thing you can knock over.

The game comes from a single developer, Daniel Stubbington, working as Stubby Games. In 2022 he released The Entropy Centre, a puzzle game that drew plenty of Portal comparisons. This time he switched genres completely: a first-person shooter built as a roguelite, so each death sends you back to the start, with permanent progress carrying over between runs. It spent time in Steam Early Access from August 2025 and launched in full on November 4 on PS5, Xbox Series X|S and PC, published by Playstack.

![VOID/BREAKER at a glance: ₪107 on the Israeli PS Store, ₪106.49 on Steam or ₪63.89 during the launch sale, and included in Game Pass](img/posts/void-breaker-overview-en.webp "What you get, and for how much. Graphic: TechSite")

## The setup: stuck in a simulation

You're the prisoner of a hostile AI that forces you to fight, die and start over inside an endless simulation. Thousands of runners before you tried to reach the end and failed. Another AI's voice in your head asks for help: just do what the simulation wants, and it'll use the data you collect to find a way out, for you and for itself.

The dry humor, the voice in your ear and the sense that something bigger is going on behind the curtain all feel very Portal, and very much like Stubbington's previous game. It isn't a story you'll remember for years, but it gives the die-and-repeat structure a good reason to exist and holds a few surprises worth finding yourself.

## How it plays

Each run moves through arenas set in shifting digital worlds: strange empty spaces, beaches, snowy cliffs and more. Every arena has a few prominent white structures plus explosives you can throw. Grenades and charges bring the structures down, and any robot caught underneath gets stunned. You'll know by the green bar above it. That's when you shoot.

![A white arena with a collapsing structure, debris in the air and robots marked with red target circles, seen in first person holding a rifle](img/posts/void-breaker-arena-collapse.webp "Knock it down first, shoot second. Image: Playstack / Stubby Games")

The formula, break then kill, is simple, but the game keeps adding exceptions. Some enemies are tougher, some types need a different approach, and elite robots put real pressure on you. Movement is quick, and the feel is closer to an old-school fast shooter than a tactical one.

## One gun, endless mods

Here's the game's most interesting decision: you pick a single weapon per run. Pistol, shotgun, machine gun, sniper rifle, SMG and others. The gun is only the starting point, though. Through the run you collect mods from chests and enemies, and each one changes something: bullets set things on fire, electrify or leave acid behind, every shot launches a missile, rounds bounce off walls and split. Other mods tweak damage, recoil, accuracy and fire rate.

![A mod pickup screen showing Chain Reaction, which makes projectiles jump to the nearest enemy, over a first-person view with a rifle](img/posts/void-breaker-chain-reaction-mod.webp "Every mod reshapes the gun. Here, bullets jump between enemies. Image: Playstack / Stubby Games")

There's also a stacking system where a well-placed mod can double or triple the effect of the mods next to it. That adds a small puzzle layer to a shooter, and it's exactly where you can see the hand of the person who made The Entropy Centre. A plain machine gun can end the run as a demolition machine that fills the screen with explosions. The game doesn't try to rein that in. If anything, it lets you break it.

As you progress you unlock schematics that widen the pool of mods that can drop. Between runs you upgrade stats, research new mods, buy weapons, open shortcuts and find terminals with more pieces of the story. That's standard for the genre, with no big surprises, but it works.

## A map with decisions

Between arenas there's a map with branching paths. Along the way you can find a shop for buying mods, mysterious obelisks offering a risk-versus-reward gamble, optional trials and odd anomalies that send you somewhere else. At each junction the question is whether to take the dangerous path with the bigger payoff or play it safe.

![A glowing green route map on a black background, with nodes for fights, shops and an obelisk](img/posts/void-breaker-route-map.webp "At every junction: risk or safety. Image: Playstack / Stubby Games")

I liked these breaks. The fights are very intense, and a moment to stop, plan and buy a new mod gives you room to breathe before the next arena.

## What worked less

Bosses are the weak spot. They're mostly fairly simple bullet-hell arenas, and they're not where most runs end. Most runs end because of the mess you made yourself: once you've flattened every structure in an arena, it's hard to see what's going on, easy to get stuck in debris, and enemies come from every side.

That's the game's broader problem. Once your build clicks, the screen fills with explosions, shrapnel, electricity and fire, and the fight becomes hard to read. If you're sensitive to visual overload, you'll tire quickly. And if you want a roguelite with deep systems outside combat, there's less here than in something like Hades.

## How long, and how much

The main campaign reaches the credits in around 12 hours, followed by an endgame with modifiers that make each run even more chaotic. That's a good length for a roguelite: long enough to keep you busy, short enough not to feel repetitive.

On the Israeli PS Store the game costs ₪107 (about $34). On Steam in Israel the regular price is ₪106.49, and at the time of writing a 40% launch sale brings it to ₪63.89 (about $20). In the US it lists at $29.99 (about ₪96). Game Pass subscribers get it at no extra cost, and PS Plus Deluxe members can try two full hours. Steam also has a free demo, and your progress carries over to the full game. There's an enhanced PS5 Pro version, and the DualSense rumble makes every shot land. There's no Hebrew, but there's very little text, so it barely matters.

:::score
## The bottom line

8/10

VOID/BREAKER is one of the most fun shooters of the year, and easily the most impressive one built by a single person. Destruction is a real part of combat, the mod system produces wild builds, and roughly 12 hours plus an endgame keeps the pace up. It loses points for simple bosses, visual overload that sometimes hides the fight, and little innovation outside the arenas. At ₪64 on sale on Steam, or through Game Pass, it's an easy recommendation.
:::

**What I liked**

- Destruction that's part of combat, not just an effect
- One gun, and mods that turn it into something else entirely
- A map with decisions, shops and obelisks
- Portal-style humor
- Free Steam demo, and included in Game Pass

**What I liked less**

- Bosses are too simple
- Visual overload that's hard to read at peak moments
- Standard between-run progression
- No Hebrew
