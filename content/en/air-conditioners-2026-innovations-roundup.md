You don't replace an air conditioner every year, so it's easy to miss what changed. But 2026 was an interesting year for the most boring appliance in the house. Makers stopped competing only on BTU and energy labels and started adding sensors, apps that show consumption, and modes that deal with humidity rather than just temperature. Meanwhile, Europe is starting a move to a completely different refrigerant.

This is our sixth year-end roundup, after [washers and dryers](/techsite/en/posts/washers-dryers-2026-innovations-roundup/), [fridges](/techsite/en/posts/fridges-2026-innovations-roundup/), [floor cleaning](/techsite/en/posts/floor-cleaning-2026-innovations-roundup/), [kitchen](/techsite/en/posts/kitchen-appliances-2026-innovations-roundup/) and [TVs](/techsite/en/posts/tvs-2026-innovations-roundup/). We went through the year's announcements, and here's what's actually new.

![Six changes in air conditioners in 2026: presence sensors, two airflow types, power use in the app, drying without chilling, R290 refrigerant and a black design](img/posts/ac-2026-trends-en.webp "What changed this year. Graphic: TechSite")

## 1. The AC knows you're in the room

This is the biggest shift of the year. In February, Samsung showed the **Bespoke AI WindFree Pro** in the US, a mini-split with radar sensing that detects whether someone is in the room, whether they're moving and how active they are. It adjusts airflow direction and strength to match. It cools fast first, then switches to WindFree, where air comes out through thousands of tiny holes instead of a direct blast.

You can already buy the idea in Israel. Before the summer Tadiran launched its [AIR ONE series](/techsite/en/posts/tadiran-air-one-2026/) with a built-in motion sensor: the air can follow you, or avoid you while still cooling the room. Leave the room with it running and after a few minutes it drops to ECO. That's exactly the feature for anyone who keeps finding the AC on in an empty kids' room.

## 2. Two kinds of airflow in one unit

In June LG launched **DUALCOOL AI** in the Middle East with what it calls REAL DUAL: two vanes that work separately. One sends a direct stream that LG says reaches up to 22 metres, for cooling a big room quickly. The other gives a soft, indirect flow, Soft Air, so you're not sitting in a cold draft. Samsung chases the same goal a different way, with WindFree.

In practice, it settles the classic living-room argument between the person who wants it cold right now and the person who refuses to have air blowing on their neck.

## 3. Power use, in the app

Until recently an AC app was mostly a remote control on your phone. This year it started showing consumption. DUALCOOL AI has **AI kW Manager** in the ThinQ app for monitoring and managing power use. Samsung, in the range it launched in India in January, claims up to 30% savings with **AI Energy Mode**, which adjusts the compressor and temperature based on usage patterns. That's Samsung's figure, not an independent measurement.

Why this matters right now in Israel: households on the time-of-use tariff pay far more between 17:00 and 22:00 from December 1, every day of the week. We covered that in our [winter tariff guide](/techsite/en/posts/winter-tou-electricity-peak-hours-appliances/). An AC that shows what it used in those hours helps you decide whether to warm the house a bit before five.

## 4. Drying without freezing the room

Samsung added **Dry Comfort** to its Indian models: a mode that uses humidity sensors and refrigerant flow control to pull moisture out of the room without overcooling it. In India it's aimed at monsoon season. Here it's relevant to anyone living on the coastal plain in August, where the problem isn't just 31 degrees but the stickiness. Almost every AC has a "dry" mode, but on most it simply cools at low power. The difference here is that the unit measures humidity and responds to it.

## 5. A natural refrigerant: R290

The quiet change of the year comes from Europe. The EU's F-gas rules are pushing makers toward refrigerants with a smaller effect on global warming. According to heise, R32, the refrigerant common in ACs today, has a global warming potential (GWP) of 675. Propane, R290, has 0.02.

Haier's 2026 European catalogue already has a dedicated range, **Pearl R290**, at 9,000 to 12,000 BTU/h (2.6 to 3.5 kW). Midea said at IFA that its portable split unit, **PortaSplit**, will switch to R290 and reach Europe in summer 2027, with a removable condensate filter and Matter support.

We haven't seen a mainstream home range in Israel move to propane yet, and it's not something to wait for if you're buying now. But if you're choosing between two similar units in a year or two, the refrigerant is worth a look.

## 6. An AC as a design object

It sounds minor, but it shows where the category is going. Tadiran sells the AIR ONE in white or black at the same price, and built the whole launch around design as much as BTU. The series has five models from 1 to about 3.5 hp, all inverter units rated A++, with Wi-Fi, a Shabbat mode approved by the Mishmeret HaShabbat institute and a five-year warranty, subject to terms.

## What else happened here

One less technical change is about where you buy. In November we reported that [TCL air conditioners will also reach Electra's chains through direct import](/techsite/en/posts/electra-tcl-air-conditioners-direct-import/), alongside the official importer. No models or prices yet, but if you're eyeing TCL for next summer, compare both channels and check who provides service.

## What to check before you buy

![An AC buying checklist: inverter and energy rating, heating, an app with usage data, a presence sensor, a real dry mode, Shabbat mode and warranty, plus a GWP comparison of R32 and R290](img/posts/ac-2026-buying-checklist-en.webp "Our checklist. Graphic: TechSite")

- **Heating, not just cooling.** In winter the AC is the heater in most Israeli homes. A modern inverter heats far more efficiently than a resistive heater, so check heating output and efficiency, not just cooling.
- **Horsepower isn't everything.** As we saw with AIR ONE, two "1 hp" units can deliver different BTU. Compare actual cooling output.
- **A presence sensor** isn't limited to premium imports anymore. If your kids walk out of rooms with the AC on, it pays for itself.
- **A real dry mode** is worth checking if you live near the sea.
- **Service and installation.** With an AC, who installs it and who comes to fix it matter almost as much as the unit. Ask for a quote that includes standard installation and removal of the old one, and compare at least two shops.

## The bottom line

The direction in 2026 is clear: an AC that understands what's happening in the room, not just the temperature. Some of it is already here, like Tadiran's motion sensor. Some, like Samsung's radar and humidity-led drying, hasn't reached Israeli shops yet. And if European rules keep pushing, in a few years we'll be asking which gas is inside our ACs too.
