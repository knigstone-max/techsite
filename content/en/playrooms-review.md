On this site we spend a lot of time on machines that clean floors. So when a game whose whole job is tidying the floor turned up near the top of Steam this month, I had to look. **Playrooms** puts you in an indoor play centre after closing time. Over 200,000 plastic balls are scattered across the floor, and you have to put them back in the ball pits. Nobody tells you why, and you can't remember how long you've worked here. Then the lights start going out.

It's made by **Two Pixels**, a small studio from Bamberg in Germany, and it came out on Steam on November 4 after a last-minute delay on launch day. It's sitting at 97% positive out of 1,959 user reviews. I went through what the game offers, how it's built and what players and critics are saying, and here's my verdict.

![Playrooms at a glance: Steam prices in Israel and the US, review score, release date, platform, length, modes and languages](img/posts/playrooms-glance-en.webp "The short version. Graphic: TechSite")

## What you actually do

Pick up balls. Carry them to a pit. Fill the pit. That's it, and that's the point. Every ball you collect is also money, and a full pit unlocks a new ability. On a retro computer in the office you buy upgrades: bigger pockets, faster grabbing, a ball magnet, and equipment like a scoop and a flashlight. Save up enough and you buy the key to the next area.

It's the loop of an incremental game in first person, and the pacing is its strongest point. Upgrades come quickly, so after half an hour you're collecting much faster than at the start, and there's always one more pit almost full. Most of the praise on Steam is about exactly that: the feeling that you'll stop after just one more.

The job stays the same right to the end. Only the rooms change. Tiled halls, a swimming pool full of balls, endless corridors, a yellow office straight out of the Backrooms, and, as the store page puts it, a hallway with more doors than it should have. The change of scenery does a lot of the work in keeping the game fresh.

![A tiled indoor swimming pool in Playrooms, filled with thousands of colourful balls, with three coloured slides at the far end](img/posts/playrooms-pool-screenshot.webp "The pool area. Image: Two Pixels")

## When the shift ends

At the end of each shift the lights go out, one by one, and there's one rule left: stay in the light. You don't want to find out what's in the dark. This is the horror half of the game, and it creates real time pressure: you're in the middle of a pit, you see the lights go off at the far end of the hall, and you have to decide whether to drop everything and run.

This is also the weaker half. Several reviews, including PlayInsider's, say the same tall figure keeps coming back, and after a few times it becomes more of a nuisance than a fright. The game's content warning mentions jumpscares with sudden loud sounds and long stretches of near-total darkness. There's no blood and no graphic violence.

## Three modes, and one for people who hate horror

This is a smart decision that I wish more games made. Before you start, you choose:

- **Cozy**: no horror at all. When the lights go out, the day just ends
- **Normal**: the game as Two Pixels intended
- **Hardcore**: get caught and your save is deleted

Cozy turns Playrooms into a relaxing tidying game, and it's a good choice for kids or anyone who doesn't do jumpscares. It's worth knowing that some of the tension, and some of the reason to hurry, goes with it.

## Story

There isn't much. No cutscenes and no narrator. You piece together what this place is from the environment and from messages on the office computer. That suits a Backrooms-style game, where the mystery is half the fun, but if you want a proper story with an arc, you won't find it here.

## Length, performance and bugs

- **Length**: players report around seven to ten hours for one run, and about twelve for everything. After that there's Hardcore.
- **Requirements**: modest. Windows 10, a GTX 1060 or RX 580, 8 GB of RAM and 10 GB of storage.
- **Steam Deck**: Valve rates it Verified, with full controller support including DualShock and DualSense.
- **Language**: fully voiced in six languages, including English. No Hebrew, but there's very little text, so it hardly matters.

At launch, players reported balls stuck in walls or under furniture, occasional crashes, frame drops when a lot of balls pile up in a small space, and problems with the vents in the final area. Two Pixels fixed some of this quickly, according to player reports, and has since released a Steam Deck update. If you're going for 100%, keep an eye on the patches.

## Who it's for

If you like incremental games, cleaning sims or that "just one more" feeling, it's an easy recommendation at this price. If you're after real horror, a story or co-op, look elsewhere. There's a free demo on Steam if you want to try before you buy.

:::score
## The bottom line

7.5/10

Playrooms takes the simplest idea there is, tidying up balls, and turns it into a loop that's hard to put down, thanks to fast upgrades and constantly changing rooms. The horror adds pressure at first, but wears thin, and the job never changes from the first minute to the last. Cozy mode opens it up to anyone, and at ₪25.25 it's one of the cheapest evenings of fun on Steam right now.
:::

**What I liked**

- A fresh mix of a tidying game and Backrooms atmosphere
- Upgrades that come quickly and keep you collecting
- Cozy mode with no horror, and Hardcore for the brave
- Rooms that keep changing and keep surprising
- Steam Deck Verified and modest requirements
- ₪25.25

**What I liked less**

- The jumpscares get repetitive
- The core task never changes
- Launch bugs, some of them still around in the final area
- Almost no story
- No co-op
