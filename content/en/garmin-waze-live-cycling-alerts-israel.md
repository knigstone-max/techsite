Anyone who rides a road bike in Israel knows the moment: a car passes far too close, or a driver simply doesn't see you on a bend. As of yesterday there's a partial fix from an unexpected direction. Waze and Garmin announced a tie-up that puts cyclists using a Garmin device on the map of drivers near them.

Gadgety reports that it works in Israel too, and it costs nothing if you already own a supported device. Google says it's rolling out worldwide now, on Android and iOS.

## What the driver sees

When a Garmin rider is close by, the driver gets a Waze alert and sees a cyclist icon moving along the road in real time. That's it. No name, no route, nothing else about the rider. The point is a simple nudge to slow down and pay attention.

Waze says it has 200 million drivers worldwide, and in Israel it's close to the default. That's what makes this interesting. The rider doesn't need drivers to install anything new. They just need them to be driving with Waze, which most already are.

![How the Garmin and Waze link works in four steps: turn it on in Garmin Connect, ride outdoors with GPS, drivers get an alert, sharing ends with the ride](img/posts/garmin-waze-how-it-works-en.webp "Four steps, and only the first one needs anything from you. Graphic: TechSite")

## What you need

- **A Garmin device with LiveTrack:** one of the company's smartwatches or an Edge bike computer.
- **Your phone:** LiveTrack runs through the Garmin Connect app, so the phone has to come along on the ride.
- **A one-time opt-in:** in the LiveTrack settings in Garmin Connect, allow sharing with Waze. It's off until you turn it on.
- **An outdoor GPS ride:** road, mountain and e-bike profiles count. Indoor sessions on a trainer, with no satellite signal, don't.

LiveTrack itself isn't new. Garmin has offered it for years, mostly so family can follow a rider live and see where they are and how fast they're going. What's new is that the same location now also reaches drivers around you, anonymously.

## And privacy

This is where both companies put the most effort. Sharing is opt-in, temporary, and tied to the activity. End the ride on your device and the alerts stop. The driver only learns that a cyclist is nearby, with no identifying details and no history.

## Why it matters here

Citing Central Bureau of Statistics data, Gadgety notes that 149 cyclists were hurt in road accidents in Israel in 2025, and the National Road Safety Authority counts 9 cyclists killed that year, not including e-bike riders. If you ride intercity roads, at dawn, or in the early winter dark, you know how much being seen matters.

There's another local angle. Waze was founded in Israel, and its side of the announcement came from Gai Berkovich, Waze's VP and general manager.

## Where it won't help

Keep expectations in check. Alerts only work for riders with a Garmin device, and only for drivers who have Waze open at that moment. Riders with just a phone, a watch from another brand, or an e-bike with no device at all won't show up. And even when an alert appears, it doesn't replace a rear light, bright clothing, or a driver keeping their distance.

Still, if you already ride with a Garmin, it costs nothing and takes one tap in the settings. Worth switching on.
