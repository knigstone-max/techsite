Israel's mobile market keeps consolidating into a handful of big players. Pelephone, part of the Bezeq group, signed a binding agreement on Monday (Nov 9) to buy 100% of WeCom Mobile for about ₪265 million (about $84 million). The deal follows months of due diligence and negotiation, after a non-binding memorandum of understanding signed in July.

It isn't done yet. The deal still needs approval from the Communications Ministry and the Competition Commissioner, and Pelephone says it is working on both.

## Who is WeCom

WeCom is a fairly small carrier, with more than 300,000 customers and around 3% of the market. Longtime market watchers will remember it by its old name, WE4G. In 2022, Yariv Lerner and the ClearMark fund bought it from Hezi Bezalel for about ₪100 million (about $32 million) and rebranded it the same year. So the sellers are getting about 2.5 times what they paid four years ago.

## What Pelephone gets

Pelephone currently has about 2.7 million subscribers, more than 1.6 million of them on 5G. Adding WeCom's customers takes it past 3 million, and according to Globes its market share rises from 23.5% to about 26%. For comparison, Cellcom had about 3.7 million customers in its second-quarter report, and Partner reported about 2.74 million mobile subscribers at the end of the third quarter on Monday.

Bezeq expects the combination to bring savings and benefits of about ₪50 million (about $16 million) in the short term, mostly from operating costs, and about ₪100 million (about $32 million) in the long run from merging the networks and spectrum.

## The last attempt fell through

This isn't Pelephone's first try at growing by acquisition. In November 2025 it signed an MoU to buy Hot Mobile, but Altice chose to sell to a group led by Delek Israel instead, for about ₪1.2 billion (about $378 million). Hanging over that deal was the concern that a Pelephone-Hot Mobile merger would control about 40% of the market and run into opposition from the Competition Authority. The sale of Hot Mobile to the Delek group closed in early October.

WeCom is a much smaller deal, so the odds of it getting stuck with regulators are lower.

## What it means for customers

For now, nothing. Until the deal is approved and closes, WeCom keeps running as usual, with its own plans and prices. The real question is what happens after: whether the brand stays on as a separate budget label, the way other secondary brands do in this market, or whether customers get moved over to Pelephone. Neither company has published a plan yet.

For the market, every small carrier that gets absorbed is another step away from the price war that followed the 2012 reform. Pelephone CEO Ilan Sigal says the deal will strengthen competition on network quality, service and innovation. The Competition Commissioner will have to decide whether to agree.
