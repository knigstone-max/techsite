There's no car dealership in GTA VI. No website to order from, no showroom. You steal most cars off the street, and if you want to keep one, you pay to register it in your name and park it in a garage. Anything you don't want to keep can go to a fence. A handful of cars come as bonuses with the Ultimate Edition or the pre-order pack, and we cover those further down.

The game counts 277 base models, plus roughly 280 special versions called Standouts: cars with their own paint, name and upgrades, waiting at fixed spots on the map. That's a lot, and two days after launch about half the base models still don't have a published name. So our rule is simple. A vehicle makes this catalog only if Rockstar named it, or at least two serious guides saw it in the game. Names spotted only in trailers stay out.

This is the first catalog in our GTA VI world guides. There's also a [motorcycle catalog](/techsite/en/posts/gta-6-motorcycles-catalog/), a [weapons catalog](/techsite/en/posts/gta-6-weapons-catalog/) and an [editions and extras guide](/techsite/en/posts/gta-6-editions-extras-guide/), all collected on the [guides hub](/techsite/en/posts/gta-6-guides-hub/). Still on the fence about buying? [Our review](/techsite/en/posts/gta-6-review/) gave it 11 out of 10.

## How you get a vehicle in GTA VI

- **Steal it:** most cars are on the street. Better-protected models need a key cloner, which unlocks during the story.
- **Register it:** a stolen car isn't yours until you pay to register it. After that it lives in your garage, and if it's wrecked you get it back.
- **Sell it:** whatever you don't keep goes to a fence. 100% completion includes $200,000 in fenced vehicles.
- **Scan it:** the WAiNK app on your character's phone logs every model you scan. You don't have to drive it. 100% completion needs 200 of the 277 base models.

### Wyman's classic car collection

The Ultimate Edition adds a special commission from an eccentric collector called Wyman. You track down abandoned classics and half-finished project cars and bring them back to life. According to IGN and PowerPyx, the list includes the '70 Riata, the '64 Mamba GT, the Reynolds Kit Car and the Deviant. IGN calls that last one the Born Free Deviant, PowerPyx the '72 Deviant.

## Rideout Customs: the Ultimate Edition's custom shop

It's not only cars. The Ultimate Edition opens Rideout Customs, a shop that turns ordinary cars into rolling art: detailed interiors, big rims and donk styling, meaning a big American car on lifted wheels. There's also One-Eyed Willie's by Lake Leonida, which does off-road mods and hand-painted jobs. More on both in the [extras guide](/techsite/en/posts/gta-6-editions-extras-guide/).

## What we don't know yet

- About 170 base models still have no published name.
- There are no speed figures that more than one source agrees on. IGN published speed ratings for some cars, but we haven't found a second source that tested them, so there are no numbers here.
- Plenty of sites list cars spotted in trailers. A car you see in a trailer isn't necessarily one you can drive, so those aren't here.
- Spelling varies between sources. GamesRadar+, for example, writes "Stainer" for the bonus car too. We kept the spelling used in the game and on Rockstar's site.

We'll update the catalog as more names come out. If you're still deciding which edition to buy, the [editions guide](/techsite/en/posts/gta-6-editions-extras-guide/) lays out what you get with each.
