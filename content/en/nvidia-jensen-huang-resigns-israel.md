Jensen Huang, who founded Nvidia and ran it for more than three decades, resigned on Thursday as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately, the company's board announced. CFO Colette Kress, at Nvidia since 2013, is interim CEO and keeps her finance job too. For the first time in its history the company also has a board chair: Harvey Jones, the former CEO and chair of Synopsys. The board is looking for a permanent CEO, both inside and outside the company.

The resignation followed several days of reports about Huang's private life. According to The Times of Israel and The Forward, they involved an affair with a Chinese businesswoman whom US officials suspect of spying for Beijing, and a second relationship with a senior Nvidia executive who reported to him. Nvidia's official announcement gives no reason.

## Why this is an Israeli story

Nvidia is one of the biggest employers in Israeli tech, and its operation here is its largest outside the US. The Times of Israel puts it at more than 5,000 employees across seven R&D centers, from Yokneam in the north to Beersheba in the south. According to Globes, operations managed from Israel accounted for 18% of the company's total revenue in the second quarter.

Huang was Israel's biggest champion inside the company. He called it Nvidia's "second home," and he was the one pushing for a huge campus in Kiryat Tivon, near Haifa.

## The Tivon campus: what's done and what isn't

According to Calcalist, Nvidia has already bought the land, about 90 dunams (roughly 22 acres), and planning is nearly finished. The plan calls for 160,000 square meters of built space, with construction starting in 2027 and move-in by 2031. The campus would let Nvidia hire up to 10,000 more people, more than doubling its local headcount.

Calcalist's view is that the existing R&D work is so deeply built into Nvidia's products, especially the networking business that came with the Mellanox deal, that it's hard to see any CEO cutting it back. The campus is different. The project is moving, but in their words it hasn't yet passed the point where canceling it would be unthinkable. A change at the top or a slowdown in AI spending could still affect it.

## Two Israelis on the shortlist talk

The Times of Israel and The Forward both mention two names with Israeli ties, though for now this is media speculation, not anything from the company. One is Michael Kagan, a Mellanox co-founder and Nvidia's CTO. The other is Johny Srouji, born in Haifa, who runs hardware and chip development at Apple. Both studied at the Technion.

## What to watch on Tuesday

Nvidia says its third-quarter results will come out as planned on Tuesday, November 17, followed by the usual investor call. That's the first chance to hear from the interim leadership. Anything said about Israel, and Tivon in particular, will get read very closely here.
