If you've sat through a long winter blackout, or spent days near the safe room during a war, you know the question that comes up after a few hours: what's happening inside the fridge? Jackery, best known for portable power stations for camping and the home, announced a product this week aimed right at that. FridgeGuard is a backup battery built for the fridge alone, and it lives on top of it or next to it full time.

## How it works

You plug FridgeGuard into the wall and plug the fridge into FridgeGuard. While the grid is up, the fridge draws power straight from it and the battery just stays topped up. Jackery calls this Bypass mode, and the point is to stop the battery from cycling all the time and wearing out. When the power drops, it takes over in 10 milliseconds or less, like a computer UPS, so the compressor never notices.

![A slim silver FridgeGuard unit on top of a fridge, labeled with its thickness, capacity and switchover time](img/posts/jackery-fridgeguard-annotated-en.webp "FridgeGuard on the fridge. Image: Jackery, graphic: TechSite")

## The numbers

- **Capacity:** 1,024Wh, which Jackery says is enough for up to 15 hours of fridge. Add the expansion battery and it's up to 30 hours.
- **Output:** one outlet, up to 800W continuous and a 1,600W peak, enough to start a compressor.
- **Size:** 67 mm thick and 10.5 kg. It can stand on the floor, hang on the wall or sit on a bracket.
- **Battery:** LiFePO4. The maker says it keeps at least 70% of its capacity after 6,000 cycles, and it carries a five-year warranty.
- **Noise:** under 40 dB.
- **App:** a phone alert when the power goes out, energy tracking, and charging scheduled for cheaper hours. If the battery runs flat before the grid comes back, you can recharge it from a solar panel or a car.

Jackery says it works with 99% of modern fridges.

## Price, and what about Israel

For now FridgeGuard is launching only in Canada, first as a Costco exclusive at CA$699.99, about ₪1,571 ($491). It reaches a handful of Costco warehouses from November 18, pre-orders on Jackery Canada's site open November 24, and the official launch there is December 11.

There's nothing for Israel yet. Keep in mind that the Canadian model is built for the North American grid, and Israel needs a device made for 230 volts. So ordering one from Amazon and plugging it in at home is a bad idea, even if it shows up there.

## Why it's interesting here

The idea isn't new. You can already run a fridge off a portable power station, and Jackery sells plenty of those. The difference is that this one stays connected and works on its own. Nobody has to remember to drag a heavy battery out of storage and swap plugs in the dark while the sirens are still going.

That said, 15 hours is Jackery's figure, and it depends heavily on the fridge. A big four-door model, very common in Israeli kitchens, draws more than a small one, and an Israeli summer in a kitchen without air conditioning won't help. Treat that number as a ceiling, not a promise.

If this product, or one like it, arrives through an official importer, it could make sense for families with medication that needs cooling, pumped breast milk or just a full freezer. Until then, a portable power station you already own does the same job, with more legwork.
