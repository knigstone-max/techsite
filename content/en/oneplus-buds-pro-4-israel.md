A few weeks after the Buds Clip landed here, OnePlus has brought its more serious pair. Cell Now, the brand's official importer, started selling the OnePlus Buds Pro 4 yesterday at a suggested ₪649 (about $205). They come in grey (Titanium Shadow) or white (Polar White), and you can split the price into up to 12 interest-free payments on OnePlus Israel's site.

The importer says the Israeli units are the European model, CE certified, with over-the-air updates. That matters more than it sounds: grey-import earbuds sometimes ship with a firmware version that never gets updates in Israel.

## Two drivers in each ear

The main change from the Buds Pro 3 is inside. Each earbud has an 11 mm bass driver and a 6 mm tweeter, each with its own DAC, and the sound was tuned with Danish speaker maker Dynaudio. OnePlus claims 50% more bass drive than the previous generation and lists a 10 Hz to 40 kHz frequency response.

For high-quality wireless audio there's LHDC 5.0, at up to 24-bit/192 kHz. It's worth knowing that iPhones don't support LHDC, and most Samsung phones don't either, so on those you'll be listening over AAC. On OnePlus phones and some other Android models you get the full package, along with a Golden Sound feature that adapts the sound to your ears and spatial audio with head tracking.

## Noise cancelling and calls

OnePlus calls its ANC Pro Adaptive Noise Cancellation and says it blocks up to twice as much noise as the Buds Pro 3. It doesn't publish a decibel figure. In adaptive mode the earbuds detect whether you're in an office, on the street or on a train and adjust on the fly. There's also a fixed ANC mode and a transparency mode.

Each earbud has three microphones plus a VPU sensor that picks up your voice through bone vibration, and AI Clear Call uses all of them to separate your voice from background noise. On a noisy Israeli bus or at a busy junction, that's the feature you'll notice most on calls.

Some of the smart features only work with OnePlus phones: live translation needs ColorOS 17 or later, two-earbud recording works on OnePlus 10 and newer, and the 39 ms game mode is limited to selected OnePlus models. Gemini by voice ("Hey Google") works more widely.

## Battery

Every earbud has a 65 mAh battery and the case holds 576 mAh. By OnePlus's figures, using AAC at 50% volume, you get 13 hours with ANC off and 53 hours with the case. With ANC on, which is how most people will use them, that drops to 6 hours, or 26 with the case. Ten minutes of charging gives up to 14 hours of listening with the case. The case charges over USB-C, on a wireless pad, or from a compatible OnePlus phone.

The earbuds are IP55 rated, which is fine for sweat and rain. The case has no rating. Each earbud weighs 5.6 g, they connect to two devices at once over Bluetooth 6.1, and they support Google Fast Pair and Microsoft Swift Pair.

## Who they're for

At ₪649 they're ₪150 more than the Buds Clip (₪499, about $157), and for that you get a sealed in-ear design, active noise cancelling and wireless charging. If you have a recent OnePlus phone, they make plenty of sense. If you use an iPhone or a Galaxy, a big part of what makes them special (LHDC, translation, recording) won't be available to you, and it's worth comparing them with what Apple and Samsung sell here at a similar price.
