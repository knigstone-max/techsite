Some games you play, and some you read. After the Wane is the second kind, and it's a reminder of why that kind is worth your time. It's an interactive graphic novel from **Nova-box**, a small studio in Bordeaux, France, that previously made Seers Isle, Along the Edge and Across the Grooves. If you've played any of those, you know the recipe: a branching story, choices that change the route, a touch of the supernatural, some romance, and always a twist.

It came out on November 10, and I played through it several times to see how much it really changes. More than I expected.

![After the Wane at a glance: Steam price in Israel and the US, release date, platforms, length and languages](img/posts/after-the-wane-overview-en.webp "In short. Graphic: TechSite")

## What it's about

Lena arrives in the capital with two clear goals. The first is to break into the city's ruthless dance world. The second is to understand why her mother, also a dancer, abandoned her at birth.

She soon finds herself between two worlds. On one side, Deborah and her elitist dance company, based in a theatre with an uncanny, uncomfortable feel. On the other, Fernand, an enigmatic figure who looks after the city's independent dance scene. And above it all there's **the voice**: a presence inside Lena's head that only she can hear, and that's no longer content to just listen.

That's all I'll say about the plot. After the Wane works best the less you know going in, and there's one moment that changes everything you thought about the game up to that point. Don't look anything up first.

## How it plays

There's no combat, no puzzles and no reflex tests. You read, and you choose. The text moves forward one sentence per click, and every few lines comes a choice: what Lena says, who she spends the evening with, where she auditions and how she answers the voice.

The game is split into three acts and branches into three main routes:

- **The prestigious dance company**, with its harsh discipline
- **The collective** at the heart of the independent scene, where Lena might find her people
- **The occult path**, for those willing to dig into the mystery

Alongside that there's your relationship with the voice, which you can work with or resist, and several romance options. Even the character's name changes: Lena, Magdalena or Madeleine, depending on who she becomes.

![Screenshot from After the Wane: a dance studio with a wooden floor, dancers in silhouette, and an inset panel of a dancer in a deep floor stretch](img/posts/after-the-wane-dance-studio.webp "Rehearsal in the studio. Screenshot: Nova-box")

## Choices that actually matter

This is what impressed me most. Plenty of games in this genre offer choices that lead to the same place in the end. Here there are scenes everyone will see, but how you get there, in what order, who's at Lena's side and who she even is by then, is up to you.

You can, for example, skip meeting a certain character and only run into them later. That changes a few lines of dialogue, and also bigger things down the line. A decision that looks small in the first hour comes back to you in the third.

And the game won't let you forget what you didn't choose. Small lines keep reminding you of the road not taken, the dream slipping away, how far Lena has drifted from the person who arrived in the city. Some moments just hurt, in the good way.

## The writing

The text is short, sharp and precise. No long paragraphs of description, just single sentences that paint a whole picture. It keeps the pace up and leaves the art to do the rest.

One caveat for Israeli players: you need good English. There's no Hebrew, and the whole game is text. The English isn't complicated, but it's literary, and if reading English is a struggle you'll miss a lot of what makes it special.

## How it looks and sounds

After the Wane is one of the most beautiful games I've seen this year. The art is hand-painted, with visible brushstrokes and pencil lines, and it makes no attempt at realism. Nova-box stresses that none of it was made with generative AI, and it shows: the art has character.

The highlight is the **dance scenes**. They're rotoscoped, traced frame by frame from real movement. The result is motion with genuine weight and breath, inside a painted world that feels like a dream. Every time a dance scene came up, I just stopped and watched.

![Screenshot from After the Wane: Lena looks out of a window over the city at night, with three choices for how to spend the evening](img/posts/after-the-wane-choices.webp "Who do you spend the evening with? Each choice opens a different path. Screenshot: Nova-box")

**The soundtrack** is orchestral, recorded with real instruments, and shifts with your choices. Jumpy piano when Lena is confused, slow strings in dramatic moments, and complete silence when that's what the scene needs. It's also sold separately for $4.99 (about ₪16).

## Length and value

A first run takes **about 4 hours**. A second and third run, to see the three main endings, are shorter and still show plenty of new material. Seeing everything adds a few more hours, and Steam achievements give you a reason to go back.

At ₪51.95 (about $15.90), it's a fair price for an experience like this. In the US it's $13.99 (about ₪46). There's also a free demo on Steam if you're not sure the genre is for you.

## Good to know

- **Mature content.** Alcohol and smoking, mild horror scenes (including spiders), and intimate scenes with veiled nudity.
- **Platforms:** Windows, Mac and Linux, on Steam, GOG and itch.io. A Switch 2 version is planned for early 2027.
- **Requirements:** almost any PC will run it. The minimum spec is 2GB of RAM and 2GB of storage.
- **Controls:** mouse, keyboard, controller (DualSense included) or touch.
- **Languages:** English and French only.

## The bottom line

8.5/10

After the Wane is a graphic novel where every choice feels like it matters, with lean, precise writing, hand-painted art, rotoscoped dance scenes that are a joy to watch and a soundtrack that responds to you. Its twist is one of the best I've seen in the genre. It's short, it needs good English, and some late routes satisfy less than others, but for ₪52 it's hard to find a more beautiful story game this year.
:::

**What I liked**

- Choices that really change the route and the character
- A surprising twist that recasts everything before it
- Hand-painted art and rotoscoped dance scenes
- An orchestral soundtrack that reacts to your choices
- Worth replaying, with three main endings
- Fair price and a free demo

**What I didn't**

- A short first run, about 4 hours
- No Hebrew, and you need solid English
- Some routes in the final act are less satisfying
- Not one for kids
