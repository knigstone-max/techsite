The Beam has always been the Sonos bar for the average Israeli living room. Not huge like the Arc, not as small as the Ray, and good enough to make you forget the TV speakers. Its weak spot was Atmos: it was virtual, with no drivers actually pointing up. The Beam Ultra is built to close that gap. It sells at Bug, the official Sonos importer in Israel, for ₪2,899 (about $908), in matte black or white. In the US it is $699, roughly ₪2,233.

I did not live with it for a month. I went through Sonos's official specs, what Bug is selling here, and what your living room needs to get the most from it. The short answer up front: if your TV has HDMI eARC and you want true Atmos without a bar the length of a sofa, this is one of the strongest options around. If your TV is older, much of the advantage is lost.

![Sonos Beam Ultra specs: ₪2,899 in Israel, $699 in the US, 9 drivers and 7.1.2 channels](img/posts/sonos-beam-ultra-specs-en.webp "The key numbers in one place. Graphic: TechSite")

## Design and size

From the outside it is still a Beam: a rounded bar, matte finish and touch controls on top. It measures 75 cm wide, 6.5 cm tall and 10.5 cm deep, and weighs 2.88 kg. Height is the number that matters most. At 65 mm it sits low enough not to block the bottom of the screen on most TVs that stand on feet. A dedicated wall mount is also available.

The top has capacitive controls for playback, volume, grouping speakers and muting the voice assistant, plus a Bluetooth pairing button. Around the back is a physical switch that cuts power to the microphones entirely. I like that. You do not have to trust software, you just switch them off.

![Sonos Beam Ultra annotated: touch controls on top, 9 drivers and 7.1.2, 65 mm tall](img/posts/sonos-beam-ultra-annotated-en.webp "What sits inside the bar. Graphic: TechSite")

## What is inside

Nine drivers, each with its own Class-D amp: five tweeters and four low-profile woofers. A center tweeter faces forward for dialogue, two horn-loaded side tweeters widen the stereo image, and two point upward to bounce sound off the ceiling. That is what lets Sonos claim 7.1.2 instead of simulated Atmos. Sonos says the four extra drivers over the original Beam bring a wider stage, deeper bass and cleaner separation in busy scenes.

On paper, that is exactly what was missing. In practice, ceiling-bounce Atmos depends on the room. In a living room with a flat ceiling of normal height it should work well. A very high, sloped or beamed ceiling will weaken the effect.

## Dialogue, night mode and tuning

AI Speech Enhancement picks out voices and offers four levels of boost in the app. If you watch Hebrew shows with heavy background music, or if someone at home keeps asking you to turn it up because they cannot follow the lines, this may be the most useful feature here. Night Sound tames explosions and lifts quiet sounds, which matters in an apartment with shared walls and sleeping kids.

Trueplay measures the room and tunes the sound, and it works on both iPhone and Android. There is also an EQ for bass, treble and loudness.

## Connections, and what your TV needs

It connects to the TV with an HDMI cable included in the box, supporting eARC and ARC. This is the part to pay attention to: Dolby Atmos needs Atmos-encoded content and a TV with compatible eARC. On an older TV with plain ARC, or no HDMI at all (an optical adapter is sold separately), you get a good soundbar, but not the thing you paid for.

Wireless covers Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth 5.3 and AirPlay 2. The USB-C port takes a wired source through the Line-In adapter, or wired networking through the Combo adapter. Both cost extra, which is a little annoying on a product close to ₪3,000.

## A system that grows

As with any Sonos, the Beam Ultra is a starting point. You can add a Sub and rear speakers, including portables like the Move 2. Sonos Positioning Technology figures out where a speaker sits using high-frequency tones and changes its role, so the speaker from the balcony can become a rear surround for movie night. Clever, but every add-on is another few thousand shekels.

## Price in Israel

₪2,899 at Bug, after a ₪2,699 pre-order price in September. Against $699 in the US, the gap is reasonable by Israeli audio standards, and you get an official importer and local service. Still, it is a serious sum for a bar with no sub. If you want bass you feel in your chest, budget for a Sub later.

:::score
## Bottom line

8/10

The Beam Ultra brings real, ceiling-firing Atmos to a bar that fits almost any Israeli living room. Dialogue boost, night mode and Trueplay on Android make it genuinely useful day to day. It loses points for adapters sold separately, its dependence on an eARC TV and a price that does not include a sub. With a recent TV and a normal-sized room, it is an excellent pick.
:::

**What I liked**

- True 7.1.2 Dolby Atmos with two up-firing drivers
- 6.5 cm height that stays below the screen
- Four-level dialogue enhancement
- Trueplay that works on Android too
- Physical microphone kill switch
- Official import and service through Bug

**What I liked less**

- Line-In, Combo and optical adapters cost extra
- Without eARC on the TV, the main advantage disappears
- The Atmos effect depends on ceiling height and shape
- ₪2,899 with no sub, and deep bass means spending more
