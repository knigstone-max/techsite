One moment in Village in the Shade sums up the whole game. You've put in a long day on the farm, watering, harvesting, feeding the cows, and you go to bed. Then, in the middle of the night, someone knocks on the door. One of this village's rules is that you never go out after dark. You go out anyway.

On the surface it's a farming sim in the Stardew Valley mould, with everything that implies: fields, animals, fishing, festivals and neighbours whose trust you have to earn. But it comes from the Nippon Ichi team behind Yomawari, a small and genuinely creepy horror series about little girls wandering a Japanese town at night. That pairing is what makes it something else.

## The village and its rules

You play a child you design yourself, who wakes up in Kagatsu, a small village in the mountains. It quickly becomes clear the place runs on eight strict rules: don't go out at night, don't talk to strangers, don't leave the village, and a few more. Nobody really explains why.

The story is split into eight chapters, one for each rule, and each one peels back another layer of what's going on. No spoilers, but there are secrets and sacrifices here, and the game isn't afraid to go somewhere sad. There's no romance, unlike most farming games. Instead it's about family and friendship, which fits the story far better.

At first the villagers are cold and guarded. You're an outsider and they don't trust you. The way in is work: take over the run-down farm on the edge of the village and bring it back to life. As the farm grows, attitudes shift. It's a smart way to tie the gameplay to the plot. Farming isn't just how you make money, it's how you earn your place.

## By day: a farm with real depth

If you've played farming games, you'll settle in fast. You grow crops in tilled soil or furrows, plant fruit bushes and trees, and process what you harvest with smokers, brewing casks and beehives. There are animals and pastures, plus fences and paths to hold back the wild grass that creeps into every empty corner. There are even cursed seeds, and it's more fun to find out what they do yourself.

Fishing is the best early money-maker, and it's worth leaning on in the first few weeks. There's also a huge quarry with more than 100 floors and a shortcut every ten, and you can't skip it, because that's where the materials for tool upgrades come from. Hunting is there if you want it, along with two shops and plenty of furniture and decorating.

All of it sits on a calendar of seasons and festivals. The cherry-blossom festival is one of the loveliest moments in the game, and a chance to see the village when it's a little less suspicious of you.

![A village festival under a blossoming cherry tree, with stalls and villagers gathering](img/posts/village-in-the-shade-festival.webp "Festival time under the cherry blossoms. Image: NIS America")

## By night: the Yomawari side

The big difference from every other farming game starts at sundown. Every so often you'll be woken by a knock, and a night outing begins. At night the village looks completely different: dark, silent and full of things you'd rather not meet.

These encounters aren't built around combat. When there's a "boss", the goal is mostly to dodge and survive until the creature is vulnerable, and only then act. There are some light puzzles too. It feels much more like Yomawari than an action game, which is exactly the point. The tension comes from what you can't see, not from how many enemies there are.

Failure is handled gently. Fail a story event and you can retry it or restart the day. Get knocked out and you wake at home with less health and stamina. Annoying, but not brutal.

The weak spot is that some night events are obtuse. Sometimes it's hard to work out what the game wants from you, and you end up wandering in the dark with no direction. That's partly the atmosphere talking, but it can also grate.

![A dark stone corridor in dim light, during a night outing](img/posts/village-in-the-shade-night.webp "At night, the village is a different place. Image: NIS America")

## Progression: magatama and a fox

Progress is tied to the village's spiritual side. You collect magatama, traditional curved jewels, and offer them at the shrine to unlock paths, fences and passive upgrades. A fox also leads you to another shrine where you can raise your stamina. It's one more small detail that links farm life to the mystery of the place.

## Looks and sound

It's a beautiful game. The backgrounds are hand-painted, the seasons really transform the village, and the small, cute Yomawari-style characters make a striking contrast with the scary moments. A tiny, adorable kid standing alone in a dark alley is more unsettling than any detailed monster.

There's no voice acting, which is fine for a game like this. The music is excellent, calm and warm by day and menacing at night. As horror goes, it's spooky rather than gory. There's a bit of blood, but it relies on atmosphere, not shock. In the US it's rated T for Teen.

## Pacing: the main problem

What will bother some players most is the pace. The opening tutorial is long, and there's sometimes a lot of waiting between story beats. You're ready for the next chapter and the game isn't. If you're here for the story, you may feel like you spend a lot of time waiting.

Saving is also a pain: you only save when you go to sleep, which moves time forward. You can't save mid-day and come back later.

On the other hand, if you love farming games and enjoy the routine, you'll barely notice. For you, the waiting is just a few more days on the farm, which is the fun part anyway.

## Length, platforms and price

Village in the Shade came out on October 27 for PC, PS5, Switch 2 and Switch. It's a long one, roughly 60 to 80 hours to finish, with 52 achievements.

On Steam in Israel it costs ₪184.99 (about $59), more than the US price of $49.99 (about ₪156), so if you're on the fence, waiting for a sale makes sense. It's light on hardware. The minimum is a Core i5-6400, 8GB of RAM and a GeForce GTX 750 Ti, with 9GB of storage. The recommended spec is a Core i5-12500, 16GB and a GTX 1650.

## Who it's for

If you loved Stardew Valley and wished it had more story and atmosphere, this is your game. Same if you loved Yomawari and wanted something longer to live in. If you're after intense horror or a story that moves fast, you'll be disappointed.

:::score
## The bottom line

8.5/10

Village in the Shade is a deep, beautiful farming game with a story you'll want to see through, and nights that bring real tension to a usually cosy genre. It loses points for its slow pacing, long tutorial and a few obtuse night events. Give it time and you get one of the most original games in the genre.
:::

**What I loved**

- Pastoral farming mixed with quiet night-time horror
- Eight rules, eight chapters, and a story that unfolds layer by layer
- Lots of farming depth: processing, animals, fishing, a 100-floor quarry
- Hand-painted art and seasons that change the village
- Excellent music
- Failure that doesn't punish too hard

**What I liked less**

- Slow pacing and long waits between story beats
- An overlong tutorial
- Saving only by sleeping, which moves time forward
- Some obtuse night events
- Pricier on Israeli Steam than in the US
