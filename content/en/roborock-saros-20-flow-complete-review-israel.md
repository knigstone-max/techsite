Israeli homes run on hard floors. Most apartments here are tiled or porcelain, so what matters in a robot isn't how hard it sucks but how well it mops. That's where most robots let you down: spinning pads that smear dirt around, water tanks that run dry halfway, and streaks left behind once the robot heads home. The Saros 20 Flow Complete, which Modan Electronics launched in Israel on October 6, goes after exactly that problem with a different idea: a roller instead of round pads.

I went through everything Modan and Roborock have published, the local pricing, and what it all means for a typical Israeli apartment. Here's where I land.

## The roller is the whole story

Instead of two spinning pads, the Saros 20 Flow Complete has a 270 mm roller mop underneath. While it cleans, 16 water jets spray it with fresh water nonstop as it spins at 240 rpm, and the dirty water goes into a separate tank inside the robot. Roborock calls this SpiraFlow 2.0.

Why does that matter? A normal mop pad gets dirtier as it goes, so by the end of a room it's spreading around whatever it picked up at the start. A roller that's constantly rinsed should clean the last metre as well as the first. Roborock also says the dense roller and hot water cut water marks by 30%, which is exactly the thing that bugs you on dark porcelain.

![The underside of the Saros 20 Flow Complete with callouts for the side brushes, dual main brush, roller mop and climbing wheels](img/posts/roborock-saros-20-underside-annotated-en.webp "What each part on the underside does")

Two more design details I like. First, the roller lifts up to 15 mm when the robot rolls onto a rug, and a roller cover closes automatically to keep wet and dry apart. Second, the camera recognises floor types and adjusts water flow, so on wood it should go easier on the water.

## The dock does the dirty work

RockDock, the docking station, is the other half of the story. It refills the robot's tank with hot water, washes the roller at 100°C according to Roborock, and dries it with 63°C hot air. It also has an auto detergent dispenser (480 ml) and empties the dust into a bag. The clean-water tank holds 4 litres and the dirty-water tank 3 litres.

Modan says the dirty-water tank was redesigned with a simpler interior and a better handle, so it's easier to empty and rinse. That sounds minor on paper, but anyone who's owned a mopping robot knows the worst chore is rinsing out that smelly tank every week.

What you do need to plan for is size. The dock is 38.1×45.4×45.1 cm and weighs 10.5 kg. In a tight apartment, it's not something you tuck under a table.

## Low, and it climbs

The robot is 8.98 cm tall. That's very low for a lidar robot, and the trick is a retractable LDS sensor. In open space it rises for a 360-degree scan, and under furniture it drops down while the robot navigates with a 100-degree rear view. With low sofas and beds with drawers, that's the difference between cleaning underneath and leaving dust there for months.

AdaptiLift Traverse 3.0 adds a climbing arm that helps it get over two-step thresholds up to 8.8 cm. In Israeli apartments with a step out to the balcony or a lip at the bathroom door, thresholds are one of the most frustrating things about robots, and that number is very high.

## Suction and hair

Suction is rated at 36,000 Pa. On hard floors, the gap between numbers this big matters less than the marketing would have you think, but it helps on rugs and with the sand that comes in from the balcony. I care more about hair handling: a DuoDivide dual main brush and two side brushes, with Roborock promising 0% tangling. If you have a shedding dog or a kid with long hair, that claim deserves testing, but it's the right goal.

One side brush swings outward, which Roborock says gives 30% more corner coverage. Combined with VertiBeam side lighting, the robot should spot walls, thresholds and rug edges, and bring the roller closer to the edges.

## Smart, sometimes too smart

An AI RGB camera recognises more than 300 obstacle types, and stains too. On coffee or cat litter it boosts suction, and on a spilled liquid it switches to mop-only. Pet detection stops the main brush so it won't spook your animals, and you can even have it take photos of them. There's a "Hello Rocky" voice assistant that works without Wi-Fi, plus Matter, Alexa, Siri and Google Home support.

The camera also enables video calls and a live patrol of the house. Handy if you want to check on the dog, but it's still a cloud-connected camera driving around your home. Go through the privacy settings in the app and turn off what you don't need.

## Battery and coverage

The battery is 6,400 mAh and takes 4 hours to fully charge. Modan says it's good for about 200 m², which is more than most Israeli apartments need. It also supports off-peak charging, handy if you're on a time-of-use electricity plan. The dust container is 2.7 litres.

## Price in Israel

The Saros 20 Flow Complete costs ₪4,990 (about $1,595) in white or black, with a 36-month warranty from the official importer. It's sold at Bug, the electronics chains and on Modan's official site. It also carries ECARF Allergy Friendly certification.

![Price comparison of the Saros 20 Flow Complete and Roborock's two new handheld wet-dry vacs in Israel](img/posts/roborock-saros-20-lineup-prices-en.webp "Roborock's three new models in Israel")

Alongside the robot, Modan launched two handheld wet-dry vacs: the F25 Pro Turbo at ₪2,190 (about $700) and the F25 Ultra Steam Gen 2 at ₪3,290 (about $1,052). If you mostly mop once a week and don't want a big dock in the house, one of those may do. If you want clean floors without lifting a finger, the robot is the way to go.

## What's not perfect

Price comes first. ₪4,990 is a lot for a robot, even one that does this much. Second, the dock is big and heavy. Third, some spec figures don't line up, such as a 301-minute runtime in quick mode and a 543.7 m² maximum area, against the 200 m² quoted elsewhere on the same page. I treat 200 m² as the conservative number. And fourth, roller mops are fairly new, so there's no data yet on how long the roller lasts or what a replacement costs.

## The bottom line

8/10

The Saros 20 Flow Complete is built for the Israeli home: hard floors, balcony thresholds and low furniture. The constantly rinsed roller, hot water and slim body tackle exactly what's frustrating about other robots. It's expensive and the dock takes up room, but if you want one robot that genuinely mops the whole place, this is one of the most interesting models to arrive here this year.
:::

**What I loved**

- A 270 mm roller mop that's rinsed with fresh water nonstop
- A dock that refills hot water and washes and dries the roller
- 8.98 cm tall, with lidar that retracts under furniture
- Climbs two-step thresholds up to 8.8 cm
- Stain and pet recognition, plus Matter support
- A 3-year importer warranty

**What I didn't**

- ₪4,990 is steep
- The dock is big and heavy (10.5 kg)
- Battery and coverage figures in the spec sheet don't agree
- A connected camera at home means you need to sort the privacy settings
- No word yet on replacement roller pricing
