Intel stock fell 28.6% on Friday, October 30. That's its worst single day since July 5, 1974, when it lost about 30%. Roughly $140 billion (about ₪437 billion) of market value disappeared in one session, and one of the reasons sits in Israel, at Fab 28 in Kiryat Gat.

## What the results showed

Intel's third-quarter profit actually beat analyst estimates. The trouble was the fourth-quarter forecast, which came in below expectations, and two things in it spooked investors. The first was the PC outlook: PC shipments are expected to fall about 12% this year and another 10% or so in 2027. The second was the shutdown at its Israeli plant.

Even after the plunge, the stock is still up about 80% for the year. But measured from its June 22 peak, it has now lost more than half its value.

## Fab 28: what we know

An explosion at the Kiryat Gat plant killed two workers and seriously injured a third. The fab was shut for about two weeks afterwards.

CEO Lip-Bu Tan said that "an unfortunate and tragic accident led to an extended shutdown in our Israel factory." CFO David Zinsner put a number on it: about $300 million (about ₪935 million) of revenue lost in the fourth quarter, and another $300 million or so in the first quarter of 2027. He said the shutdown also knocks roughly 200 basis points (2 percentage points) off gross margin.

## Why this fab matters so much

Fab 28 makes almost all of Intel's 10nm server products. Demand for those chips is strong right now, partly thanks to the AI spending wave, and Intel has nowhere else to build them at that volume.

Bernstein analyst Stacy Rasgon pointed out that without the shutdown, the 38% gross margin guidance would have been about 40%. In other words, take Kiryat Gat out and the numbers look very different.

Zinsner added a line that explains why it stings: "We expect the industry to remain supply-constrained throughout 2027 and into 2028." When chips are that scarce, every week a fab sits idle is revenue that goes to someone else.

## A bad day all round

Intel wasn't alone. The S&P 500 fell 1.8% on Friday and the Nasdaq 2.9%, dragged down mostly by worries over Big Tech's huge AI spending. Nvidia lost 4.2%.

## What it means for Israel

Intel is one of Israel's largest private employers, and Kiryat Gat is the core of its operations here. For now management is talking about a temporary shutdown and getting production back. But after an accident that killed two people, this won't be settled on a spreadsheet. Safety at the plant will stay in focus long after the lines restart.
