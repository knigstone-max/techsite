Cordless wet-dry vacuums have become a normal thing in Israeli homes. Tile and porcelain floors, dust blowing in from outside and kids dropping food are exactly what these machines are built for. The Xiaomi Mijia Wet Dry Vacuum 5 Pro, which Hamilton started selling this month, tries to add one more layer to the formula: **steam**. On top of vacuuming and mopping, it sprays 160°C steam on stains, and its dock washes the roller in hot water and dries it with hot air.

The importer's price is **₪2,490**, about $767. In Eilat, where there's no VAT, it's ₪2,110, about $650.

I went through the full spec sheet and feature list. Here's what matters before you buy.

![Xiaomi Wet Dry Vacuum 5 Pro at a glance: importer and Eilat prices, weight, tank sizes and runtime in each of the four modes](img/posts/xiaomi-wet-dry-vacuum-5-pro-glance-en.webp "The numbers that matter. Graphic: TechSite")

## What's in the box

You get the vacuum itself (model F302HW), a charging and self-cleaning dock (F302-JZ), a 1-litre clean water tank and a 0.7-litre dirty water tank, the roller, a filter, a cleaning brush and a power cable for the dock. The dock weighs 2.3 kg and measures 38.6 by 31.8 cm, so it needs a permanent corner near an outlet.

## Design and handling

The first number that jumped out at me is the weight: **6.1 kg** for the main unit. That's heavy for something you push around the whole home, and heavier still if you live somewhere with stairs and need to carry it between floors.

Xiaomi tries to make up for it in two ways. The first is **self-propulsion**. A central wheel with a direct-drive motor pushes the vacuum forward and lets it turn a full 360 degrees. On paper that should make it feel lighter than it is, and on wet-dry vacuums with powered wheels it usually does work on flat floors. The second is that it **lies fully flat**. You can lower the body to 180 degrees, almost parallel to the floor, and reach up to 114 cm under sofas and beds. Xiaomi says the head still presses down with 6.15 kg of force even in that position.

The body has an LED display showing the cleaning mode, battery, errors, a full dirty-tank warning and the drying cycle. There are voice prompts too. The floor head has a blue light that lights up dark corners and the space under furniture. It sounds like a gimmick, but that kind of light really does help you spot crumbs and hair on a light floor.

## Vacuuming and mopping

Suction is rated at **26,000Pa**, and the roller spins at 400 rpm. That's high for this category, and it should handle dry dirt, wet mess and the combination of the two, like cereal and milk on the kitchen floor.

There are four modes:

- **Standard** for daily cleaning.
- **Water pickup** for a wet bathroom floor or the puddle left after mopping.
- **High power** for stubborn stains.
- **Steam** for dried-on mess.

The dirty water tank separates solids from liquid, and that's a real improvement in day-to-day maintenance. If you've owned a wet-dry vacuum, you know the unpleasant moment of pouring grey sludge into the sink.

## Steam

This is the headline feature. Xiaomi says the steam leaves the outlet at 160°C, at 2,850 mg per minute and a 45-degree spray angle, covering about 125 square centimetres per second. One button turns it on.

Where does that help? Grease spots near the stove, sauce that dried under the table, shoe marks by the front door. In those places regular mopping sometimes just smears things around, and heat can help break them down.

There's a clear limit, though. In steam mode the battery lasts **up to 20 minutes**. If you take Xiaomi's coverage figure literally, 20 minutes at 125 square centimetres per second comes to about 15 square metres of continuous steaming. That's my arithmetic, not a Xiaomi figure, but it makes the point: steam is for spot work in the kitchen and entryway, not for mopping the whole apartment.

One more thing. Before you steam parquet, laminate or delicate stone, check your flooring manufacturer's guidance. Heat and moisture don't suit every surface.

## Hair

If you live with pets or long hair, you know this is the real test for any vacuum. This one has a PPA comb with 4 mm teeth set at 15 degrees, a four-stage anti-tangle system and a wide V-shaped intake. Xiaomi claims it can clear up to 4,500 strands of hair, wet long hair included. I can't verify that exact number, but the approach is right. Hair wrapping around a wet roller is the most annoying problem in this whole category.

## Battery and runtime

The battery is a 4,000mAh lithium-ion pack at 21.6 volts. Xiaomi's runtime figures:

- Up to **40 minutes** in water pickup mode, which Xiaomi says covers about 340 square metres.
- Up to **38 minutes** in standard mode.
- Up to **30 minutes** on high power.
- Up to **20 minutes** with steam.

For a typical Israeli apartment, 38 minutes in standard mode should cover a full pass. In a bigger home the 1-litre clean tank might run dry before the battery does, but Xiaomi hasn't said how much floor a litre covers. Full charging time isn't listed in the importer's specs either.

## The dock

Xiaomi did a good job here. When you put the vacuum back, the dock soaks and rinses the roller in **100°C** water, then dries it with a stream of hot air at a similar temperature while gently pressing it. The rated noise during drying is up to 45 decibels.

Drying is the important part. A roller left wet in its dock starts to smell within a day or two, especially in an Israeli summer. Hot-air drying is what separates a wet-dry vacuum you like using from one you start to dread. The dock is rated at 1,000 watts, so every cleaning cycle does use power.

## Warranty and service

This needs checking. The product page on Hamilton's site lists **one year on the device and one year on the battery**. The announcement for Xiaomi's seven new vacuums talked about two years on every model. Ask at the checkout what the warranty card actually says.

If you buy by December 31, 2026, you also get a year of VIP service, meaning pickup from your home and return after repair. You have to register for it, and the importer's terms apply.

## Who it's for

It suits a home with lots of hard floors, kitchen stains, kids or pets, and owners who don't mind giving the dock a permanent spot. The Wet Dry Vacuum 5 Pro covers almost everything you'd want from the category and adds steam for dried-on stains.

If you live with stairs, or you want something light to grab for two minutes, you'll feel those 6.1 kg. And if you'd rather the cleaning happened without you, a robot mop is worth a look. Xiaomi's Robot Vacuum 6 costs ₪3,190 from the same importer.

## The bottom line

7.5/10

The Xiaomi Wet Dry Vacuum 5 Pro gives you a lot for ₪2,490: strong suction, 160°C steam for stains, a body that lies flat under furniture, powered wheels and a dock that rinses in boiling water and dries with hot air. The weight, the 20-minute steam limit and the one-year-versus-two-year warranty question pull it down a bit. If you want a wet-dry vacuum that can also deal with tough kitchen stains, it deserves a serious look.

**What I liked**

- 160°C steam for dried-on stains
- 26,000Pa suction and a 400 rpm roller
- Lies flat at 180 degrees, reaching 114 cm under furniture
- Powered wheels that make pushing easier
- A dock that rinses at 100°C and dries with hot air
- A dirty tank that separates solids from liquid

**What I didn't**

- 6.1 kg is heavy to carry around
- Only 20 minutes in steam mode
- The product page lists a one-year warranty
- No published charging time
- The dock takes up space and needs a permanent outlet
