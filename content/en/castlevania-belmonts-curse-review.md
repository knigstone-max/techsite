It's been more than a decade since we got a genuinely new Castlevania, not a collection of old games and not a mobile spin-off. Meanwhile, the genre Castlevania helped invent kept going without it. Hollow Knight, Ori, Bloodstained and Dead Cells all took its ideas and ran. So Konami's choice to give the series to Evil Empire and Motion Twin, the teams behind Dead Cells, feels like things coming full circle.

Castlevania: Belmont's Curse came out on October 15, 2026 for PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Nintendo Switch, Steam and Xbox on PC. There's no Hebrew, but you get English subtitles and plenty of other languages. After a lot of hours with it, I can say this is more than a decent comeback. It understands what made Castlevania special, and it isn't afraid to change things.

## Paris, 1499

The story picks up 23 years after Castlevania III: Dracula's Curse. Paris is burning, monsters are crawling out of the shadows, and a menacing castle looms over the city. Trevor Belmont, the hero who beat Dracula in that game, heads into the burning streets, but not alone. With him is his daughter, Rose Belmont, carrying the family's holy whip, the Vampire Killer.

Rose is the lead, and she quickly gets pulled into a much bigger conspiracy. I'll stay vague, because finding out where it goes is half the fun. I will say that late-medieval Paris is a great setting. Instead of yet another Transylvanian castle, you get rooftops, cathedrals, narrow streets and fire everywhere. One of my favourite moments is a run across the rooftops with Notre-Dame burning in the background.

## The whip is the heart of it

If there's one thing this game does better than any Castlevania before it, it's the whip. In the classics, the whip was just a weapon. Here it's also how you move. You latch onto grapple points and swing, like a trapeze artist, to cross gaps, climb and come at enemies from angles they don't expect.

It takes some getting used to. For the first hour I felt like I was fighting the controls more than using them. Once it clicks, though, movement turns fluid and fun, and you start looking for creative ways to mix travel and attacks. Swing off a point straight into an enemy, catch another point mid-air, keep going. The game rewards exactly that, and that's when it's at its best.

Combat is fast. If you've played Dead Cells you'll recognise the rhythm: short strikes, dodges, and a lot of attention to timing. It's less stiff and deliberate than old-school Castlevania and closer to a modern action game, but every crack of the whip still feels like it matters.

## Weapons and Relics

The whip isn't your only tool. There are seven weapon categories to swap and combine, plus Relics that give strong offensive and defensive effects. You find most of them by exploring, which is a big reason to go back to old areas: there's nearly always something new waiting.

That mix gives you a lot of freedom. You can build something fast and aggressive, or play it safer with defence and ranged attacks. I never found one "right" build, and that's a good thing. The game leaves it up to you.

## Bosses that become cards

The most original part is the Arcana system. Like every Castlevania, there are familiar bosses: Death, Medusa and Carmilla. Next to them are new ones, the standout being Joan of Arc. The interesting bit is what happens after you win. Each boss gets sealed into one of Rose's tarot cards and becomes an Arcana, a power you can use for spells and special moves.

It's a simple, smart idea. It ties progress to your biggest victories, so beating a boss gives you more than relief. It actually changes what you can do. It fits the mood, too. Tarot cards, Death and medieval Paris go together nicely.

![Rose Belmont standing on a small metal walkway facing a giant Medusa with green snakes for hair, in a dark green stone hall](img/posts/castlevania-belmonts-curse-medusa.webp "Medusa, one of the series' familiar bosses. Image: Konami")

The fights themselves are among the best parts. Bosses are big, impressive, and demand that you learn their patterns. Some are very hard, and you'll probably lose the first time. But almost every time, I felt I lost because I messed up, not because the game was being unfair.

## Exploration and the map

As with the best Castlevania games, the map is half the experience. It mixes new areas with elements inspired by the classics, and series fans will spot plenty of nods. You can't reach everything at first. Only after you earn new abilities can you go back and open paths that were blocked.

It works well, though not perfectly. There were times I wasn't sure where to go next and spent too long wandering. If you like the feeling of getting lost and stumbling on something good, you'll be happy. If you prefer clear direction, it might annoy you.

![Rose Belmont mid-attack in a burst of colour in front of a huge astronomical clock of rings, in a gothic hall with purple windows](img/posts/castlevania-belmonts-curse-arcana.webp "One of the castle's gothic halls. Image: Konami")

## Looks and sound

It's gorgeous. The hand-drawn style has sharp lines and bold colour. Burning Paris looks stunning in orange and black, and the castle is full of cold greens and purples. It doesn't try to look like a '90s Castlevania, yet the gothic mood is intact. You can see the Dead Cells influence, which is fine. It looks like a new game, not a copy.

The soundtrack by Daniel ASADI is another highlight. Music has always been part of Castlevania's identity, and he respects that. Some tracks echo the classic themes, and there's lots of new material that suits Paris and the quick pace of combat. Voice acting is available in full in English and Japanese.

## Who it's for

This isn't an easy game. It's far from impossible, but it asks for patience and practice, especially against bosses. If you grew up on Castlevania, this is what you've waited ten years for. If you loved Dead Cells or Hollow Knight, it'll feel familiar while having its own identity. If you want something relaxing for the evening, look elsewhere.

For parents: it's rated T (13+) by the ESRB in the US and PEGI 16 in Europe. There's violence and blood, so keep that in mind.

## Price and platforms

On Steam in Israel it costs ₪107.73 (about $34.55), and the Midnight Edition is ₪143.65 (about $46.07). On the US Steam store it's $29.99. The Midnight Edition adds Trevor and Sypha style costumes, a Relic called Family's Grace, and a separate app with the soundtrack and more than 100 pieces of art and sketches from development. Unless you're a collector, the base game is plenty.

On Switch 2 you play the original Switch version through backward compatibility. There's no dedicated Switch 2 version. On PC the minimum specs are reasonable: a Core i5-8400, a GTX 1650 and 16GB of RAM.

## The bottom line

8.5/10

Castlevania: Belmont's Curse is the comeback series fans were waiting for. The whip as a movement tool, bosses that turn into tarot cards and a city on fire make it one of the best 2D action games of the year. It drops points for a clumsy first hour and stretches of the map where it's easy to get lost. At around ₪107, it's hard to argue with the value.
:::

**What I loved**

- The whip as a movement tool, which makes getting around a joy
- The Arcana system, tying bosses to progression
- Big, tough, fair boss fights
- Striking art and a great soundtrack
- A lowish price for the amount of game

**What I liked less**

- A clumsy first hour while you learn the whip
- Sometimes it's unclear where to go
- The difficulty won't suit everyone
- No dedicated Switch 2 version
