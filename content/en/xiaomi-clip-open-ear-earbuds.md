Clip-on earbuds are the gadget of the season. They don't go into the ear canal. They hook onto the edge of the ear, so you hear music and still hear the street, the bus driver and whoever's calling you from the next room. In October we covered the [OnePlus Buds Clip](/techsite/en/posts/oneplus-buds-clip-israel/) arriving in Israel, and on November 17 Xiaomi joined in with the **Xiaomi Clip**, announced globally alongside the [Xiaomi 18 Pro](/techsite/en/posts/xiaomi-18-pro-global-launch-paris/) and the [Watch S5 and Smart Band 11](/techsite/en/posts/xiaomi-smart-band-11-watch-s5-41mm-global/).

## Price and availability

- **Spain**: €149.99 (about ₪547, or $165)
- **UK**: £129.99 (about ₪567, or $170)
- Colours: Graphite Black, Pearl White, Sand Gold and Frost Purple

There's no Israeli price or date yet. Xiaomi hasn't announced a US launch either.

## The design

Each earbud weighs **5.5 grams**. The bridge between the part behind the ear and the speaker is a memory titanium wire, which Xiaomi says adapts to different ear shapes and keeps a steady grip. The speaker itself is a partly transparent sphere with a ring around the outlet that looks like a vinyl record. Not something you'll notice once it's on your ear, but it does look different from the white plastic most earbuds come in.

The case is round, wrapped in vegan leather, and you lift the buds straight out without having to turn or line them up. Xiaomi compares it to a jewellery box. It weighs 41.4 grams.

## Sound and calls

- **11 mm driver** with a 0.6 mm excursion and a metal-coated diaphragm, rated from 20Hz to 48kHz
- **LHDC 5.0**, along with AAC and SBC, over Bluetooth 5.4. LHDC works with Android phones that support it. On an iPhone you'll get AAC
- **Calls**: three microphones plus a bone-conduction voice pickup that "hears" your jaw vibrate rather than the air around you, along with a wind-noise algorithm
- **Leakage**: a 4 mm outlet aimed at the ear canal, and an antiphase wave that's meant to cut how much the person next to you hears

That last point matters with open earbuds. Since nothing seals your ear, the person next to you on the train can hear what you're listening to at high volume. Every maker in this category claims some fix for it, and how well it works can only be judged in real use.

## Battery

Xiaomi's figures, at 50% volume:

- **AAC**: up to 9 hours per charge, up to 38 hours with the case
- **LHDC 5.0**: up to 6 hours, up to 25 hours with the case
- **Calls**: up to 6 hours, up to 25 hours with the case
- **Fast charging**: 10 minutes gives up to 3.5 hours of music, as long as the case has at least 20% left

The earbuds are rated **IP57** for dust and water. The case isn't.

## The extras

- **Apple Find My**: you can find the case on Apple's network, and within Bluetooth range make it play a sound. According to TechRepublic, this works on the case, not on each bud.
- **Recording**: a triple-press on the case starts or stops recording, including live recording, audio and video recording, and call recording. If you record a call or a meeting, tell the other side.
- **Two devices at once**, audio switching between Xiaomi HyperOS devices, and the option to connect two pairs to one phone to share a film.
- **Taps**: double-tap to play or pause, triple-tap to skip a track. You can change them in the Xiaomi Earbuds app.

## Against the OnePlus Buds Clip

The OnePlus Buds Clip is already on sale in Israel through Cell Now for ₪499 (about $150). On paper:

- **OnePlus** has two drivers in each bud (11 mm and 9 mm), Bluetooth 6.1 and slightly longer battery life (9.5 and 40 hours with AAC). The buds weigh about 5.2 grams.
- **Xiaomi** has one driver, a higher water rating (IP57 against IP55), Apple Find My and the recording feature.

At European prices, the Xiaomi would come in a little above the OnePlus. Israeli prices rarely match a straight conversion, so the gap could go either way.

## Who it's for

People who walk, run or cycle and want to hear what's going on around them. People who work from home and want to hear the kids or the doorbell. iPhone owners will appreciate Find My, while Android owners with LHDC support get better sound quality.

Less suitable for: open-plan offices, noisy buses and trains, and anyone who wants proper noise cancelling. Open earbuds can't block outside noise, by design.
