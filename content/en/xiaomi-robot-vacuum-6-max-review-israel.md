The robot mop market in Israel has moved fast this autumn: rollers instead of spinning pads, hot water in the dock, robots that climb thresholds. The Xiaomi Robot Vacuum 6 Max, which importer Hamilton started selling last week, lands right in the middle of that fight, and at a lower price than the competition.

At **₪4,690** (about $1,455) it's the most expensive model Xiaomi sells here, yet still a few hundred shekels below the Roborock Saros 20 Flow Complete and the ECOVACS DEEBOT X12S we've covered. The question is where those savings show.

## Design and build

Xiaomi has gone with a new colour here: a warm metallic grey instead of the usual white or black. It looks good, more like furniture than an appliance. A light ring on top changes with the robot's state: white while working, blue when it connects to the network, flashing orange when something needs checking. It's a small touch that saves you opening the app just to see what happened.

The body is 35 cm across and 9 cm tall with the LiDAR lowered. Yes, the LiDAR lowers. When the robot spots a low gap, under a sofa or bed for example, the top sensor retracts and it can get into spaces from 9.3 cm high. That's useful, but the Saros 20 is still slimmer. If your sofa sits especially low, measure before you buy.

The dock is tall: 58 cm, and 47 cm deep with the ramp. It needs a proper spot. You won't tuck it under a sideboard.

## Suction: 35,000Pa and a brush that cuts hair

Xiaomi quotes **35,000Pa**, the highest it has offered so far. As always, that's a lab figure measured at the motor, and it says little about real differences between robots at home. What matters more day to day are the brushes: two side brushes designed so hair doesn't wrap around them, and a main brush with blades that cut hair as it works. In a home with a dog, or a kid with long hair, that's the difference between a robot that keeps going and one you're attacking with scissors every week.

On carpet, an ultrasonic sensor spots the rug, the roller lifts by 1 cm and suction goes up. Xiaomi itself recommends setting "carpet avoidance" for rugs thicker than 8 mm, with long pile or with fringes. So the Persian rug in the living room probably stays a job for the stick vacuum.

## Mopping: a roller instead of pads

This is the heart of the 6 Max. Instead of two spinning pads it uses a roller mop that gets a constant supply of clean water through **16 outlets**, presses down with steady pressure and cleans itself as it goes, so dirt doesn't get dragged from room to room. It's the same approach we saw on the Saros 20 and the eufy Omni S2, and once you're used to it, pads feel dated.

Xiaomi shows the roller lifting half-dried coffee and ketchup, exactly the kind of mark older robots just smeared around. What it won't do: a sticky spill that's fully dried under the table will still need a cloth and some elbow grease.

There are three mechanical arms too: a side brush that extends into corners, the roller itself shifting toward the wall, and a small round mop pad that swings out when the robot meets a corner or a chair leg. Xiaomi cites a TÜV Rheinland test showing 99.7% coverage around round table legs. In a typical Israeli home with a six-chair dining area, that's where most robots fall short.

## Obstacles: 6 cm

Two mechanical legs push out from underneath and lift the robot over a step of up to **6 cm**. In older Israeli apartments, with thresholds between the living room and the balcony or a small step into the kitchen, that solves a real problem. The Saros 20, for comparison, climbs up to 8.8 cm, so if you have a particularly high threshold, it still has the edge.

## Cameras and AI

Three front cameras, working with the LiDAR, recognise more than 320 types of objects and stains according to Xiaomi: 200 obstacles like cables, slippers and socks, 80 kinds of furniture and 47 kinds of stains. It can spot an earphone cable 3 mm thick. More interesting is what it does with that:

- **Dry debris:** it turns up suction.
- **Liquid:** it mops once, goes back to the dock to wash the roller, then makes a second pass.
- **Solid and liquid mixed** (say, spilled dog food in water): it steers around the area and sends you an app alert, rather than spreading it through the house.

On privacy: object recognition and the camera feed are off by default, and you have to switch them on yourself in the app. You can watch your home live and talk through the robot. Its data protection was certified to ETSI EN 303 645 by TÜV Rheinland. That doesn't make a roaming camera in your home an obvious choice, but at least the decision is yours.

## The dock: hot water, drying and a 75-day bag

![Cutaway of the Xiaomi Robot Vacuum 6 Max dock showing the path dust takes from the robot into the bag at the top](img/posts/xiaomi-robot-vacuum-6-max-dock-cutaway.webp "Where the dust goes. Image: Xiaomi")

After each run, the dock washes the roller in water at up to **85°C** and dries it with 50°C hot air. There are four wash temperatures and three drying durations. That's what stops the familiar wet-mop smell from building up in the dock. Dust goes into a sealed disposable bag, which Xiaomi says lasts up to **75 days** in a 120 m² home with three people and a pet.

There's an Israel-specific point here. When washing with hot water the dock pulls up to **1,200 watts**. If you've switched to a time-of-use tariff, from December 1 you'll pay 114.70 agorot per kWh between 17:00 and 22:00, against 46.63 agorot the rest of the day. Schedule cleaning for the morning or midday, not the evening.

## App, battery and daily use

Everything runs through Xiaomi Home: maps, no-go zones, schedules. If you own other Xiaomi gear like an air purifier, HyperOS Connect can chain them, for instance starting the purifier once cleaning finishes. Voice control works through Google Assistant and Alexa, in English. A child lock stops kids or cats from starting it by accident.

The battery is 6,400mAh and a full charge takes 4.5 hours. In a regular four- or five-room apartment that shouldn't be a bottleneck, and if it is, the robot recharges and carries on.

## Price and competition

- **Xiaomi Robot Vacuum 6 Max:** ₪4,690 (about $1,455)
- **eufy Omni S2:** roughly ₪4,500 (about $1,395) when we reviewed it
- **Roborock Saros 20 Flow Complete:** ₪4,990 (about $1,548)
- **ECOVACS DEEBOT X12S:** ₪4,999 (about $1,551)

The Saros 20 still climbs higher and gets into lower spaces. The X12S deals better with dried stains thanks to its pre-scrub spray, and its dock is bagless. The 6 Max answers with price, the corner arm and a dock that washes very hot. Hamilton gives a two-year warranty, and anyone who buys by December 31 also gets a year of VIP service with home pickup, subject to registration and terms.

## The bottom line

8/10

The Xiaomi Robot Vacuum 6 Max delivers most of what its nearly ₪5,000 rivals offer: a self-cleaning roller mop, a corner arm, 6 cm threshold climbing and a dock that washes at 85°C and dries with hot air. It doesn't win every category, and the Saros 20 still leads on climbing and height. But at ₪4,690, with an importer warranty and local service, it's one of the most balanced robot mops you can buy right now.
