Most platformers ask you to climb. Where the Seeds Fall asks for the opposite. Every stage is a descent, branch after branch, from heights where the clouds are below you, and your main enemy is gravity. A jump down can't be undone, and that's what turns such a simple game into such a tense one.

It's the first release from CygamesEdge, the new label Cygames (the company behind Granblue Fantasy and Umamusume) set up to let small teams make experimental games. It was co-developed with studio ZEREO and came out on November 4 and 5 for PS5, Switch 2 and Steam, digital only. It costs ₪39.99 on Israeli Steam, and the US list price is $9.99 (about ₪32).

![Where the Seeds Fall at a glance: $9.99 or ₪39.99 on Steam, about 3 hours of story, PS5, Switch 2 and PC, post-game modes, rating and languages](img/posts/where-the-seeds-fall-overview-en.webp "The short version. Graphic: TechSite")

## The story

The game's world has been hit by a disease called the Blight, which poisoned the ground. People fled upwards and built their homes on the branches of the World Tree, a tree the size of a mountain range. In this world there are children called Fell Children, and people see them as omens of disaster.

You play a soldier who has lost everything. He meets one of these children, she reminds him of the daughter he once had, and he decides to disobey orders and take her down to the surface. It's a quiet story, with very little text and no voice acting at all. The bond between the man and the girl is built from looks, gestures and small moments along the way. It's moving, but if you've played a few broken-man-protects-a-child games, you'll see where it's heading.

## How it plays

The idea is simple, which is why it works. You need to get from a high point to a low one. Along the way there are beams that crumble if you stand on them too long, gusts of wind that push you sideways mid-jump, and drops too long to survive.

![The character clings to an iron cage hanging from a long chain, next to two more cages, above a cliff and an abyss](img/posts/where-the-seeds-fall-cages.webp "Sometimes the way down goes through hanging cages. Image: Cygames")

Your main tool is the flower buds that grow on the tree. You can pick one up, carry it and throw it where you want to land. Where it hits, a flower blooms, and it cushions a fall that would otherwise kill you. That's where the planning comes in: how many buds you have, where to throw them, and whether the wind will push you off the flower on the way down.

The camera looks down at an angle, so when you peer over the edge you see all the height still to go. That's the game at its best. Before a big jump you stop, check the wind, look at the next branch that seems too far away, and commit. When a long descent goes off without a mistake, it feels fantastic.

## Tense, not infuriating

The game clearly takes inspiration from punishing climbing games like Jump King and Getting Over It, where one small mistake sends you back half an hour. Where the Seeds Fall is far more forgiving. The challenge is reading the terrain and planning, not pixel-perfect precision. You won't want to throw your controller at the wall.

That makes it much more approachable, even if you don't like hard games. If you came for the suffering of a rage climber, you'll find it too easy, at least in the main story.

![The character leaps between huge rock fragments in a cloud of orange dust with green leaves flying](img/posts/where-the-seeds-fall-dust-fall.webp "Some stages swap the blues for orange. Image: Cygames")

## How it looks

The World Tree is the real star. The early stages are painted in turquoise and blue, with clouds, birds and crumbling stone ruins. Other areas switch to oranges and browns, with the air full of dust. The style is soft and painterly rather than realistic, and the sense of scale when you look down works beautifully.

## Length and what comes after

The main story takes about three hours. The developers say so themselves, and it's accurate. After the credits you unlock harder maps and a Time Trial mode, and Steam has leaderboards. Speedrunners will find plenty here. If you're playing for the story, you'll finish in an evening, maybe two.

## Where to play it

It works on all three platforms, and to me Switch 2 is its natural home. Short, focused sessions are perfect for handheld play. On PC the requirements are reasonable but not low: at minimum a GTX 1660 with 6GB, 16GB of RAM, Windows 11 and an SSD. HDR is supported.

The interface and subtitles come in English, Japanese, Chinese and Korean. There's no Hebrew, but there's barely any text either, so it won't get in anyone's way.

## What works less well

- **It's too short.** Three hours is fine at this price, but just as the mechanics get interesting, the story ends.
- **The story is predictable.** The emotional beats land, but they don't surprise.
- **It's one mechanic.** Buds and wind are all there is. There are variations, but not many new ideas along the way.

## Who it's for

Anyone who likes short games built on one strong idea, Switch 2 owners looking for something for the commute, and people who enjoy the tension of climbing games but hate the frustration. If you measure games in hours per shekel, wait for a sale. There's a free demo on Steam that gives a good picture of the full game, so try it before you buy.

## The bottom line

7/10

Where the Seeds Fall is a confident start for CygamesEdge: a small, polished game built around one good idea that it doesn't stretch too far. Every jump feels like a gamble, the World Tree is gorgeous, and the quiet story does a lot with few words. It's short and predictable, but at ₪40 it's a great evening. My main problem with it is that I wanted more.
:::

**What I liked**

- A simple, tense core idea
- The World Tree and the sense of height
- Challenging without being frustrating
- A quiet story that works without words
- Fair price and a free demo

**What I didn't**

- Over in about three hours
- A predictable story
- Few new ideas along the way
