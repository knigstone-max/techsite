In 2024, Metaphor: ReFantazio was one of the most talked-about games of the year. Two years later it's coming to Switch 2, tomorrow, November 12. If you missed it on PlayStation, Xbox or PC, here's a chance to play it on the go. If you've played it, you know what's in store. The only real question is how well it runs on Nintendo's console, and the answer is messier than I'd like.

The game comes from Atlus's Studio Zero, the team led by Katsura Hashino, who directed Persona 3, 4 and 5. Characters are by Shigenori Soejima and the music by Shoji Meguro, the same names that made Persona what it is. But Metaphor takes all of that into a completely different world.

![Metaphor: ReFantazio on Switch 2 at a glance: ₪149 at Mediagame, ₪169 at Player1 and $39.99 on the US eShop](img/posts/metaphor-switch-2-overview-en.webp "What you get, and what it costs. Graphic: TechSite")

## The story: an election after a murder

There's no Japanese high school here. There's a kingdom called Euchronia, home to several races, with rigid classes and plenty of prejudice. The king has been murdered, and the killer is Louis Guiabarn, a low-born, deeply charismatic army officer who promises to tear down the system. The heir, the prince, lies cursed and unconscious.

Then, at the funeral, a spell the king left behind announces that the crown will pass to whoever wins the people's favour. From that moment the game becomes an election campaign: a royal tournament where candidates compete for popularity. You play a young man trying to save his friend the prince, and to do it he has to enter the race himself, with a small, cheeky fairy named Gallica at his side.

The game isn't subtle. It deals with racism, class, fear and hope, and its title tells you it's holding up a mirror to our world. But it does it through genuinely good characters. Heismay, who fights prejudice against his own race while carrying a grudge of his own. Brigitta, a stern shopkeeper who melts the moment she meets a stray puppy. And Louis himself, a villain you can't take your eyes off.

![A street in the royal capital, with old European-style buildings, carriages and townsfolk, and the hero walking down the middle](img/posts/metaphor-refantazio-grand-trad.webp "The royal capital, Grand Trad. Most of your social life happens here. Image: ATLUS")

## A calendar, virtues and friends

If you've played Persona, the structure will feel familiar. There's a calendar, and each day is split into time slots. In each one you decide what to do: head into a dungeon, spend time with a friend, work on one of your virtues, or push a side quest forward. Time is limited, so every choice feels like it counts.

Virtues are Metaphor's take on Persona's social stats. There are five, including wisdom, courage and eloquence, and you raise them by reading books, talking to people, giving speeches and doing things around town. Higher ranks open up dialogue options and relationships. Those bonds, with party members and other characters, give you perks in battle and unlock their personal stories, some of the best writing in the game.

![The hero standing before a crowd of townspeople, with an on-screen message that his speech improved his eloquence](img/posts/metaphor-refantazio-speech-eloquence.webp "A good speech raises your eloquence. That's how virtues work. Image: ATLUS")

## Combat: Atlus's best yet

This is where Metaphor really stands out. Combat blends two things. Out in the field you can hit enemies in real time with your sword. An enemy far weaker than you dies in one hit, no battle screen at all, a feature I'd love every RPG to copy. A tougher enemy can be stunned with a strike, letting you start a turn-based fight with the advantage. And if an enemy catches you from behind, you start in trouble.

The battles themselves use Shin Megami Tensei's turn system: hit a weakness or land a critical and you earn an extra action, miss or hit an element the enemy absorbs and you lose turns. It rewards thinking, and every boss is a small puzzle.

On top of that sits the Archetype system, which is closer to Final Fantasy's job system than to Personas. There are more than 40 Archetypes, like Warrior, Mage, Healer or Merchant, and each of your seven party members can switch between them. You unlock them through social bonds and level them up in battle. A currency called MAG lets you carry skills from one Archetype to another, which leaves a lot of room to experiment.

Be warned that difficulty spikes. Some bosses will send you back to swap Archetypes or grind a little before you can win.

![A turn-based fight against a huge, colourful boss, with the battle interface and turn icons on the side](img/posts/metaphor-refantazio-boss-battle.webp "A boss fight. Hitting a weakness earns an extra turn. Image: ATLUS")

## Dungeons and the journey

You cross the kingdom in the Gauntlet Runner, a giant armoured vehicle on mechanical legs that doubles as your mobile base, stopping at towns and dungeons along the way. The main dungeons are impressive and memorable. The side dungeons less so: brown tunnels and foggy forests that look much the same, and are easy to get lost in.

All of it comes with a superb score. Shoji Meguro wrote something very different from Persona, with Buddhist chant influences, and the battle theme, with priest Keisuke Honryo's chanting, is one of the best ever heard in an RPG. The interface and menus, as usual with Atlus, are a work of art in their own right.

On length: the main story with side content runs well past 80 hours. This is not a weekend game.

## The Switch 2 version: the problem

Here comes the "but". There are three graphics modes, Graphics, Balanced and Performance, both handheld and docked. In all three the frame rate is uncapped and aims for 60 frames per second. None of them holds it.

Performance mode hits 60 more often but looks rough: obvious blur and a coarse image, like something from the PS3 era. Graphics mode looks best and gives the most stable image, but its frame rate jumps around, sometimes even in menus. That's annoying, because Metaphor is a very menu-heavy game. I'd still pick Graphics mode, and hope for a patch that adds a 30 or 40 fps cap.

There are small upsides. The image shimmers less in motion than on the other consoles, and there's touchscreen support. Booting the game is quick, but moving between areas in-game is slow: about 10 to 11 seconds, versus 5 to 6 on PS5. When you cross areas dozens of times a night, that adds up.

The obvious comparison is Persona 3 Reload, which also launched on Switch 2 in poor shape and later got a performance update that fixed a lot. There's no promise Atlus will do the same here, but there's a precedent.

## Price, and what to know

In Israel I've seen it at ₪149 (about $47) at Mediagame and ₪169 (about $53) at Player1. On the US eShop it's $39.99 (about ₪126). There's also a 35th Anniversary Edition at $69.99 (about ₪221) and a Guidebook Edition at $49.99 (about ₪158). Atlus is cutting the price on the other platforms alongside this launch, too.

Two things to know before you buy:

- **The boxed copy is a Game-Key Card.** The box doesn't hold the game, just a key that downloads it. The download is about 62 GB, a big slice of the console's storage.
- **There's no Hebrew.** Text is in English (and other languages, not Hebrew), with English or Japanese voices. There's a lot of reading, so solid English is a must.

And some good news: there's a free demo on the eShop with the first four dungeons and seven Archetypes to unlock, and your save carries over to the full game. If you're on the fence, start there and see for yourself whether the performance bothers you.

:::score
## The bottom line

8/10

Metaphor: ReFantazio is an exceptional RPG: a story with something to say, characters who stay with you, what may be the best combat system Atlus has built, and a soundtrack you won't shake. On Switch 2 it gains portability and a lower price, and loses ground on an unstable frame rate, a blurry Performance mode and long load times. If you haven't played it and Switch 2 is your only option, play it. If you can wait for a patch, wait.
:::

**What I liked**

- A grown-up story with a great villain
- Combat that mixes real-time and turn-based play
- An Archetype system with lots of room to experiment
- One of the best soundtracks in the genre
- A low price and a generous demo

**What I liked less**

- An unstable frame rate in every mode
- A blurry Performance mode
- Slow transitions between areas
- Side dungeons that all look alike
- A boxed copy that's just a key, and no Hebrew
