Electric shavers come in two camps. Rotary models have round heads that spin, and foil models have a thin mesh with blades moving side to side underneath. Panasonic is one of the veterans of the second camp, and the Arc6 is its top model: the world's first foil shaver with six blades.

In Israel it's sold as the ES-LS9A, which comes with a clean-and-charge station. The official importer is Elias, with a 12-month warranty. iCell currently lists it at ₪2,479 (about $782) on sale, down from a regular ₪2,699 (about $851). In VAT-free Eilat it drops to ₪2,099 (about $662).

I haven't shaved with it for a month. I went through the official specs, store prices here and abroad, and what published testing has shown so far. The short version up front: if you shave every day and want the closest thing to a blade without using one, this is probably the best shaver Panasonic sells. If you shave twice a week, there are far cheaper options.

## The head: six blades, 22 directions

The head is the heart of the Arc6. Six hypoallergenic Japanese stainless steel blades sit under a thin foil. More blades means each pass catches more hairs, so you go over the same spot fewer times, and fewer passes means less irritation. The previous Arc5 had five.

The head sits on what Panasonic calls a 22D joint, meaning it moves in 22 directions and follows your jaw, chin and neck. In practice you don't have to press hard or twist your wrist to get under the chin. It's also the part you notice most when stepping up from a cheaper shaver.

There's a pop-up trimmer on the back for sideburns and mustache. It won't replace a clipper, but it's fine for tidying lines.

## The motor and the sensor

Inside is a linear motor that, together with the six blades, delivers 84,000 cutting actions a minute, one of the highest figures on the market. Next to it is a beard sensor that keeps checking how dense the hair is and turns the motor up or down. On the cheeks, where the beard is thinner, it works gently. Under the chin and on the neck, where it's thicker, it speeds up.

Published testing keeps landing on the same result: a very close shave, near what you'd get from a razor, with almost no redness even after several passes. With shaving foam it's even better. If you have stubborn flat-lying hairs that grow almost parallel to the skin, a rotary shaver may handle them better.

## Wet, dry and travel

The ES-LS9A is waterproof. Shave dry, shave in the shower with gel or foam, and rinse it under the tap at the end. That sounds obvious, but plenty of cheaper models fall short here.

The battery charges fully in an hour and lasts about 50 minutes of shaving, roughly two to three weeks of daily use. A 3-minute quick charge gets you one shave if you forgot. There's a 5-LED battery display, a travel lock so it doesn't switch on in your bag, and a carry pouch. It runs on 100 to 240 volts, so abroad you only need a plug adapter. It weighs 220g without the cap, measures 172×72×59mm and is made in Japan.

## The cleaning station

The station is what makes the ES-LS9A the expensive model in the range. You drop the shaver in upside down, and the station cleans the head, dries it and charges the battery. Anyone who has owned a shaver with a station like this knows it's hard to go back: always clean, always charged, and no taking the head apart to rinse it every few days.

The downside is the running cost. Stations like this usually use a cleaning fluid that needs replacing now and then, and the foil and blades wear out and need replacing over time. Before buying, ask the importer what the spare parts cost, because over a few years it adds up.

## The price, and the price abroad

This is the less pleasant part. On Panasonic's US store, the same ES-LS9A with the station costs $499.99 (about ₪1,585). The ES-LS8A without the station is $399.99 (about ₪1,268). So even at the sale price you pay almost ₪900 more here than in the US, a gap of about 56%.

Some of that is VAT and import costs, and some is the local warranty. On a ₪2,500 device with a motor, a battery and a station, an official importer warranty is worth something. If you're still thinking of bringing one from abroad, remember that blades and foils will have to come from somewhere too.

## What it competes with

The obvious alternative is Panasonic's own Arc5. Models like the ES-LV67 or ES-LV9C sell here for around ₪1,200 (about $378), half the price, and give a nearly as close shave with less comfort. The difference between five and six blades is real, but it isn't double. If you shave less than twice a week, you won't feel most of the Arc6's advantages, and the extra money won't come back to you.

## The bottom line

8/10

The Panasonic Arc6 ES-LS9A is the closest thing to a razor Panasonic makes, with an excellent flexible head, a strong motor with a beard sensor, full water resistance and a station that takes care of the upkeep. It loses points for costing far more than it does abroad, for the running costs of the station and spare parts, and because the Arc5 gets you almost the same result for half the price. If you shave every day and are willing to pay for the convenience, it's worth it.
:::

**What I liked**

- A very close shave with almost no irritation
- The 22D head reaches every angle
- A beard sensor that adjusts the power
- Wet and dry, rinse under the tap
- A station that cleans, dries and charges

**What I liked less**

- About 56% more expensive than in the US
- Running costs for the station and spare parts
- The Arc5 gets close for half the price
- Less suited to stubborn flat-lying hairs
