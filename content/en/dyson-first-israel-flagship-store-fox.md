Until now, seeing a Dyson up close in Israel meant finding one on a shelf at an electronics chain, wedged between a dozen other vacuums. Since Thursday evening (Oct 29), Dyson has a store of its own: its first Israeli flagship, at Big Fashion Glilot, just north of Tel Aviv.

The store is run by Harel Wiesel's Fox Group, which took over the Dyson franchise this year. It's split into two zones. One covers the home, with robot vacuums, smart cleaning gear and air purifiers. The other is for beauty, with Dyson's hair dryers and stylers.

## A new importer after 30 years

This started back in February. According to Calcalist, Fox signed a five-year exclusive distribution deal with Dyson, ending roughly 30 years in which B.N.Z.C, owned by the Cohen family, imported the brand. The split reportedly came down to disagreements over the beauty category and over opening flagship stores. Dyson also talked to Electra Consumer and Newpan before picking Fox.

Ynet estimates Dyson's Israeli sales at more than ₪200 million a year (about $64 million). Fox said at the time that it wants Israeli prices to line up with European ones and plans a dedicated online store. The Glilot store, about 200 square metres, was originally planned under the previous importer.

## What the robotics lead said

Guy Turner, the senior engineer who leads robotics at Dyson, flew in for the opening. Talking to Israel Hayom, he said Dyson's toughest competitor is Dyson itself a year from now. He also said the company is as committed to Israel as to any other market it operates in.

According to Turner, Dyson's robots can recognise more than 200 kinds of objects. The idea is for a robot to tell a spill it should mop from a sock or a cable it should steer around, instead of swallowing it.

The best example in the store is the Spot+Scrub Ai, a robot vacuum and mop that lights up the floor in green to spot stains. Dyson Israel's site lists it at ₪4,899 (about $1,571). That puts it up against the top Roborock and Ecovacs models, so it's worth comparing before you buy.

## Next up: a toothbrush

The launch came with one more reveal. Dyson plans to bring an oral care product to Israel. According to the report, it cleans between your teeth with a jet of water instead of floss, and took seven years to develop. It's expected in Israel early next year at about $500 (around ₪1,559). There's no official Israeli price yet.

## What it means if you're buying

Its own store gives Dyson room to show the whole range, including models that don't always reach the chains. If Fox really brings prices in line with Europe, that could be the bigger change. Until then, check the price both on the official site and at the chains, and make sure what you buy carries the official importer's warranty.
