Amdocs is one of the veteran software companies born in Israel, with a large campus in Ra'anana and a client list that includes some of the world's biggest mobile carriers. Last week it had one of the worst days in its history. Its annual report, released on November 3, included what read almost like a side note: Amdocs and US carrier T-Mobile have each sent the other notices claiming a material breach of the agreements between them.

The next day, according to Calcalist, the shares opened about 30% lower, a level not seen since 2014. In after-hours trading on report day they had been down as much as 45% before recovering some ground. Roughly $2 billion (about ₪6.4 billion) of market value was wiped off a company worth around $6.5 billion.

## What happened with T-Mobile

According to Amdocs, both sides are threatening to terminate the agreement or agreements and/or seek monetary and other relief, including in arbitration. Amdocs says it is ready to press its claims and defend itself, and that, as with any litigation, the outcome is uncertain.

What is the fight about? Nobody is saying. CEO Shimie Hortig, who took over about six months ago, told analysts he would not go into details because of confidentiality, only that the company is prepared to "fiercely defend" its positions. According to Light Reading, he did say the dispute centers on a recently signed agreement and escalated last quarter. T-Mobile declined to comment.

It is worth being clear on one point: nothing has been terminated, and Amdocs is still providing services to T-Mobile. But its outlook for next year already allows for a scenario where the agreements end.

## Why it hurts so much

T-Mobile is not just any customer. Research firm Omdia, cited by Light Reading, estimates it accounts for about a fifth of Amdocs' revenue. On top of the dispute, two other things are weighing on the numbers: T-Mobile has cut its discretionary spending with Amdocs, and a large one-off project moving UScellular customers onto T-Mobile's systems wrapped up in August.

The result is a grim outlook. For fiscal 2027, which ends in September 2027, Amdocs expects revenue to fall 14.3% to 17.9%. Part of that comes from leaving businesses that no longer fit its new strategy, such as selling its media unit and exiting hardware resale. Even excluding those, the decline is 7% to 11%. According to Fierce Network, non-GAAP earnings per share are expected to drop 26% to 30%.

## The rest of the picture

Fiscal 2026 itself was actually decent. Revenue reached $4.70 billion (about ₪15 billion), up 3.6%, and non-GAAP EPS rose 6%. The same day, Amdocs announced an eight-year deal with AT&T, its largest customer, built around aOS, the agentic operating system it sells to carriers. It added deals with VodafoneThree in the UK, VEON and Globe in the Philippines.

Another move shows where the company wants to go: it bought EIS, which builds a platform for insurers, for about $340 million (about ₪1.09 billion) in cash. It is Amdocs' first step into insurance, an industry it says resembles telecom, with heavy regulation, mission-critical systems and lots of data.

Hortig called the dispute "an isolated situation involving one customer" and said the rest of the business should keep growing next year. For fiscal 2028, the company expects a return to mid-single-digit revenue growth and a steadily rising dividend. The board even approved a 3% increase to the quarterly dividend, pending shareholder approval in January.

## Why Israelis should watch this

Amdocs is one of the long-standing employers in Israeli tech. It already went through a restructuring this year, and its annual report shows about $167 million in restructuring payments. A double-digit revenue decline forecast is rarely good news for staff. The company has not announced new cuts following the report, but it is worth watching over the coming months, along with the arbitration with T-Mobile, if it gets that far.
