Not long ago, Mini LED was something you bought when you were ready to spend. Now Xiaomi is bringing its X Mini LED range to Israel through official importer Hamilton, starting at ₪1,549 for the 43-inch model. The 55-inch costs ₪2,290, the 65-inch ₪2,749 and the 75-inch ₪3,490.

I haven't lived with this TV for weeks. I went through the spec sheet, what Xiaomi and the importer promise, and what's missing, to work out whether the low price is a bargain or a compromise. The answer is a bit of both.

![The Xiaomi TV X Mini LED, labelled: QD-Mini LED with local dimming and HDR10+, a 120Hz Game Boost mode on a 60Hz panel, and Google TV with a built-in Idan+ tuner](img/posts/xiaomi-tv-x-mini-led-annotated-en.webp "What you get. Image: Xiaomi / Graphic: TechSite")

## What Mini LED is, and why it matters

On an ordinary LED TV, the backlight behind the screen is more or less on all at once. Mini LED uses lots of tiny LEDs grouped into zones that can dim independently. In a dark scene with a bright moon, the zones around the moon switch off, black stays black, and the moon doesn't wash out the sky. That's local dimming.

This is QD-Mini LED, meaning there's also a quantum dot layer for stronger colour. Xiaomi quotes 93% DCI-P3 coverage and 1.07 billion colours. What it doesn't publish is the number of dimming zones, and that's exactly the figure that separates good Mini LED from average. More zones means less blooming around bright objects. At this price I'd assume the count is modest. Even so, it's a real step up from a plain LED TV for the same money.

## Picture and HDR

It's 4K, with HDR10+, HLG and Filmmaker Mode, which switches off most of the processing so films look the way the director intended. There's no Dolby Vision, a small minus if you watch a lot of Netflix and Disney+, where plenty of content comes in Dolby Vision. That content will play in regular HDR10, so it still looks good, just without the extra layer.

## Gaming: read the fine print

The panel is 60Hz. Xiaomi advertises "Game Boost 120Hz", but that mode doubles the refresh rate by dropping resolution. It isn't true 4K at 120Hz. There's ALLM, so the TV switches to game mode on its own when a console is connected, and MEMC motion smoothing up to 4K at 60.

Only one of the three HDMI ports is HDMI 2.1. The other two are HDMI 2.0. If you have a PS5 or Xbox Series X and want 4K at 120 frames, this isn't your TV. If you play now and then, football or family games, you'll be fine.

## Google TV in Hebrew, with Idan+ built in

This is where Xiaomi gets things right for Israel. It runs Google TV with a Hebrew interface and all the usual apps: Netflix, YouTube, Disney+ and the local streaming apps. Google Cast, Miracast and AirPlay are all there, so you can cast from Android or an iPhone. And there's a built-in Idan+ tuner, so you can watch the free-to-air channels without a box or a subscription.

The weaker part is a quad-core A55 chip and 2GB of RAM. That's the floor for Google TV, and TVs with this spec tend to feel sluggish at times, especially switching between apps. 32GB of storage is enough for apps and not much else. If that bothers you, a cheap streaming stick in an HDMI port fixes it.

## Sound

You get Dolby Audio, DTS:X and Xiaomi Sound. It's thin-TV sound: fine for the news and series, less so for action films. There's eARC and an optical out for a soundbar, but no headphone jack, so headphones mean Bluetooth.

## Design and ports

The frame is a one-piece metal design and fairly slim. The 55-inch model is 6.6 cm deep and weighs 11.5 kg. There are three HDMI inputs, all up to 4K at 60Hz, plus USB 2.0, LAN, Wi-Fi 5 and Bluetooth 5.0. Watch the feet: they sit out at the sides, and on the 55-inch they're 95 cm apart. If you're not wall-mounting it, you need a stand at least that wide.

## Price and where to buy in Israel

The range is sold in Xiaomi stores, on the Xiaomi Israel site and through retailers, with a two-year Hamilton warranty. Prices are ₪1,549 (about $491) for 43 inches, ₪2,290 (about $725) for 55, ₪2,749 (about $871) for 65 and ₪3,490 (about $1,105) for 75.

The best value, to my mind, is the 65-inch. It's only ₪459 more than the 55, and the price per inch is almost the same at about ₪42. The 75-inch works out to about ₪47 an inch. The 43-inch is cheap, but at that size Mini LED makes less of a difference, and for a small bedroom an ordinary TV will do the job.

## The bottom line

7/10

The Xiaomi TV X Mini LED gives you a lot of TV for the money: Mini LED with quantum dots, Google TV in Hebrew, a built-in Idan+ tuner and a two-year warranty from an official importer. It loses points for the 60Hz panel, the single HDMI 2.1 port and 2GB of RAM that can slow the interface down. For series, sport and films, especially at 65 inches, it's a very good buy. If you game on a current console, spend a bit more and look for a 120Hz panel.
:::

**What I liked**

- Mini LED with local dimming at an ordinary TV price
- Google TV in Hebrew, with Cast and AirPlay
- Built-in Idan+ tuner
- HDR10+ and Filmmaker Mode
- Two-year warranty from an official importer
- The 65-inch model is especially good value

**What I liked less**

- 60Hz panel, with 120Hz only at reduced resolution
- Just one HDMI 2.1 port
- Only 2GB of RAM
- Xiaomi doesn't say how many dimming zones there are
- No Dolby Vision and no headphone jack
