On this site we write a lot about machines that clean the house. So when a game whose whole point is tidying up the yard hit Overwhelmingly Positive on Steam, I had to look. In **One More Lawn** you're a grumpy old man on an old ride-on mower. It's Saturday, 5:42 PM, the engine won't start, the beer is warm, and nobody has cut this grass in weeks.

It's made by independent developer **Superdou**, who writes the store page in the first person and says all the radio songs are his. It came out on November 12, and 95% of 1,158 user reviews are positive. I went through what the game offers, how it's built and what players are saying, and here's my verdict.

![One More Lawn at a glance: Steam prices in Israel and the US, review score, release date, platform, co-op, jobs, map editor and languages](img/posts/one-more-lawn-glance-en.webp "The short version. Graphic: TechSite")

## What you actually do

You mow. That's all, and that's the point. Hold the key until the engine coughs to life, light a cigarette, crack a can and switch on the radio strapped to the hood. One hand on the wheel. The wheel turns in front of you, and there's a mechanical dashboard: key, blade switch, deck-height lever, lights, fuel gauge and radio.

The real star is the grass. It's thick and tall, sways in the wind, flattens under the wheels and falls blade by blade under the deck. Every pass leaves a crisp edge, and the stripes on the lawn follow the way you drove. When you're done you can watch the whole job again as a timelapse. Players who love it keep coming back to the same word: relaxing. It's the game you put on when you want to switch your brain off.

![A view from the mower in One More Lawn: hands on a red steering wheel, a dashboard with fuel gauge and radio, and a striped lawn between trees](img/posts/one-more-lawn-cockpit-screenshot.webp "From the old man's seat. Image: Superdou")

The mower can't reach everything, so you climb down. Grab the string trimmer and clean up along the fence and around the garden gnome. Out of fuel? Walk to the gas can. Nobody's bringing it to you.

## Four jobs, and every client has opinions

You start with your own front yard. Then the neighbours start calling.

- **Elvira** pays well and is picky: every blade cut, five centimetres and not a millimetre more, and nothing broken. Especially not her gnome. At the end she writes you a receipt, with comments
- **Rubén** wants the old Los Teros golf club back in shape: greens, fairways and tees, each at its own height. Between passes you can play a few holes, fish lost balls out of the pond, rake the bunkers, and pull the handbrake in the wet car park to see whether a mower can drift. It can
- **Marcela**'s niece is getting married on Saturday. 120 barefoot guests, no second takes. Load the mower onto an old ferry, cross the lake and mow an entire island. Drive into the water and the engine drowns, though you can push it out. There's also a boat, a fishing rod, a hammock, coconuts and a beach bar

It's a simple structure, but it works, because each place feels different and each client's demands change how you work. Four jobs isn't a lot, though. Anyone after dozens of hours of content will mostly find them in the map editor.

## Up to six friends on the same lawn

Online co-op supports up to six players, each on their own mower, all on the same lawn. Join through a Steam invite or a six-digit code. There's proximity voice chat, everyone's radio plays from their own hood, and there are plenty of ways to get nothing done: wave, point, dance, throw fruit at someone's head, or punch a friend in the face. No damage, just dizziness. A lot of the positive reviews come from groups of friends, and one player summed it up as coming for the radio and staying for mowing with friends.

## A map editor, and your own music

There's a built-in map editor: paint grass, sand, dirt and paths, dig ponds, raise hills, plant trees, put up fences and scatter gnomes. Hit Test Drive and mow it. You can share maps on the Steam Workshop and play other people's. That's what could keep the game going well past the four jobs.

You can also load music files from your PC into the mower's radio and share them with friends in your session. Nothing gets uploaded. For streamers, there's a Streamer Mode that blocks player-loaded music.

## What to know before buying it for kids

Pay attention here, because the game looks like a gentle, cosy thing, and partly it isn't.

- **Smoking and drinking:** the character smokes and drinks beer on screen, including while driving the mower. Drinking blurs the screen and makes steering harder
- **The radio:** the songs are comedy tracks with strong language, swearing included, and toilet and sexual humour. The radio starts off and can be switched off any time
- **Voice chat:** co-op voice chat is unmoderated
- **Ratings:** it's rated 12 in Germany and 14 in Brazil

## AI on the radio

The developer states on the store page that the radio songs were made with Suno and some sound effects with ElevenLabs, under commercial licences, then selected, edited and mixed by him. AI coding assistants were used too. The game shows a notice about it when you turn the radio on. Players are split: some say they'd prefer real singers, and many others say the silly songs are the funniest thing in the game. If it bothers you, leave the radio off or load your own music.

## What it runs on

Windows PC only. Minimum requirements are modest: 64-bit Windows 10, a Core i5-4460 or Ryzen 3 1200, 8GB of RAM and a GTX 960 or RX 460, with 2GB of free storage. The developer says that's enough for 1080p on low or 720p on medium. You can play with a steering wheel and pedals with force feedback, a gamepad, or mouse and keyboard. There are 22 languages, but no Hebrew.

:::score
## The bottom line

8/10

One More Lawn takes a chore most people put off every Saturday and turns it into one of the most relaxing games of the year. The grass looks and behaves beautifully, the stripes are satisfying, and the clients and locations give each job its own character. In co-op with friends it becomes happy nonsense. There are only four jobs, and the smoking, drinking and crude songs aren't for every age. At ₪25.49, and less on sale, it's one of the safest bets on Steam right now.
:::

**What I liked**

- Grass that reacts to wind, wheels and blades, and stripes that stay
- A timelapse of the whole job at the end
- Clients whose demands change how you play
- Up to six players, with proximity voice chat
- A map editor with Workshop support
- Low price and modest hardware needs

**What I didn't**

- Only four jobs
- Smoking and drinking while driving, and crude radio songs
- AI-generated songs, if that bothers you
- Unmoderated voice chat
- No Hebrew, and PC only
