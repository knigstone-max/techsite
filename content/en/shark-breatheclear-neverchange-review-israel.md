Anyone who owns an air purifier knows the moment. A red light comes on, and the replacement filter turns out to cost a good chunk of what you paid for the machine. Plenty of people just keep running it with a clogged filter, and it turns into a very expensive fan. Shark's BreatheClear NeverChange line goes after that problem directly: up to six years without a new filter.

The two compact models in the range have reached Israel through Sarig, Shark's local importer. The HP062 costs ₪799 (about $256) and the HP162 costs ₪990 (about $318). I went through the specs on both and dug into what the numbers actually mean. Here's my take.

## Two sizes, same brains

The difference between the two is mostly size and power. Inside, they work the same way.

The **HP062** is the small one: roughly 23 x 24 cm at the base, 29 cm tall, 2.58 kg and 20 W. Shark rates it for rooms up to 27 m². Think bedroom, kids' room, home-office corner or safe room (mamad).

The **HP162** is just under 9 cm taller (37.85 cm), weighs 3.77 kg and draws 25 W. Shark rates it for up to 70 m², which covers a typical Israeli living room or an open living-and-kitchen space.

![HP062 vs HP162: Israeli price, room size, power draw, weight and height](img/posts/shark-breatheclear-compare-en.webp "The two models in numbers: room size is the real difference")

## What "70 m²" really means

This is where the numbers get a little slippery. The 27 and 70 m² figures are the room size where the purifier swaps out all the air once an hour. That keeps the air generally cleaner, but it isn't much.

If you have allergies, a different figure matters. Shark also lists the room size for 4.8 air changes an hour: 6 m² for the HP062 and 14 m² for the HP162. So for real allergy relief, the small one suits a study or a mamad, and the big one an ordinary bedroom.

Shark isn't alone here, almost every brand leads with the big number. Still, it's worth knowing before you buy the small one for the living room.

## The six-year filter

This is what sets the range apart. Both models use four-layer filtration that Shark says captures up to 99.98% of particles: dust, pollen, pet dander and smoke. There's an activated-carbon layer for odours and a washable pre-filter called DebrisDefence. It catches hair and coarse dust before they reach the main filter, which is why the main one can last so long.

The fine print is worth reading. "Six years" comes from testing under a Chinese standard (GB/T18801-2022), running 12 hours a day at full power, until performance drops by half. So after six years the purifier still works, but at half its original output. That's reasonable, but it isn't six years of day-one performance.

The cost maths is simple. With no yearly filter to buy, the sticker price is nearly the whole lifetime cost, apart from electricity. At 20 to 25 W, that part barely registers.

## Sensors and display

Both models sample the air 3,600 times an hour, tracking particles at three sizes (PM1, PM2.5 and PM10) plus volatile organic compounds, such as cleaning-spray fumes or frying smells. In auto mode, the purifier ramps up when the air gets dirty and backs off once it's clean.

There's also a motion sensor that boosts cleaning when someone walks in, which makes sense, since moving around kicks up dust. A light sensor dims the screen in the dark, and you get a sleep mode and a child lock. The display shows air quality and filter status, so there's no guessing.

## Who it suits in Israel

Israelis tend to buy purifiers for two reasons: dust, especially on hamsin days, and spring pollen allergies. The HP162 has enough muscle for a living room on a dusty day. The HP062 makes particular sense in a mamad, a small sealed room where many families sleep or work and the air gets stuffy fast.

The importer warranty runs two years (one plus one), and both units are sold through retail chains, authorised dealers and Sarig's website.

## What's missing

Shark hasn't published a CADR (clean air delivery rate) for these models, or noise levels in decibels. Those are the two figures that help most when comparing purifiers, and without them it's hard to say how these stack up. Shark doesn't list an app or Wi-Fi either, if that matters to you. And at this price there are rivals with cheap replacement filters. If you don't mind a filter change once a year, you may find more power for the same money.

## The bottom line

7.5/10

Shark's range solves a real problem: nobody enjoys buying filters, and here you barely have to. The HP062 is a good pick for a mamad or bedroom, and the HP162 for the living room. They lose points because Shark doesn't publish CADR or noise figures, and because the headline room sizes flatter them.
:::

**What I loved**

- Up to six years with no filter to buy, plus a washable pre-filter
- Sensors for three particle sizes and VOCs, and a motion sensor that boosts cleaning
- Low power draw, 20 to 25 W
- Compact enough for a mamad or bedroom
- Two-year importer warranty

**What I liked less**

- No official CADR or noise figures
- The advertised room size is for basic cleaning, not allergies
- "Six years" is measured until performance halves
- No app or Wi-Fi listed
