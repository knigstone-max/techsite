I haven't looked forward to a Call of Duty like this in a long time. After Modern Warfare III and Black Ops 7, which to me felt like games that shipped before they were ready, I'd started to lose faith in the series. Every year it was the same ritual: buy it, play for two weeks, and feel like something was missing. Modern Warfare 4 broke that streak for me, and it did it on the very first evening.

Everything here was written after long days in front of the PS5 in my living room, DualSense in hand and headset on. The game launched on October 23, 2026 for PS5, Xbox Series X|S, PC and Nintendo Switch 2, and anyone who pre-ordered a digital edition got early access to the campaign from October 16. If you're still on a PS4, you're out of luck this time: there's no PS4 or Xbox One version. The game comes from Infinity Ward, the studio that has carried the Modern Warfare series from the start, and it shows everywhere.

## The campaign: finally, a story you want to finish

The story opens on the Korean Peninsula. North Korea launches a full-scale invasion of the South, and you step into the boots of Private Park, a young South Korean soldier seeing live combat for the first time alongside his squad. What starts as a routine patrol turns into total chaos within minutes, and the squad gets dragged into fighting through collapsing cities and desperate counterattacks. It's a brand new setting for the series, and I didn't expect it to hit me this hard. There's something about a young, scared soldier who doesn't really know what he's doing that pulls you into the war far more than any super soldier in uniform.

![A helmeted South Korean soldier in body armor stands with a rifle in front of a burning building at night](img/posts/mw4-campaign-burning-street.webp "The front line in Korea at night. Image: Activision")

The second thread brings back Captain Price, in his darkest version yet. After the assassination of General Shepherd in Modern Warfare III, Task Force 141 is disbanded and Price goes AWOL. Ghost and Gaz, his closest friends, are ordered to capture or kill their former commander, and only Kate Laswell still keeps in touch with him. With no one on his side, Price is forced to team up with Valeria Garza, leader of the Las Almas cartel. If you played the previous games, you know how strange that pairing is, and that's exactly why every scene they share crackles. While both wars rage, a devastating weapon is on its way to a mysterious enemy known as "The Archer", and that's where all the threads start to meet.

![A close-up of Captain Price with a thick beard and a hard stare](img/posts/mw4-campaign-captain-price.webp "Captain Price, now outside the law. Image: Activision")

What grabbed me most about the campaign is the variety. Trench warfare in Korea, close-quarters fighting in New York, frantic chases in Paris, SAS night raids in Mumbai, and assaults on entire cities to take back occupied territory. Every chapter feels like a different movie, and the pace barely lets you breathe. There are cinematic moments that stayed with me long after I turned the console off, and quieter ones too, where the game simply lets you feel how bad things have gotten. The tone is heavier and more grown-up than before, and the story ties up threads left open across almost a decade of Modern Warfare. If you played the previous trilogy, this feels like a worthy ending.

![A South Korean soldier with a flag patch on his shoulder sits inside a dark military vehicle](img/posts/mw4-campaign-rok-soldier.webp "The South Korean squad on the way to the front. Image: Activision")

## Multiplayer: lots of maps, a steady hand

At launch there are 20 multiplayer maps: 12 new 6v6 maps, four 2v2 Gunfight maps, three huge Ground War maps, and Kill Block, a modular training facility that changes its layout between rounds. Classic Shipment joins on October 27, and I already know I'll waste too many nights on it.

The maps themselves are very varied. Silkworm is a dense South Korean shopping district with a convenience store, a gaming den and a subway entrance. Rooftops takes place on the snowy roofs of a New York hotel, Nautical on a burning ferry at dawn, Reactor U92 inside a nuclear reactor in meltdown, and Transit 213 in an abandoned bus lot in India. Every map looks completely different, and in the first week I never once felt like I was playing the same place over and over.

![An operator vaults over a car on a Korean shopping street full of colorful signs](img/posts/mw4-korean-street-firefight.webp "A firefight on a Korean street. Image: Activision")

Kill Block is the big surprise. The map is built from pieces that reconnect every round, and Activision says it has more than 500 different configurations. In practice that means you can't memorize routes, and every round forces you to rethink where to stand and where they'll come from. If you like Gunfight, it's addictive.

But what really keeps pulling me back is how the guns feel. Infinity Ward calls the new system Ballistic Authority, and the bottom line is that bloom is gone. When you hip fire, the bullet goes where the barrel points. Recoil, weapon handling and enemy visibility have all been rebuilt, and the result is that when I lose a gunfight, I know it was me and not luck. Movement feels good too: fluid and controlled, letting you flank and find angles, but without the madness of Black Ops 7. There's time to think, and still never a dull moment.

## DMZ is back, and better than ever

If one mode had me saying "just one more round" at 2am, it's DMZ. It returns with an open map called Hajin, a huge Korean war zone with an air base, a hospital, a nuclear reactor, a mall, a wharf and a stadium. You can drop in solo or with a squad, playing an off-the-books asset sent to recover advanced military tech left in the field after the fighting.

Every trip into Hajin forces decisions: what to take, who to fight, who to make a deal with, and when to just get out. The weather shifts, military objectives pop up as you go, and enemy forces patrol the area, so no two runs ever look the same. Between deployments you return to your Forward Operating Base (FOB), which grows with you: a 3D printer to make gear, a Gunsmith for building weapons, a vendor, a stash and a firing range. Progression there is slow and satisfying, and there's always one more thing you want to unlock.

![A squad of three masked operators with night gear advances beside an armored vehicle in a forest](img/posts/mw4-dmz-squad.webp "A DMZ squad in Hajin. Image: Activision")

## On PS5 it just feels right

I played on a standard PS5, and the game runs smoothly and looks great. PS5 Pro owners get an enhanced version, since the game is marked PS5 Pro Enhanced. The DualSense does a lovely job: vibration and adaptive triggers are supported, and every weapon feels a little different in your hands. At first I thought it was a gimmick, and after one evening I didn't want to play without it.

Online supports up to 60 players, and remember that you need a PS Plus subscription to play online. The campaign works without it. Remote Play is supported too, so you can keep going on your phone or PlayStation Portal when someone at home grabs the TV.

## What it costs

On the Israeli PlayStation Store the Standard Edition costs ₪319 ($69.99 on the US store), and the Vault Edition costs ₪439 ($99.99 in the US). The Vault includes skins for Price, Valeria, Ghost and Blix, a Special Forces pack with four more skins, five signature weapon blueprints, one season of BlackCell and a DMZ deployment bonus. If you don't live in multiplayer, the Standard Edition is plenty.

## Visuals and sound

This is one of the best-looking games I've seen on PS5. The Korean nights, the fire, the smoke, the rain and the city lights look fantastic, and the characters in the cutscenes are very convincing. The sound deserves every bit of praise too: part of Ballistic Authority is realistic sound propagation, and with good headphones you can hear exactly where footsteps and gunfire are coming from. In multiplayer it's a real advantage, and in the campaign it's just chilling.

## Who it's for

If you love cinematic campaigns, this is the best one the series has had in years. If you're a multiplayer player who got fed up with the pace of recent entries, Modern Warfare 4 brings back the basic, precise feel you loved. And if you're looking for something to play with friends for the long haul, DMZ alone is worth the price of entry. If you want something relaxed or light, you'll connect less: this is a tough, violent, heavy game, rated accordingly.

## The downsides

No game is perfect, and a few things bugged me here. There's no Hebrew at all, not in the menus and not in the subtitles, so players who aren't comfortable with English will miss part of the story. Shipment only arrives a few days after launch. All three Ground War maps are set around Hajin, so there's less variety there than in the regular maps. And above all, there are in-game purchases, and online play needs PS Plus, so the real cost is higher than ₪319 if you get into it seriously.

## The bottom line

9/10

Modern Warfare 4 is the most complete Call of Duty in years: a strong campaign with a new setting and Price at his best, precise multiplayer and an addictive DMZ, all in one package. If you skipped the recent entries and were waiting for the series to come back, now's the time to return.

**What I loved**

- A cinematic, varied campaign with a new Korean setting and a dark storyline for Price
- Precise gunplay with no bloom, where every hit feels like it's on you
- 20 maps at launch, plus Kill Block, which changes every round
- DMZ in Hajin, with a base that grows with you and runs that never repeat
- Full DualSense support and an enhanced PS5 Pro version

**What I didn't**

- No Hebrew menus or subtitles
- PS Plus required for online, plus in-game purchases
- The Vault Edition is pricey (₪439)
- Shipment only arrives after launch
