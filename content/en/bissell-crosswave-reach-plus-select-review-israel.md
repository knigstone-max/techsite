Over the past month we've reviewed five wet-dry vacuums here, and nearly all of them went the same way: more power, more heat, steam, screens, apps and 6 kg of machine. Bissell's **CrossWave Reach Plus Select** (model 4509N) goes the other way. No steam, no screen, no app. What it has is a hinge that lets it lie completely flat, a head that cleans up to the wall on one side, and a dock that dries the roller with hot air. It weighs 3.99 kg and sells in Israel for **₪1,490** (about $446) from the official importer, Hamilton Group.

Bissell released it in September to mark ten years of CrossWave, one of the first lines to vacuum and wash in the same pass. I went through the spec sheet, Bissell's own Q&A, and two long tests: TechRadar's, which used it for two weeks on hardwood, tile and marble, and France's Clubic, which cleaned with it daily for three weeks on tile and lino. Here's what came out of it.

![Bissell CrossWave Reach Plus Select at a glance: prices in Israel, the US and France, runtimes, charge time, tanks, weight, noise and 180-degree lay-flat](img/posts/bissell-reach-plus-review-glance-en.webp "The numbers in one place. Graphic: TechSite")

## What it costs, and why more than abroad

- **Israel:** ₪1,490 (about $446) on the importer's site, with a 24-month warranty and delivery within 7 business days
- **US:** $329.99 (about ₪1,103) for the Reach Plus with the drying dock, before tax. The Reach without the dock is $279.99 (about ₪936)
- **France:** around €299 (about ₪1,096) for the Reach Plus Pro, which adds a spare roller and filter

So Israel pays roughly 35% more than the US pre-tax price. That's a normal gap here in this category, and it includes a local importer warranty, which on a machine full of pumps and water lines isn't nothing. Even so, the Reach Plus Select is one of the cheaper wet-dry vacs in its class in Israel, and the cheapest of the ones we've reviewed.

## How it's built

This isn't a stick vacuum with a wet roller bolted on. It's a dedicated floor washer: a slim, tall body, a handle that clips on top, a head with one large roller and a clear clean-water tank sitting on it. The clean tank holds 600 ml and the dirty tank, which clips onto the back of the body, holds 450 ml. Power and mode buttons are on the handle, and battery and mode lights face you on the body. There's no screen. Clubic's tester, who reviews vacuums all day, found his way around quickly, but noted that the mostly illustrated manual could confuse someone buying their first wet-dry vac.

There's no vacuum-only mode and no mop-only mode. It always does both. That keeps it simple, but it also means it doesn't replace a regular vacuum for rugs, the sofa or shelves.

## Lying flat at 180 degrees

This is the feature it's named for. Bissell says it's the first CrossWave to lie completely flat, and claims it reaches up to five times farther under low furniture than a Tineco model it compared against. TechRadar said it makes cleaning under the couch a moment's work, and Clubic agreed, with the obvious caveat: the body itself has to fit, so under a bed with 8 cm of clearance it won't. In Israeli flats with sofas on legs and beds with drawers, measure before counting on it.

## How well it cleans

Both tests agree that it mops very well. TechRadar spilled ketchup, mustard and coffee on the floor, and each came up in one pass. The floor was dry within minutes. Clubic cleaned coffee, sauce and mud: coffee came up straight away, mud after two passes, and sauce that had sat for hours took several passes but did come off. Both noted that it left no streaks.

Bissell credits what it calls ACTIVEFORCE, targeted pressure from the head onto the floor. Bissell says it also pushes more water off the floor, so it dries in about half the time of its own CrossWave OmniForce.

Vacuuming is good, with one clear limit. Dust, hair, crumbs, tea leaves, rice and spills all go up without fuss. Clubic stayed in Eco most of the time and said it's enough for daily cleaning. But TechRadar found that big pieces, like cereal or popcorn, just get pushed in front of the head like a curling stone. It's also weaker when pulling back, because the silicone squeegee behind the roller catches some debris. If you have small kids and bowls of cornflakes on the floor, you'll want a broom or a dry vacuum nearby.

## Walls: only on one side

Bissell sells this as ZeroGap, cleaning right up to walls and baseboards. In practice, and both tests found the same thing, it only works on the right side of the head. TechRadar measured it: on the right it gets to about a quarter of an inch from the wall, on the left about three quarters of an inch stay dirty, and at the front about an inch. Not dramatic, but you have to get used to running along walls in the right direction.

## Battery and charging

Per Bissell: up to 40 minutes in Eco, up to 30 in High and up to 20 in Turbo. TechRadar measured almost exactly that, 39:49 in Eco and 23:21 in Turbo. Clubic cleaned the whole ground floor of a house in a session of about 40 minutes, mostly in Eco, without refilling or emptying, and carried on into a good part of the upstairs. For an average Israeli apartment, that's one go.

The weaker part is charging. Bissell says about three and a half hours. Both tests measured about four and a half. The battery isn't removable, and it can't run while plugged in. Forget to put it back on the dock and today's clean moves to the evening.

## The dock: dry, clean and loud

Here Bissell did something that a lot of cheaper washers skip. When you're done, you stand it in the dock and press the clean-out cycle. Water flushes the roller and lines, then the dock dries the roller with hot air, for about half an hour according to Bissell. Clubic measured 30 to 40 minutes to a fully dry roller and called the result excellent. A wet roller sealed inside a plastic head is why most wet-dry vacs start to smell after a month, so this one really matters.

But it's loud. Clubic measured almost 70 decibels during the clean-out cycle, with bursts of a few seconds when the dock suddenly kicks in. The tester wrote that he jumped more than once. TechRadar measured about 65 dB in the clean-out cycle. While cleaning, Bissell quotes 62 to 64 dBA, and TechRadar measured 64 to 67. In a small flat, think about where the dock goes. In the hallway next to a sleeping child's room isn't a good idea.

## Upkeep

The dirty tank needs emptying and rinsing after every use. There's a strainer meant to separate hair and solids from the water, and here both tests were let down. TechRadar said everything goes into the same tank and comes out together, and Clubic said some debris stays mixed in the water even with the strainer. Not pleasant, but not worse than a lot of rivals.

Bissell recommends replacing the filter and roller about every three months. The filter is washable. You can run it on plain water, but Bissell recommends its Multi-Surface formulas. Clubic liked the scent the formula leaves, and noted that a replacement bottle costs €14.99 in France (about ₪55). Check the Israeli price first, because it's an ongoing cost.

## Hard floors only

Bissell says so outright: tile, hardwood, laminate and any sealed floor. No carpets, no rugs, no stairs and no upholstery. For Israeli homes with porcelain or granite-porcelain tile throughout, that's exactly the use. If you have a big rug in the living room, you'll need to lift it or go around it.

- **[Roborock F25 Pro Turbo](/techsite/en/posts/roborock-f25-pro-turbo-review-israel/)** costs a little more, weighs 4.7 kg, and has a trigger that boosts scrubbing for tough spots, powered wheels and a screen. It's more machine; the Bissell is simpler and lighter.
- **[Dyson Clean+Wash Hygiene](/techsite/en/posts/dyson-clean-wash-hygiene-review-israel/)** is slightly lighter (3.82 kg) and about ₪500 more. It uses no suction at all, which fixes the smell problem at the root. The Bissell does suck, so it handles hair and spills better.
- **[Tineco S9 Artist Steam Pro](/techsite/en/posts/tineco-s9-artist-steam-pro-review-israel/)** and **[Dreame H16 Pro Steam](/techsite/en/posts/dreame-h16-pro-steam-review-israel/)** add steam, but weigh 6.1 and 6.8 kg and cost more. If you have kitchen grease that won't budge, steam helps. For daily mopping, the Bissell is enough.

On a tight budget, Ivory currently has the Roborock F25 ACE on sale for ₪1,029 (about $308). We haven't reviewed it, but that's the price pressing on the Bissell from below.

## Who it's for

A flat with hard floors throughout, plenty of low sofas and beds, and someone who doesn't want to haul 6 kg around. Pet homes too: Bissell aims this at dog and cat owners, and it handles hair on hard floors well. Less so for homes with lots of rugs, anyone who needs a machine that also picks up coarse debris, or anyone who wants steam.

## The bottom line

7.5/10

The Bissell CrossWave Reach Plus Select does one thing, and does it well: it vacuums and washes hard floors in a single pass without streaks, lies flat under the sofa, and dries its roller so it doesn't smell. It's light for the category and easy to use. The downsides are real: the dock is loud, charging takes about four and a half hours, coarse debris gets pushed ahead of the head, and edge cleaning only works on one side. At ₪1,490 you pay about a third over the US price for its simplicity, and it's still a fair price for a good floor washer in Israel.
:::

**What I liked**

- Very good mopping, no streaks, dry within minutes
- 3.99 kg and easy to steer
- Full 180-degree lay-flat under furniture
- A dock that dries the roller with hot air
- A battery that covers a whole flat in one go
- Two-year importer warranty

**What I didn't**

- The dock's clean-out cycle is loud, with sudden bursts
- About four and a half hours to charge, and the battery isn't removable
- Large debris like cereal gets pushed, not picked up
- Edge cleaning only on the right side
- A strainer that doesn't separate debris from water well
- About 35% more than the US price

*Conversions use the Bank of Israel representative rate for Nov 23, 2026: ₪3.343 per dollar and ₪3.665 per euro.*
