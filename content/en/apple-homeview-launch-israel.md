For years Apple left kitchen and living-room smart displays to Google and Amazon. That ended last Thursday, when Apple HomeView, the company's first smart display, went on sale in Apple's stores and online. And like quite a few recent Apple products, it isn't coming to Israel, at least not yet.

## What it is

HomeView is a 7-inch Liquid Retina display meant to sit at the center of the home. There are two versions. The tabletop model sits on a round speaker base with a hinge that tilts the screen, in four colors: silver, starlight, rose pink and space gray. The wall-mounted model comes in silver and space gray and attaches with mounts made by Schneider.

According to ynet, it runs on an A18 chip. It plays music, handles FaceTime calls with Center Stage to keep you in frame as you move around, and shows calendars, reminders and messages. On the smart home side, it can show up to nine security camera feeds at once, control lights, temperature and locks, and recognize alarm sounds so it can alert everyone's phones.

## A screen that knows who's looking

The feature Apple is proudest of is face recognition. HomeView recognizes up to six people and shows each of them their own Messages, FaceTime, Calendar, Reminders and Find My. Apple stresses that personal content appears only when the display recognizes your face and your paired iPhone or iPad is nearby and was recently unlocked. So a kid walking past shouldn't be reading your texts.

## Siri AI, in English only

What drives the whole thing is Siri AI, the new conversational Siri. You can ask it about almost anything, it understands what's on the screen, and when HomeView is paired with an iPhone or iPad that supports Apple Intelligence, it can pull from your calendar, messages and other apps. ynet gave a nice example: ask it to find a recipe someone emailed you and add the ingredients to your shopping list.

Here's the big catch for Israeli readers. Siri AI on HomeView works only in English, and only in the US, Australia, Canada, India, New Zealand, Singapore, South Africa and the UK. Apple promises Canadian French in a future update. Hebrew isn't mentioned.

## What it costs

Both versions cost $299 (about ₪957). The wall model attaches with one of three Schneider Home mounts: a USB-C wall connector for $59.95 (about ₪192), a hardwired AC version for $79.95 (about ₪256), and a PoE version over an Ethernet cable that won't arrive until early 2027, at $129.95 (about ₪416).

Alongside the display, Apple announced a new Apple TV 4K, the HomePod mini 2, which already shipped on October 30 for $149 (about ₪477), and a line of home accessories made with LG and Schneider: cameras, a video doorbell, a smart lock and thermostats.

## Why not Israel

Sales began in seven countries: the US, the UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Singapore and India. HomeView isn't sold in the European Union at all, and Apple says outright that's because Siri AI isn't available in iOS there yet. Israel isn't on the list, and Apple hasn't said when, or whether, it will be.

If you're thinking of bringing one back from abroad, be clear about what you're paying for: a device whose value rests almost entirely on an English-speaking assistant and on an Apple Intelligence iPhone, with no local importer and no local warranty. In an English-speaking home with recent iPhones, it might be worth a try. For most Israeli households, it's still one to watch from the sidelines.
