Almost every phone in Israel has the Home Front Command (Pikud HaOref) app. The question is whether it's set up properly. The app changed a lot this year: in May 2026 it gained a system of colours and sounds that separates the different kinds of messages, and in mid-November, according to Channel 14, the Home Front Command again urged the public to make sure the app is installed and on the latest version.

Here's a clear rundown. It doesn't replace the Home Front Command's instructions, and in a real event you act on the message itself. But ten minutes of setup now saves confusion later.

## 1. Update it manually

According to Ynet, the updated version of the app doesn't install automatically. Open the App Store or Google Play, search for "Home Front Command" (פיקוד העורף) and check for an "Update" button. If you've turned off automatic updates, now's the time to turn them back on, at least for this app.

## 2. Learn the three colours

![The three Home Front Command app colours: yellow to move near a protected space, red to enter immediately, green for the all-clear, with the sound for each](img/posts/home-front-command-app-colors-en.webp "What each colour means and what to do. Graphic: TechSite")

Each message type now has its own colour, icon and sound:

- **Yellow: move close to a protected space.** An early warning when there's a possible threat to your area. It usually comes a few minutes before a siren, but sometimes no siren follows, and sometimes it arrives very close to the siren. The sound is the one people got used to in the previous round of fighting.
- **Red: enter the protected space now.** Within your area's warning time, with the rising and falling siren sound. If you hear a siren or get a red alert, go in even if there was no yellow message first.
- **Green: the event is over.** A soft, high-pitched tone. Don't leave the protected space before this message, and check it refers to your area and to the event that sent you in.

## 3. Allow precise location

To get alerts for wherever you are, the app needs location access. It asks during setup. Migdal Or's accessibility guide recommends keeping "Precise" rather than "Approximate". If you skipped this before, fix it in your phone's settings under the app's permissions.

## 4. Add up to ten areas of interest

This is the most useful change. You can set **up to ten areas of interest**: home, work, your parents' place, the kids' kindergarten. That way you get information about those places even when you're not there.

In big cities split into several alert zones, the app also asks for a street name so it can assign you to the right sub-zone. Tel Aviv, for example, has several. Don't skip it.

## 5. Tell "here" from "there"

The app separates alerts where you are from alerts in one of your areas of interest. According to the app's official page, as quoted by Pooz, alerts for areas of interest have their own sound with adjustable volume. In practice: when your phone goes off, read the place name before you run. An alert in your parents' town doesn't mean you need to go to the safe room. It means you might want to call them afterwards.

## 6. Lock screen and history

According to reports on the update, the type of event and the required action now show directly on the lock screen, without unlocking the phone. All messages are collected in the app in chronological order. If you get a green message, you can scroll back and confirm it belongs to the right event.

Check in your phone's settings that the app's notifications are allowed on the lock screen and that Do Not Disturb isn't blocking them.

## 7. Sound, flashlight and language

In the app's settings you can choose the alert type, such as a sound or a flashing light, and the language. The flashlight option helps people who are hard of hearing, or at night when the phone is across the room.

## 8. If GPS is being jammed

During periods of GPS interference, your phone may think you're somewhere else, which affects location-based alerts. The IDF's 2024 advice for that situation is to also add the place where you are, or plan to be, as an area of interest. Heading up north for a holiday or visiting family? Add the place before you leave.

![Checklist: update the app, precise location, areas of interest with a street, separate sound, lock screen, flashlight and language, and an area of interest during GPS jamming](img/posts/home-front-command-app-checklist-en.webp "The full checklist. Graphic: TechSite")

## On the TV too

According to Channel 14, the new version is also available on smart TVs and set-top boxes running Android TV and Google TV, including Cellcom and Partner boxes and some yes and HOT boxes. If you have one in the living room, look for the app in its store.

## One more thing

The app's home screen shows the warning time for your area, meaning how long you have to reach a protected space. It's worth looking at once and thinking about where the nearest protected space is in each place you spend time regularly.

Most important: the app is one tool among several. Don't assume your area is safe just because your phone is quiet. Follow the Home Front Command's current instructions, and keep your phone charged.
