Most survival games have a health bar. You get hit, the bar goes down, you eat something, the bar goes up. **Casualties: Unknown** throws the bar away. Instead there's a body: a heart that pumps a measured amount of blood, arms and legs that each get hurt separately, wounds that get infected if you don't clean them, and a head that can despair. A splinter of glass in the foot isn't a nuisance here. It can be the start of the end.

It came out on Steam on November 9, built by a single developer who goes by **Orsoniks** and published by **Oro Interactive**. Its free demo had been one of the most-played on Steam, with over half a million players according to PlayInsider, and the full game is sitting at 97% positive user reviews. I went through what the full release adds, how its systems work and what players and critics are saying, and here is my verdict.

![Casualties: Unknown at a glance: release date, number of layers, install size, and Steam prices in Israel and the US](img/posts/casualties-unknown-overview-en.webp "The short version. Graphic: TechSite")

## What it's about

You are an **Expie**, a disposable test subject. Captors you never see drop you onto an alien planet to recover lost cargo. Thousands were sent down before you, and none came back. The only way is down: **eleven layers of caves**, each stranger and more hostile than the one above.

The view is 2D, from the side. You jump, climb, slide down slopes, break open locked crates and capsules left by earlier expeditions, and read notes on an ePDA from people who were there before you and didn't make it. There's no narrator and no cut-scenes in the usual sense. The story comes from those notes, and from the way your character reacts to what happens to them.

That's the part that makes it work. Your Expie whimpers, wheezes, panics, and yells "GET AWAY!" when something comes too close. There's no voice acting, just sounds, but within a few minutes you stop seeing a sprite and start seeing a small frightened creature that depends on you. Several reviews describe exactly this: deaths that feel personal, and runs you care about.

## The body is the game

This is the heart of Casualties: Unknown, and nothing else on the market quite does it.

![Screenshot from Casualties: Unknown: a body diagram with injuries marked limb by limb, a "Remove shrapnel" prompt on the left thigh, and vital-sign panels showing blood, heart rate, breathing and temperature](img/posts/casualties-unknown-body-screen.webp "The health screen: every limb on its own, with vital signs on the sides. Beta build. Screenshot: Orsoniks / Oro Interactive")

The health screen shows a body diagram. Every limb gets its own wounds, and every wound has a treatment. A cut bleeds until you bandage it. A dirty wound gets infected and needs antibiotics. Too much blood lost and you pass out. Bones break and joints dislocate, and you need a splint. Then there's hypothermia, poisoning, and even strokes from overexertion.

Treatment is hands-on. You don't press "heal". You drag a dressing around the limb with the mouse, and a "Remove shrapnel" prompt means you pull the shard out yourself. Plants in the caves provide remedies, and scraps you find become bandages and splints.

The side panels show more than most games would dare: blood volume, heart rate, breathing, body temperature, oxygen. In the full release an opiate and venom readout was added. It looks overwhelming at first, and that's deliberate. Learning to read your body is learning to play.

The extreme case says it all. **Losing a leg doesn't end the run.** With a tourniquet, antibiotics and some luck you can survive an amputation, and the full release adds **prosthetics** in three tiers that get you moving again, in the best case even better than before. That's the moment the game stops being a "survival game" and becomes a story about one specific stubborn creature.

## Your head matters too

Mood is a survival stat of its own. Fear and despair make everything harder, and Orsoniks treats it as something you have to manage, like hunger or blood loss. The Steam page carries a content warning: **depression, violence, dismemberment, drug use and self-harm**. This isn't a game that brushes those themes in passing. They're part of how it works. If any of them hit close to home right now, give it a miss, and that's a completely reasonable call.

## Inventory, crafting and the rest of the loop

- **The inventory is a wheel with a weight limit.** A stimulant pen competes for space with a dressing, and every slot is a decision.
- **Traits**: Intelligence, Strength and Resilience grow over a run.
- **Crafting**: tools from whatever you find, roughly 300 items in total.
- **Hazards**: mines, sound cannons, pressure plates, giant insects, and deeper down, geysers and currents.
- **Settings**: from the main menu you can adjust loot and danger levels, and there's a more relaxed mode for those who want to learn the body without dying every five minutes.

## What the full game adds over the demo

That's the big question for anyone who sank hours into the free demo. Its five layers stay on Steam, though Orsoniks says it won't be updated. The full game adds:

- **Layers 6 to 11**: the second half of the descent
- **Three endings**
- **Two more characters**, three in all, each with its own playstyle. Each one unlocks only by finishing a run with the previous one
- **Endless mode**, prosthetics, new traps, item customisation and **40 achievements**
- A long list of balance changes, according to the patch notes

Some of the negative Steam reviews say the full game doesn't add enough over the demo. The counter-argument is the ending: the demo doesn't have one, and the full game ends in a scene, not a credit roll, with more endings waiting.

## Where it drags

I'm not going to pretend it's flawless. The criticism repeats across reviews, and it's fair:

- **Layers 6 to 9 feel thin.** After a dense first half, the deeper layers feel more like a sprint to the exit than exploration. One player wrote that from layer 5 on, the game "wants you to do a mad dash". PlayInsider says much the same.
- **No save and quit mid-layer.** You save only after clearing a depth. If you've got 20 minutes before dinner, that's a problem.
- **Tutorials locked behind progress**, and crafting that turns into a grind.
- **Effects that hide the screen.** When double vision, blur and darkness stack up, sometimes you just can't see. That's the point, but there's no accessibility option to tone it down.
- **Leftover bugs**: ragdoll physics and water can behave strangely, and traps sometimes hide under objects. Patch 1.0.1 arrived two days after launch and fixed some of it.
- **Unlocking characters**: having to finish the game to unlock the next character, and then again for the one after, will put some people off.

![Screenshot from Casualties: Unknown: the inventory wheel with item slots around the character, and an item description panel on the right](img/posts/casualties-unknown-inventory.webp "The inventory wheel: every slot is a decision. Beta build. Screenshot: Orsoniks / Oro Interactive")

## Graphics, sound and tech

Pixel art with painted key art, and lighting that does most of the work: glowing plants, pitch-black caves, fog, and a distinct palette for each layer. Injuries are shown graphically, but consistently, not for shock value.

The soundtrack is by Orsoniks himself: more than 50 tracks and over three hours of music, also sold separately on Steam. It carries the oppressive mood, and the Expie's whimpers and gasps make every injury land without a single line of dialogue.

On the technical side it's tiny: **500 MB** of storage, Windows 10 64-bit and a Core i3-8130U at minimum. It'll run on almost any laptop, and PlayInsider reports Valve rates it **Playable** on Steam Deck. It's Windows only, with no Mac or Linux version on Steam, and text is in English and Simplified Chinese only. There's no Hebrew, but there's not much reading outside the notes, and the language is simple.

## Price in Israel

On the Israeli Steam store it costs **₪76.99** (about $23), with a launch discount of 15% that brings it to **₪65.44** (about $19.70) until November 23. In the US it's $22.99 (about ₪76), or $19.54 (about ₪65) during the discount. The free demo is still on Steam. If you're unsure, start there. If you get hooked, the full game is the second half of the same thing, plus an ending.

## Who it's for

Fans of Project Zomboid, Rain World or Barotrauma, who want a game where every decision has a price and the tension comes from thinking rather than reflexes. Anyone who's been looking for a survival game with Metal Gear Solid 3-style medical care, as one YouTuber put it.

Less for: anyone who wants quick sessions with a save at any point, anyone who's sensitive to dark themes, and anyone hoping for a big open world. The caves are procedurally generated and fairly basic in structure, and the deep layers don't give you much reason to stop and look around.

## The bottom line

8/10

Casualties: Unknown is the most detailed body-survival game I've come across, and one of the most touching. Bleeding, infection, fractures, amputation and prosthetics, together with a character whose fear you can hear, turn every run into a personal story. Its second half is thinner than its first, there's no save mid-layer, tutorials and characters are locked behind progress, and the darkness is sometimes too much, in more than one sense. For ₪65 to ₪77, it's still one of the most original indie games of the year.
:::

**What I liked**

- A body simulation with nothing comparable on the market: limbs, blood, infection, amputation, prosthetics
- A character that reacts to everything and makes you care
- Hands-on treatment: dragging dressings, pulling out shrapnel
- Three characters, three endings and an endless mode
- Adjustable difficulty and loot from the main menu
- Runs on almost any computer, 500 MB in all
- A strong soundtrack of more than three hours

**What I liked less**

- Layers 6 to 9 feel like a sprint to the exit
- No save and quit mid-layer
- Tutorials and characters locked behind progress
- Visual effects that hide the screen, with no accessibility options
- Some bugs remain after the patch
- Windows only, English and Chinese only
- Very dark themes that won't suit everyone
